### Printed p. i  (PDF 7)

In this part of the Ṛksaṃhitā there are the Sūktas of Maṇḍala 1, **122–136** (as printed "೧೨೨-೧೩೬"). Of these, Sūktas 122–126 have as their seer Kakṣīvān Dairghatamasa Auśija, and Sūktas 127–136 have as their seer Parucchepa Daivodāsi.

About the seer Kakṣīvān, the Bṛhaddevatā and other works say this: a king named Kaliṅga had no children. He was old and unable to beget a son. At that time the seer Dīrghatamas, son of Ucathya, came near the king, and the king asked him to beget a son on his behalf, since he had none, in his royal wife. In earlier times, when people had no children for some reason, there was the custom of obtaining sons for the continuance of the line from a qualified Brāhmaṇa; this is described clearly in the Purāṇas and the Mahābhārata. The seer agreed to the king's request, and the king told his chief queen to go to the seer. The queen, being ashamed to have intercourse with a seer who was old and ugly, did not wish it, and in her place she adorned her maidservant named Uśik beautifully with her own clothes and ornaments and sent her to the seer. When the woman Uśik came to him, the seer, knowing that she was a maidservant and not the queen, sprinkled her with mantra-consecrated water, made her pure like a wife, and then had intercourse with her. The son born to Uśik by that seer is the seer Kakṣīvān. It is said of him that he is extremely famous among those who perform sacrificial rites:

> एवं वै पर आट्णारः कक्षीवाँ औशिजो वीतहव्यः श्रायसस्त्रसदस्युः पौरुकुत्स्नः प्रजाकामा अचिन्तयत ॥

> *evaṃ vai para āṭṇāraḥ kakṣīvā~ auśijo vītahavyaḥ śrāyasastrasadasyuḥ paurukutsnaḥ prajākāmā acintayata ‖*

(Taittirīya Saṃhitā, as printed "೫-೬-೫-೩" [?].) This is said in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā of the Yajurveda. Therefore this seer is extremely famous both in composing mantras and among those who perform sacrificial rites. This seer is the seer of the Sūktas 116–125 of Maṇḍala 1 of the Ṛgveda (in all 146 ṛks), of ṛks 1–5 of Sūkta 126 (5 ṛks), and of Sūkta 74 of Maṇḍala 9 (9 ṛks), altogether 160 ṛks (as printed: "೧೧೬-೧೨೫ … ಒಟ್ಟು ೧೪೬ … ೧೨೬ … ೧-೫ … ೫ … ೭೪ … ೯ … ಒಟ್ಟು ೧೬೦"). ### Printed p. ii  (PDF 8)

In the nineteenth Anuvāka of the first Maṇḍala there are the 7 Sūktas from 127 to 133; in the next Anuvāka, the twentieth, there are the Sūktas from 134 to 139 (as printed). For the 13 Sūktas that result from these two Anuvākas, the seer is "Parucchepo Daivodāsiḥ", that is, Parucchepa, son of Divodāsa. We have told about Divodāsa earlier. Divodāsa, or Divodāsa Atithigva, was a king renowned in very ancient (Vedic) times, son of Vadhryaśva and father of Sudāsa. His name occurs in many places in the Ṛgveda. It is said in various places that his chief enemy was Śambara or Dāsa, the leader of the forest people who lived in the hills and mountains. No Sūktas seen by Divodāsa himself occur anywhere in the Ṛgveda; therefore modern scholars differ on whether his son, the seer Parucchepa, is the seer of these Sūktas. Be that as it may, according to the Anukramaṇikā — by the verse *agnim ekādaśa parucchepo daivodāsir āgneyaṃ tu pārucchepaṃ sarvam atyaṣṭi tv atidhṛtiḥ ṣaṣṭiḥ* [as printed; wording not individually verified] — Parucchepa is declared the seer of the 13 Sūktas mentioned above (127–139). Yāska says in his Nirukta:

> परुच्छेप ऋषिः । पर्ववच्छेपः परुषिपरुषि शेपोऽस्येति वा ।

> *parucchepa ṛṣiḥ | parvavacchepaḥ paruṣiparuṣi śepo 'syeti vā |*

(Nirukta 10-42 [?] — the last digit is doubtful in the small type, and the reader's OCR reads 10-42.) That is, he is called so because "he grew up in comfort and was well built in all his joints" — this is the main intention; the etymology of the word *parucchepa* is not so important here. This seer's story is also told in the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa (adhyāya 22 [?], khaṇḍa 5-6 [?]) and in the Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇa (13-4-5 [?]). In these 13 Sūktas seen by Parucchepa there are in all 100 ṛks [?: as printed "೧೦೦"]. This seer's son Anānata Pārucchepi is the seer of 3 ṛks of Sūkta 111 of Maṇḍala 9. All these ṛks have one special distinguishing feature: they are in metres named Atyaṣṭi, Atiśakvarī and Atidhṛti. Sūkta 127 is in Atiśakvarī; the remaining Sūktas (except for some ṛks) are composed in Atyaṣṭi. This kind of metre has 7 pādas and in all 68 syllables; in some places one or two syllables more or less are also found. We have described these metres, with their characteristics and examples, in detail in Ṛ. Saṃ. Part 1, pp. 221–231. Another special feature of the ṛks seen by Parucchepa is told by Yāska:

> अभ्यासे भूयांसमर्थं मन्यन्ते यथाहो दर्शनीयाहो दर्शनीयेति ।
> तत्परुच्छेपस्य शीलम् ॥

> *abhyāse bhūyāṃsamarthaṃ manyante yathāho darśanīyāho darśanīyeti |*
> *tatparucchepasya śīlam ‖*

(Nirukta 10-42 [?], the same reference again.)

### Printed p. iii  (PDF 9)

That is, in the ṛks seen by this seer repetition (*abhyāsa*, twofold utterance) is especially to be seen. Ordinarily, in the ṛks of metres such as Gāyatrī, Triṣṭubh and Jagatī there are only two parts, a first half and a second half. But in the ṛks seen by Parucchepa, in Atyaṣṭi and the like, there are three halves (*ardhas*); in the first and third halves, in the final part of each, one or two words are repeated again and again, like a refrain. For example:

> अग्निं होतारं मन्ये दास्वन्तं वसुं सूनुं सहसो जादवेदसं विप्रं न जातवेदसम् ।

> *agniṃ hotāraṃ manye dāsvantaṃ vasuṃ sūnuṃ sahaso jādavedasaṃ vipraṃ na jātavedasam |*

(printed *jādavedasaṃ* in the first place, *jātavedasaṃ* in the second [sic?]). In this ṛk the word *jātavedasam* is repeated twice; between the repetitions are the words *vipraṃ na*. This repeated word always stands at the end of the sentence.

> निष्ठमानो यमुते नायते धन्वासहा नायते ।

> *niṣṭhamāno yamute nāyate dhanvāsahā nāyate |*

[printed in small type; the first words are read doubtfully, `[?]`]. In this ṛk the words *nāyate* are repeated twice, and between them stands the word *dhanvāsahā*. Yāska explains this matter of repetition by saying "*abhyāse bhūyāṃsam arthaṃ manyante yathāho darśanīyāho darśanīyeti*" and giving its meaning. The commentators on the Nirukta say of this sentence:

> अभ्यासे भूयांसमर्थं मन्यन्ते । शब्दाभ्यासे यत्रान्यो विशेषो नास्ति तत्रैवं विशेषमाचार्या मन्त्रार्थसतत्त्वविदो [?] मन्यन्ते-यदुतार्थ-भूयस्त्वमिति । न हि कस्मात् स एव शब्दः पुनरभ्यस्यते । लोकेऽपि चैतद् दृष्टं शब्दाभ्यासेऽर्थभूयस्त्वमिति । यथा-अहो दर्शनीये! अहो दर्शनीये! इति । गुणातिशये हि स्त्रियाः शब्दाभ्यासः । तस्मान्मन्त्रेष्वपि तद्देवेति [?] न्याय्यम् ॥

> *abhyāse bhūyāṃsamarthaṃ manyante | śabdābhyāse yatrānyo viśeṣo nāsti tatraivaṃ viśeṣamācāryā mantrārthasatattvavido [?] manyante-yadutārtha-bhūyastvamiti | na hi kasmāt sa eva śabdaḥ punarabhyasyate | loke 'pi caitad dṛṣṭaṃ śabdābhyāse 'rthabhūyastvamiti | yathā-aho darśanīye! aho darśanīye! iti | guṇātiśaye hi striyāḥ śabdābhyāsaḥ | tasmānmantreṣvapi taddeveti [?] nyāyyam ‖*

That is, when one word is uttered twice in a sentence, if the pair of utterances do not have two different meanings, the repetition of the word indicates an excess of meaning — that is, it is used to give prominence to the matter that was stated, or to make the matter settle well in the mind. For example, when describing a woman's beauty one says "aho darśanīye, aho darśanīye" ("oh how beautiful, oh how beautiful"). At that point the doubling of the word expresses "ah, what beauty — what beauty!" and so makes the excess of beauty plain; this is a usage in the language of the world. Likewise it should be understood here. But Sāyaṇa ordinarily explains the repeated words of these ṛks by giving them different meanings.

This doubling has another purpose too. Many ṛks of the Ṛgveda were composed so as to be suitable for singing. When songs set to *rāga* and so on are sung …

### Printed p. iv  (PDF 10)

… the refrain and the pallavi and anupallavi are sung again and again, which is a well-known thing to all in the world. Therefore these ṛks, too, must have been composed with the aim of being sung in many ṛks so as to be audible clearly. For that reason it is also said in the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa that these Pārucchepa mantras are to be used in the recitation of the Śastra mantras. From this point of view, a large part of the Sāmaveda consists of ṛks of the Ṛgveda.

When one understands Vedic mantras in their meaning and recites or sings them with devotion and faith, with the accents, both the reciter and the listeners hearing the Vedic sound have a joy that cannot be put into words — a matter that has come within the experience of many. Some modern scholars, such as Westerners, look at the Vedic mantras only from the point of view of the primacy of meaning, and so they have no chance to learn the full beauty of the Vedic mantras; nor do they try. Some say that although these ṛks are in metre there are many omissions and violations of grammar, and that they belong to "Prauḍha" (mature, ornate) poetry. But their view is not acceptable. For at that time there was no written language. Everything had to be learned by heart. Therefore it is natural that more attention was given to pronunciation and audibility. And while writing was not known and grammar was little developed, it is natural that the words and idioms in use at that time were used. And we must not forget that the grammars composed by Pāṇini and others were composed on the basis of Vedic words. Grammar comes from language, not language from grammar. Language is the root; grammar follows it. Therefore it is not right to apply today's grammatical rules by force to the forms of the words and so on seen in the Vedic mantras.

We have said before that many ṛks were composed from the point of view of singing; for that reason, in many places short syllables are used as long and long syllables as short; this is seen here and there. For example, in words such as *adha, gha, sma* the long syllable is in the *saṃhitā-pāṭha*; likewise, *ṛtāvṛdhā* is pronounced *ṛtavṛdhā*, and so on. In many places one or two syllables of words are lost, for example *ātmanā* is written *tmanā*, and in place of *bhāvayavyasya* one finds *bhāvyasya*. Such losses and additions are not new to modern language: when singing compositions, singers sing *kṛṣṇamūrti* as "kṛṣṇā mūrati" and *kīrti* as "kīrutī". Such omissions and additions are many; since the Vedic mantras have been handed down, taught by pure utterance alone, such slight faults (which are not really faults) are not unnatural.

### Printed p. v  (PDF 11)

That the Sūktas seen by Parucchepa belong to very ancient mantras there is no doubt on the matter. The meaning of many words and sentences in these ṛks is very obscure and unclear, and so the meaning does not fit well in many places. Most of these mantras have been included among the Śastra mantras. Their details are in the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa as follows:

> देवासुरा वा एषु लोकेषु समुयतन्त ते वै देवाः षष्ठेनैवाह्नेभ्यो लोकेभ्योऽसुरान् प्राणुदन्त तेषां यान्यन्तर्हस्तीनानि वसून्यासंस्तान्यादाय समुद्रं प्रास्यन्त [?] त एतेनैव छन्दसानुहायान्तर्हस्तीनानि वसून्यादवत [?] तद्यदेतत्पदं पुनःपदं स एवाङ्कुश आसञ्जनाय । आ द्विषतो वसु वत्त्रे निरेनमेभ्यः सर्वेभ्यो लोकेभ्यो नुदते य एवं वेद ॥

> *devāsurā vā eṣu lokeṣu samuyatanta te vai devāḥ ṣaṣṭhenaivāhnebhyo lokebhyo 'surān prāṇudanta teṣāṃ yānyantarhastīnāni vasūnyāsaṃstānyādāya samudraṃ prāsyanta [?] ta etenaiva chandasānuhāyāntarhastīnāni vasūnyādavata [?] tadyadetatpadaṃ punaḥpadaṃ sa evāṅkuśa āsañjanāya | ā dviṣato vasu vattre nirenamebhyaḥ sarvebhyo lokebhyo nudate ya evaṃ veda ‖*

(Aitareya Brāhmaṇa, as printed "೨೨-೬": Ai. Brā. 22-6.) Once the gods and the Asuras fought in these worlds. Then, on the sixth day of the sacrifice (the "dvādaśāha"), the gods drove the Asuras out of these worlds. Then the Asuras took the wealth they had in their hands and fled and threw it into the ocean. Afterwards the gods pursued the Asuras and, by the power of reciting the ṛks seen by Parucchepa, got back the wealth the Asuras had thrown into the ocean. When getting it back, the seventh pāda of the ṛks seen by Parucchepa (the pāda that is repeated, the *punaḥ-parihita* pāda) helped them. In the world, to pull from a deep place something that has fallen, people use a hook (marginal English word "hook" printed); that is natural. In the same way the seventh pāda of the ṛks seen by this seer (the pāda "*sumṛḻīko na āgahi*" and the like) draws up the wealth in the ocean and makes it come to the gods; that is the meaning. The man who, knowing this matter well, performs the rite by reciting the mantras accordingly, conquers his enemies, drives them from this world and himself gets their wealth.

The application of the ṛks seen by Parucchepa and their inner meaning are told in the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa in this way (the reference printed here is not legible to me in the small type [?]):

> पारुच्छेपीरुपदधाति पूर्वो सननोः [?] पुरस्तात्स्थितयाज्यानां रोहितं वै नामैतच्छन्दो यत्पारुच्छेपमेतेन वा इन्द्रः सप्त स्वर्गाँल्लोकानरोहत् । रोहति सप्त स्वर्गाँल्लोकान्य एवं वेद । तदाहुर्यत्पञ्चपदा एव पञ्चमस्याह्नो रूपं षट्पदाः षष्ठस्याथ कस्मात्सप्तपदाः षष्ठेऽहञ्क्रि [?]-यन्त इति । षड्भिरेव पद्यैः षष्ठमहराप्नुवन्त्यपच्छिद्यैवैतदहर्यत्सप्तमं [?] तदेव सप्तमेन पदेनाभ्यारभ्य वसन्ति वाचमेव तत्पुनरुपयन्ति सन्तत्यै ॥

> *pārucchepīrupadadhāti pūrvo sananoḥ [?] purastātsthitayājyānāṃ rohitaṃ vai nāmaitacchando yatpārucchepametena vā indraḥ sapta svargā~llokānarohat | rohati sapta svargā~llokānya evaṃ veda | tadāhuryatpañcapadā eva pañcamasyāhno rūpaṃ ṣaṭpadāḥ ṣaṣṭhasyātha kasmātsaptapadāḥ ṣaṣṭhe 'hañkri [?]-yanta iti | ṣaḍbhireva padyaiḥ ṣaṣṭhamaharāpnuvantyapacchidyaivaitadaharyatsaptamaṃ [?] tadeva saptamena padenābhyārabhya vasanti vācameva tatpunarupayanti santatyai ‖*

The Kannada gloss then says: The ṛks seen by Parucchepa are called *rohita* ("ascending"). For by reciting these ṛks Indra ascended, at one time, to the seventh heaven. The seven heavens here are the seven divisions of heaven in the world of heaven, according to differences of excellence. The man who performs the sacrifice in this way, reciting these mantras, attains the seven heavens.

### Printed p. vi  (PDF 12)

On the sixth day of the sacrifice called "dvādaśāha" or "daśarātra", the morning pressing (*prātaḥsavana*) and midday pressing (*mādhyandinasavana*) (*savana* means the rite of extracting the Soma from the creeper), seven priests of the group of *hotṛ* priests recite the *praiṣa*, *yājyā* and *puronuvākyā* mantras. That is, first one Pārucchepa ṛk is to be recited, and then one *prasthita-yājyā* mantra is to be recited. In this connection the Pārucchepa mantras to be recited are:

> नृषन्निन्द्र वृषपाणास इन्दवः (ऋ. सं. १-१३९-६)

> *nṛṣannindra vṛṣapāṇāsa indavaḥ (ṛ. saṃ. 1-139-6)*

— and from this ṛk, [the ṛks] up to the last ṛk of this Sūkta (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-139-11, as printed); and

> पिबा सोममिन्द्र सुवानमद्रिभिः (ऋ. सं. १-१३०-२)

> *pibā somamindra suvānamadribhiḥ (ṛ. saṃ. 1-130-2)*

— and from this ṛk, [the ṛks] up to the last ṛk of this Sūkta (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-130-10, as printed; the Sūkta numbers 139 and 130 were read with the help of the reader's OCR, since the image's "9" and "4" are easily confused). These ṛks are to be recited one by one. The ṛks seen by Parucchepa have the name *rohita* ("ascending"). For by reciting these ṛks Indra ascended to the seventh heaven at one time or another. Seven heavens here are the seven divisions of heaven, in the world of heaven, by differences of excellence. The man who in this way recites these mantras and performs the sacrifice will also attain the seven heavens.

Here there is one special matter. On the fifth day of this sacrifice it is the custom to recite at the savana-time the ṛks of five pādas (the *paṅkti* metre). So on the sixth day ṛks with six pādas have to be recited. Yet why are the Pārucchepa ṛks, which have seven pādas, recited on that day? — it is said that on all six days, with the six pādas of the ṛk, one crosses the six heavens, and with the seventh pāda one attains the seventh heaven, *saptama-svarga*. For since the seventh pāda is a repetition of the ṛk, it is reckoned, within the ṛk, as six pādas. To the seventh pāda, as it is of excellent praise, it is said that by reciting it one may obtain the best seventh heaven; that is the main intention.

Regarding the use of the Sūktas seen by Parucchepa in the Śastra mantras, the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa says the following, which is explained here:

> द्यौर्वै देवता षष्ठमहर्वहति त्रयस्त्रिंशस्तोमो रैवतं सामातिच्छन्दाश्छन्दो यथादेवतमेतेन यथास्तोमं यथासाम यथाच्छन्दसं राध्नोति य एवं वेद ॥

> *dyaurvai devatā ṣaṣṭhamaharvahati trayastriṃśastomo raivataṃ sāmāticchandāśchando yathādevatametena yathāstomaṃ yathāsāma yathācchandasaṃ rādhnoti ya evaṃ veda ‖*

(Ai. Brā. 22-7 [?], as printed "೨೨-೭".) The Kannada then explains: on the sixth day of the Daśarātra sacrifice described above, the deity is Dyaus (heaven), the Stoma recited is the *trayastriṃśa*, the Sāman is *Raivata*, and the metre is *Aticchandas*; the man who knows this attains the deity, the Stoma, the Sāman and the metre, each according to what is proper.

### Printed p. vii  (PDF 13)

The details of the *trayastriṃśa Stoma* we have already told. Nevertheless in the Tāṇḍya Brāhmaṇa the description of this Stoma is as follows:

> एकादशभ्यो हिङ्करोति स तिसृभिः स सप्तभिः स एकया । एकादशभ्यो हिङ्करोति स एकया स तिसृभिः स सप्तभिः । एकादशभ्यो हिङ्करोति स सप्तभिः स एकया स तिसृभिरन्तो वै त्रयस्त्रिंशः ॥

> *ekādaśabhyo hiṅkaroti sa tisṛbhiḥ sa saptabhiḥ sa ekayā | ekādaśabhyo hiṅkaroti sa ekayā sa tisṛbhiḥ sa saptabhiḥ | ekādaśabhyo hiṅkaroti sa saptabhiḥ sa ekayā sa tisṛbhiranto vai trayastriṃśaḥ ‖*

That is: the *tṛca* to be recited (the group of three ṛks) in the first *paryāya*, the first ṛk to be recited three times, the second ṛk seven times, the third ṛk once (so that eleven ṛks are recited). In the second *paryāya* the first ṛk of the *tṛca* is recited once, the second ṛk three times, the third ṛk seven times (altogether 11 ṛks). In the third *paryāya* the first ṛk is recited seven times, the second once, the third three times (altogether 11 ṛks). In this way, since the number of ṛks recited in the three *paryāyas* is three times eleven, that is thirty-three, this Stoma has the name *trayastriṃśa-stoma*. The Sāman produced from the ṛk "*revatīr naḥ sadhamāde*" (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-30-13 [?]) has the name *Raivata*. A metre with more syllables than Gāyatrī is called *Aticchandas*. On this sixth day the deities are Vāk, Gau and Dyau. The Stoma is to be praised with the *trayastriṃśa*; with the Raivata Sāman it is to be sung. The ṛks of the Aticchandas metre are to be recited (for the Śastra mantras). These are the special programmes of this sixth day.

> यद्वै समानोदर्कं तत्षष्ठस्याह्नो रूपं यद्वैव तृतीय-महस्तदेतत्पुनर्यत्षष्ठं [?] यदश्ववद्यदन्तवद्यत्पुनरावृत्तं यत्पुनर्निवृत्तं यद्रेतवद्यत्पर्यस्तवद्यत्रिवद्यद्रथन्तररूपं [?] यदुत्तमे पदे देवता निरुच्यते यदसौ लोकोऽभ्युदितः ॥

> *yadvai samānodarkaṃ tatṣaṣṭhasyāhno rūpaṃ yadvaiva tṛtīya-mahastadetatpunaryatṣaṣṭhaṃ [?] yadaśvavadyadantavadyatpunarāvṛttaṃ yatpunarnivṛttaṃ yadretavadyatparyastavadyatrivadyadrathantararūpaṃ [?] yaduttame pade devatā nirucyate yadasau loko 'bhyuditaḥ ‖*

(Ai. Brā. 22-7 [?], as printed "೨೨-೭"; the second line is in bold small type and a few letters are doubtful.) The programme that is carried out on the third day of this sacrifice is also to be observed on the sixth day. In reciting the mantras on the third day, the *aśvavat*, *antavat* and so on that were said are also to be recited on the sixth day again. But on the sixth day there is one special order.

> यत्पारुच्छेपं यत्सप्तपदं यन्नाराशंसं यन्नाभानेदिष्ठं यद्रैवतं यदतिच्छन्दा यत्कृतं [?] यत्तृतीयस्याह्नो रूपमेतानि वै षष्ठस्याह्नो रूपाणि ।

> *yatpārucchepaṃ yatsaptapadaṃ yannārāśaṃsaṃ yannābhānediṣṭhaṃ yadraivataṃ yadaticchandā yatkṛtaṃ [?] yattṛtīyasyāhno rūpametāni vai ṣaṣṭhasyāhno rūpāṇi |*

On that day the ṛks seen by Parucchepa with seven pādas, the ṛks seen by Nābhānediṣṭha, and the ṛks praising the deity *Narāśaṃsa* are to be recited; the Sāman called *Raivata*, for singing …

### Printed p. viii  (PDF 14)

… is to be sung. The ṛks of the Aticchandas metre (metres with more syllables than the Gāyatrī) are to be used. The *bhūtakāla* (past-tense) forms of usage are to be present. Whatever special orders have been said to be followed on the third day, or have not been said, are all to be observed on the sixth day too. That doing so — the recitation of the mantras seen by Parucchepa — is the special feature of the programme of the sixth day.

> अयं जायत मनुषो धरीमणीति षष्ठस्याह्न आज्यं भवति पारुच्छेपमतिच्छन्दाः सप्तपदं षष्ठेऽहनि षष्ठस्याह्नो रूपं ।

> *ayaṃ jāyata manuṣo dharīmaṇīti ṣaṣṭhasyāhna ājyaṃ bhavati pārucchepamaticchandāḥ saptapadaṃ ṣaṣṭhe 'hani ṣaṣṭhasyāhno rūpaṃ |*

The *ājya-śastra* mantra recited on the sixth day must be ṛks of the Sūkta seen by Parucchepa, "*ayaṃ jāyata manuṣo dharīmaṇi*". Their metre is Aticchandas; that is, they have more syllables than ordinary metres; and in these ṛks there are seven pādas (3 pādas in the first half, 2 in the middle half, 2 in the last half, altogether 7 pādas). All these are the special features of the sixth day.

> स्तीर्णं बर्हिरुप नो याहि वीतये आ वां रथो नियुत्वान्वक्षदवसे सुषुमा यातमद्रिभिर्युवां स्तोमेभिर्देवयन्तो अश्विनावर्मुह [?] इन्द्र वृषन्निन्द्रास्तु [?] श्रौषळोषूणो अग्ने शृणुहि त्वमीळितो ये देवासो दिव्येकादशस्थेयमददादभसमृणच्युतमिति प्रलुगं पारुच्छेपमतिच्छन्दाः सप्तपदं षष्ठेऽहनि षष्ठस्याह्नो रूपं ॥

> *stīrṇaṃ barhirupa no yāhi vītaye ā vāṃ ratho niyutvānvakṣadavase suṣumā yātamadribhiryuvāṃ stomebhirdevayanto aśvināvarmuha [?] indra vṛṣannindrāstu [?] śrauṣaḷoṣūṇo agne śṛṇuhi tvamīḷito ye devāso divyekādaśastheyamadadādabhasamṛṇacyutamiti pralugaṃ pārucchepamaticchandāḥ saptapadaṃ ṣaṣṭhe 'hani ṣaṣṭhasyāhno rūpaṃ ‖*

(Ai. Brā. 22-7 [?], as printed "೨೨-೭"; a few letters of this long passage are doubtful.) The Śastra mantras called *Praüga* contain the following ṛks. They are ṛks seen by Parucchepa; the metre is Aticchandas with more syllables; they have seven pādas. Their order is (the list below is as printed in the Kannada page; the numerals are read with the help of the reader's OCR, and doubtful ones are marked):
