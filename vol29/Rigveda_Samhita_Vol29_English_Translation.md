## Sūkta 72 (Maṇḍala 10)
**NOTE**

**Introduction**

At the beginning of the bhāṣya on the first Ṛk of this sūkta, Sāyaṇa has indicated—आदितिदाक्षायण्यनेन सूक्तेन स्वयं यथादित्यानजनयत्तद्ब्रवीति (*āditidākṣāyaṇyanena sūktena svayaṃ yathādityānajanayattadbravīti*)—that is, that Aditi, the daughter of Dakṣa Prajāpati, herself tells in this sūkta the order of origin of her sons the Ādityas, or the gods. The sons of Aditi are called Ādityas or Āditeyas. It is the custom to call these very beings the gods and the dwellers in heaven. In the Kośa it is said आदितेया दिविषदो लेखा अदितिनंदनाः (*āditeyā diviṣado lekhā aditinaṃdanāḥ*).

### Printed p. 2

The order of origin of the gods that is told in the Purāṇas and the like differs from the order of origin of the gods that is told in the Ṛgveda. Although it is the custom to call the Ādityas by the general word "gods", the number of the Ādityas, that is, of the sons of Aditi, is limited. In some places this limit of number is stated in various ways as six, seven or eight. In the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa and the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka their number is stated clearly as eight, and their names are also given. Although we explained this matter earlier, we shall discuss it at length in the later Ṛks of this sūkta, that is, in Ṛks 8 and 9. There are also some other gods who are not sons of Aditi. Their matter is not set out in this sūkta. Of the chief among them, Agni, Vāyu and the others, we have written briefly earlier about their origin; we shall explain it later as occasion arises.

In this sūkta the state that existed before creation, the nature of the manifest and the unmanifest, called sat and asat, and a brief account of the measures of time such as the yuga are indicated. We shall explain these matters in detail in the Special Topics of the respective Ṛks.

This sūkta was composed by the seer Bṛhaspati, traditionally famed as Brahmaṇaspati. In it the truth seen by Bṛhaspati and the order of creation of the world and the like are set out. Like the seers Aghamarṣaṇa, Dīrghatamas, Hiraṇyagarbha and others who set out spiritual truth, Bṛhaspati too is a renowned seer.

In this section, which is the sixth sūkta of the tenth Maṇḍala, Bṛhaspati has described the nature of the creation of the world according to his own direct vision. **NOTE** This description is not so helpful an instrument for exact philosophical knowledge. The language is profound and does not make clear what the seer's thought is. The bhāṣyakāra too has indicated his difficulty in this matter. Even so, it is one of the excellent sections that set out spiritual truth.

देवानां जाना विपन्यया प्र वोचाम (*devānāṃ jānā vipanyayā pra vocāma*)—"Let us praise the order of origin of the gods with our words of praise": this is the first resolve of the sūkta. In this very context an attempt is made to set out the primal principle of the world.

देवानां पूर्वे युगेऽसतः सदजायत । (*devānāṃ pūrve yuge 'sataḥ sadajāyata |*)
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-62-2) **NOTE**

The substance of this section is that in the first age of creation of the gods, sat was born from asat. The meaning is that the principle that was asat was the root of the world that is of the nature of sat. Here asat does not mean non-existence, a void in which nothing is. Sāyaṇa's explanation is that sat means a thing that has name and form and is embodied, and asat means a thing that is without name and form and is unembodied.

### Printed p. 3

The two principles sat and asat stand to each other in the relation of cause and effect; how can the principle of asat be the root of the phenomenal world? What is its true nature? This will be told later in the Special Topics of the sūkta नासदासीत् (*nāsadāsīt*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-129). The exact meaning of the word yuga, which indicates the time of the creation of the world, has to be examined in the present context. What is meant by the earlier yuga and the later yuga? What is the primal consciousness behind these divisions of yuga and behind the creation and the like that took place in each yuga? What is its resolve? These and other matters also have to be examined.

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> तत्र देवानामिति नवर्चमनुवाकापेक्षया चतुर्थं सूक्तमानुष्टुभं
> देवदेवत्यं । लोकनाम्नः पुत्रो बृहस्पतिरांगिरस एव वा बृहस्पतिर्यषिः । अथवा
> दक्षस्य दुहितादितियृषिः। तथा चानुक्रांतं। देवानां नव लौक्यो वा बृहस्पतिर्दाक्षायण्यदितिर्वा दैवमानुष्टुभमिति । गतः सूक्तविनियोगः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Sāyaṇa says that, counting by anuvākas, this is the fourth sūkta of the nine-Ṛk anuvāka beginning देवानां (*devānāṃ*). It is in the Anuṣṭubh metre and its deity is the company of gods. The seer is Bṛhaspati, son of one named Loka, or else Bṛhaspati Āṅgirasa; or else the seer is Aditi, the daughter of Dakṣa. He supports this by citing the Anukramaṇī, which gives the same alternatives of seer, together with the deity of the gods and the Anuṣṭubh metre. The application of the sūkta in ritual has been stated already.

Anuvāda: As the seventy-one sūktas of the tenth Maṇḍala were completed in the previous part, the seventy-second sūkta, beginning देवानां (*devānāṃ*), is now begun. It is the fourth sūkta in the sixth Anuvāka of the tenth Maṇḍala. It has nine Ṛks. The seer of this sūkta is Bṛhaspati, son of the seer Loka, or Bṛhaspati, son of the seer Aṅgiras; or else the goddess Aditi, daughter of Dakṣa Prajāpati, is the seer; the deity is the gods; the metre is Anuṣṭubh. In the Anukramaṇī it is said देवानां नव लौक्यो वा बृहस्पतिर्दाक्षायण्यदितिर्वा दैवमानुष्टुभं (*devānāṃ nava laukyo vā bṛhaspatirdākṣāyaṇyaditirvā daivamānuṣṭubhaṃ*). The application in ritual has been stated earlier.

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> अदितिर्दाक्षायण्यनेन सूक्तेन स्वयं यथादित्यानजनयत्तद्ववीति । बृहस्पत्यृषिपक्षे स ऋषिरदितेः सकाशादादित्योत्पत्ति प्रकारमाह । वयं देवानामादित्यानां जाना जन्मानि प्र वोचाम । प्रकथयाम । विपन्यया विस्पष्टया वाचा ।
> वयमिति वोचामेति चोभयत्र पूजार्थं बहुवचनं । आधैकवदाह । यो
> देवानां गणः पूर्व युग उत्पन्नोऽपुक्षेषु शस्यमानेषु यागे शस्त्रेष्टनुष्ठीयमानेषूत्तरे युगे वर्तमानं स्तुवंतं स्तोतारं पश्यात् पश्यति । अनेकेष्टपि
> युगेषु गतेषु कर्मसु स्तूयमानो वर्तत इत्यर्थः ।

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Aditi, daughter of Dakṣa, tells by this sūkta how she herself bore the Ādityas. On the view that the seer is Bṛhaspati, that seer tells from Aditi's side the manner of the origin of the Ādityas. "We", he says, proclaim the births of the gods, the Ādityas, with a clear speech: विपन्यया (*vipanyayā*) means clear, distinct speech. The plural in वयं (*vayaṃ*) and in वोचाम (*vocāma*) is used in both places out of reverence. Then he speaks of the single company: that company of gods which was born in the earlier age sees the worshipper who is present in the later age and is praising, when the praises are being recited in the sacrifice and the śastras are being performed. The meaning is that, many ages having passed, it remains praised in the rites.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- यः (*yaḥ*)—(though born in the earlier ages) which company of gods
- उत्तरे युगे (*uttare yuge*)—in the later ages
- उक्तेषु (*ukteṣu*)—the praises that are of the form of utterance
- शस्यमानेषु (*śasyamāneṣu*)—at the times when they are recited
- पश्यत् (*paśyat*)—which can look upon us, such (a company)
- देवानां (*devānāṃ*)—of the gods
- जाना (*jānā*)—the orders of origin
- वयं (*vayaṃ*)—we
- नु (*nu*)—now
- विपन्यया (*vipanyayā*)—with clear words of praise
- प्र वोचाम (*pra vocāma*)—let us praise and recite

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

Those gods, the Ādityas and others, who, although born in the earlier ages, can still look upon men with a gracious eye now and in the later ages, when the praise-mantras that praise them are recited, the orders of origin of such gods, the Ādityas and others, let us now praise and recite with clear words of praise.

##### English translation
Let us proclaim with a clear voice the generations of the gods (the divine company), who, when their praises are recited, look (favourably on the worshipper) in this latter age.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

It is said that the seer of this Ṛk is Bṛhaspati, son of the seer Loka, or Bṛhaspati, son of the seer Aṅgiras, or Aditi, the daughter of Dakṣa. The idea in saying that any one of these may be the seer of this sūkta is that any one of the three may have set out the matter that is explained in this sūkta. If it is taken that Aditi is the seer of this sūkta, it is quite natural that she herself should tell the order of birth of her sons the Ādityas. When it is said that Bṛhaspati is the seer, it may be understood that the seer Bṛhaspati announces that he will tell the order of origin of the gods and, accordingly, sets out the order of their birth.

देवानां नु जाना (*devānāṃ nu jānā*)—आदित्यानां जन्मानि (*ādityānāṃ janmāni*). How were the Ādityas born in Aditi? How many are they? When were they born? To set out such matters the seer has used here the word जाना (*jānā*), that is जन्मानि (*janmāni*).

विपन्यया (*vipanyayā*)—विस्पष्टया वाचा (*vispaṣṭayā vācā*). With clear sentences; the seer declares that he will set out the matter with sentences whose explanation is so clear that there is no room for doubt.

वयं (*vayaṃ*)—"We": the seer is one, yet he uses the plural वयं (*vayaṃ*); is that not incorrect? In answer to this the bhāṣyakāra says वयमिति वोचामेति चोभयत्र पूजार्थं बहुवचनं (*vayamiti vocāmeti cobhayatra pūjārthaṃ bahuvacanaṃ*): here the two words वयं (*vayaṃ*) and वोचाम (*vocāma*) are used in the plural for the sake of honour. Besides, it is still the custom that poets and authors use the plural in their works where the singular of an individual would be used. Therefore the use of the plural here is correct; this is the meaning.

उक्तेषु शस्यमानेषु यः उत्तरे युगे पश्यात् (*ukteṣu śasyamāneṣu yaḥ uttare yuge paśyāt*) (तस्यजाना प्र वोचाम (*tasyajānā pra vocāma*))—यः देवानां गणः यागकाले शस्त्रेष्टनुष्ठीयमानेषु वर्तमानं स्तुवंतं स्तोतारं पश्यति (*yaḥ devānāṃ gaṇaḥ yāgakāle śastreṣṭanuṣṭhīyamāneṣu vartamānaṃ stuvaṃtaṃ stotāraṃ paśyati*) (तं देवगणस्य जन्मनि प्रकथयाम (*taṃ devagaṇasya janmani prakathayāma*)). When a sacrifice is performed, it is the custom, at the time of invoking the gods, to praise the gods' excellences and so invoke them. It is proper to set out the order of the gods' birth at such a time. Therefore the seer describes here that, while the praise-mantras are being recited and the gods are being praised, he will tell the order of origin of the gods. If one asks whether describing the order of birth of those gods amounts to singing their excellences, this is described in the second half of this Ṛk. In the later Ṛks, that is, in Ṛks 2 and 3, it is said that the birth of the gods took place at the time of creation, before the creation of all others; that is, the creation of

### Printed p. 6

men and other living beings took place long after the gods were created. Yet, that is, although created long ago, the gods too, it is said here, can even now look upon the sacrifices and other rites that men perform. That is, the gods are not short-lived like men and others; the span of their life is very long; they can live for a long time. It is with the aim of describing such excellences of the gods that the seer, in the second half of this Ṛk, describes the limit of the gods' life by comparison with the limit of the life of men.

उत्तरे युगे (*uttare yuge*)—अनेकेष्टपि युगेषु गतेषु कर्मसु स्तूयमानो वर्तत इत्यर्थः (*anekeṣṭapi yugeṣu gateṣu karmasu stūyamāno vartata ityarthaḥ*). In the following Ṛks 2 and 3 the words पूर्व युगे (*pūrva yuge*) and प्रथमे युगे (*prathame yuge*) are used. That is, the beginning of creation is called the first yuga or the earlier yuga; the meaning that appears here is that the yugas that go on after it and pass away, and the yuga that is now going on, are called the later yuga. Although the word yuga is used in some places in the Ṛgveda, they are used in different meanings. Only in the present Ṛk is the word yuga used in the meaning of a great division of time. It is therefore fitting to examine here the various meanings of the word yuga.
