## Sūkta 72 (Maṇḍala 10)
**NOTE**

**Introduction**

At the beginning of the bhāṣya on the first Ṛk of this sūkta, Sāyaṇa has indicated—आदितिदाक्षायण्यनेन सूक्तेन स्वयं यथादित्यानजनयत्तद्ब्रवीति (*āditidākṣāyaṇyanena sūktena svayaṃ yathādityānajanayattadbravīti*)—that is, that Aditi, the daughter of Dakṣa Prajāpati, herself tells in this sūkta the order of origin of her sons the Ādityas, or the gods. The sons of Aditi are called Ādityas or Āditeyas. It is the custom to call these very beings the gods and the dwellers in heaven. In the Kośa it is said आदितेया दिविषदो लेखा अदितिनंदनाः (*āditeyā diviṣado lekhā aditinaṃdanāḥ*).

### Printed p. 2

The order of origin of the gods that is told in the Purāṇas and the like differs from the order of origin of the gods that is told in the Ṛgveda. Although it is the custom to call the Ādityas by the general word "gods", the number of the Ādityas, that is, of the sons of Aditi, is limited. In some places this limit of number is stated in various ways as six, seven or eight. In the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa and the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka their number is stated clearly as eight, and their names are also given. Although we explained this matter earlier, we shall discuss it at length in the later Ṛks of this sūkta, that is, in Ṛks 8 and 9. There are also some other gods who are not sons of Aditi. Their matter is not set out in this sūkta. Of the chief among them, Agni, Vāyu and the others, we have written briefly earlier about their origin; we shall explain it later as occasion arises.

In this sūkta the state that existed before creation, the nature of the manifest and the unmanifest, called sat and asat, and a brief account of the measures of time such as the yuga are indicated. We shall explain these matters in detail in the Special Topics of the respective Ṛks.

This sūkta was composed by the seer Bṛhaspati, traditionally famed as Brahmaṇaspati. In it the truth seen by Bṛhaspati and the order of creation of the world and the like are set out. Like the seers Aghamarṣaṇa, Dīrghatamas, Hiraṇyagarbha and others who set out spiritual truth, Bṛhaspati too is a renowned seer.

In this section, which is the sixth sūkta of the tenth Maṇḍala, Bṛhaspati has described the nature of the creation of the world according to his own direct vision. **NOTE** This description is not so helpful an instrument for exact philosophical knowledge. The language is profound and does not make clear what the seer's thought is. The bhāṣyakāra too has indicated his difficulty in this matter. Even so, it is one of the excellent sections that set out spiritual truth.

देवानां जाना विपन्यया प्र वोचाम (*devānāṃ jānā vipanyayā pra vocāma*)—"Let us praise the order of origin of the gods with our words of praise": this is the first resolve of the sūkta. In this very context an attempt is made to set out the primal principle of the world.

देवानां पूर्वे युगेऽसतः सदजायत । (*devānāṃ pūrve yuge 'sataḥ sadajāyata |*)
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-62-2) **NOTE**

The substance of this section is that in the first age of creation of the gods, sat was born from asat. The meaning is that the principle that was asat was the root of the world that is of the nature of sat. Here asat does not mean non-existence, a void in which nothing is. Sāyaṇa's explanation is that sat means a thing that has name and form and is embodied, and asat means a thing that is without name and form and is unembodied.

### Printed p. 3

The two principles sat and asat stand to each other in the relation of cause and effect; how can the principle of asat be the root of the phenomenal world? What is its true nature? This will be told later in the Special Topics of the sūkta नासदासीत् (*nāsadāsīt*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-129). The exact meaning of the word yuga, which indicates the time of the creation of the world, has to be examined in the present context. What is meant by the earlier yuga and the later yuga? What is the primal consciousness behind these divisions of yuga and behind the creation and the like that took place in each yuga? What is its resolve? These and other matters also have to be examined.

> तत्र देवानामिति नवर्चमनुवाकापेक्षया चतुर्थं सूक्तमानुष्टुभं
> देवदेवत्यं । लोकनाम्नः पुत्रो बृहस्पतिरांगिरस एव वा बृहस्पतिर्यषिः । अथवा
> दक्षस्य दुहितादितियृषिः। तथा चानुक्रांतं। देवानां नव लौक्यो वा बृहस्पतिर्दाक्षायण्यदितिर्वा दैवमानुष्टुभमिति । गतः सूक्तविनियोगः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Sāyaṇa says that, counting by anuvākas, this is the fourth sūkta of the nine-Ṛk anuvāka beginning देवानां (*devānāṃ*). It is in the Anuṣṭubh metre and its deity is the company of gods. The seer is Bṛhaspati, son of one named Loka, or else Bṛhaspati Āṅgirasa; or else the seer is Aditi, the daughter of Dakṣa. He supports this by citing the Anukramaṇī, which gives the same alternatives of seer, together with the deity of the gods and the Anuṣṭubh metre. The application of the sūkta in ritual has been stated already.

Anuvāda: As the seventy-one sūktas of the tenth Maṇḍala were completed in the previous part, the seventy-second sūkta, beginning देवानां (*devānāṃ*), is now begun. It is the fourth sūkta in the sixth Anuvāka of the tenth Maṇḍala. It has nine Ṛks. The seer of this sūkta is Bṛhaspati, son of the seer Loka, or Bṛhaspati, son of the seer Aṅgiras; or else the goddess Aditi, daughter of Dakṣa Prajāpati, is the seer; the deity is the gods; the metre is Anuṣṭubh. In the Anukramaṇī it is said देवानां नव लौक्यो वा बृहस्पतिर्दाक्षायण्यदितिर्वा दैवमानुष्टुभं (*devānāṃ nava laukyo vā bṛhaspatirdākṣāyaṇyaditirvā daivamānuṣṭubhaṃ*). The application in ritual has been stated earlier.

> अदितिर्दाक्षायण्यनेन सूक्तेन स्वयं यथादित्यानजनयत्तद्ववीति । बृहस्पत्यृषिपक्षे स ऋषिरदितेः सकाशादादित्योत्पत्ति प्रकारमाह । वयं देवानामादित्यानां जाना जन्मानि प्र वोचाम । प्रकथयाम । विपन्यया विस्पष्टया वाचा ।
> वयमिति वोचामेति चोभयत्र पूजार्थं बहुवचनं । आधैकवदाह । यो
> देवानां गणः पूर्व युग उत्पन्नोऽपुक्षेषु शस्यमानेषु यागे शस्त्रेष्टनुष्ठीयमानेषूत्तरे युगे वर्तमानं स्तुवंतं स्तोतारं पश्यात् पश्यति । अनेकेष्टपि
> युगेषु गतेषु कर्मसु स्तूयमानो वर्तत इत्यर्थः ।

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Aditi, daughter of Dakṣa, tells by this sūkta how she herself bore the Ādityas. On the view that the seer is Bṛhaspati, that seer tells from Aditi's side the manner of the origin of the Ādityas. "We", he says, proclaim the births of the gods, the Ādityas, with a clear speech: विपन्यया (*vipanyayā*) means clear, distinct speech. The plural in वयं (*vayaṃ*) and in वोचाम (*vocāma*) is used in both places out of reverence. Then he speaks of the single company: that company of gods which was born in the earlier age sees the worshipper who is present in the later age and is praising, when the praises are being recited in the sacrifice and the śastras are being performed. The meaning is that, many ages having passed, it remains praised in the rites.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- यः (*yaḥ*)—(though born in the earlier ages) which company of gods
- उत्तरे युगे (*uttare yuge*)—in the later ages
- उक्तेषु (*ukteṣu*)—the praises that are of the form of utterance
- शस्यमानेषु (*śasyamāneṣu*)—at the times when they are recited
- पश्यत् (*paśyat*)—which can look upon us, such (a company)
- देवानां (*devānāṃ*)—of the gods
- जाना (*jānā*)—the orders of origin
- वयं (*vayaṃ*)—we
- नु (*nu*)—now
- विपन्यया (*vipanyayā*)—with clear words of praise
- प्र वोचाम (*pra vocāma*)—let us praise and recite

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

Those gods, the Ādityas and others, who, although born in the earlier ages, can still look upon men with a gracious eye now and in the later ages, when the praise-mantras that praise them are recited, the orders of origin of such gods, the Ādityas and others, let us now praise and recite with clear words of praise.

##### English translation
Let us proclaim with a clear voice the generations of the gods (the divine company), who, when their praises are recited, look (favourably on the worshipper) in this latter age.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

It is said that the seer of this Ṛk is Bṛhaspati, son of the seer Loka, or Bṛhaspati, son of the seer Aṅgiras, or Aditi, the daughter of Dakṣa. The idea in saying that any one of these may be the seer of this sūkta is that any one of the three may have set out the matter that is explained in this sūkta. If it is taken that Aditi is the seer of this sūkta, it is quite natural that she herself should tell the order of birth of her sons the Ādityas. When it is said that Bṛhaspati is the seer, it may be understood that the seer Bṛhaspati announces that he will tell the order of origin of the gods and, accordingly, sets out the order of their birth.

देवानां नु जाना (*devānāṃ nu jānā*)—आदित्यानां जन्मानि (*ādityānāṃ janmāni*). How were the Ādityas born in Aditi? How many are they? When were they born? To set out such matters the seer has used here the word जाना (*jānā*), that is जन्मानि (*janmāni*).

विपन्यया (*vipanyayā*)—विस्पष्टया वाचा (*vispaṣṭayā vācā*). With clear sentences; the seer declares that he will set out the matter with sentences whose explanation is so clear that there is no room for doubt.

वयं (*vayaṃ*)—"We": the seer is one, yet he uses the plural वयं (*vayaṃ*); is that not incorrect? In answer to this the bhāṣyakāra says वयमिति वोचामेति चोभयत्र पूजार्थं बहुवचनं (*vayamiti vocāmeti cobhayatra pūjārthaṃ bahuvacanaṃ*): here the two words वयं (*vayaṃ*) and वोचाम (*vocāma*) are used in the plural for the sake of honour. Besides, it is still the custom that poets and authors use the plural in their works where the singular of an individual would be used. Therefore the use of the plural here is correct; this is the meaning.

उक्तेषु शस्यमानेषु यः उत्तरे युगे पश्यात् (*ukteṣu śasyamāneṣu yaḥ uttare yuge paśyāt*) (तस्यजाना प्र वोचाम (*tasyajānā pra vocāma*))—यः देवानां गणः यागकाले शस्त्रेष्टनुष्ठीयमानेषु वर्तमानं स्तुवंतं स्तोतारं पश्यति (*yaḥ devānāṃ gaṇaḥ yāgakāle śastreṣṭanuṣṭhīyamāneṣu vartamānaṃ stuvaṃtaṃ stotāraṃ paśyati*) (तं देवगणस्य जन्मनि प्रकथयाम (*taṃ devagaṇasya janmani prakathayāma*)). When a sacrifice is performed, it is the custom, at the time of invoking the gods, to praise the gods' excellences and so invoke them. It is proper to set out the order of the gods' birth at such a time. Therefore the seer describes here that, while the praise-mantras are being recited and the gods are being praised, he will tell the order of origin of the gods. If one asks whether describing the order of birth of those gods amounts to singing their excellences, this is described in the second half of this Ṛk. In the later Ṛks, that is, in Ṛks 2 and 3, it is said that the birth of the gods took place at the time of creation, before the creation of all others; that is, the creation of

### Printed p. 6

men and other living beings took place long after the gods were created. Yet, that is, although created long ago, the gods too, it is said here, can even now look upon the sacrifices and other rites that men perform. That is, the gods are not short-lived like men and others; the span of their life is very long; they can live for a long time. It is with the aim of describing such excellences of the gods that the seer, in the second half of this Ṛk, describes the limit of the gods' life by comparison with the limit of the life of men.

उत्तरे युगे (*uttare yuge*)—अनेकेष्टपि युगेषु गतेषु कर्मसु स्तूयमानो वर्तत इत्यर्थः (*anekeṣṭapi yugeṣu gateṣu karmasu stūyamāno vartata ityarthaḥ*). In the following Ṛks 2 and 3 the words पूर्व युगे (*pūrva yuge*) and प्रथमे युगे (*prathame yuge*) are used. That is, the beginning of creation is called the first yuga or the earlier yuga; the meaning that appears here is that the yugas that go on after it and pass away, and the yuga that is now going on, are called the later yuga. Although the word yuga is used in some places in the Ṛgveda, they are used in different meanings. Only in the present Ṛk is the word yuga used in the meaning of a great division of time. It is therefore fitting to examine here the various meanings of the word yuga.

**The various meanings and uses of the word yuga**

The word yuga, in the meaning of a measure of time, is described at length in the Purāṇas and the like. Among them, the order in the Bhāgavata (Skṛ. 3-11-1) **NOTE** and in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa is as follows.

Vidura said:

यदात्थ ब्रह्मरूपस्य हरेरद्भुतकर्मणः । कालाख्यं लक्षणं ब्रह्मन्यथा वर्णय नः प्रभो ॥ (*yadāttha brahmarūpasya hareradbhutakarmaṇaḥ | kālākhyaṃ lakṣaṇaṃ brahmanyathā varṇaya naḥ prabho ‖*)
(Śrī. Bhā. 3-10-10) **NOTE**

"O great seer, you have described the great deeds of Hari, who is of the form of Brahman. In the present context, how has Hari, the original cause of the creation of the world, divided the principle that is of the form of time, in order to fix the measure of creation, continuance and the like? What is the nature of time? Explain it and tell us," so Vidura asks in prayer. In answer to it, Maitreya sets out the principle that is of the form of time in this way—

### Printed p. 7

Maitreya said:

गुणव्यतिकराकारो निर्विशेषोऽप्रतिष्ठितः । पुरुषस्तदुपादानमात्मानं लीलयासृजत् ॥ (*guṇavyatikarākāro nirviśeṣo 'pratiṣṭhitaḥ | puruṣastadupādānamātmānaṃ līlayāsṛjat ‖*)
(Śrī. Bhā. 3-10-11)

The great Prakṛti, which has an eternal state, undergoing modifications and changing, is what is called time. This time has no form, and no birth or death. But only in ordinary usage does this time have divisions. Making this time the instrumental cause, the Lord created the whole world as His sport.

विश्वं वै ब्रह्मतन्मात्रं संस्थितं विष्णु मायया । ईश्वरेण परिच्छिन्नं कालेनाव्यक्तमूरिना ॥ यथेदानीं तथाग्रे च पश्चादप्येतदीदृशं । सर्गो नवविधस्तस्य प्राकृतो वैकृतस्तु यः ॥ (*viśvaṃ vai brahmatanmātraṃ saṃsthitaṃ viṣṇu māyayā | īśvareṇa paricchinnaṃ kālenāvyaktamūrinā ‖ yathedānīṃ tathāgre ca paścādapyetadīdṛśaṃ | sargo navavidhastasya prākṛto vaikṛtastu yaḥ ‖*)
(Śrī. Bhā. 3.10-12, 13)

The whole world that undergoes creation is full of Brahman. For this world created by the māyā of Brahman, time, which is of unmanifest nature and is the controller of the whole world, is the cause of all. The division of time is only for the transactions of this world. But eternal time has no divisions. By the power of this time that controls the world there is the ninefold creation and also the tenth creation, called prākṛta-vaikṛta.

कालद्रव्यगुणैरस्य त्रिविधः प्रतिसंक्रमः । आद्यस्तु महतस्सर्गो गुणवैषम्यमात्मनः ॥ द्वितीयस्त्वहनो यत्र द्रव्यज्ञानक्रियोदयः । भूतसर्गस्तृतीयस्तु तन्मात्रो द्रव्यशक्तिमान् ॥ चतुर्थ ऐन्द्रियः सर्गो यस्तु ज्ञानक्रियात्मकः । वैकारिको देवसर्गः पंचमो यन्मयं मनः ॥ षष्ठस्तु तमसः सर्गो यस्त्वबुद्धिकृतः प्रभो । षडिमे प्राकृताः सर्गा वैकृतानपि मे शृणु ॥ (*kāladravyaguṇairasya trividhaḥ pratisaṃkramaḥ | ādyastu mahatassargo guṇavaiṣamyamātmanaḥ ‖ dvitīyastvahano yatra dravyajñānakriyodayaḥ | bhūtasargastṛtīyastu tanmātro dravyaśaktimān ‖ caturtha aindriyaḥ sargo yastu jñānakriyātmakaḥ | vaikāriko devasargaḥ paṃcamo yanmayaṃ manaḥ ‖ ṣaṣṭhastu tamasaḥ sargo yastvabuddhikṛtaḥ prabho | ṣaḍime prākṛtāḥ sargā vaikṛtānapi me śṛṇu ‖*)
(Śrī. Bhā. 3-10-14-17)

According to the rule of eternal time there are three kinds of dissolution: the daily dissolution, of the nature of deep sleep, that occurs in the time fixed for creation; the occasional dissolution caused by causes such as the fire from the mouth of Saṅkarṣaṇa; and the dissolution of Prakṛti, arising from the state of equilibrium of the guṇas, sattva and the others. Thus there are three

kinds. By the resolve of the Supreme Self, among the creations, the Mahat-principle, which is the disturbance of the equilibrium of the guṇas, is the first creation. In the creation of Brahmā, living beings, men, ancestors and gods are born according to their respective deeds.

In the manvantaras the Lord protects this world with the principle of sattva, both in person and, through Manu and the others, in the form of representatives. ##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Sāyaṇa says that Brahmaṇaspati is Aditi, the lord of food (ब्रह्मणः (*brahmaṇaḥ*) being food). As a smith blows up a fire with his bellows to make it blaze, so she blew together, that is, brought forth, these births of the gods. देवानां पूर्व्य युगे (*devānāṃ pūrvya yuge*) means in the first creation. From their material cause, the asat, that is Brahman, which because it is without name and form is like non-being, the sat, the gods and the rest endowed with name and form, was born, appeared. For the Śruti says असद्वा इदमग्र आसीत्ततो वै सदजायत (*asadvā idamagra āsīttato vai sadajāyata*) (Tai. U. 2-7). It is not to be objected that it is unfitting for a world of the nature of sat to have asat as its cause, for the followers of the Chāndogya, after raising the objection कथमसतः सज्जायेत (*kathamasataḥ sajjāyeta*) and thus questioning asat as cause, settle the matter by सत्येव सोम्येदमग्र आसीत् (*satyeva somyedamagra āsīt*) (Chā. U. 6-2-2). If it is asked what then is the way of the sentences that teach asat as cause, the answer is that they intend the unmanifest state, as the Śruti says तद्धेदं तर्ह्यव्याकृतमासीत् (*taddhedaṃ tarhyavyākṛtamāsīt*) (Śata. 14-4-2-15). **NOTE** If so, how is there origin of the gods and the rest from Aditi? Because, as in वायोरग्निः (*vāyoragniḥ*) and the like, origin is from the substratum. Or else: it is to be construed that what is the cause of the gods arose from Brahman, who is both sat and asat; the reasoning stated above holds equally on this view.

As a blacksmith presses his bellows and makes the fire grow, Brahmaṇaspati filled these gods with vitality and made them grow. In the time that precedes the coming to be of the gods, the gods and the rest, endowed with names and forms, were born from the unembodied principle that is without name and form.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

Two kinds of meaning may be given here for the word Brahmaṇaspati. Brahmaṇaspati means Prajāpati, the protector of food, or the Lord of all (Sarveśvara), or Aditi. Aditi also means the power that governs the universe. We shall explain this matter at length in the Special Topics of the fourth Ṛk of this sūkta.

ब्रह्मणस्पतिः एताः कर्मार इव अधमत् (*brahmaṇaspatiḥ etāḥ karmāra iva adhamat*)—As a blacksmith, who works in iron, to heat the iron presses the fire (the live coals) with his bellows, makes much air come, and so makes the fire blaze, so the Lord of all filled the gods with His unobstructed power and made them endowed with vitality. All kinds of powers are in the Lord of all. The idea is that He established a part of those powers in the gods and made them possessed of vitality. The vitality that is in the Supreme Self is like an ocean. As the ocean is the original source of all the bodies of water on the earth, rivers, wells, tanks and the rest, so all the vitality that is in the gods and in other living beings is to be understood as a part of the vitality of that Supreme Self or Lord of all. Therefore the main idea of this sentence is that at the time of creation the Lord of all filled a part of the vitality that is His own portion into the gods and the rest, and made them gods who are not inert, who can move about, can think, and have the power of action.

देवानां पूर्वे युगॆ असतः सत् अजायत (*devānāṃ pūrve yugè asataḥ sat ajāyata*)—Here देवानां पूर्वे युगे (*devānāṃ pūrve yuge*) means before Prajāpati (or the Lord of all) created the gods, in the first creation. At that time, that is before creation, there were no things at all endowed with names and forms (that is, mountains, rivers, trees, animals, men and the like). There was only the unmanifest form. That is, the Supreme Self, by Himself alone, endowed with His infinite power that is beyond the reach of the senses, pervaded everywhere. This form of the Supreme Self has names such as unmanifest, without qualities and asat. That is what is indicated here by the word असत् (*asat*). In this sentence the seer describes the order of creation: from this unmanifest form, sat, that is, mountains and the rest and living beings and the like that are perceptible to the senses, came into being. This matter is supported at length by the Śruti sentence आसद्वा इदमग्र आसीत् । ततो वै सदजायत (*āsadvā idamagra āsīt | tato vai sadajāyata*) (Tai. U. 2-7) (the Taittirīya Upaniṣad), by the Chāndogya Upaniṣad sentences कथमसतः सज्जायेत। (*kathamasataḥ sajjāyeta|*) and सत्येव सोम्येदमग्र आसीत् (*satyeva somyedamagra āsīt*) (Chā. U. 6-2-2), by the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa sentence तद्देदं तर्हव्याकृतमासीत् (*taddedaṃ tarhavyākṛtamāsīt*) (Śa. Brā. 14-2-15), by the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka sentence वायोरग्निः (*vāyoragniḥ*) (Tai. Ā. 8-1), and by the Ṛgveda sentence नासदासीन्नो सदासीत् (*nāsadāsīnno sadāsīt*) (10-129-1). This last sentence in the Ṛgveda we shall explain at length later, in its own context.

The latter half of the previous Ṛk is recited here as the first half of this Ṛk. But in place of the word पूर्वे (*pūrve*) the word प्रथमे (*prathame*) is used. There is no difference in meaning. Since the plant kingdom is the chief food of the gods, of men and of all other kinds of living beings, it is reasonable and fitting to say that the plant kingdom (trees and the like) was created before the creation of living beings.

It should not be objected that this is contradictory, since a product that has been born of something would here also become the cause of that very thing. Or else, by the nature of the gods, the two may be born of each other, each being the origin of the other.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

In the first half of this Ṛk it is said that from the trees the earth was born, and from the earth the quarters. Some explanation is necessary on this point. Everyone knows that the plant kingdom and trees and the like are on the earth. Since the earth is necessary for their birth, the earth must have existed before the trees and the like. But here it is said that the earth was born from the trees; to say that the trees existed first and the earth came into being afterwards is mutually contradictory, is it not? The answer is this: before the earth, water was created, and that water was spread everywhere. The Śruti sentences आपो वा इदमग्रे सलिलमासीत् (*āpo vā idamagre salilamāsīt*) and आपो वा इदं सर्वं विश्वा भूतान्यापः (*āpo vā idaṃ sarvaṃ viśvā bhūtānyāpaḥ*), and the Smṛti sentence अप एव ससर्जाद् (*apa eva sasarjād*), also establish that Prajāpati first, that is, before creating the earth, created water. Besides, the Upaniṣad sentence अग्नेरापः । अद्भः पृथिवी (*agnerāpaḥ | adbhaḥ pṛthivī*) also says that the earth came into being from water. Therefore water is the origin of the earth. Is there a birth of plants and the like in water? One may say that there is. That various kinds of plants exist in the lower part of the sea is a matter known to all. Or else the word उत्तानपद (*uttānapada*) here may be taken to mean "growing or spreading upward". That a solid mass like the earth arises, in the mass of the sea water, from a kind of living creature that grows upward (coral) is well known to modern scientists. Or else it may be said, if one wishes, that the earth, made solid from the water that is of the nature of sap, came into being.

As for the matter of the quarters being born from the earth, only this much can be said. At the time of creation, that is, before the earth was created, when water covered everything everywhere and there were no divisions such as above and below, an arrangement of the quarters was not possible. After the earth was created, the arrangement of the quarters, east, west, south, north, above and below, came about of itself; that is, the need for it arose. Therefore it is said here that the quarters were born from the earth.
In the latter half of this Ṛk, in अदितिर्दक्षो अजायत दक्षाद्वदितिः परि (*aditirdakṣo ajāyata dakṣādvaditiḥ pari*), there are two sentences. That Dakṣa was born from Aditi is the first sentence; that Aditi was born from Dakṣa is the second sentence. Is not the meaning (the matter) in these sentences mutually contradictory? That an effect arises from a cause is right; but how can an effect become the origin of the cause? How is this apparent contradiction resolved? The answer may be given in two or three ways. Thus: the word Aditi in the first sentence may be taken to mean the power that governs the universe, or the Supreme Brahman. Then it becomes consistent that from the Supreme Brahman Dakṣa was born, and from Dakṣa his daughter Aditi was born. Or, as Yāska has said—

**4. Is there identity between Aditi and the atmosphere?**
In the Ṛk 10-63.2 Aditi is described, together with water and the earth, as a source of the gods. The meaning of that mantra is: "O gods, all your names are sacred and worthy of worship. You who are born in the womb of Aditi, in the womb of the earth, and अब्बरू (*abbarū*) **NOTE**, hear our prayer."
[This division of the gods is threefold: those dwelling in heaven, those dwelling in the atmosphere and those dwelling on the earth. But this usage occurs not only in this context, but also in 10-49-2. "All the gods, born of heaven, born of the atmosphere and born of the earth, worship me alone as Indra", thus runs this division. (In both these examples the word अप् (*ap*) may be explained as the atmosphere.) In the same way the same divisions are found precisely in the Ṛks 18-65-9; 2-38-11; 10-45-1 and 7-35-11 **NOTE**. (10-65-9)
those born of the earth and those born of the heavenly world are mentioned separately, besides the Ādityas and their mother Aditi; in this context it is stated that Aditi and the heavenly world are different.
**5. Evidence that Aditi is a deity different from the earth.**
Although in some of the sentences quoted above identity of Aditi with the atmosphere is stated, it cannot be said that the same relation of identity is shown in the other parts of the Saṃhitā. At times there are contexts that show the earth and Aditi to be one. But there are also descriptions that differ from this. It has been stated above that, according to the readings of the Nirukta, the word is at one time a synonym of the earth and at another of heaven and earth. In one or two contexts the word Aditi is an epithet of the earth, as in "the earth, in the form of Aditi, together with sons who perform excellent rites, stands by the favour of Agni" and so on (1. 72-9) (A. Ve. 13-1-38). But in many other contexts it is known precisely that she is a deity different from the earth. For example,
"May heaven and earth, the waters, and the sun together with the stars hear our prayer"
thus it is prayed in the mantra 3-54-19, and then, in what follows it, that is, in the Ṛk 3-54-20,
"May Aditi, together with the Ādityas, hear our prayer"
it is prayed. Therefore there is no doubt that Aditi is different from the earth. If we take a few more examples,
"I pray to and call upon Indra, Agni, Mitra, Varuṇa, Aditi, the atmosphere, the earth,

"O heaven, in the form of the father, O earth, the benefactress, O Agni, in the form of a brother, O Vasus, be givers of happiness to us. O Ādityas, O goddess Aditi, give us complete protection"
(6-51-5)
"May Mitra, Varuṇa, Aditi, the presiding deity of the ocean, the earth and heaven be givers of joy to us" (9-97-58)
"May heaven and earth, wise and discerning, protect us. May Aditi, the mother of Mitra and Varuṇa, protect us from calamities." (10-36-2,3)
"Heaven and earth, Yama, Aditi, Tvaṣṭṛ ... Viṣṇu are worthy of our worship" (18-92-11) **NOTE**

In support of the same opinion there is in the Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā also this prayer: "पृथिवी चमेऽदितिश्च मे (*pṛthivī came 'ditiśca me*) .... may the earth, Aditi and Diti .... satisfy me" [Vā. But in some Brāhmaṇa sentences precise identity of Aditi and the earth is stated. For example:—

This earth is Aditi; she is the support of the world.
"इयं वै पृथ्वी अदितिः सा इयं देवानां पत्नि (*iyaṃ vai pṛthvī aditiḥ sā iyaṃ devānāṃ patni*)" (Śa. Brā. 5-3-1-4)
This earth is Aditi. She is the worshipful consort of the gods. Since there are Brāhmaṇa sentences of this kind, the precise meaning of the word Aditi must be stated only as suits each context.
It has been stated earlier that Yāska indicates that Aditi is the chief of all the deities dwelling in the atmosphere. But the same Yāska, grouping the Ādityas, the sons of Aditi, with the deities dwelling in heaven,

**6. Aditi and Diti:—**
"O Mitra and Varuṇa, at dawn mount your golden chariot and look upon Aditi and Diti"
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 5.62-8)
In the mantra हिरण्यरूपं (*hiraṇyarūpaṃ*), which gives this meaning, Aditi is designated together with Diti. The two words Aditi and Diti are meaningful as showing a difference from each other; or else one fills up the deficiency of the other. In this way the mantra above indicates the relation and the difference of these two deities, who are mutually opposed in one sense and mutually complementary in another.
[The same words are used also in the Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā [10-16]. For this the commentator has stated the purport in the adhidaiva sense. For the word Aditi he has given the meaning "free from poverty" (adīna) and for the word Diti the meaning "poor" (dīna). That is, one who performs what is prescribed is Aditi; one of atheistic conduct is Diti. Such a difference has been shown.]
Sāyaṇa, in his commentary on the mantra Ṛ. Saṃ. 5.62.8, has explained Aditi as the indivisible earth, and Diti as the divisible earth, that is, the earth with individual creatures and the like. The German scholar Roth has explained the word Aditi as the eternal and the word Diti as the non-eternal.
(Journal of German Oriental Society. vol. VI p. 71)
Taking all the contexts together, it appears that Aditi may be called the deity reflecting the atmosphere and Diti the one reflecting the earth. It may also be said that these two principles together reflect the complete state which includes all the conditions of Nature.
For the word Diti used in the mantra Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-2-11, Sāyaṇa has given the meaning a generous giver, and for the word Aditi the meaning a miser who does not give. He has explained: दितिं— दातारं ; अदितिं— अदातुः पंचम्यर्थे द्वितीया ! (*ditiṃ— dātāraṃ ; aditiṃ— adātuḥ paṃcamyarthe dvitīyā !*).
There are also some contexts in which only Diti is used, without Aditi. For example—
"O Agni, O worshipful Savitṛ and Bhaga, grant us fame and offspring.

### Printed p. 32
Diti also grants us the things we desire"
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-15-12)
In the mantra त्वमग्ने (*tvamagne*), which gives this meaning, Sāyaṇa has given for the word Diti only the meaning "goddess". Here Diti is not joined with Aditi. In this context English or German scholars have supplied their own meaning for the word Diti according to their own imagination. For example, Roth has said: "Diti is a special deity reflecting either generosity or increase of wealth." Max Muller has stated: "In this context the word Aditi itself has been changed into Diti by a variant reading." In the mantras A. Ve. 15-6-7; 15-18-4; Vā. Saṃ. 18-22 it is precisely stated that Diti and Aditi are separate deities. In A. Ve. 7-7-1 it is indicated who are the sons of Diti and who are the sons of Aditi. These sons of Diti are later, in the Purāṇas and the like, regarded as Daityas and enemies of the gods.
**7. Aditi can be the deity-principle that reflects universal nature.**
There are also mantras which state that Aditi reflects Nature, which pervades the universe. Whatever things there are that belong to heaven and to the atmosphere, whatever beings there are, divine and human, whatever special principle there is that belongs to past, future, present and the rest, of all these Aditi is the origin, and she is the self of all.
“पदितिद्र्यौरदतिरंतरिक्षं........................आदितिर्जनित्वं (*paditidryauradatiraṃtarikṣaṃ........................āditirjanitvaṃ*)”
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-89-10)
The undivided Aditi is the giver of birth to the whole universe. She is the mother of the world, she is the father of the world. She is every thing that is born. She is the place of origin of all. She is the atmosphere, she is the whole universe. She is the governor of all that is born and that will be born.

Whoever recited this mantra, it is a mantra to be recited by a man caught in calamity, with the desire to escape from the calamity.
**8. Aditi is the deity who forgives sin;—**

**10. The order of the creation of the universe as described in the 72nd sūkta of the 10th maṇḍala of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā: the nature of the origin of Aditi, Dakṣa, the Ādityas and the other gods:—**

Its details are as follows.

(3) In the beginning the manifestation of the existent arose from the non-existent. From it the quarters were made manifest. After that, from the trees ............

The purport is that as soon as Mārtāṇḍa was produced, the egg died.
Explaining the 8th mantra above, the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa says as follows.
अष्टौ ह वै पुत्रा आदितेः । यांस्वेतद्देवा आदित्या इत्याचक्षते सप्त हैव तेऽविकृतग्ं हाष्टमं जनयांचकार मार्तांडग्ं संदेघो हैवास यावानेवोर्ध्वस्तावांस्तिर्यज् पुरुषसम्मित इत्यु हैक आहुः (*aṣṭau ha vai putrā āditeḥ | yāṃsvetaddevā ādityā ityācakṣate sapta haiva te 'vikṛtagṃ hāṣṭamaṃ janayāṃcakāra mārtāṃḍagṃ saṃdegho haivāsa yāvānevordhvastāvāṃstiryaj puruṣasammita ityu haika āhuḥ*)
(Śa. Brā. 3-1-3-3)
Aditi had eight sons. But of them only seven gods are called Ādityas by men. For the eighth, Mārtāṇḍa, without hands, feet and the like, without any special quality, endowed only with the quality of a lump (mārdana **NOTE**) and free of distinctions, grew as long as he was high and was born so; he became only of the measure of a man's body: such is the opinion of some.
त उ हैत ऊचुः । देवा आदित्या यदस्मानन्वजनिमा तदमुयेव भूद्धं. तेमं विकरवामेति तं विचक्रुर्यथायं पुरुषो विकृतस्तस्य यानि माग्‌ंसानि संस्कृत्य सन्यासुस्ततो हस्ती समभवत्तस्मादाहुर्न हस्तिनं प्रतिगृह्णीयात्पुरुषाचानो हि हस्तीति यमु ह तद्विचक्रुः स विवस्वानादित्यस्तस्येमाः प्रजाः ॥ (*ta u haita ūcuḥ | devā ādityā yadasmānanvajanimā tadamuyeva bhūddhaṃ. temaṃ vikaravāmeti taṃ vicakruryathāyaṃ puruṣo vikṛtastasya yāni māgṃsāni saṃskṛtya sanyāsustato hastī samabhavattasmādāhurna hastinaṃ pratigṛhṇīyātpuruṣācāno hi hastīti yamu ha tadvicakruḥ sa vivasvānādityastasyemāḥ prajāḥ ‖*) (Śa. Brā. 3-1-3-4)

Seeing Mārtāṇḍa thus without distinctions, the Ādityas were frightened. "If this Mārtāṇḍa does not resemble our form, then death is near to us. Therefore we must make him take a form," and so they transformed him into the shape of a man. When, in order to give him their form, they cut his body and set it right, and threw away the flesh taken out, an elephant was produced from it. This new person, endowed with special qualities such as name and form, they called Vivasvān; this Vivasvān became the cause of the origin of all beings.
The same sentence is quoted also in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā, where it is said that this second embryo of Aditi took the form of an egg. (Tai. Saṃ. 6-5.6-1)
Yāska, explaining the purport of the mantra 10-22-4 **NOTE** above, has said in the sentences of the Nirukta as follows.
आदित्यो दक्ष इत्याहुः । आदित्यमध्ये च स्तुतः । अदितिः दाक्षायणी । अदितेर्दक्षो अजायत दक्षाद्वदितिः परि(१०-७२-४) इति। तत्कथमुपपद्येत । समानजन्मानौ स्यातां इति । अपि वा देवधर्मेणेतरेतरजन्मानौ स्यातामितरेतरप्रकृती ॥ (*ādityo dakṣa ityāhuḥ | ādityamadhye ca stutaḥ | aditiḥ dākṣāyaṇī | aditerdakṣo ajāyata dakṣādvaditiḥ pari(10-72-4) iti| tatkathamupapadyeta | samānajanmānau syātāṃ iti | api vā devadharmeṇetaretarajanmānau syātāmitaretaraprakṛtī ‖*) (Ni. 11-23)
Dakṣa is praised and renowned as one of the sons of Aditi and as belonging to the group of the Ādityas. But the sentence "From Dakṣa Aditi was born; from Aditi Dakṣa was born" makes known that Aditi is also the daughter of Dakṣa. How can this be possible? Both may have one and the same place of origin, or, in keeping with the natural power of the gods, one may have been born from the other, and they may have received greatness from one another. In the same way there is another Śruti sentence that states that each became the womb for the origin of the other.
"तस्माद्विराडजायत विराजो अधि पूरुषः (*tasmādvirāḍajāyata virājo adhi pūruṣaḥ*)" (10-90-5)
From the Puruṣa the Virāṭ was born; and that from the Virāṭ the Puruṣa was born is also well known; therefore the order of origin of Dakṣa and Aditi may be of the same kind. Other sentences setting out the mutual relation of Dakṣa and Aditi may also be examined. असच्च सच्च........ वृषभश्च धेनु: (*asacca sacca........ vṛṣabhaśca dhenu:*) (10-5-7)
1. "The principle that is unmanifest and possessed of the truth of the highest reality, being established in the seed of Dakṣa and in the womb of Aditi, then became a principle that includes the two powers in the form of female and male."

### Printed p. 39
2. "दक्षस्य वादिते जन्मनि........ विसुरूपेषु जन्मषु (*dakṣasya vādite janmani........ visurūpeṣu janmaṣu*)"
(10-64-5)
O Aditi, as soon as Dakṣa was born, and in accordance with his resolve, you nourished the two lords in the form of Mitra and Varuṇa.
Thus, although the mantras 10-72-4 and 5 state both relations together, that Dakṣa is father and son of Aditi, sentences such as 2-27-1 state clearly that Dakṣa is a son of Aditi and one of the Ādityas. Besides, in some other places the gods have the epithet "having Dakṣa as father". For example—
1. "सुज्योतिष: सूर्य दक्षपितृननागास्ते सुमहो वीहि देवान् (*sujyotiṣa: sūrya dakṣapitṛnanāgāste sumaho vīhi devān*)"
(6-50-2)
O great Sūrya, approach the gods who have Dakṣa as father. (Sāyaṇa here has explained the word father as grandfather. Since Aditi is the daughter of Dakṣa and the gods are sons of Aditi, Dakṣa is the grandfather of these gods.)
2. "या धारयंत देवाः सुदक्षा दक्षपितरा । असुराय प्रमहसा ॥ (*yā dhārayaṃta devāḥ sudakṣā dakṣapitarā | asurāya pramahasā ‖*)"
(7-66-2)
Mitra and Varuṇa, who have Dakṣa as father, have established their divine lordship, and so on.
In the same way, in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā also,
ये देवा मनोजाता मनोयुजः सुदक्षा दक्षपितारस्ते नः पांतु ॥ (*ye devā manojātā manoyujaḥ sudakṣā dakṣapitāraste naḥ pāṃtu ‖*)
(Tai. Saṃ. 1-2-3-1)
May the gods, mind-born, yoked by the mind, having Dakṣa himself as father, protect us.
In the examples above it cannot be said precisely that the word Dakṣa is the designation of a particular individual. In 8-25-5 Mitra and Varuṇa are described not only as "powerful sons of Dakṣa" (सूनू दक्षस्य सुक्रतू (*sūnū dakṣasya sukratū*)) but also as "grandsons of strength" (नपाता शवसो महः (*napātā śavaso mahaḥ*)). The way in which Sāyaṇa has explained the mantra 6.50-2 has been stated above. In the Ṛk 7-66-2 quoted above he has explained दक्षपितारा (*dakṣapitārā*) as बलस्य पालकौ (*balasya pālakau*), protectors of strength. The epithets सहसस्पुत्रः (*sahasasputraḥ*) and सहसःसूनुः (*sahasaḥsūnuḥ*) and the like are used in some places with reference to Agni (8-49-2; 8-60-11 and so on) and in some places with reference to Indra (8.79-3 and so on).
