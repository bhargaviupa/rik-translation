You read printed pages A–B of volume N. Files: base = Kannada book `volN/Rigveda_Samhita_VolN_Kannada_Text.md` (`<!-- printed P -->` marks; supplied text, numerals correct); English `volN/Rigveda_Samhita_VolN_English_Translation.md` (`### Page P` or `### Printed p. Kind codes: `docs/ERROR_KINDS.md` (S1 digit, S2 number from memory, S3 printed number missing, S7 quotation misread, S13 Kannada prose/names misread, S14 wrong sense, S15 wrong attribution, S16 addition, S17 omission, S18 grammar page misstated, H29 misprint silently put right).

Read each page's Kannada against the English once, in this order:
- **R1**: a number or reference missing, changed or from memory; a name or term wrong; roles reversed; an addition the Kannada does not have; an omission inside a paragraph; a misprint silently put right.
- **R6**: a Kannada word that is not a word; a name or term misread for a look-alike; vowel signs of the wrong script; a sentence cut at a page break.
- **R3** (reduced, Nov 25 2026): a Sanskrit quotation in the Kannada commentary that is not a word → a row of `kn_misprints.tsv`. The English Devanagari/IAST lines are made from the Kannada by script and are **not edited by hand** (see below); only English prose around a quotation is read.
Do NOT read the Saṃhitā, Pada or Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya blocks (`>` blockquote lines in the Kannada; Devanagari blocks in the English). Do not re-translate. Legacy marks (`[?]`, `[sic]`, "as read", `**GLOSS**`) stay: they are not findings. A grammar page whose English says it is a brief note: check numbers and sūtra names only.

**Decision rule.** A number or reference is taken from the Kannada (the user's decision: its numerals are correct, even where it looks odd) → `applied`. When a printed `[?]` stands next to a number the Kannada has, the Kannada number replaces the whole doubtful form. A text place is `applied` only where the Kannada sentence, the grammar of the quotation or the gloss that follows it decides; otherwise `question`. Where the Kannada prints a non-word (misprint) and the English has silently corrected it, that is a `question`, never a silent change either way. Never settle a number or letter from memory or another edition; do not visit any website; never type Devanagari or IAST yourself: for a Sanskrit repair copy the letters from the Kannada text (the English Devanagari/IAST of a quotation are made from it) and say in the basis which letters changed.

**No questions to the editor (user, Nov 25 2026: "I can't keep reviewing your question list").** Decide every place yourself by these defaults; nothing is asked unless it is a printed number that is impossible (that goes to `open.tsv`, columns exactly `kind, pages, text`, kind `other`, and is applied as printed):
- a Kannada non-word or misprint where the English has the real form → English kept; one row per page in `volN/review/second_pass/kn_misprints.tsv` (columns `page, place, as_found, question, why_not_settled, decision`: put "Kannada misprint X; English has Y" in `question`, leave `why_not_settled` empty, `decision` = "English kept");

## `[?]` marks — resolve them (user, Nov 27 2026: "correct as many ? as you can; all numbers and most words in the Kannada file are correct")
This section **replaces** the line "Legacy marks stay: they are not findings" for `[?]` only (`[sic]`, "as read", `**GLOSS**` still stay). `volN/review/second_pass/qmarks.tsv` lists every `[?]` (columns id, line, page, class, before, after, evidence, proposal); take the rows of your pages (`awk -F'\t' '$3>=A && $3<=B' …`). `python3 tools/rv/recheck_page.py N PAGE` shows the English and the Kannada of a page together. For every row, find the place in the Kannada page and settle it:
1. **The Kannada has the reading and it is certain** (a number or reference: always; a word: when the Kannada word is a real word or name and fits the sentence) → an `en` edit that puts the Kannada's reading in the English and drops the ` [?]`; kind `Q1`, basis "Kannada p. P reads …". The Kannada's numbers are taken as printed, even if odd. For a number, the whole doubtful form is replaced by the Kannada's form (digits as Latin figures, separators as the English has them).
2. **The Kannada agrees with the English** (the doubt was the old reader's) → an `en` edit that removes only the ` [?]` (`old` = the text with the mark, `new` = without).
3. **The Kannada is also doubtful, a misprint, or the place is not in the Kannada** → the mark stays; one row in `decided_by_default.tsv` (or `kn_misprints.tsv` for a Kannada non-word).
4. **Marks on Devanagari lines and their IAST twins** (the old quotation blocks and inline Sanskrit): settle only where the Kannada of the page has the quotation; copy the letters from the Kannada, make the Devanagari/IAST with `python3 tools/rv/sp_sanskrit.py "KANNADA"` (never type them), one edit line each for the Devanagari and the IAST; `sp_apply.py` refuses Devanagari the Kannada does not support. Where the Kannada word itself is a non-word, step 3.
5. **Marks inside our own remarks** (class `remark`: sentences like "the last digit is not settled by the zoom"): the remark is removed later by `cleanup_remarks.py`. Settle the number/word in the English text it speaks of (if that text has its own mark, rule 1) but do not edit the remark itself.
One findings row per settled mark (reader `R1`, kind `Q1`/`Q2`, decision `applied`/`kept`); the report counts marks: seen / removed with a correction / removed unchanged / kept.
6. **Devanagari/IAST edits** (Vol 10, batches 4–6): copy `old` for a Devanagari edit from the English file itself (never rebuild it from the IAST: sandhi differs); a Devanagari edit and its IAST twin are applied together or not at all; if `sp_apply.py` refuses Devanagari, use the exact `sp_sanskrit.py` output; never run `sp_apply.py --dry` on an edits file that was already applied.
