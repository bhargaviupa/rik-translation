# `[?]` reader's brief — marks only (any legacy volume N; the dispatcher gives N and a mark range)

Made Nov 27 2026 (user: "if you cannot resolve the ? marks the second pass is of no use"). Run **after** `python3 tools/rv/qmark_strip.py N --write` (it already cleared the marks in Sanskrit blocks, boilerplate remarks and the as-read references the Kannada page confirms). You settle the marks that are left, **nothing else**: you do not read whole pages for other faults (that is `SECOND_PASS_BRIEF.md`, a later and optional step).

**Input.** `python3 tools/rv/qmark_resolve.py N` (rerun by the dispatcher after the strip) rewrites `volN/review/second_pass/qmarks.tsv` (id, line, page, class, before, after, evidence, proposal). You get a range of ids. For each mark: `python3 tools/rv/recheck_page.py N PAGE` shows the English and the supplied Kannada of the page (run from the repo root; if the output is long, filter out `>` lines or look only at the lines around the mark with `grep -n`). The Kannada pages P−1, P, P+1 are the evidence. The Kannada numerals are correct (user, Nov 24 2026), most Kannada words too.

**Settle every mark, one of five ways**
1. **Number or reference** (`Ni. 1-[?]`, `Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-4[?]-2`, `Pā. Sū. 3-[?]`, with or without an old `as read "…"`): take the reference from the Kannada page: the one with the same work abbreviation and the same context (the word or sūtra it is cited for). `en` edit: replace the whole doubtful form (the number, the `as read "…"`, every `[?]` of it) by the Kannada's number in Latin figures; kind `Q1`.
2. **The Kannada has no number there** → the number is removed together with its mark and any `as read` (rule: no number where the print has none); kind `S3`.
3. **A word or phrase**, the Kannada has a real word that fits → put the Kannada's reading in the English, drop ` [?]`; kind `Q1`. If it is Sanskrit (a Devanagari/IAST word inside the English prose): copy the letters from the Kannada, make the IAST with `python3 tools/rv/sp_sanskrit.py "KANNADA"`, never type Devanagari/IAST.
4. **The Kannada agrees with the English** → remove only ` [?]`; kind `Q2`.
5. **The Kannada has a non-word, a different reading you cannot decide, or does not have the place** → the mark stays; one row in `volN/review/second_pass/kn_misprints.tsv` (Kannada non-word) or `decided_by_default.tsv` (other); kind `Q2`, decision `kept`.
**Marks inside a remark that also carries a translation** ("Sense: …", "means …"): never delete the remark; settle the mark by 1–5 and leave the rest of the remark untouched. A remark that is only a doubt note and whose mark you settled may be shortened by removing the doubt clause, not the translation.
**Several marks in one place** (`1-[?]-[?]-[?]`, a Devanagari line and its IAST twin): one edit that settles all of them; a Devanagari edit and its IAST twin are applied together or not at all.

**Records (append only).** `findings.tsv` (columns `page, kind, reader (R1), place, as_found, proposed, basis, decision (applied|kept), log`), one row per settled place; the edit file `volN/review/second_pass/edits/mA-B.tsv` (`page, target, old, new, kind, basis`; target `en`; `old` copied from the file, standing exactly once on that page; for Devanagari copy `old` from the English file itself, never rebuild it from the IAST); then `python3 tools/rv/sp_apply.py N <edit file>` ONCE for real (never `--dry` on an applied file); `not_found`/`ambiguous`/refused edits go into `mA-B_2.tsv`. Never run `sp_report.py`, never commit or push, never type Devanagari or IAST.

**Report (short, counted from commands you ran):** marks seen; settled by each of the five ways; kept (with the reason by kind); edits applied/failed; five example repairs old → new; anything in this brief that did not work.

**Never delete a `**GLOSS**` marker** (a line or a clause containing it keeps the marker even when its doubt note goes); `[sic]` stays too. (Vol 7, Nov 27 2026: three markers were removed with their remark by readers and put back.)
