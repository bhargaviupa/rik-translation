The greatness of Agastya is extraordinary, and is very famous throughout the Smṛtis, Purāṇas and the like. The manner of his origin itself indicates his greatness.

Mitra and Varuṇa, two of the twelve Ādityas, were both infatuated at the time of a sacrificial session (*satra*) on seeing the celestial woman Urvaśī. Then their semen was shed and fell into the pitcher (*kumbha*) in which the *vasatīvarī* [as supplied] stood. Immediately after that, two sages, Vasiṣṭha and Agastya, ascetics and endowed with vigour, came into being. The great ascetic Agastya became one possessing only a *śamyā* [as supplied], and both Vasiṣṭha and Agastya became possessed of the radiance of the Āditya: the section that describes this episode is given elsewhere.

The part of Mitra, who has the form of this world, and the part of Varuṇa, who has the form of the celestial world, joined together and became the cause of Agastya's origin. The radiant form of Mitra, which consists of food, and the great form of vigour (*ojas*) of Varuṇa are fully blended in Agastya. And as it is described in the sentence क्रतुदक्षौ ह वा अस्य मित्रावरुणौ (*kratudakṣau ha vā asya mitrāvaruṇau*) (Śa. Brā. 4. 1. 4. 1) (my tentative gloss: "Mitra and Varuṇa are indeed his resolve and skill"), of Mitra and Varuṇa, Mitra is of the form of resolve (*saṅkalpa*) and Varuṇa is of the nature of spiritual power. These two, resolve and power, are Agastya's great qualities. Like Mitra, Agastya is one who makes the resolve for the accomplishment of an undertaking (कारसिद्धिगॆ (*kārasiddhigè*) as supplied), and like Varuṇa one who accomplishes it. Like Mitra he is of the form of Brahma, and like Varuṇa of the form of *kratu*; therefore he is also of the nature of the supreme *kratu-dakṣa*.

**Anuvāda (Kannada).** The sūkta beginning with ता वामद्य (*tā vāmadya*) (the first words are joined to the label "Anuvāda—" in the supplied text) is the fifth sūkta in the twenty-fourth anuvāka of the first maṇḍala. There are six ṛks in this sūkta. Agastya is the seer of this sūkta; the Aśvin deities themselves are the deity; the metre is *triṣṭubh*. As it was said earlier that the sūktas from the sūkta beginning युवो रजांसि (*yuvo rajāṃsi*) onward, five sūktas (excepting the third sūkta), are applied for the *triṣṭubh*-metre mantras connected with the Aśvina-śastra at the time of reciting the mantras of the morning litany (*prātaranuvāka*), the same application holds for this sūkta; of those sūktas this is the fifth.

**प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)**

नासत्या (*nāsatyā*) — O Aśvin deities free from untruth. ता वां (*tā vāṃ*) — you, who are famed as protectors. अद्य (*adya*) — on this day of the sacrifice. उषसि (*uṣasi*) — when the Uṣas deity. उच्छंत्यां (*ucchaṃtyāṃ*) — at the time of dispelling the darkness. हुवेम (*huvema*) — we call, praying (and likewise) अपरं (*aparaṃ*) — on other sacrifice days also, ता (*tā*) — we call those two. दिवः नपाता (*divaḥ napātā*) — O Aśvin deities, grandsons of the heavenly world. कुह चित् संतौ (*kuha cit saṃtau*) — wherever you may be. वह्निः (*vahniḥ*) — I, the bearer of praise, अर्यः (*aryaḥ*) — and the impeller of praise (the Hotṛ), सुदास्तराय (*sudāstarāya*) — for the sacrificer who offers excellent oblations, उक्षैः (*ukṣaiḥ*) — call with śastra mantras, praying.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

O Aśvin deities, grandsons of the heavenly world and free from untruth, you who are famed as protectors: on this day of the sacrifice, at the time when the Uṣas deity dispels the darkness, and also on other sacrifice days, wherever you may be, I, the bearer and impeller of praise (the Hotṛ), pray to you and call you with mantras in the form of śastras, for the sacrificer who offers excellent oblations.

The Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya has stated that this sūkta has an application in the morning-litany Āśvina-śastra mantras. The story connected with the Āśvina-śastra is told in the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa as follows.

प्रजापतिर्वै सोमाय राज्ञे दुहितरं प्रायच्छत्सूर्यां सावित्रीं तस्यै सर्वे (*prajāpatirvai somāya rājñe duhitaraṃ prāyacchatsūryāṃ sāvitrīṃ tasyai sarve*)
देवा वरा आगच्छंस्तस्या एतत्सहस्रं वहतुमन्वाकरोद्यदेतदाश्विनमित्या- (*devā varā āgacchaṃstasyā etatsahasraṃ vahatumanvākarodyadetadāśvinamityā-*)
चक्षतेऽनाश्विनं हैव तद्यदर्वाक्सहस्रं तस्मात्तत्सहस्रं वैव शंसेद्भूयो वा (*cakṣate 'nāśvinaṃ haiva tadyadarvāksahasraṃ tasmāttatsahasraṃ vaiva śaṃsedbhūyo vā*)
प्राश्य धृतं शंसेद्यथा ह वा इदमनो वा रथो वाक्तो वर्तत एवं है (*prāśya dhṛtaṃ śaṃsedyathā ha vā idamano vā ratho vākto vartata evaṃ hai*)
वाक्तो वर्तते । शकुनिरिवोत्पतिष्यन्नाह्वयीत । (*vākto vartate | śakunirivotpatiṣyannāhvayīta |*)

(Ai. Brā. 17-1 [the print has "ಇ ಬ್ರಾ." as supplied])

Formerly Prajāpati resolved to give his daughter Sūryā to King Soma in marriage, and then all the gods came with the wish of choosing her. Prajāpati then fixed a rule that the thousand mantras known as the Āśvina-śastra must be recited and that they must not fall short of that. As in the world a chariot wheel moves easily after it has been oiled, mantras can be recited easily after drinking ghee; so one must first drink ghee and then recite. And as a bird makes its body light when it is ready to fly, the suitor who woos her must also have that quality, he said.

तस्मिन्देवा न समजानत ममेदमस्तु ममेदमस्त्विति ते संजानाना अब्रु- (*tasmindevā na samajānata mamedamastu mamedamastviti te saṃjānānā abru-*)
वन्नाजिमस्यायामहै स यो न उज्वेष्यति तस्येदं भविष्यतीति तेऽग्नेरे- (*vannājimasyāyāmahai sa yo na ujveṣyati tasyedaṃ bhaviṣyatīti te 'gnere-*)
वाधि गृहपतेरादित्यं काष्ठामकुर्वत तस्मादाग्नेया प्रतिपद्भवत्याश्विन- (*vādhi gṛhapaterādityaṃ kāṣṭhāmakurvata tasmādāgneyā pratipadbhavatyāśvina-*)
स्याग्निर्होता गृहपतिः स राजेति । (*syāgnirhotā gṛhapatiḥ sa rājeti |*)

(Ai. Brā. 17-1 [as supplied "ಇ. ಬ್ರಾ."])

Then the gods, not wishing to fight one another, together decided that all should take part in a contest and that whoever won in that contest would be entitled to choose her. Then they ran from Agni, the household lord (*gṛhapati*), up to Sūra [as supplied].

तासां वै देवतानामाजिं धावंतीनामभिसृष्टानामग्निर्मुखं प्रथमः (*tāsāṃ vai devatānāmājiṃ dhāvaṃtīnāmabhisṛṣṭānāmagnirmukhaṃ prathamaḥ*)
प्रत्यपद्यत तमश्विनावन्वागच्छतां तमब्रूतामपोदिह्यावां वा इदं (*pratyapadyata tamaśvināvanvāgacchatāṃ tamabrūtāmapodihyāvāṃ vā idaṃ*)
जेष्याव इति स तथेत्यब्रवीत्तस्य वै ममेहाप्यस्त्विति तथेति तस्मा अप्यत्रा- (*jeṣyāva iti sa tathetyabravīttasya vai mamehāpyastviti tatheti tasmā apyatrā-*)
कुरुतां तस्मादाग्नेयमाश्विने शस्यते । (*kurutāṃ tasmādāgneyamāśvine śasyate |*)

(Ai. Brā. 17-2 [as supplied "ಇ ಬ್ರಾ."])

While all the gods were taking part in this contest and running, Agni got ahead of everyone. Then the Aśvin deities approached Agni and asked: "Give us the way; let us win in this contest; help us." Then Agni agreed, but asked that a share of the victory be given to him as well. For that very reason he too has a share in the Āśvina-śastra mantras.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

O Aśvin deities, who obtain satisfaction in the soma juices offered by us and are showerers of desires: in order to make us satisfied, you also obtain complete satisfaction. **विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

ऊर्म्या—रात्रिनामैतत् । यद्वा । ऊर्मिरिति सोमनाम । (*ūrmyā—rātrināmaitat | yadvā | ūrmiriti somanāma |*) — "this is a name of night; or *ūrmi* is a name of Soma." The Nirukta authors have read this word among the names of night (Ni 2-18). Therefore here too the sense "night" may be taken; this is one alternative. If this sense is given, how is the word *ūrmi* formed? Explaining this, Sāyaṇa elsewhere, where this word is used, shows the example and explains thus:

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

O Aśvin deities, free from untruth, nourishers of the world in the forms of the sun and the moon, and divine in nature: for welfare in the form of śastra mantras, and for coming with the daughter of Sūrya seated in your chariot, you set out like arrows, swiftly and straight, for the contest. **विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

पूषन् (*pūṣan*) — this word is used as an epithet of the Aśvin deities. Because they are nourishers of the world, their quality and work are praised by this epithet. By the derivation of this word the quality of Pūṣā, one of the twelve Ādityas, is also described.

प्रूषा- यद्रश्मिपोषं पुष्यति तत् प्रूषा भवति । (*prūṣā- yadraśmipoṣaṃ puṣyati tat prūṣā bhavati |*)

(Ni. 2-16 [the print has "ಸಿ." for "ನಿ."])

Because he nourishes by his rays, the Āditya has the name Pūṣā: this is the etymology of the Nirukta authors. In the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa also:

(Śa. Brā. 14-4-2-25)

It is said that this deity is called Pūṣan because he nourishes everything. This sense may refer to the Āditya; or, since as the carrier of oblations he is the cause of the nourishment of all, gods, men and others, it may refer to Agni; or, if it is taken that the Āditya himself is one of the other forms of Agni, then there is no difficulty. (Ni. 12-1)

By this Nirukta sentence, since there is a view that the Aśvin deities are the sun and the moon, this epithet must naturally apply to them. Telling this same etymology,

the Bṛhaddevatā sentence also gives the same view: because he destroys the darkness and nourishes the earth so that it flourishes, he has the name Pūṣan. Moreover,
