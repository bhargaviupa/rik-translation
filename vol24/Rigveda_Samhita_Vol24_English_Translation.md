## Sūkta 21 (Maṇḍala 8)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> चतुर्थेऽनुवाके दश सूक्तानि । तत्र वयमु त्वामित्यष्टादशर्चं प्रथमं
> सूक्तं । अत्रानुक्रमणिका । वयमु द्व्यूनांत्ये द्वचे चित्रस्य दानस्तुतिरिति ।
> ऋषिश्चान्यस्मादिति परिभाषया काण्वः सोभरिर्यषि ॥ काकुभं प्रागाथं हेत्युक्तत्वादस्यापि सूक्तस्यायुजः ककुभो युजः सतोबृहत्यः । अंत्ये द्वचे चित्रस्य दानं

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

In the fourth anuvāka there are ten sūktas, and among them "vayam u tvām", of eighteen Ṛks, is the first. Sāyaṇa cites the Anukramaṇikā for it: the last two verses are a praise of Citra's gift (dānastuti); the seer is Sobhari Kāṇva by the general rule that the seer is the one named unless another is stated; the metre is Prāgātha of the Kākubha kind, the odd verses being Kakubh and the even verses Satobṛhatī. Of the deity, the last two verses belong to Citra's dānastuti.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The remaining verses are addressed to Indra. The use of the sūkta is that stated in the Laiṅgika rule. In the Ukthya rite, in the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin's recitation, the pair beginning "vayam u tvām" serves as the Prāgātha Stotriya; this is stated in a sūtra (Ā. 6-1). In the Abhiplava Ukthya rites, at the third pressing, this Prāgātha is an optional Stotriya of the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin, as the sūtra states. When, in this same recitation, the Prāgātha "tvaṃ na indrā bhara" is used as Stotriya, the first verse of this sūkta, "vayam u tvā", becomes the first of the Anurūpa triplet; the sūtra on this (Ā. 7-8) says that the Anurūpa is made up by combining the verses beginning "vayam u tvām apūrva", "yo na idam idam pura" and "yāhīma indavaḥ".

In the Kannada: the fourth anuvāka of the eighth maṇḍala has ten sūktas, of which this, beginning "vayam u tvām", is the first, with eighteen Ṛks. The Anukramaṇikā says that the last two verses are a praise of Citra's gift. The seer is Sobhari Kāṇva; as before, the odd verses are in Kakubh metre and the even verses in Satobṛhatī metre. The deity of the last two verses is Citra's dānastuti; the remaining verses are addressed to Indra. The use of the sūkta is Laiṅgika. In the Ukthya sacrifice, in the recitation of the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin priest, the verse "vayam u tvām" is to be recited as the Prāgātha Stotriya, as explained by the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra (Ā. 6-1). In the Abhiplava-related Ukthya sacrifices also, at the third pressing, the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin is to recite the above verse as an optional Stotriya Prāgātha; the relevant sūtra has been given before. When, in the above recitations, the Prāgātha "tvaṃ na indrā bhara" (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-98-10) is recited as the Stotriya, this first Ṛk of the sūkta is to be recited as the first verse of the Anurūpa triplet, as explained by the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra (Ā. 7-8).

> माजे । चित्रं । हवामहे ॥ १ ॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> हे अपूर्व त्रिपु सवनेषु प्रादुर्भूतत्वादभिनवेंद्र भरंतः सोमलक्षणैरन्नैस्तां पोषयंतो वयं वाजे । वाजंति गच्छंति योद्धारोऽत्रेति वाजयंत्यायुधान्यत्रेति वा वाजः संग्रामः । तस्मिंश्चित्रं चायनीयं विविधरूपं
> श्वामु त्वामेवावस्यवो रक्षणमात्मन इच्छंतः संतो हवामहे । त्वामाह्वयामः।
> शत्रु दृष्टांतः । स्फूरं न यथा भरंतो व्रीह्यादिभिर्गृहं पूरयंतो जना
> वाजेऽन्न विषये स्फूरं स्कूलं गुणाधिकं कच्चित् कंचिन्मानवं यथाह्वयंति तद्वत्॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Apūrva Indra, so called because you appear newly at each of the three pressings: we, nourishing you with food in the form of Soma, desiring protection for ourselves, invoke you alone, who are manifold and worthy of worship in battle (vāja). Sāyaṇa explains vāja as battle: the place to which warriors go, or where weapons are set in motion. The illustration: as people filling their house with rice and other grain call upon some stout man, eminent in qualities, in a matter of food, so we call upon you.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- अपूर्व्य (*apūrvya*)—O Indra, newly born at every sacrifice
- स्कूलं न कच्चित् (*skūlaṃ na kaccit*)—as one calls to one's help a stout man able to bear a burden
- भरंतः (*bharaṃtaḥ*)—nourishing you with Soma juice
- वयं (*vayaṃ*)—we
- वाजे (*vāje*)—in battle
- चित्रं (*citraṃ*)—possessed of an attractive form
- त्वां उ (*tvāṃ u*)—you alone
- अवस्यवः (*avasyavaḥ*)—seeking protection
- हवामहे (*havāmahe*)—we call

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, newly born at every sacrifice, as one calls to one's help a stout man able to bear a burden, we who nourish you with Soma juice call upon you alone, who have an attractive form, in battle for protection.

##### English translation
Unpreceded Indra, cherishing you (with sacrificial food), desirous of your protection, we invoke you who are manifold in battle, as (men call upon) some stout (person for help).

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The first pāda is addressed directly: O Indra, in rites such as the Agniṣṭoma we approach you for protection. The second pāda is in the third person: that Indra who overcomes (dhṛṣat) enemies, ever young and fierce, may he come to us; or else, may he make us full of energy. The second half of the verse is again direct: we, your friends, that is, those of the same repute or kinsmen, choose you alone as the one to be resorted to, the protector of all; the particle hi has its well-known sense.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O lord of horses, protector of cattle, lord of Urvarā, that is, of land rich in every crop: these Somas, O Indra, are yours, pressed for you. Therefore come. Having come, O lord of Soma, drink the Soma.

- उर्वरापते (*urvarāpate*)—and protector of crops, O Indra
- इमे इंदवः (*ime iṃdavaḥ*)—these Soma juices are ready for you
- आ याहि (*ā yāhi*)—be pleased to come
- सोमपते (*somapate*)—O Indra, protector of Soma
- सोमं (*somaṃ*)—the Soma
- पिब (*piba*)—drink

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, keeper of horses, keeper of cattle, protector of crops, these Soma juices are ready for you. Be pleased to come; O Indra, protector of Soma, drink the Soma.

##### English translation
Lord of horses, of cattle. of corn-land, these libations (are for you) come, lord of the Soma, drink the effused Soma juice.

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> हे इंद्र अबंधवो बंधुरहिता विप्रासो मेधाविनो वयं बंधुमंतं  
> बंधुभिर्देवैरंगिरोभिर्वा तद्वंतं त्वा त्वां । हिरवधारणे । त्वामेव येमिम ।  
> बंधुत्वेन नियच्छाम ॥ यच्छतेर्लिटि रूपं ॥ तथा सति हे वृषभ कामानां वर्षितरिंद्र ते तव या यानि धामानि शरीराणि तेजांसि वा विद्यंते तेभिस्तैर्विश्वेभिः  
> सर्वैर्धामभिः सह सोमपीतये सोमपानार्थमा गहि ! आगच्छ ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Indra, we, kinless and wise, hold you alone as our kinsman; you are possessed of kinsmen, that is, of the gods or of the Aṅgirases. Therefore, O showerer of desires, Indra, come with all those bodies or lights of yours that exist, to drink the Soma. **NOTE**

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- इंद्र (*iṃdra*)—O Indra
- अबंधवः (*abaṃdhavaḥ*)—kinless
- विप्रासः (*viprāsaḥ*)—and wise
- वयं (*vayaṃ*)—we
- बंधुमंतं (*baṃdhumaṃtaṃ*)—provided with kinsmen such as the gods
- त्वा हि (*tvā hi*)—you alone
- येमिम (*yemima*)—we shall choose as kinsman
- वृषभ (*vṛṣabha*)—O Indra, showerer of desires
- ते (*te*)—your
- या (*yā*)—which
- धामानि (*dhāmāni*)—glories there are
- तेभिः (*tebhiḥ*)—such
- विश्वेभिः (*viśvebhiḥ*)—with all glories
- सोमपीतये (*somapītaye*)—for drinking the Soma
- आ गहि (*ā gahi*)—be pleased to come

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, we, kinless and wise, shall choose as our kinsman you alone, who are accompanied by kinsmen such as the gods. O Indra, showerer of desires, together with all those glories of yours that exist, be pleased to come to drink the Soma.

##### English translation
Intelligent, but destitute of kin, let us connect ourselves, Indra, with you, who abound with kinsmen : come, showerer (of benefits), with all your glories, to drink the Soma.
