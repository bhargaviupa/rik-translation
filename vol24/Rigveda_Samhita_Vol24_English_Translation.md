## Sūkta 21 (Maṇḍala 8)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> चतुर्थेऽनुवाके दश सूक्तानि । तत्र वयमु त्वामित्यष्टादशर्चं प्रथमं

> ऋषिश्चान्यस्मादिति परिभाषया काण्वः सोभरिर्यषि ॥ काकुभं प्रागाथं हेत्युक्तत्वादस्यापि सूक्तस्यायुजः ककुभो युजः सतोबृहत्यः । अंत्ये द्वचे चित्रस्य दानं

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

In the fourth anuvāka there are ten sūktas, and among them "vayam u tvām", of eighteen Ṛks, is the first. Sāyaṇa cites the Anukramaṇikā for it: the last two verses are a praise of Citra's gift (dānastuti); the seer is Sobhari Kāṇva by the general rule that the seer is the one named unless another is stated; the metre is Prāgātha of the Kākubha kind, the odd verses being Kakubh and the even verses Satobṛhatī. Of the deity, the last two verses belong to Citra's dānastuti.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The remaining verses are addressed to Indra. The use of the sūkta is that stated in the Laiṅgika rule. In the Ukthya rite, in the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin's recitation, the pair beginning "vayam u tvām" serves as the Prāgātha Stotriya; this is stated in a sūtra (Ā. 6-1). In the Abhiplava Ukthya rites, at the third pressing, this Prāgātha is an optional Stotriya of the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin, as the sūtra states. When, in this same recitation, the Prāgātha "tvaṃ na indrā bhara" is used as Stotriya, the first verse of this sūkta, "vayam u tvā", becomes the first of the Anurūpa triplet; the sūtra on this (Ā. 7-8) says that the Anurūpa is made up by combining the verses beginning "vayam u tvām apūrva", "yo na idam idam pura" and "yāhīma indavaḥ".

In the Kannada: the fourth anuvāka of the eighth maṇḍala has ten sūktas, of which this, beginning "vayam u tvām", is the first, with eighteen Ṛks. The Anukramaṇikā says that the last two verses are a praise of Citra's gift. The seer is Sobhari Kāṇva; as before, the odd verses are in Kakubh metre and the even verses in Satobṛhatī metre. The deity of the last two verses is Citra's dānastuti; the remaining verses are addressed to Indra. The use of the sūkta is Laiṅgika. In the Ukthya sacrifice, in the recitation of the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin priest, the verse "vayam u tvām" is to be recited as the Prāgātha Stotriya, as explained by the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra (Ā. 6-1). In the Abhiplava-related Ukthya sacrifices also, at the third pressing, the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin is to recite the above verse as an optional Stotriya Prāgātha; the relevant sūtra has been given before. When, in the above recitations, the Prāgātha "tvaṃ na indrā bhara" (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-98-10) is recited as the Stotriya, this first Ṛk of the sūkta is to be recited as the first verse of the Anurūpa triplet, as explained by the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra (Ā. 7-8).

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> हे अपूर्व त्रिपु सवनेषु प्रादुर्भूतत्वादभिनवेंद्र भरंतः सोमलक्षणैरन्नैस्तां पोषयंतो वयं वाजे । वाजंति गच्छंति योद्धारोऽत्रेति वाजयंत्यायुधान्यत्रेति वा वाजः संग्रामः । तस्मिंश्चित्रं चायनीयं विविधरूपं
> श्वामु त्वामेवावस्यवो रक्षणमात्मन इच्छंतः संतो हवामहे । त्वामाह्वयामः।
> शत्रु दृष्टांतः । स्फूरं न यथा भरंतो व्रीह्यादिभिर्गृहं पूरयंतो जना
> वाजेऽन्न विषये स्फूरं स्कूलं गुणाधिकं कच्चित् कंचिन्मानवं यथाह्वयंति तद्वत्॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Apūrva Indra, so called because you appear newly at each of the three pressings: we, nourishing you with food in the form of Soma, desiring protection for ourselves, invoke you alone, who are manifold and worthy of worship in battle (vāja). Sāyaṇa explains vāja as battle: the place to which warriors go, or where weapons are set in motion. The illustration: as people filling their house with rice and other grain call upon some stout man, eminent in qualities, in a matter of food, so we call upon you.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- अपूर्व्य (*apūrvya*)—O Indra, newly born at every sacrifice
- स्कूलं न कच्चित् (*skūlaṃ na kaccit*)—as one calls to one's help a stout man able to bear a burden
- भरंतः (*bharaṃtaḥ*)—nourishing you with Soma juice
- वयं (*vayaṃ*)—we
- वाजे (*vāje*)—in battle
- चित्रं (*citraṃ*)—possessed of an attractive form
- त्वां उ (*tvāṃ u*)—you alone
- अवस्यवः (*avasyavaḥ*)—seeking protection
- हवामहे (*havāmahe*)—we call

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, newly born at every sacrifice, as one calls to one's help a stout man able to bear a burden, we who nourish you with Soma juice call upon you alone, who have an attractive form, in battle for protection.

##### English translation
Unpreceded Indra, cherishing you (with sacrificial food), desirous of your protection, we invoke you who are manifold in battle, as (men call upon) some stout (person for help).

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The first pāda is addressed directly: O Indra, in rites such as the Agniṣṭoma we approach you for protection. The second pāda is in the third person: that Indra who overcomes (dhṛṣat) enemies, ever young and fierce, may he come to us; or else, may he make us full of energy. The second half of the verse is again direct: we, your friends, that is, those of the same repute or kinsmen, choose you alone as the one to be resorted to, the protector of all; the particle hi has its well-known sense.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O lord of horses, protector of cattle, lord of Urvarā, that is, of land rich in every crop: these Somas, O Indra, are yours, pressed for you. Therefore come. Having come, O lord of Soma, drink the Soma.

- इमे इंदवः (*ime iṃdavaḥ*)—these Soma juices are ready for you
- आ याहि (*ā yāhi*)—be pleased to come
- सोमपते (*somapate*)—O Indra, protector of Soma
- सोमं (*somaṃ*)—the Soma
- पिब (*piba*)—drink

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, keeper of horses, keeper of cattle, protector of crops, these Soma juices are ready for you. Be pleased to come; O Indra, protector of Soma, drink the Soma.

##### English translation
Lord of horses, of cattle. of corn-land, these libations (are for you) come, lord of the Soma, drink the effused Soma juice.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

समस्मिन् (*samasmin*). Explaining the word samasya, Yāska has said सममिति परिग्रहार्थीयं सर्वनामानुदात्तं (*samamiti parigrahārthīyaṃ sarvanāmānudāttaṃ*). That is, the word samam is a pronoun; samam means "all". This word is generally used in the sense of inclusion, and it is wholly unaccented. He says that this word is used in various cases and numbers, and to support this he has given some examples.

मा नः समस्यॆ दूढ्यर्णपरिद्वेषसो अंहतिः (*mā naḥ samasyè dūḍhyarṇaparidveṣaso aṃhatiḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-75-9). In this Ṛk the word is used in the genitive case. Here samasyai means "of all".

उतो समस्मिन्नाशीहि नो वसो (*uto samasminnāśīhi no vaso*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-21-8). In this Ṛk, the present one, the word is used in the locative case. For the verb śiśīhi in this sentence Yāska has said शिशीतिर्दानकर्मा (*śiśītirdānakarmā*), that is, the meaning is giving, bestowing.

उरुष्याणो अघायतः समस्मात् (*uruṣyāṇo aghāyataḥ samasmāt*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 5-24-3). Here the ablative case is used.

नभंतामन्यके समे (*nabhaṃtāmanyake same*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-39-1 to 10). In these Ṛks the word is used in the nominative plural.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

In the Ukthya rite, in the recitation of the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin, this Prāgātha beginning "yo na idam idam" is the Anurūpa; the sūtra says that the pair of Prāgāthas beginning "yo na idam idaṃ purā" are all Kakubh (Ā. 6-1). In the Abhiplava Ukthya rites also, in the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin's recitation, this is an optional Anurūpa (Ā. 7-8). When in the Abhiplava Ukthya rites at the third pressing, in the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin's recitation, the Prāgātha "tvaṃ na indrā bhara" is the Stotriya, the Anurūpa triplet is to be put together, and in it this verse "yo na idam idam" is the second; the sūtra states that the Anurūpa is put together from "yo na idam idaṃ purā" and "yāhīma indavaḥ" (Ā. 7-8). Meaning: O friends, that is, priests and sacrificers of the same repute, that Indra who formerly brought to us this excellent wealth, present and visible, namely that bringer of riches, I, Sobhari, praise for you, for the gain of wealth and for protection.

O Indra, you do not find, that is, you do not resort to or rely upon, for friendship a man who is merely wealthy (revant), a rich man devoid of giving and the like, who does not sacrifice. You do not resort to them.

> प्रगाथाः । अश्विनौ देवता । तथा चानुक्रांतं। ओ त्यमाश्विनं त्रिप्रगाथादि बृहत्यनुष्टुबेकादशाद्वे ककुम्मध्येजोतिषी इति ॥ प्रातरनुवाक आश्विने क्रत्वा बार्हते

> आ. ४-१५ । इति ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The sūkta beginning ओ त्यमद्व इत्यष्टादशर्चं (*o tyamadva ityaṣṭādaśarcaṃ*), of eighteen Ṛks, is the second sūkta; its seer is Sobhari Kāṇva. Of its verses, the first, third and fifth are Bṛhatī; the second, fourth and sixth are Satobṛhatī; the seventh is Bṛhatī; the eighth is Anuṣṭubh. By the continuation of what was said, "Kākubha Prāgātha", the remaining four are Kākubha Pragāthas. The deity is the Aśvins. Accordingly the Anukramaṇikā: ओ त्यमाश्विनं त्रिप्रगाथादि बृहत्यनुष्टुबेकादशाद्वे ककुम्मध्येजोतिषी (*o tyamāśvinaṃ tripragāthādi bṛhatyanuṣṭubekādaśādve kakummadhyejotiṣī*) **GLOSS**. In the Prātaranuvāka, in the Āśvina rite, in the Bārhata metre and in the Āśvina-śastra, the first seven Ṛks are used. This is laid down by the sūtra ओ त्यमद्व आ रथमिति सप्त (*o tyamadva ā rathamiti sapta*) (Ā. 4-15).

In the Kannada: this sūkta beginning "o tyam ahve" is the second sūkta in the fourth anuvāka. It has eighteen Ṛks. Its seer is Sobhari Kāṇva; the Aśvins

> शोभनाह्वानं पुरुस्पृहं बहुभिः स्पृहणीयं भुज्युं ॥ भुज पालने ॥ सर्वस्य

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Sobhari, praise the chariot of the Aśvins with sumati, that is, with auspicious praises. Of what kind is it? Pūrvāpuṣa: the nourisher of former praisers by gifts of wealth and the like; suhava: well invoked in battles; purusprha: desired by many; bhujyu (from the root bhuj, to protect): the protector of all; vājeṣu pūrvya: the one who goes in front in battles; sacanāvat: worshipped by all; vidveṣas: the especial hater of enemies; anehas: harmed by no one, or free from sin. The construction is: praise the chariot.

- सोभरे (*sobhare*)—O Sobhari
- पूर्वापुषं (*pūrvāpuṣaṃ*)—that which nourished all the former sacrificers
- सुहवं (*suhavaṃ*)—that which has an excellent invocation
- पुरुस्पृहं (*puruspṛhaṃ*)—that which is worthy of the regard of many
- भुज्युं (*bhujyuṃ*)—that which protects all
- वाजेषु (*vājeṣu*)—in battles
- पूर्व्यं (*pūrvyaṃ*)—that which rushes forward before all
- सचनावंतं (*sacanāvaṃtaṃ*)—that which is to be worshipped by all
- विद्वेषसं (*vidveṣasaṃ*)—that which is to hate enemies
- अनेहसं (*anehasaṃ*)—and that which is without sin: the chariot of the Aśvin gods
- सुमतिभिः (*sumatibhiḥ*)—praise with excellent praises

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Sobhari, praise with excellent praises the chariot of the Aśvin gods, which nourished all the former sacrificers, which has an excellent invocation, which is worthy of the regard of many, which protects all, which rushes forward before all in battles, which is to be worshipped by all, which is to hate enemies, and which is without sin.

##### English translation
Celebrate, Sobhari, with praises (that chariot), the benefactor of former (encomiasts), the worthily invoked. the desired of many, the preserver, the foremost in battles, the relied upon by all, the scatterer of enemies, the exempt from ill.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Purubhūtamā: the two who exceedingly cause the many enemies to come into being (bhāvayitārau) **NOTE**. Devā: the two gods, shining by nature and praiseworthy. - अर्वाचीना (*arvācīnā*)—so that they come towards us

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

The Aśvin gods, who greatly subdue enemies, who are of divine nature, who go to the house of the sacrificer who offers the oblation, and who are famous, we worship well and draw, in this sacrificial rite, for our protection, with oblations accompanied by praise, so that they come towards us.

##### English translation
We incite by our adorations on this occasion the two divine Aswins, the overcomers of foes, that they may come down for our protection and proceed to the dwelling of the donor (of the offering).

- इषण्यति (*iṣaṇyati*)—follows
- शुभस्पती (*śubhaspatī*)—O Aśvin gods, protectors of water
- धेनुरिव (*dhenuriva*)—as a cow runs lovingly towards her calf
- वां (*vāṃ*)—your
- सुमतिः (*sumatiḥ*)—auspicious thought
- आस्मान् अच्छ (*āsmān accha*)—towards us
- आ धावतु (*ā dhāvatu*)—may it come quickly and join us

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Aśvin gods, one wheel of your chariot moves everywhere; the other wheel follows you, the impellers of the world. O Aśvin gods, protectors of water, as a cow runs with love towards her calf, so may your auspicious thought come quickly to us, towards us.

##### English translation
One of the wheels of your car moves in every direction; the other, impellers of actions, remains with you: may your favour, lords of rain, hasten towards us as a cow (to her calf)!

#### Ṛk 5

O Aśvins, your chariot is trivandhura. Vandhura is the driver's place; the chariot is furnished with vandhura of three kinds. Furnished with the three vandhuras, hiraṇyābhīśu, having golden ropes for the horses and the rest, that chariot of yours which, being famed everywhere, pari bhūṣati: overcomes heaven and earth by its own strength; or else it adorns them on all sides with its own radiance. O Nāsatyas, come with that chariot spoken of before.

- हिरण्याभीशुः (*hiraṇyābhīśuḥ*)—and furnished with a golden bridle
- वां (*vāṃ*)—your
- यः (*yaḥ*)—which
- रथः (*rathaḥ*)—chariot
- श्रुतः (*śrutaḥ*)—being famed everywhere
- द्यावापृथिवी (*dyāvāpṛthivī*)—both heaven and earth
- परिभूषति (*paribhūṣati*)—adorns all round
- तेन (*tena*)—with that chariot
- आ गतं (*ā gataṃ*)—graciously come

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Aśvin gods, called Nāsatya, come graciously with that chariot of yours which, furnished with this threefold vandhura of the two shafts and the yoke-staff, and furnished with a golden bridle, is famed everywhere and adorns both heaven and earth all round.

##### English translation
Your celebrated chariot, Aswins, that is threebanked, and caparisoned with gold, graces heaven and earth : come with it, Nasatyas.

> ताभि॒रा या॑तं वृष॒णोप॑ मे॒ हवं॑ वि॒श्वप्सुं॑ वि॒श्ववा॑र्यं ।  
> इ॒षा मंहि॑ष्ठा पुरु॒भूत॑मा नरा॒ याभिः॒ क्रिविं॑ वावृ॒धुस्ताभि॒रा ग॑तं ॥ १२ ॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> ताभिः । आ । यातं । वृषणा । उप । मे । हवं । विश्वप्सुं । विश्ववार्यं ।  
> इषा । मंहिष्ठा । पुरुभूतमा । नरा । याभिः । क्रिविं । ववृधुः । ताभिः । आ । गतं ॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> वृषणा वृषणौ वर्षणशील् हे अश्विन् विश्वप्पुं । प्सुरिति रूपनाम ! स्तोत्रशस्त्रात्मकत्वेन नानारूपं विश्ववार्यं सर्वैर्देवैर्वणीयं मे मदीयं हवं
> युष्मद्विषयमाह्वानमाभिमुखीकृत्य ताभिरूतिभिरुप यातं । युवामागच्छतं ।
> याभिर्नरा नर सर्वस्य नेतारावश्विनाविषा ॥ इष इच्छायां ॥ हवींशीच्छंतौ
> मंहिष्ठा मुंहिष्ठावतिशयेन धनानां दातार् पुरुभूतमा पुरुभूतम् युद्धेस्वत्यंतं पुरुधा भवंतौ यद्वातिशयेन बहूनां शत्रूणामभिभवितार् भवंतौ
> क्रिविं कूपं प्रति याभिरूतिभिरुदकानि वावृधुः अवर्धयतः ॥ द्वयोर्बहुवचनं
> पूजार्थं ॥ कूपपतितो वंदनो मायाभिः कूपं पूरयित्वा युवाभ्यामुक्तापितः खलु । तथा मंत्रः । उद्वंदनमैरयतं स्वर्दृशे । ऋ. १-११२-५ । ४३ ।
> शाभिस्तैः पालनैरा गतं । अस्मद्रक्षणार्थमागच्छतं ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Aśvins, showerers, viśvapsu: psu is a word for form; having manifold forms, as consisting of stotra and śastra; viśvavārya: to be chosen by all the gods. Making my invocation, which is addressed to you, turn towards you, come with those protections. Narā: the leaders of all, the two Aśvins; iṣā: desiring oblations; maṃhiṣṭā: exceedingly givers of wealth; purubhūtamā: exceedingly becoming manifold in battles, or exceedingly overcoming many enemies. With the protections with which, to a well (kriviṃ), you made the waters increase: the plural for two is for honour. Vandana, having fallen into a well, was raised out by you, after you filled the well by your wonders. Accordingly the mantra उद्वंदनमैरयतं स्वर्दृशे (*udvaṃdanamairayataṃ svardṛśe*) (Ṛ. 1-112-5) **GLOSS**. By those protections come, for our protection.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- वृषणा (*vṛṣaṇā*)—O Aśvin gods, who cause rain
- विश्वप्सुं (*viśvapsuṃ*)—having manifold forms
- विश्वनार्यं (*viśvanāryaṃ*)—and dear to all
- मे (*me*)—my
- हवं आ (*havaṃ ā*)—towards the praise
- शाभिः (*śābhiḥ*)—with those same protections
- उप यातं (*upa yātaṃ*)—come
- नरा (*narā*)—leaders
- इवा (*ivā*)—desiring oblations
- मंहिष्ठा (*maṃhiṣṭhā*)—liberal givers of wealth
- पुरुभूतमा (*purubhūtamā*)—and attaining manifold manifestation, O Aśvin gods
- याभिः (*yābhiḥ*)—with which protections
- क्रिविं (*kriviṃ*)—the well
- वावृधुः (*vāvṛdhuḥ*)—having made it grow, you protected the seer named Vandana
- शाभिः (*śābhiḥ*)—with all those protections
- आ गतं (*ā gataṃ*)—graciously come

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Aśvin gods, causers of rain, come with those same protections towards my praise, which has manifold forms and is dear to all. O leaders of the rite, desirous of oblations, liberal givers of wealth, attaining manifold manifestation, O Aśvin gods, with whatever protections you made the well grow and protected Vandana, with all those same protections graciously come.

##### English translation
Then, showerers, come to my manifold all-propitiating invocation
with those (protections), with which, leaders (of rites), you, who are
gratified (by oblation), munificent (in gifts), and the overcomers of
numerous (foes), gave augmentation to the well ; with such (protections)
come hither.

Those two previously mentioned Aśvins, who spread out weapons in battle or riches for the praisers: at this very time of the day, that is, in the morning, I, offering homage, address them, sitting near them. Then, with oblations and with praises, we call just those two; we ask for riches and the like.

- इदा चित् (*idā cit*)—at this very time, that is, in the morning

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> सु॒प्रा॒व॒र्गं सु॒वीर्यं॑ सु॒ष्टु वार्य॒मना॑धृष्टं रक्ष॒स्विना॑ ।  
> अ॒स्मिन्ना वा॑मा॒याने॑ वाजिनीवसू॒ विश्वा॑ वा॒मानि॑ धीमहि ॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> सुप्रावर्गं । सुवीर्यं । सुष्टु । वार्यं । अनाधृष्टं । रक्षस्विना ।  
> अस्मिन् । आ । वां । आयाने । वाजिनीवसू इति वाजिनीऽवसू । विश्वा । वामानि ।  
> धीमहि ॥ १८ ॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> सुप्रावर्गं शोभनं प्रवर्जनं यस्य तत् । स्तोतृभ्यः स्वयमेव
> प्रयच्छतीत्यर्थः । तथाविधं सुवीर्यं शोभनवीर्यं सुष्टु शोभनं यथा
> भवति तथा वार्यं सर्वैर्वणीयं रक्षस्विना बलवताप्यनाधृष्टमनभिभवनीयं
> धनमा धीमहि । त्वत्तो वयं धारयामः । तदेवाह । वाजिनीवसू बलयुक्त-

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Supravargam: that whose giving is good, that is, which it gives of itself to the praisers. Of that kind, suvīryam: of good strength; suṣṭu: so that it is good; vāryam: to be chosen by all; anādhṛṣṭam: unassailable even by the mighty. Such wealth we take up; we hold it from you. That very thing he says: vājinīvasū, endowed with strength
