## SBh 1.154.0.1

The sūkta beginning ⟦विष्णोर्नु कम्⟧, of six Ṛks, is the fifteenth sūkta; it is of Dīrghatamas, in the triṣṭubh metre, and addressed to Viṣṇu. On this the Anukramaṇikā says: ⟦विष्णोः षड्वैष्णवं हि⟧, "'Viṣṇoḥ': six Ṛks, addressed to Viṣṇu indeed". In the Ukthya days of the Abhiplava six-day periods, at the third pressing, when the stoma is increased, the pair of sūktas beginning with this one is employed for the acchāvāka's reciting beyond the stoma. It is laid down in the section ⟦स्तोमे वर्धमाने⟧, "when the stoma is increased": ⟦विष्णोर्नु कमिति सूक्ते परोमात्रयेत्यच्छावाकः⟧ (⟦आश्व. श्रौ. ७. ९⟧), "the acchāvāka recites the two sūktas beginning 'viṣṇor nu kam' and the one beginning 'paro mātrayā'". Likewise, when at the third pressing there is Soma left over, each next following form of the sacrifice is to be gone on to, up to the Atirātra; when there is Soma left over even beyond that, a śastra is to be produced for that very purpose. There this same sūkta is used. It is laid down in the section ⟦सोमातिरेके⟧, "when Soma is left over": ⟦महाँ इन्द्रो नृवद्विष्णोर्नु कम्⟧ (⟦आश्व. श्रौ. ६. ७⟧), which names the sūkta beginning "mahām̐ indro nṛvat" and the sūkta beginning "viṣṇor nu kam".

## SBh 1.154.0.2

In the Āgnimāruta-śastra the first Ṛk is employed. It is laid down in the section ⟦अथ यथेतम्⟧: ⟦विष्णोर्नु कं वीर्याणि प्र वोचं तन्तुं तन्वन्रजसो भानुमन्विहि⟧ (⟦आश्व. श्रौ. ५. २०⟧), which names the Ṛk beginning "viṣṇor nu kaṃ vīryāṇi pra vocam" and the Ṛk beginning "tantuṃ tanvan rajaso bhānum anv ihi".

## SBh 1.154.1.1

O men, of ⟦विष्णोः⟧, of the god whose nature it is to pervade, the ⟦वीर्याणि⟧, the heroic deeds, ⟦नु⟧ ⟦कम्⟧, very quickly, ⟦प्र⟧ ⟦वोचम्⟧, I declare. Although ⟦नु कम्⟧ is here a pair of words, still, because Yāska has said ⟦नवोत्तराणि पदानि⟧ (⟦नि. ३. १३⟧), "the next nine are words", and because in another śākhā it is recited as one, the pair of words ⟦नु कम्⟧ is in the very same sense as ⟦नु⟧. Which are those deeds? To that he says: ⟦यः⟧, the Viṣṇu who ⟦विममे⟧, fashioned in a special way, the ⟦पार्थिवानि⟧, connected with the earth, ⟦रजांसि⟧, whose nature it is to colour, the rajases that preside over the three worlds beginning with the earth and have the form of Agni, Vāyu and Āditya. Here the three worlds too are denoted by the word pṛthivī. And so there is another mantra: ⟦यदिन्द्राग्नी अवमस्यां पृथिव्यां मध्यमस्यां परमस्यामुत स्थः⟧ (⟦ऋ. सं. १. १०८.९⟧), "Whether, O Indra and Agni, you are on the lowest earth, on the middle one, or on the highest".

## SBh 1.154.1.2

In the Taittirīya too, after beginning with ⟦योऽस्यां पृथिव्यामस्यायुषा⟧, "You who are on this earth, with life", it is said: ⟦यो द्वितीयस्यां तृतीयस्यां पृथिव्याम्⟧ (⟦तै. सं. १. २. १२. १⟧), "who on the second, on the third earth". Therefore the three worlds are denoted by the word pṛthivī. Moreover, ⟦यः⟧, and the Viṣṇu who ⟦अस्कभायत्⟧, propped as the support of those, the ⟦उत्तरम्⟧, raised higher, exceedingly wide, ⟦सधस्थं⟧, common abode, the mid-region which is the resting-place of the three worlds; the meaning is that he fashioned it. By this it comes to be said that he created the three worlds too, which rest on the mid-region. Or else: the Viṣṇu who fashioned in various ways the things connected with the earth, the rajases, the seven worlds below the earth. The word rajas denotes a world, because Yāska has said ⟦लोका रजांस्युच्यन्ते⟧, "The worlds are called rajāṃsi". Moreover, and he who propped the higher, the one raised higher, coming above, the common abode, fit for the doers of merit to dwell in together, the set of seven worlds beginning with Bhūr; the meaning is that he created it. Of the root skambh, in place of śnā, which is enjoined by ⟦स्तम्भुस्तुम्भु⟧, there is by interchange the substitute śāyac, according to ⟦छन्दसि शायजपि⟧, "in the Veda śāyac too".

## SBh 1.154.1.3

Or else: he fashioned the things that have the earth as their cause, the rajases, the worlds; the meaning is, the triad of worlds beginning with Bhūr. Because the other worlds are for the enjoying of the works earned on the earth, the earth is their cause. Moreover, and he who propped the higher, the more excellent one, which is above all the worlds. Its excellence is from there being no return from it. He propped the common abode, the place where the worshippers stay together, the world of Satya; the meaning is that he set it up firm. Doing what? ⟦त्रेधा⟧ ⟦विचक्रमाणः⟧, striding in various ways, in three manners, over the worlds created by himself. The threefold striding of Viṣṇu is well known in passages of the Veda such as ⟦इदं विष्णुर्वि चक्रमे⟧ (⟦ऋ. सं. १, २२, १७⟧), "Viṣṇu strode over this". For that very reason he is ⟦उरुगायः⟧, sung by the great, or sung very abundantly. Of such a Viṣṇu, who has done thus, I declare the heroic deeds.

## SBh 1.154.2.1

At the third pressing, precisely when there is Soma left over, a śastra is to be produced. There this triplet beginning ⟦प्र तत्⟧ is the anurūpa. It is laid down in the section ⟦सोमातिरेके⟧, "when Soma is left over": ⟦प्र तद्विष्णुः स्तवते वीर्येणेति स्तोत्रियानुरूपौ⟧ (⟦आश्व. श्रौ. ६. ७⟧), "the stotriya and the anurūpa are the ones ending with 'pra tad viṣṇuḥ stavate vīryeṇa'". It is laid down in the section ⟦वाजपेयेनाधिपत्यकामः⟧, "one who desires lordship, with the Vājapeya": ⟦प्र तत्ते अद्य शिपिविष्ट नाम प्र तद्विष्णुः स्तवते वीर्येण⟧ (⟦आश्व. श्रौ. ९. ९⟧), which names the Ṛk beginning "pra tat te adya śipiviṣṭa nāma" and the Ṛk beginning "pra tad viṣṇuḥ stavate vīryeṇa".

## SBh 1.154.2.2

Because "whose" is going to be said, "he" is understood. He, of great majesty, by ⟦वीर्येण⟧, by his own heroic deed of the form stated before, ⟦स्तवते⟧, is praised by all.

## SBh 1.154.2.3

The vikaraṇa śap comes, by interchange, in the passive. There is an illustration of his being praised for his heroic deed: ⟦मृगो⟧ ⟦न⟧, like a lion or the like. As a lion, the hunter of those opposed to him, ⟦भीमः⟧, producing fear, ⟦कुचरः⟧, a doer of blameworthy acts such as harming, or one who goes to places hard of access, ⟦गिरिष्ठाः⟧, staying in high places such as mountains, is praised by all. On this matter there is the Nirukta: ⟦मृगो न भीमः कुचरो गिरिष्ठाः । मृग इव भीमः कुचरो गिरिष्ठा मृगो मार्ष्टेर्गतिकर्मणो भीमो बिभ्यत्यस्माद्भीष्मोऽप्येतस्मादेव । कुचर इति चरतिकर्म कुत्सितमथ चेद्देवताभिधानं क्वायं न चरतीति वा । गिरिष्ठा गिरिस्थायी गिरिः पर्वतः समुद्गीर्णो भवति पर्ववान् पर्वतः पर्व पुनः पृणातेः प्रीणातेर्वा⟧ (⟦निरु. १, २०⟧), "'Fearsome like a mṛga, kucara, giriṣṭhāḥ': fearsome as a mṛga is, kucara, giriṣṭhāḥ. Mṛga is from the root mṛj, which has the sense of going. Bhīma: they are afraid of him; bhīṣma too is from this same. Kucara: he does a blameworthy act; but if it is a name of the deity, then: where does this one not move? Giriṣṭhāḥ: staying on a mountain. Giri is a mountain: it is raised up. Parvata is that which has joints; and parvan again is from the root pṝ or from the root prī".

## SBh 1.154.2.4

In the same way this one too is a mṛga, a seeker-out of enemies; bhīma, terrible, the one from whom all have fear. Fear of the Supreme Lord is well known in passages of the Veda such as ⟦भीषास्माद्वातः पवते⟧ (⟦तै. आ. ८.८. १⟧), "From fear of him the wind blows". Moreover he is kucara, a doer of blameworthy acts such as the slaying of enemies, or one who moves about in all the lands, in the three worlds; and likewise ⟦गिरिष्ठाः⟧, staying in a world that is raised high like a mountain. Or else: one who is at all times present in speech in the form of the mantras and the like. Such **NOTE**{the unit has ⟦ईडशोऽयं⟧; īdṛśo 'yam, "such a one, this", is translated: is the first word a typo?} is this one, and he is praised for his own greatness. Moreover, that Viṣṇu is praised, ⟦यस्य⟧, in whose ⟦उरुषु⟧, wide, three in number, ⟦विक्रमणेषु⟧, puttings-forth of the foot, ⟦विश्वा⟧, all, ⟦भुवनानि⟧, the multitudes of beings, ⟦अधिक्षियन्ति⟧, dwell, resting on them.

## SBh 1.154.3.1

⟦विष्णवे⟧, to the all-pervading one: may ⟦शूषम्⟧, the strength, the greatness, born of our rites and the like, and ⟦मन्म⟧, the thought, the praise, or else the strength that is worthy of being thought upon, ⟦एतु⟧, reach Viṣṇu.

## SBh 1.154.3.2

The fourth case-ending is used because the object is the recipient. To one of what kind? To ⟦गिरिक्षिते⟧, him who stays in speech, or in a place that is high like a mountain; to ⟦उरुगायाय⟧, him who is sung by many; to ⟦वृष्णे⟧, the showerer of desires. May the strength reach him who is of such great majesty. What is his distinction? It is said: ⟦यः⟧, the Viṣṇu who, ⟦एकः⟧ ⟦इत्⟧, being one only, without a second, with ⟦त्रिभिः⟧ ⟦पदेभिः⟧, with three feet, ⟦विममे⟧, fashioned in a special way, ⟦इदं⟧, this well-known, visible, ⟦दीर्घम्⟧, exceedingly extended, ⟦प्रयतं⟧, fixed, ⟦सधस्थ⟧ **NOTE**{the unit has ⟦सधस्थ⟧ without the final nasal; the Ṛk's sadhastham is translated: is it a typo?}, common abode, the three worlds.

## SBh 1.154.4.1

⟦यस्य⟧, the Viṣṇu whose three ⟦पदानि⟧, puttings-forth of the foot, ⟦पूर्णा⟧, full, with ⟦मधुना⟧, with the sweet, divine nectar, ⟦अक्षीयमाणा⟧, not wasting away, by ⟦स्वधया⟧, by food, ⟦मदन्ति⟧, gladden the people who resort to them; ⟦य⟧ ⟦उ⟧, who alone, the ⟦पृथिवीं⟧, the well-known earth, ⟦द्याम्⟧ ⟦उत⟧, and the mid-region, whose nature it is to shine, and ⟦विश्वा⟧ ⟦भुवनानि⟧, all the multitudes of beings, and the fourteen worlds. Or else: by the word pṛthivī are taken the seven worlds that lie below, Atala, Vitala and the rest; by the word dyu, the seven worlds beginning with Bhūr, which have the form of its subdivisions **NOTE**{the unit has ⟦तद्वान्तररूपाणि⟧, translated as tad-avāntara-rūpāṇi, "having the form of its subdivisions": is that the word meant?}. Thus the fourteen worlds; and all the worlds, that is, all the multitudes of beings too that are in them. ⟦त्रिधातु⟧: the aggregate of three elements is tridhātu. In such a way that it is marked by the triad of elements in the form of earth, water and fire, he ⟦दाधार⟧, held them. The lengthening of the reduplication is because the root belongs to the group beginning with tuj.

## SBh 1.154.4.2

The meaning is that he produced them. In the Āraṇyaka of the Chandogas, after the creation of the three elements has been stated by ⟦तत्तेजोऽसृजत तदन्नमसृजत ता आप ऐक्षन्त⟧, "That created fire; that created food; those waters considered", the creation that is the making threefold is established by ⟦हन्ताहमिमास्तिस्रो देवतास्तासां त्रिवृतं त्रिवृतमेकैकां करवाणि⟧ (⟦छा. उ. ६. ३. २-३.⟧), "Come, I, these three deities; let me make each one of them threefold, threefold", and what follows. Or else the construction is: he held ⟦त्रिधातु⟧, the three times, or the three guṇas.

## SBh 1.154.5.1

In the Ātithyā this Ṛk beginning ⟦तदस्य⟧ is the yājyā of the principal offering. It is laid down in the section ⟦अथातिथ्या⟧, "now the Ātithyā": ⟦इदं विष्णुर्वि चक्रमे तदस्य प्रियमभि पाथो अश्याम्⟧ (⟦आश्व. श्रौ. ४. ५⟧), which names the Ṛk beginning "idaṃ viṣṇur vi cakrame" and the Ṛk beginning "tad asya priyam abhi pātho aśyām".

## SBh 1.154.5.2

Of ⟦अस्य⟧, of this great Viṣṇu, the ⟦प्रियं⟧, the dear, ⟦तत्⟧, that which is well known as to be resorted to by all, ⟦पाथः⟧. This is a name of the mid-region, because Yāska has said ⟦पाथोऽन्तरिक्षं पथा व्याख्यातम्⟧ (⟦निरु. ६. ७⟧), "Pāthas is the mid-region; it is explained by the word for path". The meaning is: the imperishable world of Brahman. ⟦अश्याम्⟧, may I attain it. That same place is further specified: ⟦यत्र⟧, in which place, ⟦देवयवः⟧, those who desire for themselves the god, Viṣṇu, whose nature it is to shine, who wish to reach him by sacrifice, giving and the like, the ⟦नरः⟧, the men, ⟦मदन्ति⟧, experience satisfaction. The construction is: that may I attain. Once more that same place is further specified.

## SBh 1.154.5.3

Of ⟦उरुक्रमस्य⟧, of him who strides exceedingly over the whole world, as the self of each thing, and for that very reason **NOTE**{the unit has ⟦एब⟧; eva is translated: is it a typo?} of ⟦विष्णोः⟧, of the pervading Supreme Lord, in the ⟦परमे⟧, the excellent, unsurpassed, consisting of pure happiness, ⟦पदे⟧, place, there is an ⟦उत्सः⟧, a flowing forth, of ⟦मध्वः⟧, of the sweet. That may I attain. The meaning is: a place such that in it there is no fear of hunger, thirst, old age, death, return and the like, and enjoyments such as streams of nectar are obtained by the mere wish. He says that there is nothing greater than that: ⟦इत्था⟧, in this way, in the manner stated, ⟦स⟧ ⟦हि⟧ ⟦बन्धुः⟧, he indeed is as a kinsman to all doers of good, or a benefactor, because for those who have reached his place there is no return. His being a kinsman is from the passage of the Veda ⟦न च पुनरावर्तते⟧, "And he does not return again". The word ⟦हि⟧ serves to show that this is well known in all the Veda, the smṛtis, the Purāṇas and the rest.

## SBh 1.154.6.1

O wife and sacrificer, ⟦वां⟧, for your sake, ⟦ता⟧, those, well known as to be gone to, ⟦वास्तूनि⟧, places fit to dwell in happily, ⟦गमध्यै⟧, for the going of you two, ⟦उश्मसि⟧, we desire. The meaning is: for that we pray to Viṣṇu. "Those" was said; which are they? He says: ⟦यत्र⟧, in which dwelling-places, the ⟦गावः⟧, the rays, are ⟦भूरिशृङ्गाः⟧, possessed of extreme height, or to be resorted to by many; ⟦अयासः⟧, moving, goers, exceedingly extended. Or else: yāsaḥ are goers; they are not such. The meaning is that they are possessed of extreme light. ⟦अत्राह⟧, here indeed, in the world of heaven, which is the support of the dwelling-places **NOTE**{the unit has ⟦वास्त्वाधारभूतं⟧ beside the locative ⟦द्युलोके⟧; it is translated as the locative vāstvādhārabhūte agreeing with it: is that meant?}, of ⟦उरुगायस्य⟧, of him who is to be sung by many great-souled ones, who is worthy of praise, of ⟦वृष्णः⟧, of the showerer of desires, Viṣṇu, that, such as it is, well known everywhere in the Purāṇas and the rest as to be gone to, the highest, unsurpassed, ⟦पदं⟧, place, ⟦भूरि⟧, exceedingly abundant, ⟦अव⟧ ⟦भाति⟧, flashes forth by its own greatness.

## SBh 1.154.6.2

This mantra has been explained by Yāska, who explains that the word go denotes a ray: ⟦तानि वां वास्तूनि कामयामहे गमनाय यत्र गावो भूरिशृङ्गा बहुशृङ्गा भूरीति बहुनो नामधेयं प्रभवतीति सतः शृङ्गं श्रयतेर्वा शृणातेर्वा शम्नातेर्वा शरणायोद्गतमिति वा शिरसो निर्गतमिति वायासोऽयनाः । तत्र तदुरुगायस्य विष्णोर्महागतेः परमं पदं परार्ध्यस्थमवभाति भूरि । पादः पद्यतेः⟧ (⟦निरु. २. ७⟧), "We desire those dwelling-places for you two to go to, where the rays are bhūriśṛṅga, many-horned. Bhūri is a name for 'much', it being that which prevails. Śṛṅga is from the root śri, or from the root śṝ, or from the root śam; or because it has risen up for protection, or because it has come out of the head. Ayāsaḥ: goers. There that highest place of the urugāya, of Viṣṇu of great going, standing in the highest, shines down abundantly. Pāda is from the root pad".
