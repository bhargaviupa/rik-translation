## SBh 1.148.0.1

The hymn beginning ⟦मथीद्यत्⟧, of five Ṛks, is the eighth hymn; it is of Dīrghatamas, is in the triṣṭubh metre and is addressed to Agni. The Anukramaṇikā is ⟦मथीद्यत्⟧. Since it stands within the group of three hymns, as the third, its ritual use has been stated before.

## SBh 1.148.1.1

⟦होतारं⟧: the invoker of the gods. ⟦विश्वाप्सुम्⟧: apsu is a name of form; he is of manifold form, through the division into the terrestrial fire, the fire of lightning, the fire of the belly and so on, or through the division into the Āhavanīya **NOTE**{The Wikisource text has ⟦अहवनीयादिभेदेन⟧ with a short first vowel, taken as a typo for āhavanīyādibhedena; is that right?} and the others. Or else his having all forms comes from the diversity of his flames in the forms of Kālī, Karālī and the rest. ⟦विश्वदेव्यं⟧: good at the rites that are fit for all the gods. ⟦यदीं⟧, which Agni here, ⟦मातरिश्वा⟧, the wind, who breathes in the mid-region, the fashioner of rain and the like, ⟦विष्टः⟧, having entered within the pieces of wood, ⟦मथीत्⟧, churned; the meaning is that he made him grow. The root is ⟦मथे विलोडने⟧, math in the sense of stirring. The vṛddhi is prohibited by the rule ⟦हृयन्तक्षण⟧ and so on.

## SBh 1.148.1.2

He is further specified once more. ⟦यम्⟧, which Agni, the sacrificers formerly ⟦नि⟧ ⟦दधुः⟧, hold, ⟦वपुषे⟧, for the accomplishment of the body of the sacrifice, ⟦मनुष्यासु⟧, among those who do their work after having thought, ⟦विक्षु⟧, among the people who are the priests. The word manuṣya is explained in many ways in the Nirukta: ⟦मनुष्याः कस्मान्मत्वा कर्माणि सीव्यन्ति मनस्यमानेन सृष्टा मनोरपस्यं मनुषो वा⟧, "Why are they called manuṣyāḥ? Having thought, they sew their works; they were created by one who was thinking; or they are the offspring of Manu, or of Manuṣ" (⟦निरु. ३. ७⟧). **NOTE**{In this quotation the Wikisource text has ⟦मनोरपस्यं⟧, taken as a typo for manor apatyaṃ, "the offspring of Manu", the word Sāyaṇa uses in the next sentence; is that right?} Or else: formerly the gods placed him, in the form of the fire of the belly, among the people, the living beings, who are the offspring of Manu, for their own form, that is, for the manifesting of the proper form of sacrifice and the like, or for the sustaining of the body.

## SBh 1.148.1.3

An illustration of this. ⟦स्वर्णं⟧: that which goes well, or that which impels well; like the sun, which is ⟦चित्रं⟧, worthy of reverence, ⟦विभावं⟧, possessed of manifold light: as they hold it for the bringing about of light and the like, so. The word svar **NOTE**{The Wikisource text has svaśabdaṃ, taken as svaḥśabdaṃ, "the word svar", which the quotation explains; is that right?} Yāska has explained thus: ⟦स्वरादित्यो भवति सु अरणः सु ईरणः स्वृतो रसान्त्स्वृतो भासं ज्योतिषां स्वृतो भासेति वा⟧, "svar is the sun: it goes well; it impels well; it has gone well to the juices; it has gone well to the light of the luminaries; or it is well reached by light" (⟦निरु. २. १४⟧). Him whom, being such, they set down, him he churned: thus the construction is with the earlier part.

## SBh 1.148.2.1

⟦मन्म⟧: a hymn of praise, which is to be thought upon, or the oblation and the like; ⟦ददानमित्⟧: me, who am indeed making it for Agni, the deceivers, the enemies, do not ⟦ददभन्त⟧: they are not able to deceive, to injure.

## SBh 1.148.2.2

Of the root dambh, by the rule ⟦बहुलं छन्दसि⟧, "variously in the Veda", there is ślu of the conjugational sign. By an exchange there is the substitute ant.

## SBh 1.148.2.3

What is my distinction? It is told. Of ⟦तस्य⟧, of such a giver, ⟦मम⟧, of me, the ⟦वरूथं⟧, what is worthy to be chosen, the praise

## SBh 1.148.2.4

and the like, this ⟦अग्निः⟧, Agni, ⟦चाकन्⟧, desires exceedingly. The root kan has the sense of desiring. From it, ending in the elision of yaṅ, **NOTE**{The Wikisource text has ⟦यङ्गलुगन्तात्⟧, plainly a typo for yaṅlugantāt, "ending in the elision of yaṅ"; is that right?} in laṅ, there is the Vedic absence of the augment aṭ. Or it is Vedic simply because of the word "variously"; the lengthening of the reduplication is because the root belongs to the group beginning with tuj. Since this is so, therefore they do not harm: that is the meaning. Not Agni alone desires, but all the gods desire: this he says. Of me, ⟦अस्य⟧, this sacrificer, ⟦भरमाणस्य⟧, who am making, ⟦उपस्तुतिं⟧, ⟦कारोः⟧, the praiser, ⟦विश्वानि⟧, all the things, which consist of the giving of the oblation and the like, all the gods ⟦जुषन्त⟧. Therefore they do not harm: that is the meaning.

## SBh 1.148.3.1

⟦यज्ञियासः⟧, those fit for the sacrifice, the sacrificers or the priests, ⟦यं⟧, which Agni, produced by the churning, ⟦नु⟧, quickly, ⟦जगृभ्रे⟧, take, ⟦नित्ये⟧ ⟦चित्⟧ ⟦सदने⟧, in the permanent place alone, the place for setting down the fire, which is the Gārhapatya.

## SBh 1.148.3.2

Of the root grah, "to take", in liṭ, there is re by the rule ⟦इरयो रे⟧, "re in place of ire". There is bh by the rule ⟦हृग्रहोर्भः⟧, "bh of hṛ and grah".

## SBh 1.148.3.3

From the sūtra ⟦नित्यं गतश्रियः⟧ ⟦ध्रियते नित्यं गार्हपत्यम्⟧, "for one who has attained fortune it is kept permanently; the Gārhapatya permanently", the Gārhapatya is permanent. Because it rests on that, the seat too is called permanent. Having taken him, ⟦प्रशस्तिभिः⟧, with praises, with lauds, they ⟦दधिरे⟧, hold him, for the sake of the Āhavanīya. And having held him, ⟦इष्टौ⟧, the sacrifice, which is the means of seeking, being the occasion, ⟦गृभयन्तः⟧, the priests, ⟦प्र⟧ ⟦सू⟧ ⟦नयन्त⟧, carry him forward well. An illustration of this. ⟦ररहाणाः⟧: going with speed.

## SBh 1.148.3.4

The root is ⟦रहि गतौ⟧, rah in the sense of going. There is kānac in place of the Vedic liṭ. By the maxim ⟦अनित्यमागमशासनम्⟧, "the teaching of an augment is not invariable", num is absent. ⟦रथ्यः⟧: possessed of chariots, yoked to them. By the rule ⟦छन्दसीवनिपौ⟧, "in the Veda, ī and vanip",

## SBh 1.148.3.5

there is, after the word ratha, the vowel ī in the sense of the possessive suffix. ⟦अश्वासो⟧ ⟦न⟧: like horses. As they, bearing him, lead the owner of the chariot to the desired place, so.

## SBh 1.148.4.1

Here he is praised in the form of the forest-fire. ⟦दस्मः⟧, the destroyer, this Agni, ⟦नि⟧ ⟦रिणाति⟧, injures utterly, ⟦पुरूणि⟧, the many trees and the like. The root is ⟦री गतिरेषणयोः⟧, rī in the senses of going and injuring. The vowel is short because the root belongs to the group beginning with pū.

## SBh 1.148.4.2

By what means? That is told: ⟦जम्भैः⟧, with his flames, which stand in the place of teeth. ⟦आत्⟧, after the burning, ⟦वने⟧, in that which consists of a mass of trees and the like, ⟦विभावा⟧, with manifold

## SBh 1.148.4.3

light endowed, he ⟦रोचते⟧, becomes blazing. ⟦आत्⟧, after that, ⟦वातः⟧, the wind, who is the friend of Agni, in conformity with ⟦अस्य⟧ ⟦शोचिः⟧, his flame, ⟦अनु⟧ ⟦द्यून्⟧ (the word anu is in the distributive sense), every day, ⟦वाति⟧, blows favourably. He goes taking the flames with him. An illustration of this: ⟦शर्यां⟧ ⟦न⟧, like an arrow, ⟦असनां⟧, going, from ⟦अस्तुः⟧, the thrower. What is called śara

## SBh 1.148.4.4

is the head of an arrow, made of iron, a means of injury; śaryā is that which abounds in it. Or else what is called śara is a particular kind of wood, a subordinate species of bamboo; like an arrow that is a modification of that. ⟦शर्या इषवः शरमय्यः⟧, "śaryāḥ are arrows, made of śara" (⟦निरु. ५ .४⟧): so says Yāska. Or else śara itself is śaryā. Or śara is injury; that which does it is śaryā, an arrow. As the wind, or the impetus produced by the one who impels it,

## SBh 1.148.4.5

drives it on favourably, so.

## SBh 1.148.5.1

⟦यम्⟧, which Agni, ⟦सन्तं⟧, existing, ⟦गर्भे⟧, in the middle of the fire-stick, which protects like a womb, ⟦रिपवः⟧, men, ⟦न⟧ ⟦रेषयन्ति⟧, do not cause pain. Likewise ⟦रिषण्यवः⟧, the other injurers: the word is laid down ready-made before kyac by the rule ⟦दुरस्युर्द्रविणस्युर्वृषण्यति रिषण्यति⟧ (⟦पा सू ७ ४. ३६⟧); by the rule ⟦क्याच्छन्दसि⟧, "after kya in the Veda", there is the suffix u.

## SBh 1.148.5.2

They do not injure him. Of what kind are they? ⟦रेषणाः⟧: those whose nature is injuring. Or else: the modes of injury that are practised by them do not injure him. Moreover those who are ⟦अन्धाः⟧, devoid of the power of knowledge, unknowing, with regard to his ⟦अभिख्या⟧, his renown on all sides, his greatness, and who for that very reason are ⟦अपश्याः⟧, non-seers; or else those who, though knowing, do not meditate on him, the meaning being those who do not worship him: both of these too, being of such a kind, ⟦न⟧ ⟦दभन्⟧, do not harm, do not injure.

## SBh 1.148.5.3

The purport is: because he is of service even through worldly benefit. If it is asked "then who know his true form?", it is told. ⟦नित्यासः⟧: the constant ones, those of unswerving devotion, or those devoted to the obligatory rites, the Agnihotra and the rest. By the favour of Agni they themselves too will become eternal: with reference to this future eternity they are figuratively called eternal. Such sacrificers, being ⟦प्रेतारः⟧, those who satisfy him alone with sacrifice and the like, ⟦अरक्षन्⟧, protect, ⟦ईम्⟧, him; the meaning is that they serve him in the form of sacrifice and the like.
