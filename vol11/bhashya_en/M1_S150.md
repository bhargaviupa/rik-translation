## Ṛk 1

**NOTE**{the supplied text has no introduction to this sūkta; the reference text has one} O Agni, to you (⟦त्वा⟧, that is, ⟦त्वां⟧) I speak (⟦वोचे⟧) much (⟦पुरु⟧, that is, ⟦बहु⟧). Or else the connection is "having given much". The meaning is: I utter wishes such as "Give a son, give wealth". Is it idly? No, he says: since I am one who has given (⟦दाश्वान्⟧), that is, I have given the desired oblation, therefore I speak. It is not to be thought, "How should anything be given to one who speaks in the same way as others do?" For I, O Agni, am yours alone (⟦तव⟧ ⟦स्वित्⟧), and, facing you (⟦आ⟧), I am one who goes to you (⟦अरिः⟧); I am a servant by bringing the oblation and the rest. There is an illustration of this. As in the house (⟦शरणे⟧) of a great (⟦महस्य⟧) chastiser, as it were (⟦तोदस्येव⟧), that is, of a master who disciplines, in his house a slave born in the house, or the like, lives bound on every side (⟦आ⟧), so do I too. Since this is so, therefore I speak of the much that I desire; and do you give all of it: this is the meaning. On this there is the Nirukta: ⟦दाश्वांस्त्वामेवाभिह्वयाम्यरिरमित्र ऋच्चतेः ईश्वरोऽप्यरिरेतस्मादेव । यदन्यदेवत्या अग्नावाहुतयो हूयंत इत्येतद्दृष्टैवमवक्ष्यत् तोदस्येव शरण आ⟧ ⟦महस्य तुदस्येव शरणेऽधि महतः⟧ (⟦नि. ५-७⟧), "Having given much, I call upon you alone. Ari is an enemy; the word is from ṛcchati. A lord too is ari, from this same. Having seen that the oblations belonging to other deities are offered in Agni, he would have spoken thus. 'As in the house of a great goader': as in the house of a great goader".

## Ṛk 2

In the previous mantra it was said, "I make known the much that I myself desire". Here, however, he prays that no gift should be given to those who are unlike himself, who are without giving and the like. O Agni, I speak to you in a special way. Because a preverb is heard, a suitable verb is to be supplied. Or else, because it stands near, ⟦वोचे⟧ is carried over. I speak the opposite of what I made known **NOTE**{the supplied ⟦यद्वजिज्ञपन्⟧ is translated as "what I made known" (first person), which the context requires; the reference text has yat vyajijñapan, a third-person plural form: which is meant?} for our own sake. The meaning is: I say that nothing should be given to one whose nature is about to be described. He states their nature. ⟦अनिनस्य⟧ means of one who has no master, of one who makes you not his master; and likewise ⟦धनिनः⟧, of one who possesses wealth in full. Since a gift to one who is already wealthy is purposeless, it is forbidden. Or else the meaning is: of one who possesses wealth that is of no use for sacrifice and the like. Moreover ⟦प्रहोषे⟧, to offer in an eminent way, ⟦अररुषः⟧, of one who does not give, in the form of the sacrificial fee. Or else the connection is "of one who has no master, to offer": the meaning is, of one who is not able to offer in an eminent way. Here by the word ina the ability that resides in a master is indicated. The word ⟦चित्⟧ has the sense of adding. Moreover ⟦कदा चन प्रजिगतः⟧, of one who does not ever praise the gods in an eminent way. Here ⟦चन⟧ is a combination of two particles: of these the word ca is used in adding, the word na in negation. Moreover ⟦अदेवयोः⟧, of one who does not desire the gods for himself. The meaning is: I say specially that nothing should be given to these. By describing the nature of those who are blamed, since he himself is not such, it comes to be said that the words "I speak much" are indeed proper. Or else ⟦चन⟧ is a synonym of the word cit. Even of one who is corrupt in the manner stated but who at some time praises, that is, who praises you at any time whatever, I speak specially: I say specially that to such a one something should be given. What need is there to say that it should be given for our sake? This is the purport.

## Ṛk 3

O Agni, ⟦विप्र⟧, wise one, the mortal who worships you, that (⟦सः⟧) mortal (⟦मर्त्यः⟧), the sacrificer, becomes in heaven (⟦दिवि⟧), in the world of heaven, ⟦चंद्रः⟧, one who gladdens all, like the moon. Or else he becomes the moon itself. The Chandogas hand down that sacrificers attain to being the moon: ⟦पितृलोकादाकाशमाकाशाच्चंद्रमसमेष सोमो राजा⟧ (⟦छां. उ. ५-१०-४⟧), "From the world of the fathers to space, from space to the moon; this is King Soma"; and ⟦सोमलोके विभूतिमनुभूयेति⟧, "having enjoyed lordly power in the world of Soma" **NOTE**{the reference text gives a reference for this second quotation (Praśna Upaniṣad 5.4); the supplied text gives none}. So too in the Muṇḍaka the attaining of the world of heaven is handed down: ⟦तमाहुतयः सुवर्चसः सूर्यस्य रश्मिभिर्यजमानं वहंति तं नयंत्येताः सूर्यस्य⟧ ⟦रश्मयो यत्र देवानां पतिरेकोऽधिवास⟧, "Saying 'Come, come', the radiant oblations carry that sacrificer by the rays of the sun; these rays of the sun lead him to where the one lord of the gods dwells", and ⟦एष वः पुण्यः सुकृतो ब्रह्मलोक⟧, "This is your holy, well-made world of Brahman" (⟦मुं. उ- १-२-५,६⟧). The same man is described further. ⟦महः⟧, even than the great, he is ⟦व्राधन्तमः⟧, the most grown; the meaning is that he is the best even of the other gods. Therefore, O Agni, may we be yours (⟦ते⟧, that is, ⟦तव⟧), ⟦प्रप्रेत्⟧, in an eminent way indeed, ⟦वनुषः⟧, sharers. Or else: having pleased you, ⟦प्रप्रेत् स्याम⟧, may we be eminent indeed.
