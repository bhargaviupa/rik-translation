## Introduction

The sūkta ⟦युवं वस्त्राणि⟧ is the twelfth, of seven Ṛks; it is of Dīrghatamas. By the general rule for what is not specified, it is in the triṣṭubh metre. It belongs to Mitra and Varuṇa, because earlier ⟦मैत्रावरुणं⟧, "for it belongs to Mitra and Varuṇa", was said. The ritual use of the sūkta is to be known from its indications. In the animal sacrifice for Mitra and Varuṇa the first Ṛk is the yājyā, the offering verse, of the omentum. This is laid down in the section ⟦प्रदानानामिति⟧: ⟦युवं वस्त्राणिपीवसा वसाथे प्र बाहवा सिसृतं जीवसे नः⟧ (⟦आ. ३-८⟧). The same Ṛk is employed at the samāvartana, at the putting on of new garments. And this is laid down: ⟦युवं वस्त्राणि पीवसा⟧ ⟦वसाथे इत्यहते वाससी आच्छाद्य⟧ (⟦आ. गृ. ३-८-९⟧), "having put on two unwashed garments with the verse so beginning".

## Ṛk 1

O Mitra and Varuṇa, ⟦पीवसा⟧, you two fat ones, ⟦युवं⟧, you two. Or else ⟦पीवसा⟧ means thick, unrent. ⟦वस्त्राणि⟧, garments fit for covering, ⟦वसाथे⟧, you put on. Or else: with ⟦पीवसा⟧, with gross, abundant splendour, you hold garments; the meaning is that you bear unbroken splendours which stand in the place of garments. Moreover the ⟦सर्गाः⟧, the creations, of you two are ⟦अच्छिद्रा⟧, unbroken, and ⟦मंतवो ह⟧, worthy of being thought upon. You two, who are such, ⟦विश्वा⟧, all, ⟦अनृतानि⟧, untruths, unpleasant things, sins, ⟦अव⟧ with the verb that follows, destroy. In ⟦विश्व⟧ here there is the keeping of the original form, and the shortening, as is said in ⟦संहितायामृत्यक इति⟧, that is, in the Saṃhitā, by the rule ṛty akaḥ. With ⟦ऋतेन⟧, with the fruit, or with the sacrifice which is the means to it, ⟦सचेथे⟧, you are joined; the meaning is that you join it to us.

## Ṛk 2

⟦एषां⟧: among these two. The plural is for the sake of honour; or else the plural has regard to their followers. Among these two ⟦त्वः⟧, with the particle after it, one alone, ⟦एतत्⟧, this action about to be stated, in the form of an ability, ⟦वि चिकेतत्⟧, perceives in a special way; the meaning is that he carries it out. He is described: that one is ⟦सत्यः⟧, not to be obstructed, or one who is among the good; ⟦मंत्रः⟧, thoughtful; ⟦कविशस्तः⟧, to be praised by the kavis, the wise; ⟦ऋघावान्⟧, possessing the power to harm. It has been said that by this the possibility of the ability about to be stated has been established. Expecting the question "What is that?", he says ⟦त्रिरश्रिं⟧. A weapon that has three edges is ⟦त्रिरश्रि⟧; him who has that: the suffix of possession is dropped. This is an indication: the meaning is, him who is furnished with all weapons. Or else it means one who has this name. ⟦चतुरश्रि⟧: he who has even more weapons than that. Not the ability of weapons only: but ⟦उग्रः⟧, himself of uplifted strength, he ⟦हंति⟧, he slays the enemy of exceeding strength. Moreover, by the ability of only one of you two, ⟦देवनिदो ह⟧, the revilers of the gods, for their part, ⟦प्रथमाः⟧, although they are able, ⟦अजूर्यन्⟧, become decayed of themselves. From the root jṝ, with śyan, the u comes by the rule ⟦बहुलं छंदसी⟧. Even one of you two is of great strength; what need is there to speak of the ability of the two united: this is the purport. Or else: ⟦एषां⟧, of you two, ⟦एतत्⟧, this ability to be stated further on, one of you, marked as true and so on, ⟦वि चिकेतत्⟧, knows in a special way. The rest is as before.

## Ṛk 3

O ⟦मित्रावरुणा⟧, Mitra and Varuṇa, of ⟦पद्वतीनां⟧ **NOTE**{the reference text here has the gloss pādavatīnām ("that have feet") after this word, and further on the word vāṃ of the Ṛk before "of you two"; the supplied text has neither}, of creatures such as men, ⟦प्रथमा⟧, she who comes to be first, is ⟦अपात्⟧, footless: Uṣas. Because she does not, like the sun, the moon and the rest, go her round by herself every day, and because her own going is by the going of the sun alone, she is spoken of figuratively both as "footless" and as "she goes". ⟦तत्⟧, such a well-known action of you two, ⟦क आ चिकेत⟧, who knows it face to face? The root is kit, "in knowing". The meaning is that it is worthy of praise. Because Mitra and Varuṇa are the deities of day and night, and because the interval between day and night is itself Uṣas, this is their action. Moreover ⟦भारं⟧, the sustaining, of this world, ⟦गर्भो⟧, the infant Sun, who stands as the embryo of you two — because he arises at the time between day and night, and those two are themselves Mitra and Varuṇa, by the passage of the Veda ⟦अहोरात्रे वै मित्रावरुणावि⟧, "Day and night indeed are Mitra and Varuṇa" **NOTE**{the reference text adds a reference for this quotation (Taittirīya-saṃhitā 2.4.10.1); the supplied text gives none} — he ⟦आहरति चित्⟧, he does indeed carry on every side. Or else he takes away its ⟦भारं⟧, its torpor. ⟦चिदि⟧ is used in restriction. How is that? That is said: ⟦ऋतं⟧, the truth, in the form of the light, the movement and the like of the world, he ⟦पिपर्ति⟧, he fills. ⟦अनृतं⟧, what is unlike the aforesaid, the darkness and the stopping of movement and the like, ⟦नि तारीत्⟧, he sinks down, he makes to sink; the meaning is that he destroys it. The infant's action, in the form of bearing the burden of the world, is from your ability: so it is.

## Ṛk 4

⟦कनीनां⟧: of the lovely, shining Dawns, who stand in the place of maidens. From the root kan, which has the sense of shining, there is the suffix iñ; the feminine ṅīṣ comes by the rule ⟦कृदिकारादक्तिन⟧; or else, before the ending ām, there is the Vedic samprasāraṇa of the word kanyā. Their ⟦जारं⟧, the Sun, ⟦परि पश्यामसि⟧, we see all round, ⟦प्रयंतमित्⟧, only going forward. ⟦उपनिपद्यमानं⟧, sitting down even for a single moment, we do not see him. This too is the ability of those two. The same one is described further. ⟦अनवपृग्ज्ञानवपृग्ज्ञानि⟧, the word of the Ṛk and its plural form, and for that very reason spread out: such splendours ⟦वसानं⟧, wearing; ⟦प्रियं⟧, dear to all; of ⟦मित्रस्य⟧ and of ⟦वरुणस्य⟧ the ⟦धाम⟧, the place of splendour. Or else ⟦प्रियं धामे⟧ are connected: the place of the affection of those two. Such a Sun, moving about by your leave, we see.

## Ṛk 5

That same Sun, ⟦अनश्वः⟧, although without a swift-going horse, and likewise ⟦अनभीशुः⟧, without the reins which stand as the bridle that is the support to hold by, has nevertheless ⟦अर्वा⟧ ⟦जातः⟧, become possessed of swift going. This Sun, being of such a kind, ⟦कनिक्रदत्⟧, making a sound exceedingly — from the root krand, with the intensive suffix yaṅ dropped, in śatṛ, the present participle, the form is given ready-made in the group beginning with dādharti — being ⟦ऊर्ध्वसानु⟧, rising higher and higher, ⟦पतयत्⟧, goes. ⟦अचित्तं⟧, not within the reach of thought, ⟦ब्रह्म⟧, the great action of the form described: ⟦मित्रे⟧ and ⟦वरुणे⟧, in Mitra and in Varuṇa, with the ⟦धाम⟧, the place of splendour, of both, ⟦युवानो⟧, mingling it, laying it upon the place of those two; and likewise ⟦प्र गृणंतो⟧, praising eminently, saying "It has its source in the splendour of you two", they ⟦जुजुषुः⟧, they serve. The meaning is that men praise the action of the Sun, which consists in moving through the mid-region without support, as dependent on Varuṇa and Mitra.

## Ṛk 6

⟦मामतेयं⟧, the son of Mamatā, me, Dīrghatamas, ⟦ब्रह्मप्रियं⟧, the best lover of the great rite, or the gladdener of the great Agni, ⟦अवंतीः⟧, gladdening, may the ⟦धेनवः⟧, the cows, ⟦सस्मिन्⟧, in their own, ⟦ऊधन्⟧, udder, ⟦पीपयन्⟧ with ⟦आ⟧, make to swell on every side. From the root pyāy, in the causative, the substitution of pī is Vedic. The meaning is: by the milk that is in the udder, which is to be offered to you two, they protect us; by the passage of the Veda ⟦मित्रावरुणाभ्यां पयस्य⟧, "for Mitra and Varuṇa the payasyā" **NOTE**{the reference text adds a reference for this quotation (Taittirīya-saṃhitā 7.5.22.1); the supplied text gives none}. Having thus given the oblation, ⟦वयुनानि⟧, the knowledge that concerns performance, ⟦विद्वान्⟧, knowing, that is, understanding that the remainder of what is offered is to be eaten, Dīrghatamas, of ⟦पित्वः⟧, of the food left over from the offering, such food, ⟦आसा⟧, with the mouth, to eat it, ⟦भिक्षेत⟧, may he beg of you. Moreover ⟦विवासन्⟧, attending on you two, ⟦अदितिं⟧, unbroken, not deficient, this rite ⟦उरुष्येत्⟧. The meaning is: they have made it swell in such a way that he may beg for what is left and may protect the rite by completing it.

## Ṛk 7

In the animal sacrifice for Mitra and Varuṇa this is the anuvākyā, the invitatory verse, of the omentum. And this is laid down: ⟦आ नां मित्रावरुणा⟧ ⟦हव्यजुष्टिमा यातं मित्रावरुणा⟧ (⟦आ. २ ८⟧) **NOTE**{the reference text does not have this opening passage on the ritual use; the reference stands as ⟦आ. २ ८⟧, while the introduction places the offering verse of the same rite at 3-8 of the same work: is 3-8 meant here too?}. O ⟦देवौ⟧, you whose nature it is to shine, ⟦मित्रावरुणा⟧, Mitra and Varuṇa, ⟦वां⟧, you two, to the ⟦हव्यजुष्टिं⟧, the enjoying of the oblation, by ⟦नमसा⟧, by praise marked by obeisance, by ⟦अवसा⟧, by protection as the motive, ⟦ववृत्यां⟧ with ⟦आ⟧, may I turn hither. Or else: by obeisance and by ⟦अवसा⟧, by food, may I turn you hither. Moreover, O Mitra and Varuṇa, the ⟦ब्रह्म⟧ that belongs to us (⟦अस्माकं⟧), the great rite now being performed, in ⟦पृतनासु⟧, in battles, ⟦सह्याः⟧, may it overcome the enemies. The meaning is: by this rite may I overcome the enemies. Moreover our ⟦दिव्या⟧, arising in heaven, ⟦वृष्टिः⟧, rain, ⟦सुपारा⟧, carrying across well: "may it be" is to be supplied. For when there is rain the rite is completed by means of rice and the like: this is the purport.
