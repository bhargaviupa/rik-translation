## Introduction

In the twenty-second anuvāka there are eight sūktas. Of these the first sūkta is ⟦अबोध्यग्निर्ज्म⟧; it has six Ṛks, its seer is Dīrghatamas, and it is addressed to the Aśvins. Because of the word tu in "jāgataṃ tu" stated before, this sūkta also is in the jagatī metre. The last two Ṛks are triṣṭubh. The Anukramaṇikā says: ⟦अबोधि षळाश्विनं त्वंत्ये⟧, "abodhi: six, addressed to the Aśvins; but the last two are triṣṭubh". Its ritual use is in the jagatī metre in the prātaranuvāka and in the Āśvina-śastra. It is laid down in the sūtra, in the section beginning "athāśvinaḥ": ⟦एष स्य भानुः⟧, "abodhy agnir jmaḥ; eṣa sya bhānuḥ" (⟦आ. ४-१५⟧).

## Ṛk 1

This ⟦अग्निः⟧, having been set in place, ⟦ज्मः⟧, of jmā, of the earth characterised as the altar, being connected with it, ⟦अबोधि⟧: has been awakened. Jmā is a name of the earth, because it is stated among its names in "gmā, jmā". **NOTE**{the reference text adds a Nighaṇṭu reference (1.1.3) after this quotation; the supplied text has none} Moreover the sun rises. ⟦मही⟧, the great, dawn, ⟦अर्चिषा⟧, by her excellent lustre, being ⟦चंद्रा⟧, gladdening to living beings, ⟦व्यावः⟧: she drives the darknesses away. From the root vas in luṅ there is the Vedic elision of cli; the augment āṭ is by the rule "it is seen in the Veda also". Since this dawn rises, and since the sun rises, and since this fire is awakened, for this reason, O Aśvins, the ⟦रथं⟧ that belongs to you, ⟦यातवे⟧, for going to the place of the sacrifice to the gods, with two asses, āyukṣātām: yoke it, facing this way. Likewise ⟦सविता⟧, the approver of every act, ⟦देवः⟧, the god, ⟦जगत्⟧, that which moves, the multitude of living beings, ⟦पृथक्⟧, in accordance with the act of each one, ⟦प्रासावीत्⟧: let him impel, let him give his approval.

## Ṛk 2

O Aśvins, ⟦यत्⟧, when, ⟦वृषणं⟧, that which rains down rain and the like, the ⟦रथं⟧ ⟦युंजाथे⟧, you yoke, then ⟦नः⟧ ⟦क्षत्रं⟧, our strength, or the Kṣatriya class, ⟦घृतेन⟧, with water, ⟦मधुना⟧, with what is sweet, besprinkle; the meaning is, make it grow. Or else: ⟦घृतेन⟧, with that which has the form of flowing, ⟦मधुना⟧, with the sweet nectar, besprinkle it; the meaning is, with the madhu that is in your chariot make our strength grow. That the chariot of the Aśvins is full of madhu is well known in such passages as ⟦मधुवाहनो रथ⟧, "the chariot that carries madhu". Moreover, ⟦अस्माकं⟧ ⟦पृतनासु⟧, among our own people in the form of men, sons, servants and the like, ⟦ब्रह्म⟧, the lustre of the brahman, ⟦जिन्वतं⟧. Pṛtanā is a name of men, because it is stated among their names in ⟦विवस्वंतः पृतना⟧. **NOTE**{the reference text adds Nighaṇṭu references (2.3.25 and 2.7.25) after this and the next name-list quotation of this Ṛk; the supplied text has none} Or else: ⟦पृतनासु⟧, among the peoples of others, ⟦ब्रह्म⟧, their abundant food, as ours, ⟦जिन्वतं⟧: gladden. Brahma is a name of food, because it is stated among its names in ⟦ब्रह्म वर्च⟧. And we, ⟦शूरसाता⟧, that is, in śūrasāti, in the battle that is attended by the striking and the like of heroes, ⟦धना⟧, their riches of many kinds, ⟦भजेमहि⟧: may we obtain.

## Ṛk 3

Let the ⟦रथः⟧ of the Aśvins, ⟦अर्वाज्⟧, facing towards us, ⟦यातु⟧: go. Of what kind? ⟦त्रिचक्रः⟧, furnished with three wheels, ⟦मधुवाहनो⟧, the carrier of madhu, ⟦जीराश्वः⟧, having swift-going horses, ⟦सुस्तुतः⟧, being for that very reason praised by us, ⟦त्रिवंधुरो⟧, having three pieces of timber, low and raised. The vandhura is the place where the charioteer rests. ⟦मघवा⟧, wealthy, and for that very reason ⟦विश्वसौभगः⟧, endowed with all good fortune. Let such a chariot of the Aśvins, ⟦नः⟧, our, ⟦द्विपदे⟧, for the offspring, sons and the like, and ⟦चतुष्पदे⟧, for the cattle, ⟦शं⟧, happiness, ⟦वक्षत्⟧: let it bring it here. The augment aṭ is added to the root vah in leṭ; and there is sip.

## Ṛk 4

O Aśvins, ⟦युवं⟧, you two, ⟦नः⟧, our, ⟦ऊर्जं⟧, strength, or the food that is able to give life, ⟦वहतं⟧: make it reach us. Moreover, ⟦नः⟧, us, ⟦मधुमत्या⟧, endowed with sweetness, ⟦कशया⟧, with speech in the form of the granting of what is desired, ⟦मिमिक्षतं⟧: besprinkle, gladden. Kaśā is a name of speech, because it is stated among its names in "kaśā, dhiṣaṇā". **NOTE**{the reference text adds Nighaṇṭu references (1.11.43 and 1.12.11) after this and the next name-list quotation of this Ṛk; the supplied text has none} Or else: ⟦मधुमत्या⟧, having sweet rain, endowed with sweetness, ⟦कशया⟧, with the whip that strikes the horses, besprinkle us. Madhu is a name of water, because it is stated among its names in ⟦मधु पुरीषमिति⟧. By the whip, by the striking of the horses, they will come quickly and give the fruit in the form of rain: hence the whip is said to possess madhu. Moreover, ⟦आयुः⟧, the length of life, ⟦तारिष्टं⟧ with ⟦प्र⟧: make it grow. The root tṝ preceded by pra has the sense of making grow. Likewise ⟦रपांसि मृक्षतं⟧: cleanse the sins without remainder. ⟦रपो रिप्रमिति⟧, "rapas and ripra are two names of sin" (⟦नि. ४-२१⟧): so Yāska. Moreover, ⟦द्वेषो⟧, what is hateful, the enemy and the like, ⟦सेधतं⟧: destroy. And this is to come about: ⟦सचाभुवा⟧, being together, possessing a being-together in our affairs, ⟦भवतं⟧; the meaning is, may you two come to be of one mind. The verb is not unaccented because the sentence is a different one.

## Ṛk 5

O Aśvins, ⟦युवं ह⟧, you two indeed, ⟦जगतीषु⟧, in those whose nature is to move, the cows, ⟦अंतरिति⟧, within, ⟦गर्भं धत्थः⟧: you hold the embryo. Not in the cows only, but ⟦युवं⟧, you two, ⟦विश्वेषु⟧, in all ⟦भुवनेषु⟧, the multitudes of beings, within, hold the embryo; because by way of the rain, as being of the form of food, you nourish the seed that is in the womb. Moreover, O ⟦वृषणौ⟧, you who rain down the things desired, ⟦युवं⟧, you two, ⟦अग्निं⟧ and the waters and the trees ⟦ऐरयेथां⟧: have set in motion. Having taken to yourselves Agni for the enjoyment of the oblations, and the rain-waters, and by the waters the herbs, trees and the rest: these you have sent forth for our sake.

## Ṛk 6

O Aśvins, ⟦युवं⟧, you two, ⟦ह⟧, indeed, ⟦भेषजेभिः⟧, with remedies, with medicines; for the lexicon says ⟦भेषजौषधभैषज्यान्यगदो जायुरगदं तत्रौषधमिति⟧, "bheṣaja, auṣadha, bhaiṣajya, agada, jāyu" and "agada: auṣadha is used in that sense". By means of them you are ⟦भिषजा⟧, physicians. The word ha is meant to indicate what is well known from such passages as "the Aśvins indeed are the physicians of the gods". ⟦अथो⟧, and also, ⟦रथ्येभिः⟧, with horses that draw the chariot, you are ⟦रथ्या⟧, possessors of a chariot. The word ha is meant to indicate what is well known from such passages as ⟦आ वां रथो अश्विना⟧, "hither your chariot, O Aśvins" (⟦ऋ. १-११८-१⟧). The particular connection of these two with the chariot is well known in other mantras such as ⟦सवितोषसा रथमृताय चित्रं धृतवंतमिष्यति⟧, "for before the dawn Savitṛ sends forth your bright chariot, rich in ghee, for the rite" (⟦ऋ. १-३४-१०⟧). ⟦अथो⟧, and also, your ⟦क्षत्रं⟧, strength, is ⟦अधि⟧ ⟦ह⟧: exceeding, indeed. The word ha is used of what is well known. Since this is so, therefore, O ⟦उग्रा⟧, Aśvins of uplifted power, ⟦वां⟧, to you two, ⟦यः⟧, the sacrificer who, being haviṣmān, furnished with oblations such as clarified butter and the sacrificial cake, ⟦मनसा⟧, with a mind that depends on you, ⟦ददाश⟧, gives the oblation and the like, him ⟦धत्थः⟧: you uphold; the meaning is, you favour him exceedingly. The purport is: you two, of abundant strength, having mounted the chariot and come to our place of sacrifice to the gods, upholding me, the giver of the oblation, heal us at our sacrifice.
