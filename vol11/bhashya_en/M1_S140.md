## Introduction

In the twenty-first anuvāka there are seventeen sūktas. Among them the one beginning ⟦वेदिषद⟧ is the first sūkta; it has thirteen Ṛks. Its seer is Dīrghatamas, the son of Ucathya. It belongs to Agni. The last two Ṛks are triṣṭubhs. The rest are jagatīs, by the convention of "ending in triṣṭubh". The tenth is a triṣṭubh or a jagatī. The convention of the atyaṣṭi metre has ceased. On this the Anukramaṇikā says: ⟦वेदिषदे सप्तोना दीर्घतमा औचथ्य आग्नेयं तु द्वित्रिष्टुबंतं⟧ ⟦तु त्रिष्टुब्बशमी वेति⟧, "The sūkta beginning vediṣade has seven fewer; Dīrghatamas the son of Ucathya; but it belongs to Agni; but it ends in two triṣṭubhs; the tenth is optionally a triṣṭubh." In the Prātaranuvāka, in the section belonging to Agni, in the jagatī metre, the six sūktas beginning with this one, leaving out the third, are employed. Likewise in the Āśvina-śastra there is the employment of the six sūktas, leaving out the third. And this is laid down in the sūtra: ⟦वेदिषद इति षण्णां तृतीयमुद्धरेत्⟧, "Of the six beginning with vediṣade he should take out the third" (⟦आ. ४-१३⟧).

## Ṛk 1

O Adhvaryu, for him who is ⟦वेदिषदे⟧, who always sits on the altar; who is ⟦प्रियधामाय⟧, that is ⟦प्रियधाम्ने⟧, whose place is dear, to whom that which is characterised as the uttaravedi is dear; who is ⟦सुद्युते⟧, of beautiful shining; for Agni (⟦अग्नये⟧), who is distinguished by qualities such as leading in front, ⟦प्र भर⟧ the ⟦योनिं⟧, the place called the altar, as one does ⟦धासिमिव⟧, like food in the form of an oblation. ⟦प्र भर⟧ is "bring forward"; the meaning is "prepare it excellently". And that place — ⟦वस्त्रेणेव⟧, as one spreads over with a cloth for the sake of softness, in that way — with ⟦मन्मना⟧, with the equipment that is worthy to be thought on, or with the sacrificial grass, ⟦वासय⟧ the place of the altar; that is, cover it. Of what kind that place is, is now said. It is ⟦शुचिं⟧, pure, because impure things, Asuras and the like have departed through the purifying, the sprinkling and so on; it is ⟦ज्योतीरथं⟧, standing in the place of a chariot of the light, that is, of the ghee and the like, or of Agni, because it is the support; it is ⟦शुक्रवर्णं⟧, of bright colour because it is provided with Agni; for that very reason it is ⟦तमोहनं⟧, the slayer of darkness, of gloom, or of ignorance. The idea is: because, through the sacrifice and the like that are accomplished by Agni, it brings about the examination of knowledge.

Or else: in that ⟦योन्⟧, that place, as people hide with a cloth a thing that is to be hidden, in that way ⟦वासय⟧ that Agni; that is, lodge him, establish him. Or cover him with ⟦मन्मना⟧, with what is worthy to be thought on, pleasing, resembling a cloth, namely soft wood and the like. Of what kind is he? He is ⟦ज्योतीरथं⟧, provided with a chariot of radiance. The rest is as before.

## Ṛk 2

⟦द्विजन्मा⟧ is he who is born from the two araṇis. Or else, his having two births is because he arises through the churning and through the consecration of the establishing of the fire. The suffix manin is by the rule ⟦अन्येभ्योऽपि दृश्यंत⟧, "It is seen after others also". This Agni ⟦ऋज्यते⟧ towards (⟦अभि⟧) the ⟦अन्नं⟧ that is ⟦त्रिवृत्⟧, threefold — the food, what is to be eaten, of three kinds in the form of ghee, the sacrificial cake and Soma; that is, he reaches it. He leads it towards himself, facing it; he eats it. The root is ⟦ऋज गतिस्थानार्जनोपार्जनेषु⟧, "ṛj in the senses of going, standing, acquiring and earning". The suffix śyan is by transposition. Or else the word is ⟦भृज्यते⟧; the root is ⟦भ्रस्टो पाके⟧, "bhrasj in the sense of cooking"; or, he makes it cooked. Moreover ⟦ई⟧ means "this". In ⟦ईमित्यत्र⟧, "in īm here", the dropping of the m in the Saṃhitā is a Vedic usage. What is ⟦जग्धं⟧, eaten, when the ⟦संवत्सरे⟧, the year, is complete, again ⟦ववृधे⟧; that is, Agni makes it grow. Since what is worn out does not grow again, he makes the sacrificer, through the increase of the oblation, capable of sacrificing every year: this is the meaning. Or else: the oblation, eaten once, again when the year is complete, lying in the belly for a year, grows, or makes the eater grow. Moreover this Agni, being ⟦वृषा⟧, a showerer, by the ⟦आसा⟧, the mouth, by the ⟦जिह्वया⟧, the tongue, of the ⟦अन्यस्य⟧, the one, namely the oblation, becomes ⟦जेन्यः⟧, abundant; "becomes" is to be supplied. Or else: being a showerer, in one form, by the tongue of another, namely of the hotṛ and the others in the sacrifice, that is, by the ladle which is indicated by that tongue, he becomes ⟦जेन्यः⟧, grown great. Likewise, by ⟦अन्येन⟧, another form, that of the forest fire, being ⟦वारणः⟧, the one who wards off all, he ⟦मृष्ट⟧ down (⟦नि⟧) the ⟦वनिनो⟧, the trees that belong to the forest; that is, he wipes them down; the meaning is that he burns them. From the root mṛj, in the Vedic laṅ, the ātmanepada is by transposition.

## Ṛk 3

Of this (⟦अस्य⟧) Agni the ⟦मातरा⟧, who stand in the place of mothers, the two who bring him forth, ⟦उभा⟧, that is both, the two mothers called the araṇis, ⟦वेविजे⟧, that is, they move. The root is ⟦ओविजी भयचलनयोः⟧, "vij in the senses of fear and moving". From this root ending in the elision of yaṅ, the ātmanepada and the singular number are by transposition. The dropping of the t is by ⟦लोपस्त आत्मनेपदेष्टिति⟧, "elision of t in the ātmanepada endings". The absence of the loss of accent is by ⟦चादिलोपे विभाषेति⟧, "optionally when ca and the like are dropped". At the beginning of the churning they are ⟦कृष्णपुत्रौ⟧, attaining blackness of colour through contact with the fire, or making the ground attain it; likewise they are ⟦सक्षितौ⟧, going to a common task, the producing of Agni; the two mothers, being such, ⟦तरेते⟧ towards (⟦अभि⟧) the ⟦शिशुं⟧, Agni who, like an infant, is very small at the time of his birth; and they reach him, facing him. Of what kind is the infant? He is ⟦प्राचाजिह्वं⟧, having flame, which stands in the place of a tongue, situated in the forward region; ⟦ध्वसयंतं⟧, destroying the darkness; ⟦तृषुच्युतम⟧, coming out quickly from the two araṇis, being born swiftly, without trouble, from the womb. ⟦आ⟧ means "a little"; ⟦साच्यं⟧ means "to be united with"; with the powder of soft wood and the like he is ⟦कुपयं⟧, to be guarded. He is the ⟦वर्धनं⟧, the increase, that is, the one who makes increase, of the ⟦पितुः⟧, the protector, the sacrificer. The meaning is that the two mothers bring forth the infant Agni, who is endowed with the features just described, which he has in common with an infant of the world.

## Ṛk 4

For ⟦मनवे⟧, the one who has thought, for ⟦मानवस्यते⟧, the one who desires men, namely the priests, for the sake of the rite — for the sacrificer, for his sake, the flames of Agni ⟦उप युज्यंते⟧, are employed, by the Adhvaryu and the others in the sacrifices. Because the sūkta belongs to Agni, it is understood from fitness that flames are meant. Of what kind are they? They are ⟦मुमुक्ष्वो⟧, that is ⟦मुमुक्षव⟧, wishing to release the sacrificer by means of the offering, making him reach the world of Brahman. The absence of guṇa is by ⟦जसादिषु च्छंदसि नावचनमिति⟧, "in the Veda there is a statement of option before jas and the rest". That the flames are a means of attaining the world of Brahman by way of the offering is heard in the scripture, which begins with ⟦काली कराली⟧ and says: ⟦एतेषु यश्चरते भ्राजमानेषु यथाकालं चाहुतयो ह्याददायन्⟧ ⟦तं नयंत्येताः सूर्यस्य रश्मयो यत्र देवानां पतिरेकोऽधिवासः⟧, "He who performs when these are shining, and at the proper time — the offerings, taking him up, these lead him, as rays of the sun, to where the one lord of the gods has his abode" (⟦मुं. उ १-२-४, ५⟧). They are ⟦रघुद्रुवः⟧, going quickly; ⟦कृष्णसीतासः⟧, having black paths. The particle u has the sense of "and". They are ⟦जुवो⟧, given to speed, acting swiftly in their own operations; ⟦असमना⟧, of unlike minds: some go facing east, some facing west, and so they are of various minds. The singular number is by transposition. Or else ⟦असमना⟧ means "of different colours". And their being of different colours is heard in that same place: ⟦काली कराली च मनोजवा च सुलोहिता या च सुधूम्रवर्णा । स्पुलिंगिनी विश्वभुवी च⟧ ⟦देवी लेलायमाना इति सप्त जिह्वा इति⟧, "Kālī, and Karālī, and Manojavā, and Sulohitā, and she who is Sudhūmravarṇā, Sphuliṅginī, and the goddess Viśvabhuvī: these are the seven flickering tongues." They are ⟦अजिरासो⟧, given to going; ⟦रघुस्यदः⟧, flowing swiftly; ⟦वातजूता⟧, driven by the wind. The first member keeps its original accent by the rule ⟦तृतीया कर्मणीति⟧, "a word in the third case, before a participle in the sense of the object". They are ⟦आशवो⟧, given to pervading.

## Ṛk 5

⟦आत्⟧ means "thereafter". Of this (⟦अस्य⟧) Agni who is being praised, ⟦ते⟧, those well-known sparks, ⟦वृथा⟧, all together, ⟦ईरते⟧; that is, they go in all directions. Of what kind are they? They are ⟦ध्वसयंतः⟧, destroying the darknesses; and they are ⟦करिक्रतो⟧, making exceedingly, what is of black colour and ⟦अभ्वं⟧, great, a path of overpowering advance, and a ⟦महि⟧, great, ⟦वर्षः⟧, form, namely light. At what time, is now said. ⟦यत्⟧ means "when": when, the ⟦महीं⟧, great, ⟦अवनिं⟧, earth, ⟦सीं⟧, on all sides, ⟦मर्मृशत्⟧, touching again and again — ⟦मृशेर्यज्लुगंताच्छतर्यभ्यस्तानामादिरित्याद्युदात्तत्वं⟧: from the root mṛś ending in the elision of yaṅ, before the suffix śatṛ, the first syllable is udātta by the rule "the first syllable of reduplicated stems" — ⟦अभिश्वसन्⟧, stirring on all sides, ⟦स्तनयन्⟧, making a sound, ⟦नानदत्⟧, sounding exceedingly — the suffix and the accent are as before — he ⟦एति⟧, goes, forth and all around. The meaning is: just when he blazes, licking here and there with his tongues and making the sound "bhugu-bhugu", then the sparks arise.

## Ṛk 6

The Agni who (⟦यः⟧) among the ⟦बभ्रूषु⟧, the plants of brown colour, or those skilled in supporting the worlds, ⟦अधि⟧, exceedingly, ⟦नम्मते⟧, bends; that is, he enters the pieces of wood. From the root nam, in leṭ, there is the augment aṭ; the suffix śnā is by transposition. Or else, in laṭ, there is the Vedic elision called ślu of the suffix śap; by transposition there is the absence of the rule that only the first consonant of the reduplication remains. ⟦भूषन्न⟧: as if adorning the beings themselves with his own lustre. Moreover, as he enters, ⟦वृषेव⟧, like a bull that impregnates, the ⟦पत्निः⟧, the plants that protect and prosper him alone, ⟦रोरुवत्⟧ — ⟦रौतेर्यज् लुगंताच्छतर्यभ्यस्तानामादिरित्याद्युदात्तत्वं⟧: from the root ru ending in the elision of yaṅ, before the suffix śatṛ, the first syllable is udātta by the rule "the first syllable of reduplicated stems" — sounding exceedingly, he ⟦एति⟧ towards (⟦अभि⟧); that is, he goes all around them. The meaning is that, having first entered the plants, afterwards, when he has awakened, he burns those very plants. Moreover, having done so, he is ⟦ओजायमानः⟧. ⟦ओज⟧ is a name of strength. He himself behaves as if he were strength. By ⟦कर्तुः क्यज् सलोपश्चति⟧, "kyaṅ after the agent, and the elision of s", there are kyaṅ and the elision of s. Without smoke and the like, being of the form of lustre alone, ⟦तन्वश्च शुंभते⟧: he also makes shine the flames that are his own body; the meaning is that he nourishes them. Moreover, with his flames grown great, being ⟦दुर्गृभिः⟧, unable to be seized by anyone at all, ⟦भीमो न⟧, like a fearsome beast — as that beast, being hard to seize, shakes its horn, so this one also ⟦दविधाव⟧, shakes exceedingly, his ⟦शृंगा⟧, horns, the flames that rise high like horns. It is a form in liṭ from the root dhū ending in the elision of yaṅ. And its inclusion among ⟦दाधर्त्यादावितिशब्देन⟧, dādharti and the rest, is to be seen as made by the word iti.

## Ṛk 7

That (⟦सः⟧) Agni is ⟦संस्तिर⟧, covered. From the root stṛ preceded by sam there is the suffix ka, because it belongs to the group beginning with mūlavibhuja (⟦पा ३-२-५-२⟧). The first syllable of the second member is udātta by ⟦परादिश्चंदसि बहुलमित्युत्तरपदाद्युदात्तत्वं⟧, "the beginning of the latter word, variously in the Veda". Likewise he is ⟦विस्तिरः⟧, at some time spread out, and ⟦सं गृभायति⟧; that is, he gathers up the plants. Moreover he, ⟦जानन्नेव⟧, indeed understanding "I must become grown for the sake of the sacrificer; therefore I am to be joined with the flames", being ⟦नित्यो⟧, unbroken, ⟦शये⟧ upon (⟦आ⟧) the ⟦जानतीः⟧, the flames, which understand "we must become grown for the sake of the sacrificer"; that is, he lies upon them; the meaning is that he resorts to them. The dropping of the t is by ⟦लोपस्त आत्मनेपदेष्टिति⟧, "elision of t in the ātmanepada endings". The flames, being such, ⟦पुनः⟧ — having indeed been grown before — ⟦वर्धंते⟧, grow, in such a way that they again become fit for the sacrifice. They ⟦यंति⟧, reach, also (⟦अपि⟧) Agni, who is ⟦देव्यं⟧, fit for the dealings of sacrifice and the like, or worthy of praise. Although Agni and the flame are one, yet, as in "the head of Rāhu", the relation of the goer and what is gone to is figurative. Moreover the flames, thus grown, or the plants, ⟦सचा⟧, together with Agni, having joined with the ⟦पित्रोः⟧, the two protectors of the world, ⟦कृण्वते⟧, make, for heaven and earth a ⟦रूपं⟧, a form, made of lustre, that is ⟦अन्मत्⟧, not existing before. The meaning is that they touch the two worlds.

## Ṛk 8

Him (⟦तं⟧), Agni, the ⟦अग्रुवः⟧, who stand in front — or else, this is a name of the fingers: they are straight like fingers — the ⟦केशिनीः⟧, the flames, which have a blackness arising at the top that stands in the place of hair, ⟦सं रेभिरे हि⟧; that is, they indeed clasp him; they embrace him. Moreover, doing so, though ⟦मम्रूषीः⟧, dead, having come to the state of embers — it is a form with the feminine suffix ṅīp from the root mṛ ending in the suffix kvasu — for ⟦आयव⟧, for Agni who is coming, their husband, ⟦पुनः⟧, again, not regarding their own affliction, ⟦ऊर्ध्वाः⟧ they ⟦प्र तस्तुः⟧; that is, they stood forth with faces upward, raised up. The meaning is that, though almost dead, they rose up to meet him. When they have done so, that husband Agni, ⟦प्रमुंचन्⟧, removing thoroughly, the ⟦जरां⟧, the loss of vigour, of those (⟦तासां⟧) flames; ⟦नानदत्⟧, sounding exceedingly, as though uttering a spell for the removal of old age; and not merely the loss of old age, but also ⟦जनयन्⟧, producing, the ⟦परं⟧, unsurpassed, ⟦असुं⟧, breath, and the ⟦जीवं⟧, the capacity to hold the breath, that is ⟦अस्तृतं⟧, unharmed even by the throwing on of wood, water and the like, he ⟦एति⟧, goes; that is, he comes into the presence of those flames. As in the world certain loving women, having sported intensely with their lover, afterwards, when he has gone abroad, wasted by the separation and dying, afterwards, when he has come back, disregarding their own weakness, strive with joy to embrace him; and that husband, taking away their old age by the uttering of a spell and giving them life by a fitting gift, protects them: this sense is to be kept in view here.

## Ṛk 9

The forest fire is praised. This Agni, ⟦रिहन्⟧, licking, ⟦परि⟧, all around, the ⟦अधीवासं⟧ of the ⟦मातुः⟧, of the earth, the maker of beings; that is, the grass, shrubs, herbs and the like that stand in the place of a covering upon her. ⟦अहेति⟧: aha is used of what is well known. Or else: the ⟦अधीवासं⟧ of the mother, the earth, the garment worn upon her, ⟦परि⟧, all around, ⟦रिहन्⟧, bringing it about. Accompanied by ⟦सत्वभिः⟧, living beings, that are ⟦तुविग्रेभिः⟧, sounding abundantly, or of abundant going, with ⟦ज्रयः⟧, with speed, he ⟦वि याति⟧; that is, he goes in various ways. Moreover, to the ⟦पद्वते⟧, the one that has feet, the two-footed and the four-footed, ⟦दधत्⟧, giving, ⟦वयो⟧, food, such as is fitting for each. It is well known that in a forest **NOTE**{Ṛk 9: the word before "in a forest fire" stands as ⟦विवने⟧ in the supplied text and in the reference text alike; is "in a forest" what is meant?}, in a forest fire, tigers and the like eat the living beings that flee in fear. Doing what? ⟦सदा⟧, always, ⟦रेरिहत्⟧, burning the grass and the like. Moreover the ⟦श्येनी⟧, dark-coloured, ⟦वर्तनिः⟧, path, ⟦सचते⟧, attends, in due order (⟦अनु⟧), him as he goes about. Wherever Agni goes, there the path also becomes black. ⟦अहेति⟧: aha is used of what is well known.

## Ṛk 10

O Agni, ⟦दीदिहि⟧, shine, in ⟦अस्माकं⟧ ⟦मघवत्सु⟧, in those that are connected with us and have food, namely the sacrifices or the houses. ⟦अध⟧ is atha, "thereafter". ⟦श्वसीनान्⟧ is having breath, having movement through the flames, or having our breathing; ⟦वृषभः⟧ is the showerer of the things desired; ⟦दमूनाः⟧ is having a mind for giving, having a mind for restraint, or having a subdued mind; "be so everywhere" is to be supplied. You who are of the character just described, ⟦शिशुमतीरनास्य⟧: by the word śiśu that condition is indicated; having cast down, having left, the flames that have infancy, ⟦अदीदेः⟧, shine intensely. Of what kind are you? In ⟦युत्सु⟧, in battles, ⟦वर्मेव⟧, like armour, ⟦परिजर्भुराणः⟧, warding off our haters again and again. Or else: in ⟦युत्सु⟧, in battles, be ⟦परिजर्भुराणो⟧ like armour. From the root hṛ ending in the elision of yaṅ, the suffix śānac is by transposition. The first syllable is udātta by ⟦अभ्यस्तानामादिरित्याद्युदात्तत्वं⟧, "the first syllable of reduplicated stems". You, sir, who have the qualities described **NOTE**{Ṛk 10: the supplied text has ⟦भवान्मघवत्सु⟧, "you, sir, … in the maghavat", where the reference text has "bhava maghavatsu", "be so; in the maghavat"; which is to stand?}, shine in the ⟦मघवत्सु⟧, the sacrifices.

## Ṛk 11

O Agni, ⟦इदं⟧, this, is our oblation in the form of Soma and the like. Of what kind is it? It is ⟦सुधितं⟧, that is, well placed, well given, ⟦अधि⟧, upon, the ⟦दुःधितात्⟧, the badly placed, hard wood and the like. ⟦मन्मनो⟧ means "than what is worthy to be thought on"; ⟦प्रियात्⟧ with ⟦चित्⟧ means "even than what is pleasing to you". The word u is for emphasis; the word cit has the sense of the word api. Let it be (⟦अस्तु⟧) ⟦ते⟧, to you, ⟦प्रेयो⟧, exceedingly dearest. What then? He says: O Agni, the lustre of ⟦ते⟧, your, ⟦तन्वः⟧, body, that is, of the flame kindled by the ghee, which (⟦यत्⟧) ⟦रोचते⟧, shines, ⟦शुचि⟧, spotless, and ⟦शुक्रं⟧, bright — together with that (⟦तेन⟧), distributing ⟦रत्नं⟧, what is delightful, gems, pearls and the like, ⟦अस्मभ्यं⟧ ⟦वनसे⟧; that is, favour us. The ātmanepada of the root van is by transposition.

## Ṛk 12

O Agni, ⟦उतापि च⟧: uta means "and also". For ⟦रथाय⟧, for speeding on; for ⟦गृहाय⟧, for that which is the abode of all; ⟦नो⟧, our, for the sacrificer — a ⟦नावं⟧, a boat that carries across the round of existence, a boat consisting of the Soma sacrifice. For in scriptural texts such as ⟦यज्ञो वै सुतर्मा नौरित्यादिषु⟧, "The sacrifice indeed is a boat good for crossing" **NOTE**{Ṛk 12: the reference text adds here the reference "ai. brā. 1. 13", which the supplied text does not have.}, it is praised as having the form of a boat. Of what kind is it? It is ⟦नित्यारित्रां⟧, provided with the wooden implements for drawing the water, in the form of the fixed priests; and ⟦पद्वतीं⟧ — the suffix matup is udātta by ⟦ह्रस्वनुड्भामिति⟧, "after a short vowel and after nuṭ" — provided with feet in the form of the substance, the deity, the mantra and the like. Such a boat, consisting of the sacrifice, ⟦रासि⟧; that is, give it, bring it about. The root rā has the sense of giving, because it is read among the names of that action: ⟦दासति रातीति⟧, "dāsati, rāti" **NOTE**{Ṛk 12: the reference text adds here the reference "ni. 3. 20. 4", which the supplied text does not have.}. That same boat is further described. ⟦उतापि च⟧, and also, the boat which (⟦या⟧) makes ⟦अस्माकं⟧ ⟦वीरान्⟧, the sons and others connected with us, ⟦मघोनो⟧, possessed of food characterised as the oblation; and the boat which ⟦पारयात्⟧, that is, would carry across, would take over, the ⟦जनांश्च⟧, the sacrificers, us also. From the root pāray, in leṭ, there is the augment āṭ. It would make us reach the far shore of that which consists of many sufferings such as birth and death, namely the world of Brahman, the seat of unsurpassed happiness; because ⟦एष वः पुण्यः सुकृतो ब्रह्मलोक⟧, "This is your holy, well-made world of Brahman" **NOTE**{Ṛk 12: the reference text adds here the reference "mu. u. 1.3.6", which the supplied text does not have.}, is heard of those who perform sacrifice and the like. And having thus made us reach it, the boat which would make us reach ⟦शर्म⟧, happiness, the unsurpassed Brahman; because it is said: ⟦परस्यांते कृतात्मानः प्रविशंति परं पदमित्युक्तत्वात्⟧, "At the end of the para those whose selves are perfected enter the highest place"; this is by way of producing purity of mind, according to the scriptural text ⟦विविदिषंति यज्ञेन दानेनेति⟧, "They seek to know by sacrifice, by giving" **NOTE**{Ṛk 12: the reference text adds here the reference "bṛ. u. 4. 4. 22", which the supplied text does not have.}.

## Ṛk 13

O Agni, ⟦जुगुर्याः⟧ towards (⟦अभि⟧) ⟦न⟧, our, ⟦उकथं⟧, the śastra; that is, praise it, facing it; encourage it; accept it as "well recited". The root is ⟦ग्य शब्दे⟧, "gṝ in the sense of sound". In liṅ there is the elision called ślu of the conjugational suffix by ⟦बहुलं छंदसीति⟧, "variously in the Veda"; the u is by ⟦बहुलं छंदसीत्युत्वं⟧, "variously in the Veda". Likewise let ⟦द्यावाक्षामा⟧, heaven and earth also, encourage it by providing the means of the sacrifice. Likewise let the ⟦सिंधवः⟧, the rivers that are given to flowing, which are ⟦स्वगूर्ताः⟧, going of their own accord, ⟦यंतः⟧, making reach us, ⟦गव्यं⟧, the milk and the like that come from the cow, by way of producing grass and the like for the cows, and ⟦यव्यं⟧ — this is an indication: the sacrificial cake and the like that are produced from barley, rice and so on — encourage it. Likewise the ⟦अरुण्यः⟧, yoked with cows of ruddy colour — the possessive suffix is elided — the Dawns who are the means of ruddiness; because they are its cause they are called by that word. ⟦दीर्घाहा⟧, on all the days, at all times, the ⟦इषं⟧, the desired food, juice and the like, that is ⟦वरं⟧, to be chosen, the best, and other means of sacrifice also, let them ⟦वरंत⟧, make reach us. The root is ⟦वृज् संभक्तौ⟧, "vṛ in the sense of sharing". From this root with the causative sense contained within it there is the Vedic laṅ; the suffix śap is by transposition. Let them accomplish it. All this is by the grace of Agni; thus, since it is a praise of Agni alone, the sūkta's belonging to Agni is not contradicted.
