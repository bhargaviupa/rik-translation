## Ṛk 1

**NOTE**{the supplied text has no introduction to this sūkta; the reference text has one} O Adhvaryu and the rest, ⟦वः⟧, belonging to you, ⟦पांतं⟧, whose nature is to protect, that which is to be drunk, the Vedic ⟦अंधसः⟧, that is, the food in the form of Soma **NOTE**{the supplied text reads ⟦छांदसोऽंधः⟧ after "to be drunk"; the reference text has no "Vedic" here and reads "or to be drunk; andhasaḥ: andhaḥ, the food in the form of Soma" — is a clause on the Vedic case-form lost or misread here?}, ⟦प्रार्चत⟧: prepare it in an excellent way. For whom? ⟦धियायते⟧, for him who desires praises, ⟦महे⟧, the great, ⟦शूराय⟧, the valiant one, Indra, and ⟦विष्णवे⟧, for the pervading god who bears this name. What is the pre-eminence of these two? It is told. ⟦या⟧, which two, Indra and Viṣṇu, ⟦पर्वतानां⟧, of those that possess gratifying, or that possess filling. The word parvata is explained by Yāska thus: ⟦पर्वतः पर्व पुनः पृणातेः प्रीणातेर्वेति⟧, "that which has joints is a parvata; and 'joint' again is from the root meaning to fill or from the root meaning to gratify". Of the worlds so characterised, ⟦सानुनि⟧, on the elevated region; the meaning is, in the whole world. Or else, on the elevated region of the clouds, since the word parvata is stated among the names of the cloud. There ⟦तस्थतुः⟧: they stand. Of what kind are the two? ⟦अदाभ्या⟧, not to be harmed, not to be overpowered by others, and for that very reason ⟦महः⟧, great. Or else ⟦महः⟧ means in splendour, in their own lustre, and it is connected with the word ⟦सानुनि⟧. For this there is an illustration: ⟦साधुना⟧, by one able to bring one to the desired place, ⟦अर्वता⟧, by a swift horse, as it were. As by means of it one mounts a very elevated region, so these two have mounted a high station. Therefore offer worship for those two.

## Ṛk 2

When there is a surplus of Soma at the third pressing, this is an optional yājyā, the offering-verse. And so it is laid down in the sūtra: ⟦त्वेषमित्था समरणं शिमावतोरिति वा याज्या⟧, "or the yājyā is 'tveṣam itthā samaraṇaṃ śimīvatoḥ'" (⟦आ. ६-७⟧). **NOTE**{the supplied text has this statement of the ritual use with its sūtra reference; the reference text does not have it}

O Indra and Viṣṇu, ⟦शिमीवतोः⟧, of you who have such works as the granting of what is desired, ⟦वां⟧, of you two, ⟦इत्था⟧, in this manner, ⟦त्वेषं⟧, the blazing, ⟦समरणं⟧, the proper going to the place of the sacrifice, ⟦सुतपा⟧, the sacrificer who has drunk the Soma that remained after the offering, ⟦उरुष्यति⟧: protects, that is, honours by sacrifice. Or else: ⟦शिमीवतोः⟧, of you who have such works as striking, ⟦वां⟧, of you two, ⟦त्वेषं⟧, the bright, the one endowed with valour, ⟦समरणं⟧, that which is attended by a proper going together with one another, which consists in the cleaving of the cloud for the granting of rain, the sacrificer ⟦उरुष्यति⟧: protects; the meaning is, he praises. What is the pre-eminence of these two? It is told. ⟦या⟧, which two, Indra and Viṣṇu, ⟦मर्त्याय⟧, for the man, the sacrificer who gives the oblation, ⟦प्रतिधीयमानमित्⟧, that which is to be bestowed in return, which has the form of the fruit, ⟦असनां⟧, that is, asana, whose nature is to move, whose nature is to be given, food and the like, ⟦अस्तुः⟧, of the one who throws what is desired, or who drives out the enemies, ⟦कृशानोः⟧, from Agni, ⟦उरुष्यथः⟧: you make it go on without interruption. The meaning is: having accepted the oblation offered in the fire, you will give the fruit too through his mouth alone.

## Ṛk 3

⟦ताः⟧, those well-known oblations in the form of Soma, offered by the sacrificer, ⟦ईं⟧, this, ⟦अस्य⟧, of this Indra, ⟦महि⟧, great, ⟦पौंस्यं⟧, the deed of a man, the strength whose form is about to be told, ⟦वर्धंति⟧: they make it grow. Or ⟦ईं⟧ is without meaning. And that Indra ⟦नयति⟧ the power produced by the drinking of Soma ⟦नि⟧ to ⟦मातरा⟧, heaven and earth: he conveys it to them thoroughly. For what purpose? ⟦रेतसे⟧, for the capacity of living beings to beget sons and the like, ⟦भुजे⟧, for their enjoyment or their protection. Where? That is told. ⟦दिवः⟧, of the shining one, the sun, ⟦अधि⟧, exceedingly, ⟦रोचने⟧, in the shining orb. ⟦नाम⟧, the bending, the power established in heaven and earth, ⟦पुत्रः⟧, the protector of many, all people, ⟦पितुः⟧, from the protector Viṣṇu, by his favour. Or else: ⟦पितुः⟧, because he is his own begetter, being himself born **NOTE**{the supplied text reads ⟦स्वोत्पादकत्वात्त्वयमुत्पन्नं⟧; the reference text has svotpādakāt svayam utpannaṃ, "from his own begetter, being himself born" — is the ablative of the reference text the intended reading?}, ⟦अवरं⟧, the lower name, called "grandson", ⟦परं⟧, the name "son", which is higher than that, and, in relation to both of these, ⟦तृतीयं⟧, the third name, "father", ⟦दधाति⟧: he bears. For the Śruti says: ⟦पिता पुत्रः पौत्रः⟧, "three indeed are the seeds: father, son, grandson" (⟦तै- सं. ५-६-८-४⟧). The meaning of this is as follows. The oblations of Soma offered in the fire reach the orb of the sun and nourish Indra and Viṣṇu, who are other forms of Savitṛ of the twelve selves. Those two, being satisfied, rain by means of the cloud. By that rain, by way of the crops and the like, they make the offspring grow in the form of sons, grandsons and the rest. Of such great majesty are Indra and Viṣṇu.

## Ṛk 4

⟦अस्य⟧, of this one of great majesty, ⟦पौंस्यं⟧, each such manliness, the excellence of his valour, ⟦गृणीमसि⟧: we praise, we laud. Of what kind is he? ⟦इनस्य⟧, of the lord of all, ⟦त्रातुः⟧, of the protector, ⟦अवृकस्य⟧: a vṛka is one who injures, an enemy and the like; of him who is free from such, ⟦माळ्पुषः⟧, of the sprinkler, the ever young. Among the deeds of valour that are to be praised he mentions one. ⟦यः⟧, Viṣṇu, who, ⟦पार्थिवानि⟧, the earth and the rest, or those that possess extension; by the force of the sense it is understood that ⟦रजांसीति⟧ is meant. That all the three worlds are denoted by the word pṛthivī has been shown before. These, ⟦त्रिभिरिद्विगामभि⟧, by three varied goings only, ⟦उरु⟧, in such a way that it is wide, ⟦क्रमिष्ट⟧: he strode; the meaning is, he strode out. ⟦उरुगायाय⟧, for that which is to be widely sung, ⟦जीवसे⟧, for the protection of the three worlds. Or else ⟦उरुगायाय⟧: the fourth case is in the sense of the sixth; such valour of Viṣṇu the urugāya ⟦गृणीमसि⟧. For what purpose? ⟦जीवसे⟧, for life, for protection.

## Ṛk 5

⟦अस्य⟧, of this ⟦स्वर्दृशः⟧, the seer of heaven or of everything, Viṣṇu, ⟦द्वे इत् क्रमणे⟧, two steps only, ⟦मर्त्यः⟧, a man, ⟦अभिख्याय⟧, having made known by all prosperity and the like **NOTE**{what is meant by ⟦सर्वभूत्यादिना⟧ in the gloss of abhikhyāya? The reference text has the same word}, ⟦भुरण्यति⟧: goes to, resorts to. The verb bhuraṇyati belongs to the kaṇḍvādi class and has the sense of going, because it is read among the verbs of that sense in ⟦भुरण्यति शवतीति⟧, "bhuraṇyati, śavati". **NOTE**{the reference text adds a Nirukta reference (2.14.16) after this quotation; the supplied text has none} Because it is well known, the world of earth, and because the rain comes from it, the mid-region: these two steps he knows. ⟦अस्य⟧, of this Viṣṇu, ⟦तृतीयं⟧, the third step, called the world of heaven, no man whatever ⟦नकिः⟧, not at all, ⟦दधर्षति⟧: he does not overpower it by his understanding; the meaning is, he is not able to know it. Not man only, but ⟦वयश्चन⟧, even the Maruts, the goers. Of what kind are they? ⟦पतयंतः⟧, able to go everywhere, and likewise ⟦पतत्रिणः⟧, those whose nature is to fly, Garuḍa and the like, or the winds, ⟦नकिरा दधर्षति⟧: they are not at all able. The augment aṭ is added to the root dhṛṣ in leṭ. The purport is: because the Satya-world is exceedingly remote, it is beyond the range of all.

## Ṛk 6

This Viṣṇu, who is the sun, ⟦चतुर्भिः साकं⟧, together with four, ⟦नवतिं च⟧, ninety; the meaning is, ninety-four. The divisions of time of this number, ⟦नामभिः⟧, by his own modes of bending, by particular impulsions, in such a way that it becomes round, ⟦व्यतीन्⟧, those whose nature is to move in various ways, ⟦वृत्तं चक्रं न⟧, like a wheel furnished with many spokes: as one makes that whirl by hurling it upon an enemy, so the divisions of time of the said number ⟦अवीविपत्⟧: he makes them tremble, he makes them whirl. The root is ⟦टुवेपृ कंपने⟧, "ṭuvepṛ, in the sense of trembling"; it is the form from the causative stem in luṅ with caṅ. Which, then, are they? It is told. The year is one. The half-years are two. The seasons are five. The months are twelve. The half-months are twenty-four. The days-and-nights are thirty. The watches are eight. The lagnas beginning with Meṣa, which come round in a single day, are twelve. Taken together, those of the number ninety with four in addition he has made to tremble. But the sun himself also revolves like the others; how is it said that he makes them revolve? This is no fault, because he is another form of Viṣṇu, the fixed one, who makes these revolve. Or else because the revolving of the others depends on his own revolving; therefore it is said that he himself makes them revolve. Viṣṇu, who thus has time as his self **NOTE**{the supplied text reads ⟦कालात्मको⟧, "having time as his self"; the reference text reads kalātmakaḥ}, is ⟦बृहच्छरीरो⟧, of great body, because in his self as Virāj the bodies of all gods, men and the rest are his own body. He establishes this very greatness of body: ⟦विमिमानः⟧, measuring out his self in various ways, dividing it and setting it up as gods and animals. Or else: ⟦ऋक्वभिः⟧, by those who have praise or who have mantras, ⟦विमिमानः⟧. Although he is all-pervading, yet, because he is dependent on devotion, he is measured by praise. ⟦युवा⟧, whose nature is to mingle everywhere, or the ever young; for that very reason ⟦अकुमारः⟧, not small; Mahāviṣṇu, being such, ⟦आहवं⟧, to the call, ⟦प्रति⟧ ⟦एति⟧: he goes to the place of the sacrifice.
