## Ṛk 1

**NOTE**{the supplied text has no introduction to this sūkta; the reference text has one} O men, of ⟦विष्णोः⟧, of the god whose nature it is to pervade, the ⟦वीर्याणि⟧, the heroic deeds, ⟦नु⟧ ⟦कं⟧, very quickly, ⟦प्र वोचं⟧, I declare. Although ⟦नु कमिति⟧ is here two words, still, because Yāska has said ⟦नवोत्तराणि⟧ ⟦पदानि⟧ (⟦नै. ३-१२⟧), "the nine words that follow" **NOTE**{the reference of this quotation stands as ⟦नै. ३-१२⟧; the reference text has Nirukta 3.13: which is right?}, and because in another śākhā it is recited as one, the two words ⟦नु कमिति⟧ are in the very sense of nu. Which are those deeds? To that he says: the Viṣṇu who (⟦यः⟧) ⟦विममे⟧, fashioned in a special way, the ⟦पार्थिवानि⟧, connected with the earth, ⟦रजांसि⟧, whose nature it is to colour, those which preside over the three worlds beginning with the earth, in the form of Agni, Vāyu and Āditya. Here the three worlds too are denoted by the word pṛthivī. And so there is another mantra: ⟦यदींद्राग्नी अवमस्यां पृथिव्यां मध्यमस्यां परमस्या मुत स्थः⟧ (⟦ऋ. १-१०८-९⟧), "Whether, O Indra and Agni, you are on the lowest earth, on the middle one, or on the highest". In the Taittirīya too, beginning with ⟦योऽस्यां पृथिव्यामस्यायुषे⟧, "You who are on this earth, with life", it is said ⟦द्वितीयस्यां तृतीयस्यां पृथिव्यामिति⟧, "who are on the second, on the third earth" (⟦तै. सं. १-२-१२-१⟧). Therefore the three worlds are denoted by the word pṛthivī. Moreover the Viṣṇu who ⟦अस्कभायत्⟧, propped as their support, the ⟦उत्तरं⟧, raised higher, exceedingly wide, ⟦सधस्थं⟧, common abode, the mid-region which is the resting-place of the three worlds; the meaning is that he fashioned it. By this it comes to be said that he also created the three worlds that rest on the mid-region. Or else: the Viṣṇu who ⟦विममे⟧, fashioned in various ways, the ⟦पार्थिवानि⟧, connected with the earth, ⟦रजांसि⟧, the seven worlds below the earth. The word rajas denotes a world, because Yāska has said ⟦लोका रजांस्युच्यंत⟧, "The worlds are called rajāṃsi". And moreover, who propped the ⟦उत्तरं⟧, raised higher, coming above, ⟦सधस्थं⟧, common abode, fit for the doers of merit to dwell in together, the set of seven worlds beginning with Bhūr; the meaning is that he created it. From the root skambh, in place of the vikaraṇa śnā enjoined by ⟦स्तंभुस्तुंबिति⟧, there is the substitute śāyac, by interchange, according to ⟦छंदसि शायजपीति⟧. Or else: ⟦पार्थिवानि⟧, having the earth as their cause, ⟦रजांसि⟧, worlds, he fashioned; the meaning is, the three worlds beginning with Bhūr. Because the other worlds are for the enjoying of the works earned on the earth, the earth is their cause. And moreover, who propped the ⟦उत्तरं⟧, the more excellent, which is above all the worlds — its excellence is from there being no return from it — the ⟦सधस्थं⟧, the common abode of the worshippers, the world of Satya; the meaning is that he set it up firm. Doing what? ⟦त्रेधा⟧ ⟦विचक्रमाणः⟧, striding variously, in three ways, over the worlds created by himself. The threefold striding of Viṣṇu is well known in passages of the Veda such as ⟦विष्णुर्वि चक्रमे⟧ (⟦ऋ. १-२२-१७⟧), "Viṣṇu strode over this". For that very reason he is ⟦उरुग्गायः⟧, sung by the great, or sung very abundantly. Of such a Viṣṇu, who has done thus, I declare the heroic deeds.

## Ṛk 2

**NOTE**{the reference text has no passage on the ritual use at this place; it gives the ritual use, in other words, in its introduction to the sūkta} At the third pressing, when there is Soma left over, a śastra has to be produced. There this triplet, ⟦प्र तत्⟧, is the anurūpa. It is laid down in the section ⟦सोमातिरेक इति⟧: ⟦प्र तद्विष्णुः स्तवते वीर्येणेति⟧ ⟦स्तोत्रियानुरूपौ⟧ (⟦आ. ६-७⟧), "the two so beginning are the stotriya and the anurūpa". In the section ⟦वाजपेयेनाधिपत्यकाम इति⟧ it is laid down: ⟦प्र तत्ते अद्य शिपिविष्ट नाम प्र तद्विष्णुः स्तवते वीर्येण⟧ (⟦आ. ९-९⟧). Because ⟦यस्येति⟧, "whose", is going to be said, ⟦स इति⟧, "he", is understood. He, of great majesty, by ⟦वीर्येण⟧, by his own heroic deed of the form stated before, ⟦स्तवते⟧, is praised by all. The vikaraṇa śap comes, by interchange, in the passive. There is an illustration of being praised for heroism: ⟦मृगो न⟧, like a lion or the like. As a lion, the hunter of those opposed to him, ⟦भीमः⟧, producing fear, ⟦कुचरः⟧, a doer of blameworthy acts such as harming, or one who goes to places hard of access, ⟦गिरिष्ठाः⟧, staying in high places such as mountains, is praised by all. On this matter there is the Nirukta: ⟦मृगो न भीमः कुचरो गिरिष्ठाः । मृग इव भीमः कुचरो गिरिष्ठा मृगो⟧ ⟦मार्पर्गेर्गतिकर्मणो भीमो बिभ्यत्यस्माद्भिष्मोऽप्येतस्मादेव । कुचर इति चरतिकर्म कुत्सितमथ चेद्देवताभिधानं क्वायं न चरतीति वा । गिरिष्ठा गिरिस्थाया गिरिः⟧ ⟦पर्वतः समुद्दीर्णो भवति पर्ववान् पर्वतः पर्व पुनः पृणातेः प्रीणातीर्वा⟧ (⟦नि. १-२०⟧), "'Fearsome like a mṛga, kucara, giriṣṭhāḥ': fearsome as a mṛga is, kucara, giriṣṭhāḥ. Mṛga is from the root mṛj in the sense of going. Bhīma: they are afraid of him; bhīṣma too is from this same. Kucara: he does a blameworthy act of moving about; but if it is a name of the deity, then: where does he not move? Giriṣṭhāḥ: staying on a mountain. Giri is a mountain: it is raised up. Parvata is that which has joints; and parvan again is from the root pṝ or from prī". In the same way this one too is ⟦मृगो⟧, the seeker-out of enemies; ⟦भीमो⟧, terrible, he from whom all have fear. Fear of the Supreme Lord is well known in passages of the Veda such as ⟦भीषास्माद्वातः पवते⟧ (⟦तै. आ. ८-८-१⟧), "From fear of him the wind blows". Moreover he is ⟦कुचरः⟧, a doer of blameworthy acts such as the slaying of enemies, or one who moves about in ⟦कुषु⟧, in all the lands, in the three worlds; and likewise ⟦गिरिष्ठा⟧, staying in a world that is raised high like a mountain. Or else: one who is at all times present in ⟦गिरि⟧, in speech in the form of the mantras and the like. Such a one is praised for his own greatness. Moreover that Viṣṇu is praised, in whose (⟦यस्य⟧) ⟦उरुषु⟧, wide, three in number, ⟦विक्रमणेषु⟧, puttings-forth of the foot, ⟦विश्वा⟧, all, ⟦भुवनानि⟧, the multitudes of beings, ⟦अधिक्षियंति⟧, dwell, resting on them.

## Ṛk 3

⟦विष्णवे⟧, to the all-pervading one, may ⟦शूषं⟧, the strength, the greatness, that is born of our rites and the like, and ⟦मन्म⟧, the thought, the praise — or else the ⟦शूषं⟧, the strength, that is worthy of being thought upon — ⟦एतु⟧, reach Viṣṇu. The dative is used because the object is treated as the recipient. To one of what kind? To ⟦गिरिक्षिते⟧, him who abides in speech, or in a place that is high like a mountain; to ⟦उरुगायाय⟧, him who is sung by many; to ⟦वृष्णे⟧, the showerer of desires. May the ⟦शूषं⟧ reach him who is of such great majesty. What is his distinction? It is said: the Viṣṇu who (⟦यः⟧), ⟦एकः⟧ ⟦इत्⟧, being one only, without a second, with ⟦त्रिभिः पदेभिः⟧, with three feet, ⟦विममे⟧, fashioned in a special way, ⟦इदं⟧, this well-known, visible, ⟦दीर्घं⟧, exceedingly extended, ⟦प्रयतं⟧, fixed, ⟦सधस्थं⟧, common abode, the three worlds.

## Ṛk 4

That Viṣṇu whose (⟦यस्य⟧) three ⟦पदानि⟧, puttings-forth of the foot, ⟦पूर्णा⟧, full, with ⟦मधुना⟧, with the sweet, divine nectar, ⟦अक्षीयमाणा⟧, not wasting away, by ⟦स्वधया⟧, by food, ⟦मदंति⟧, gladden the people who resort to them; ⟦य उ⟧, who alone, ⟦पृथिवीं⟧, the well-known earth, ⟦द्यां⟧ ⟦उत⟧, and the mid-region, whose nature it is to shine, and ⟦विश्वा भुवनानि⟧, all the multitudes of beings, and the fourteen worlds. Or else: by the word pṛthivī are taken the seven worlds that lie below, Atala, Vitala and the rest; by the word dyu, the seven worlds beginning with Bhūr, which come next to those **NOTE**{the supplied ⟦तदनांतररूपाणि⟧ is translated as "which come next to those"; the reference text has tadvāntararūpāṇi here: what is the word?}. Thus the fourteen worlds; and ⟦विश्वा भुवनानि⟧, all the multitudes of beings too that are in them. ⟦त्रिधातु⟧: the aggregate of three elements is ⟦त्रिधातु⟧. In such a way that it is marked by the triad of elements in the form of earth, water and fire, he ⟦दाधार⟧, held them. The lengthening of the reduplication is because the root belongs to the group beginning with tuj. The meaning is that he produced them. In the Āraṇyaka of the Chandogas, after the creation of the three elements has been stated by ⟦तत्तेजोऽसृजत तदन्नमसृजत ता आप ऐक्षंतेति⟧, "It created fire; it created food; those waters considered", the creation by making threefold is established by ⟦हन्ताहमिमास्तिस्रो देवतास्तासां त्रिवृतं त्रिवृतमेकैकां⟧ ⟦करवाणीत्यादिना⟧, "Come, I, these three deities; of them let me make each one threefold", and what follows (⟦छा. उ. ६-२-३ ; ६-३-३⟧) **NOTE**{the reference for the two quotations stands as ⟦छा. उ. ६-२-३ ; ६-३-३⟧; the reference text gives only Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.3.2-3, after the second: which is right?}. Or else the construction is: he held ⟦त्रिधातु⟧, the three times, or the three guṇas.

## Ṛk 5

In the Ātithyā this Ṛk, ⟦तवस्येत्येषा⟧, is the yājyā, the offering verse, of the principal offering. It is laid down in the section ⟦अथातिथ्येति⟧: ⟦इदं विष्णुर्वि चक्रमे तवस्य प्रियमभि पाथो अश्यां⟧ (⟦आ- ४-५⟧). Of ⟦अस्य⟧, of this great Viṣṇu, the ⟦प्रियं⟧, dear, ⟦तत्⟧, that which is well known as to be resorted to by all, ⟦पाथः⟧ — this is a name of the mid-region, because Yāska has said ⟦साथोऽंतरिक्षं पथा व्याख्यातं⟧ (⟦नि. ६-७⟧), "Pāthas is the mid-region; it is explained by the word for path" — the meaning is, the imperishable world of Brahman: ⟦अश्यां⟧, may I attain it. The same is described further: ⟦यत्र⟧, in which place, the ⟦देवयवो⟧, those who desire for themselves the god, Viṣṇu, whose nature it is to shine, who wish to reach him by sacrifice, giving and the like, the ⟦नरो मदंति⟧, the men experience satisfaction. The construction is: that may I attain. Once more the same is described further. Of ⟦उरुक्रमस्य⟧, of him who strides exceedingly over the whole world, as the self of each thing, and for that very reason of ⟦विष्णोः⟧, of the pervading Supreme Lord, in the ⟦परमे⟧, the excellent, unsurpassed, consisting of pure happiness, ⟦पदे⟧, place, there is an ⟦उत्सः⟧, a flowing forth, of ⟦मध्वो⟧, of the sweet. That may I attain. The meaning is: such a place, where there is no fear of hunger, thirst, old age, death, return and the like, and where enjoyments such as streams of nectar are obtained by the mere wish. He says that there is nothing greater than that: ⟦इत्था⟧, in this way, in the manner stated, ⟦स हि बंधुः⟧, he indeed is the kinsman of all doers of good, or their benefactor, because for those who have reached his place there is no return. He is the kinsman by the passage of the Veda ⟦न च पुनरावर्तत⟧, "And he does not return again". The word ⟦हि⟧ serves to show that this is well known in all the Veda, the smṛtis, the Purāṇas and the rest.

## Ṛk 6

O wife and sacrificer, ⟦वां⟧, for your sake, ⟦ता⟧, those, well known as to be gone to, ⟦वास्तूनि⟧, places fit to dwell in happily, ⟦गमध्यै⟧, for the going of you two, ⟦उष्मसि⟧, we desire. The meaning is: for that we pray to Viṣṇu. "Those" was said: which? He says: ⟦यत्र⟧, in which dwelling-places, the ⟦गावो⟧, the rays, are ⟦भूरिशृंगा⟧, possessed of extreme height, or to be resorted to by many; ⟦अयासः⟧, goers, exceedingly extended. Or else ⟦यासो⟧ means goers, and they are not such; the meaning is that they are possessed of extreme light. ⟦अत्र⟧ ⟦अह⟧, here indeed, in the world of heaven, which is the support of the dwelling-places, of ⟦उरुगायस्य⟧, him who is to be sung by many great-souled ones, who is worthy of praise, of ⟦वृष्णः⟧, the showerer of desires, Viṣṇu, ⟦तत्⟧, that, such as it is, well known everywhere in the Purāṇas and the rest as to be gone to, ⟦परमं⟧, unsurpassed, ⟦पदं⟧, place, ⟦भूरि⟧, exceedingly abundant, ⟦भाति⟧ with ⟦अव⟧, flashes forth by its own greatness. This mantra has been explained by Yāska, who explains that the word go denotes a ray: ⟦तानि वां वास्तूनि कामायामहे गमनाय यत्र⟧ ⟦गावो भूरिशृंगा बहुश्चंगा भूरीति बहुनो नामधेयं प्रभवतीति सतः शृंगं⟧ ⟦श्रयतेर्वा शृणातेर्वा शम्मातेर्वा शरणायोद्गतमिति ना शिरसो निर्गतमिति⟧ ⟦वायासोऽयनाः । तत्र तदुरुगायस्य विष्णोर्महागतेः परमं पदं पराध्यस्थमवभाति भूरि । पादः पद्यतेः⟧ (⟦नि. २-७⟧), "We desire those dwelling-places for you two to go to, where the rays are bhūriśṛṅga, many-horned. Bhūri is a name for 'much': it is said of what prevails. Śṛṅga is from the root śri, or from śṝ, or from śam; or because it has risen up for protection, or because it has come out of the head. Ayāsaḥ: goers. There that highest place of the urugāya, of Viṣṇu of great going, standing in the highest, shines down abundantly. Pāda is from the root pad".
