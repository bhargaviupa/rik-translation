## Introduction

The sūkta beginning ⟦समिद्ध⟧, of thirteen Ṛks, is the third sūkta; it is in the anuṣṭubh metre. Of the first Ṛk the deity is Agni named Samidh, the kindled Agni. Of the second and the following Ṛks, Tanūnapāt, Narāśaṃsa, Iḷa, Barhis, the divine Doors, Dawn and Night, the two divine hotṛs who are wise, the three goddesses Sarasvatī, Iḷā and Bhāratī, Tvaṣṭṛ, Vanaspati and Svāhākṛti: these are the deities in order. Of the last Ṛk the deity is Indra. "Sarasvatī, Iḷā and Bhāratī" is the explanation of "the three goddesses". On this the Anukramaṇikā says: ⟦समिद्द आप्रिय आनुष्टुभमंतैंद्रीति⟧, "The sūkta beginning samiddhaḥ: Āprī verses; in anuṣṭubh; the last belongs to Indra." Here, by the fuel, Tanūnapāt and the rest, which denote the parts of the sacrifice, the sacrifice itself is spoken of; therefore that alone is the deity: this is the opinion of Kātthakya. Agni himself is spoken of by the kindling-stick and the rest; therefore he alone is the deity: this is the opinion of Śākapūṇi. In the Nirukta, after other views have been shown in many ways with ⟦किंदेवताः प्रयाजानुयाजा⟧, "Of what deity are the fore-offerings and the after-offerings?", with ⟦च्छंदोदेवता⟧, "They have the metres as their deities", and so on, and after a brāhmaṇa and the like have been cited for that purpose, it is settled that the other views are explanatory praise and that they belong to Agni alone, in the passage beginning ⟦अथ किंदेवताः प्रयाजानुयाजा⟧, "Now, of what deity are the fore-offerings and the after-offerings?" (⟦नि ८ २१⟧). In the animal sacrifice this is the Āprī sūkta of those born in the family of Aṅgiras. It is laid down in the sūtra, in the section ⟦एकादश प्रयाजा⟧, "eleven fore-offerings": ⟦समिद्दो अद्येति सर्वेषां यथऋषि ना⟧, "The hymn samiddho adya for all; or according to the seer" (⟦आ. ३-२⟧). This is because of the mention of "according to the seer" there. The Āprī sūktas are of three kinds. Among them four, those of Vasiṣṭha, Atri, Vadhryaśva and Gṛtsamada, have Narāśaṃsa. Those of Medhātithi and Dīrghatamas and the one of the praiṣas have both Narāśaṃsa and Tanūnapāt. The others have Tanūnapāt. Among them this sūkta, because it is of Dīrghatamas, has both.

## Ṛk 1

O Agni, ⟦समिद्धः⟧, well kindled, you who bear this name, ⟦अद्य⟧, on this day of sacrifice, ⟦वह⟧ (⟦आ⟧) the ⟦देवान्⟧, the eaters of the oblations, Agni and the rest; that is, make them come to our sacrifice. For ⟦यतस्रुचे⟧, for the sacrificer who has raised the ladle for the sacrifice; for his sake. And, having brought them, for ⟦सुतसोमाय⟧, for him who has pressed the Soma for the sacrifice, for ⟦दाशुषे⟧, for him who has given the oblation — although the gods are being invoked only now, yet with regard to an earlier performance, or with regard to what is to come, he is called ⟦दाशुष⟧; or, for the sacrificer who is a giver of the oblation — for his sake ⟦तनुष्व⟧, extend, the ⟦तंतुं⟧, the sacrifice, which is ⟦पूर्व्यं⟧, good for the ancient gods, or belonging to former time.

## Ṛk 2

O ⟦तनूनपात्⟧. In the view of Kātthakya, ⟦तनूनपादाज्यमिति⟧, "Tanūnapāt is ghee": the ⟦तन्नो⟧ are the cows, because enjoyments arise in them; from them comes milk, from milk ghee; thus ghee is the grandson. The sacrifice possesses it. In the view that Agni is meant, the waters are called ⟦तन्न⟧; from them come the herbs and trees; from those comes Agni; thus he is the grandson. The idea is: from the two araṇis. O Agni who bear this name, of the ⟦विप्रस्य⟧, the wise one, the ⟦शशमानस्य⟧, the one who praises, who lauds you, the ⟦दाशुषो⟧, the one who has given the oblation, the ⟦मावतो⟧, the sacrificer who is like me — the suffix matup **NOTE**{Ṛk 2: the supplied text names the suffix ⟦मतुप्⟧ here, the reference text "vatup"; which is to stand?} is by ⟦युष्मदस्मद्भ्यां छंदसि सादृश्य⟧, "after yuṣmad and asmad, in the Veda, in the sense of likeness"; the ā is by ⟦आ सर्वनाम्न⟧, "ā in place of the final of a pronoun" — the ⟦यज्ञं⟧ that is ⟦घृतवंतं⟧, provided with flowing ghee, and ⟦मधुमंतं⟧, provided with sweetness, possessed of it through a sweet substance such as Soma, having come near to it (⟦उप⟧) you ⟦मासि⟧; that is, you fashion it; or, you become measured. The meaning is that you stay until its completion. The root is ⟦मा माने⟧, "mā in the sense of measuring"; it belongs to the class beginning with ad.

## Ṛk 3

Among the ⟦देवेषु⟧, he is ⟦शुचिः⟧, pure, or radiant; ⟦पावको⟧, the purifier of food that is impure; ⟦अद्भुतः⟧, as though not having been, coming to be at this very moment; the meaning is: a wonder. He is ⟦देवो⟧, given to shining; ⟦यज्ञियो⟧, fit for bringing the sacrifice about. Such a ⟦नराशंसो⟧, Agni who is to be praised by men, by the priests who lead the rite, having come from ⟦दिवः⟧, from the world of heaven, with ⟦मध्वा⟧, with sweet juice, ⟦मिमिक्षति⟧ (⟦आ⟧) our ⟦यज्ञं⟧ ⟦त्रिः⟧; that is, he sprinkles it three times all around; the meaning is that he makes it savoury. Or else: with sweet fruit he ⟦त्रिरा मिमिक्षति⟧; the meaning is that he joins it. In the view of Kātthakya, ⟦नराशंसो यज्ञ⟧, "Narāśaṃsa is the sacrifice": the god who presides over the sacrifice, shining, having come from the world of heaven, ⟦त्रिरा मिमिक्षति⟧ the sacrifice that is being performed. This god Narāśaṃsa ⟦त्रिरा⟧ — by force of the preposition a fitting verb is supplied — comes three times; or, he brings the fruit three times. What remains is to be construed as before.

## Ṛk 4

O Agni, ⟦ईळितः⟧, worthy to be praised by all, you who bear the name Iṭ, ⟦इह⟧, in this sacrifice, ⟦वह⟧ (⟦आ⟧), call, ⟦इंद्रं⟧, who is ⟦चित्रं⟧, worthy of reverence, and ⟦प्रियं⟧, the one who gladdens us, or who is pleased to accept the oblation. O ⟦सुजिह्व⟧, you whose flame is beautiful through the drinking of ghee, ⟦इयं⟧, this, ⟦मम⟧, my, ⟦मतिः⟧, speech in the form of praise, which is a means of thinking, in order to reach ⟦त्वा⟧, you, ⟦अच्छ⟧, facing you, ⟦वच्यते⟧: it is our thought itself that is spoken. The particle hi is an expletive. Before the suffix yak of the root vac, since the single substitution of the preceding vowel by ⟦संप्रसारणाच्चेति⟧, "and after a saṃprasāraṇa", is made optional by ⟦ना छंदसीति⟧, "optionally in the Veda", in its absence there is the substitution of a semivowel. Here the Ṛk may also be explained as referring to the sacrifice that presides over the iḍ, the sacrificial cake and the like. In the same way, in what follows, it is the sacrifice itself that is spoken of by the parts of the sacrifice and by the deities connected with the sacrifice; so it is to be explained as referring to that.

## Ṛk 5

In the ⟦यज्ञे⟧, the Soma sacrifice, which is ⟦स्वध्वरे⟧, possessed of fine subordinate sacrifices such as the prāyaṇīya, ⟦स्तृणानास⟧, spreading out, ⟦बर्हिः⟧, the darbha grass, the ⟦यतस्रुचो⟧, the priests, whose vessels, the juhū and the others, are held in order, ⟦वृंजे⟧ for ⟦इंद्राय⟧ a ⟦शर्म⟧, a means of happiness, a house, that is ⟦देवव्यचःतमं⟧, going to the gods in the highest degree, and ⟦सप्रथः⟧, spread out on all sides; that is, they set it apart; the meaning is that they prepare it.

## Ṛk 6

⟦द्वारो⟧ ⟦देवीः⟧, the deities who preside over the doors of the sacrifice, ⟦वि श्रयंतां⟧; that is, let them resort variously; let them become opened. For what purpose? ⟦देवेभ्यः प्रयै⟧: to go forth, for the arrival of the gods. The word is formed irregularly by ⟦प्रयै रोहिष्यॆ अव्यथिष्यॆ⟧, "prayai, rohiṣyai, avyathiṣyai" (⟦पा. ३-४-१०⟧). Of what kind are they? They are ⟦ऋतवृधो⟧, those who make the sacrifice grow, worthy of worship; ⟦महीः⟧, great; ⟦पावकास⟧, those who purify; ⟦पुरुस्पृहो⟧, much to be longed for; ⟦असश्चतः⟧, not clinging; the meaning is: far apart from one another.

## Ṛk 7

They are ⟦भंदमाने⟧, being praised by all; ⟦उपाके⟧, close to one another; ⟦सुपेशसा⟧, of beautiful form. ⟦पेश⟧ is a name of form. They are ⟦यह्वी⟧, great, or having the form of offspring, because they are born every day. ⟦यहुरित्यपत्यनाम⟧: yahu is a name of offspring, because it is read among its names: ⟦यहुः सूनुरिति⟧, "yahu, sūnu" **NOTE**{Ṛk 7: the reference text adds here the reference "ni. 2. 2. 11", which the supplied text does not have.}. They are ⟦ऋतस्य मातरा⟧, the two makers of the sacrifice. Because the completion of the sacrifice comes from their arrival, the motherhood of the sacrifice is ascribed to them figuratively. Or else, it is because the Atirātra and the others are to be accomplished by day and night. ⟦नक्तोषासा⟧, the deities of day and night, distinguished by the qualities stated, ⟦सुमत्⟧, of their own accord; the meaning is: not asked by us. Or else: ⟦सुमत्⟧; on the ⟦बर्हिः⟧, the strewn darbha grass, ⟦आ⟧, all around, let them ⟦सीदतां⟧ (⟦आ⟧); that is, let them sit down. Or else: ⟦ऋतस्यॆति⟧, the word ṛtasya, is a sixth case in the sense of the object: let them sit at the sacrifice; and, having come to it, let them sit of their own accord on the ⟦बर्हिः⟧, the strewn darbha grass.

## Ṛk 8

They are ⟦मंद्रजिह्वा⟧, having flames that gladden the gods; ⟦जुगुर्वणी⟧, the two who favour the sacrificers that laud, that praise, intensely. From the root gṝ ending in the elision of yaṅ, with the suffix kvip, the form is completed in the Vedic way; with that as the accompanying word, the suffix in is added to the root van by the rule beginning ⟦वनतेश्चंदसि⟧, "in the Veda, of van, san, rakṣ and math". They are ⟦कवी⟧, wise, seeing far; ⟦दैव्या होतारा⟧, the two hotṛs connected with the gods, this one and the one whose place is the middle region, both of them. The ⟦यज्ञं⟧ that is ⟦सिद्ध्रं⟧, being a means to the fruit — the suffix rak is added to the root sidh by the rule beginning ⟦स्थायितंचीत्यादिना⟧ — and ⟦दिविस्पृशं⟧, touching the gods who dwell in the world of heaven; the idea is: because the oblation is accepted by them; such a sacrifice, ⟦नो⟧, ours, ⟦इमं⟧, this one, ⟦अद्य⟧ let them ⟦यक्षतां⟧; that is, let them perform it, or honour it. The meaning is: let them carry it through rightly.

## Ṛk 9

⟦शुचिः⟧, pure; among the ⟦मरुत्सु⟧, that is, by an indication through the contrary, among those who do not die, among the ⟦देवेषु⟧, those given to shining — or, among the praisers, among the ⟦मरुत्सु⟧, the priests — ⟦अर्पिता⟧, fixed; ⟦होत्रा⟧, she who accomplishes the offering; ⟦भारती⟧, the speech that belongs to Bharata, the Sun, and has heaven as its place. Likewise ⟦इळा⟧, the earthly speech, in the form of the praiṣa and the like. And ⟦मही⟧, great, ⟦सरस्वती⟧ — ⟦सर⟧ is a name of water; she is possessed of it — the speech of the middle region, in the form of thunder and the like. These three, the deities who preside over the speech of the three places, being ⟦यज्ञिया⟧, worthy of the sacrifice, let them ⟦सीदंतु⟧, sit down, on the ⟦बर्हिः⟧, the strewn grass.

## Ṛk 10

⟦नो⟧, for our sake; ⟦तत्⟧, such; ⟦तुरीपं⟧, swift and pervading; ⟦अद्भुतं⟧, appearing at once, being as though it had not been. Or else, this is a name of what is great: great in its essence. ⟦पुरु वारं⟧: ⟦अरं⟧, that is alam, capable, indeed for the sake of many living beings. Or else ⟦पुरु वारं⟧ is "much and abundant"; the word vā has the sense of "and"; ⟦अरं⟧ is alam. ⟦त्मना⟧, by itself, ⟦पुरु⟧: this word "much" is an adverb. Such water ⟦त्वष्टा⟧, the maker of rain and the like — the word tvaṣṭṛ is explained by Yāska thus: ⟦तृष्णा तूर्णमश्नुत इति⟧ ⟦नैरुक्तास्विषेर्वा स्याद्वृद्धिकर्मणस्त्वक्षतीर्वा स्यात्करोतिकर्मणः⟧, "Tvaṣṭṛ: 'he pervades quickly', say the etymologists; or it may be from tviṣ, which has the sense of growing; or it may be from tvakṣ, which has the sense of making" (⟦नि. ८-१३⟧) — the fire of lightning that bears this name, the god who is ⟦अस्मयुः⟧, desiring us, for ⟦नः पोषाय⟧, for our nourishment, and for ⟦राये⟧, for prosperity, the water of the character described, which is situated in the ⟦नाभा⟧, that is nābhau, in that which binds the rain, the place of the navel of the cloud, let him ⟦वि ष्यतु⟧; that is, let him make it go in various ways. The root is ⟦षोऽंतकर्मणि⟧, "so in the sense of ending". The elision of the o is by ⟦ओतः श्यनीत्योकारलोपः⟧, "of o before śyan". The ṣ is by ⟦उपसर्गात्सुनोतीति⟧, "after a preposition, of sunoti and the rest". Or else: in ⟦नो⟧ ⟦नाभा⟧, in the navel of the sacrifice that belongs to us, on the uttaravedi; ⟦राये⟧ — the fourth case is in the sense of the sixth — for the ⟦पोषाय⟧, the nourishment, of wealth, cows and the like. The root so with a preposition has the sense of releasing. Let him release rain of the character described.

## Ṛk 11

O ⟦वनस्पते⟧, protector of the forests, god who presides over the sacrificial post, Agni: ⟦अवसृजन्⟧, letting them go freely, setting the priests to work each in his own task, ⟦त्मना⟧, by yourself, ⟦उप यक्षि⟧ the ⟦देवान्⟧, the eaters of the oblation; that is, having come near, sacrifice to them. The elision of śap is by ⟦बहुलं छंदसीति शपो लुक्⟧, "variously in the Veda". The ⟦देवो⟧, the shining one, ⟦मेधिरो⟧, possessed of wisdom, Agni the āhavanīya, among the gods, in the midst of both, ⟦सुसूदति⟧ the ⟦हव्या⟧, the oblations; that is, he sends them on; he accepts them. The root is ⟦षूद क्षरणे⟧, "sūd in the sense of flowing". The elision called ślu of śap is by ⟦बहुलं छंदसीति शपः श्लुः⟧, "variously in the Veda". The augment aṭ is by ⟦लेटोऽडाटावित्यडागमः⟧, "aṭ and āṭ of leṭ". Or else: it is the Agni of the sacrificial post himself who is spoken of in the third person. That Agni accepts the oblations well.

## Ṛk 12

To ⟦पूषण्वते⟧, the one who is possessed of Pūṣan — the augment nuṭ is by ⟦अनो नुडिति⟧, "nuṭ after an"; the suffix matup is udātta by ⟦ह्रस्वनुड्भ्यां मतुबिति⟧, "matup after a short vowel and after nuṭ" — to ⟦मरुत्वते⟧, the one who is possessed of the Maruts; to ⟦विश्वदेवाय⟧, the host of all the gods; and to ⟦वायवे⟧, the one given to going, the god Vāyu; likewise to ⟦गायत्रवेपसे⟧ — ⟦वेप⟧ is a name of form; the word gāyatra is an indication of the other sāmans — to him whose form is the gāyatra; to such an ⟦इंद्राय⟧, in order to give the ⟦हव्यं⟧, the oblation, ⟦स्वाहा कर्तन⟧; that is, make the utterance of svāhā, O priests. In the loṭ of the root kṛ, the substitution of tanap is by ⟦तप्तनप्तनथनाश्चति⟧, "and tap, tanap, tana and thana".

## Ṛk 13

O ⟦इंद्र⟧, ⟦स्वाहाकृतानि⟧: the making, the doing, the uttering, preceded by the word svāhā, for the deities who are about to be sacrificed to — those are the svāhākṛtis; the ⟦हव्यानि⟧, the oblations, that are possessed of them. Here the meaning is: the oblation over which svāhā has been made, because of the giving of the vaṣaṭ-call at the fore-offerings. Having come near (⟦उप⟧) to that oblation, for ⟦वीतये⟧, for eating, ⟦गहि⟧ (⟦आ⟧); that is, come. The elision of śap of the root gam is Vedic. The elision of the nasal is by the rule beginning ⟦अनुदात्तोपदेशेत्यादिनानुनासिकलोपः⟧. Likewise, O ⟦इंद्र⟧, ⟦आ गहि⟧; that is, come with the praises in view. Having come, ⟦श्रुधि⟧, hear, the ⟦हवं⟧, our invocation. Since the priests ⟦हवंते⟧, call, ⟦त्वां⟧ in the ⟦अध्वरे⟧, the sacrifice whose nature is non-injury, therefore hear. The saṃprasāraṇa of the root hve is by ⟦हृऋतेर्बहुलं छंदसीति संप्रसारणं⟧, "variously in the Veda".
