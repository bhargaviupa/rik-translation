## Ṛk 1

⟦होतारं⟧: the invoker of the gods. ⟦विश्वाप्सुं⟧: apsu is a name of form; he is of manifold form, through the division into the terrestrial fire, the fire of lightning, the fire of the belly and so on, or through the division into the Āhavanīya and the others. Or else his having all forms comes from the diversity of his flames in the forms of Kālī, Karālī and the rest. ⟦विश्वदेव्यं⟧: good at the rites that are fit for all the gods. ⟦यदीं⟧: which Agni here ⟦मातरिश्वा⟧, the wind, who breathes in the mid-region that fashions rain and the like, ⟦विष्टः⟧, having entered within the pieces of wood, ⟦मथीत्⟧, churned; the meaning is that he made him grow. The root is math, in the sense of stirring; the vṛddhi is prohibited by the rule "hmyantakṣaṇa".

He is further specified once more. Which Agni the sacrificers formerly ⟦नि दधुः⟧, hold, ⟦वपुषे⟧, for the accomplishment of the sacrifice, ⟦मनुष्यासु⟧, among those who do their work after having thought, ⟦विक्षु⟧, among the people who are the priests. **NOTE**{The supplied text reads yajñasiddhaye, "for the accomplishment of the sacrifice"; the reference text reads yajñaśarīrasiddhaye, "for the accomplishment of the body of the sacrifice".} The word manuṣya is explained in many ways in the Nirukta: ⟦मनुष्याः कस्मान्मत्वा कर्माणि सीव्यंति मनस्यमानेन सृष्टा मनोरपत्यं मनुषो वा⟧, "Why are they called manuṣyāḥ? Having thought, they sew their works; they were created by one who was thinking; or they are the offspring of Manu, or of Manuṣ" (⟦नि. ३-७⟧). Or else: formerly the gods ⟦नि दधुः⟧, placed him, in the form of the fire of the belly, ⟦मनुष्यासु⟧, among those who are the offspring of Manu, ⟦विक्षु⟧, among the people, the living beings, ⟦वपुषे⟧, for their own form, that is, for the manifesting of the proper form of sacrifice and the like, or for the sustaining of the body.

An illustration of this: ⟦स्वर्ण⟧: that which goes well, or that which impels well; like the sun, which is ⟦चित्रं⟧, worthy of reverence, ⟦विभावं⟧, possessed of manifold light: as they hold the sun for the bringing about of light and the like, so. The word svar Yāska has explained thus: ⟦स्वरादित्यो भवति सु अरणः सु ईरणः सृतो रसान्त्सृतो भासं, ज्योतिषां सृतो भासेति वा⟧, "svar is the sun: it goes well; it impels well; it has gone well to the juices; it has gone well to the light of the luminaries; or it is well reached by light" (⟦नि. २-१४⟧). Him whom, being such, they set down, him he churned: thus the construction is with the earlier part.

## Ṛk 2

⟦मन्म⟧: a hymn of praise, which is to be thought upon, or the oblation and the like; ⟦ददानं⟧ ⟦इत्⟧: me, who am indeed making it for Agni, the deceivers, the enemies, ⟦न ददभंत⟧, are not able to deceive, to injure. Of the root dambh, by the rule "bahulaṃ chandasi", there is ślu of the conjugational sign; by an exchange there is the substitution of ant. What is my distinction? It is told. Of ⟦तस्य⟧, such a giver, ⟦मम⟧, of me, the ⟦वरूथं⟧, the praise and the like that is worthy to be chosen, this Agni ⟦चाकन्⟧, desires exceedingly. The root kan has the sense of desiring; from it, ending in the elision of yaṅ, in laṅ, there is the Vedic absence of the augment aṭ. Or else it is Vedic simply by the word "variously"; the lengthening of the reduplication is because the root belongs to the group beginning with tuj. Since this is so, therefore they do not harm: that is the meaning.

Not Agni alone desires, but all the gods desire: this he says. Of me, ⟦अस्य⟧, this sacrificer, ⟦उपस्तुतिं भरमाणस्य⟧, who am making praise, ⟦कारोः⟧, the praiser, ⟦विश्वानि⟧, all the acts, which consist of the giving of the oblation and the like, all the gods ⟦जुषंत⟧. Therefore they do not harm: that is the meaning.

## Ṛk 3

⟦यज्ञियासो⟧: those fit for the sacrifice, the sacrificers or the priests; ⟦यं⟧, which Agni, produced by the churning, they ⟦जगृभ्रे⟧, take, ⟦नु⟧, quickly, ⟦नित्ये⟧ ⟦चित्⟧ ⟦सदने⟧, in the place for setting down the fire which is the Gārhapatya, and which alone is permanent. Of the root grah, in liṭ, there is the substitution of re by the rule "irayo re"; there is bh by the rule "hṛgrahor bhaḥ". From the sūtra "nityaṃ gataśriyo dhriyate nityaṃ gārhapatyam", "for one who has attained fortune it is kept permanently; the Gārhapatya permanently", the Gārhapatya is permanent. Because it is the support of that, the seat too is called permanent. Having taken him, ⟦प्रशस्तिभिः⟧, with praises, with lauds, they ⟦दधिरे⟧, hold him, for the sake of the Āhavanīya. And having held him, ⟦इष्टौ⟧, the sacrifice, which is the means of seeking, being the occasion, the priests, ⟦गृभयंत⟧, ⟦प्र सू नयंत⟧, carry him forward well. An illustration of this: ⟦ररहाणा⟧, going with speed. The root is rah, in the sense of going; there is kānac in place of the Vedic liṭ; by the maxim "anityam āgamaśāsanam", "the teaching of an augment is not invariable", num is absent. ⟦रथ्यो⟧: possessed of chariots, yoked to them. By the rule "chandasīvanipau" there is the vowel ī, in the sense of the possessive suffix, after the word ratha. ⟦अश्वासो⟧: like horses. As they, bearing him, lead the owner of the chariot to the desired place, so.

## Ṛk 4

Here he is praised in the form of the forest-fire. ⟦दस्मः⟧, the destroyer, this Agni, ⟦नि रिणाति⟧, injures utterly, ⟦पुरूणि⟧, the many trees and the like. The root is rī, in the senses of going and injuring; the vowel is short because the root belongs to the group beginning with pū. By what means? That is told: ⟦जंभैः⟧, with his flames, which stand in the place of teeth. ⟦आत्⟧, after the burning, ⟦वने⟧, in that which consists of a mass of trees and the like, being ⟦विभावा⟧, endowed with manifold light, he ⟦रोचते⟧, becomes blazing. ⟦आत्⟧, after that, ⟦वातः⟧, the wind, who is the friend of Agni, in conformity with ⟦अस्य⟧ ⟦शोचिः⟧, his flame, ⟦अनु द्यून्⟧ (the word ⟦अनु⟧ is in the distributive sense), every day, ⟦वाति⟧, blows favourably; he goes taking the flames with him. An illustration of this: ⟦शर्यां न⟧, ⟦असनां⟧, going, from ⟦अस्तुः⟧, the thrower. What is called śara is the head of an arrow, made of iron, a means of injury; the word means her who abounds in that. Or else what is called śara is a particular kind of wood, a subordinate species of bamboo; it is like an arrow that is a modification of that. ⟦शर्या इषवः शरमय्य⟧, "śaryāḥ are arrows, made of śara": so says Yāska (⟦नि- ५-४⟧). Or else śara itself is śaryā. Or śara is injury; that which does it is śaryā, an arrow. As the wind, or the impetus produced by the one who impels it, drives it on favourably, so.

## Ṛk 5

⟦यं⟧, which Agni, ⟦संतं⟧, existing, ⟦गर्भे⟧, in the middle of the fire-stick, which protects like a womb, the ⟦रिपवो⟧, men, do not ⟦रेषयंति⟧, do not cause pain. Likewise the ⟦रिषण्यवः⟧, the other injurers (the word is laid down ready-made before kyac by the rule "durasyur draviṇasyur vṛṣaṇyati riṣaṇyati", ⟦पा. ७-४-३६⟧; by the rule "kyāc chandasi" there is the suffix u), do not injure him. Of what kind are they? ⟦रेषणा⟧: those whose nature is injuring. Or else: the modes of injury that are practised by them do not injure him. Moreover those who are ⟦अंधाः⟧, devoid of the power of knowledge, unknowing, with regard to his ⟦अभिख्या⟧, his renown on all sides, his greatness, and who for that very reason are ⟦अपश्याः⟧, non-seers; or else those who, though knowing, do not meditate on him, the meaning being those who do not worship him: both of these, being of such a kind, ⟦न दभन्⟧, do not harm, do not injure. The purport is: because he is of service even through worldly benefit. If it is asked "then who know his true form?", it is told: ⟦नित्यासो⟧, the constant ones, those of unswerving devotion, or those devoted to the obligatory rites, the Agnihotra and the rest. Or, since by the favour of Agni they themselves too will become eternal, they are figuratively called eternal with reference to their future eternity. Such sacrificers, being ⟦प्रेतारो⟧, those who satisfy him alone with sacrifice and the like, ⟦अरक्षन्⟧, protect, ⟦ईं⟧, him; the meaning is that they serve him in the form of sacrifice and the like.
