## Ṛk 1

Of the ⟦देवस्य⟧, the one given to shining, the Sun, the ⟦दर्शतं⟧, what is to be seen by all, ⟦तत्⟧, that well-known, ⟦भर्गः⟧, lustre, ⟦इत्था⟧, thus, in the manner in which it is seen by all, for ⟦वपुषे⟧, for the functioning of the body, for its firmness, ⟦धायि⟧; that is, it is held by all. ⟦बट्⟧ and ⟦इत्था⟧ are both names of truth, because they are read among its names: ⟦बट् श्रत् सत्रेत्थति⟧, "baṭ, śrat, satrā, itthā" **NOTE**{Ṛk 1: the reference text adds here the reference "ni. 3. 10. 1. 5", which the supplied text does not have.}. That the body is sustained through the digesting of what has been eaten by the fire of the belly is well known. He states the reason for this: ⟦यतः सहसो जनि⟧. Since (⟦यतः⟧) he was born by force at the time of the churning, therefore he is held for the sake of strength: this is the meaning. ⟦यदीं⟧: that which is this well-known lustre of Agni, which benefits the world — to it ⟦मतिः⟧, my mind, ⟦उप⟧ ⟦ह्वरते⟧; that is, it goes near, or, having gone near, it stays. The root is ⟦ह्व कौटिल्ये⟧, "hvṛ in the sense of crookedness". And it ⟦साधते⟧: by that very lustre it accomplishes what it desires. Moreover, by the lustre of ⟦ऋतस्य⟧, of Agni who accomplishes the sacrifice, the ⟦धेनाः⟧, the voices of all living beings, which are ⟦सस्रुतः⟧, going alike, proceeding all together — the suffix kvip is added to the root sru in the sense of the agent — ⟦अनयंत⟧; that is, they lead; they carry on the dealings of the world. The ātmanepada is by transposition. Or else: ⟦ऋतस्य⟧ ⟦धेना⟧, the voices that belong to the sacrifice, in the form of mantras, which are ⟦सस्रुतो⟧, proceeding all together through the mouths of the hotṛ and the others, ⟦अनयंत⟧; that is, they lead. They carry out the praise of Agni. Or, by the lustre of Agni, they carry out the sacrifice; because of the scriptural text ⟦तेजोमयिया वागिति⟧, "Speech is made of lustre" (⟦छां. उ. ६_५_४⟧). For this reason the mind goes near: this is the meaning. ⟦धेनेति⟧: dhenā is a name of speech, because it is read among its names: ⟦धेना ग्ना⟧, "dhenā, gnāḥ" **NOTE**{Ṛk 1: the reference text adds here the reference "ni. 1. 11. 39", which the supplied text does not have.}.

## Ṛk 2

This Agni is ⟦पृक्षः⟧, the one who brings food about, by cooking and the like; ⟦वपुः⟧, possessed of a body, that is, the cause of the growth of the body; ⟦पितुमान्⟧, joined with food characterised as the oblation; ⟦नित्यः⟧, everlasting. Being such, first, having the earth as his place, he ⟦शये⟧ (⟦आ⟧); that is, he lies there. The meaning is that, accomplishing worldly action such as burning and cooking, and Vedic action such as the sacrifice, he stays fixed on the earth. Likewise in the ⟦सप्तशिवासु⟧ ⟦मातृषु⟧, in the rains, which make the seven worlds auspicious, which stand in the place of mothers and do good, he lies a ⟦द्वितीयं⟧, a second time. He exists as having the middle place, or in the form of lightning. For the ⟦दोहसे⟧, for the milker, that is, for the ray of the Sun which makes the water that is in the Sun rain down, and which is connected, as a helper, with ⟦अस्य⟧ ⟦वृषभस्य⟧, with this showerer, this very fire of lightning — the suffix asun is added to the root duh in the sense of the agent — for the activity of that ray he lies a ⟦तृतीयं⟧, a third time. He exists as having heaven as his place. Or else: for the milker, the ray, connected with ⟦अस्य⟧ ⟦वृषभस्य⟧, with this showerer, the well-known Sun; for the sake of its activity. It is well known that the rays of the Sun, in the hot season, draw up the water that is in the earth, convey it to the Sun, hold it there for a long time, then come again to the atmosphere, stay in the clouds, and rain down in the rainy season. Hence the ray is a milker. Such an Agni of three places, greatly blessed, the ⟦योषणो⟧, those given to mingling, all the quarters, ⟦जनयंत⟧; that is, they bring him forth. The meaning is that he is spread out in all the quarters. Of what kind is he? He is ⟦दशप्रमतिं⟧: having eminent thought in the ten quarters, for the sake of being worshipped. Or else: brought forth by the ten fingers at the time of the churning, and pervading the three worlds and the ten quarters: this is the meaning.

## Ṛk 3

⟦ईं⟧ is "him", Agni. ⟦यत्⟧ is "for which reason". ⟦महिषस्य⟧: this is a name of what is great, because it is read among its names: ⟦महिषोऽभ्य ऋभुक्षा⟧, "mahiṣa, abhva, ṛbhukṣāḥ" **NOTE**{Ṛk 3: the reference text adds here the reference "ni. 3. 3. 8", which the supplied text does not have.}. Of the great one, the sacrifice, from its ⟦बुध्नात्⟧, its root, beginning from the establishing of the fire; ⟦वर्पसः⟧: this is a name of form; for the accomplishment of its form, which consists of a beginning and a conclusion; the ⟦ईशानासः⟧, those capable of their own operations, the ⟦सूरयो⟧, the wise priests, ⟦शवसा⟧, by strength, in the form of mantras and the like, or bodily, ⟦निष्क्रंत⟧; that is, they bring him out; they accomplish him; they bring him forth from the wood. Or else: they bring this Agni out, from the ⟦बुध्नात्⟧, from the beginning, starting from the churning, for the accomplishment of ⟦महिषस्य वर्पसो⟧, of the great form which is capable of accepting the offerings. From the root kṛ, in the Vedic luṅ, there is the elision of cli by ⟦मंत्रे घसेति⟧, "in a mantra, after ghas"; the substitution of the ending is by transposition. Moreover ⟦यदीमेनमेवाग्निं⟧: this very Agni, ⟦प्रदिवः⟧, from of old — the meaning is: beginning from time without beginning, not merely now — as formerly, so now, when there is, as the occasion, the ⟦आधव⟧, the pouring in, the throwing in, of ⟦मध्वः⟧, of honey, of what is like honey, him who is ⟦गुहा संतं⟧, being in the cave, on the altar, being hidden in the pieces of wood, ⟦मातरिश्वा⟧, the wind that moves in the atmosphere, in due order (⟦अनु⟧) ⟦मथायति⟧, shakes; the meaning is that he awakens him. Or else: he who breathes, who moves, in the mother (⟦मातरि⟧), in the mother of the fruit, the sacrifice, is ⟦मातरिश्वा⟧, the sacrificer; him who is ⟦गुहा⟧ ⟦संतं⟧, being hidden in the two araṇis, for his becoming manifest, in due order he ⟦मथायति⟧, churns; the meaning is that he brings him forth. Since he is of such a form, therefore he is to be praised: this is the meaning.

## Ṛk 4

⟦यत्⟧ is "because". ⟦पितुः⟧, of the food characterised as the animal — the first case stands in place of the sixth — of that, ⟦परमात्⟧, by reason of its being highest; the meaning is: for the sake of its excellence. For that purpose he ⟦प्र परि णीयते⟧; that is, he is led around excellently; the meaning is that the fire is carried round it. Or else: from the ⟦पितुः⟧, the producer, ⟦परमात्⟧, the excellent one, from the Agni characterised as the gārhapatya, towards the āhavanīya he ⟦प्र परि णीयते⟧, is led forth excellently. The word pari has no meaning. Moreover the ⟦पृक्षुधः⟧, those that are placed in the midst of the burning at the animal offering, the sacrificial cake and the like. Or else: there is saṃprasāraṇa of the word pra **NOTE**{Ṛk 4: the supplied text has ⟦प्रशब्दस्य⟧, "of the word pra", where the reference text has "maśabdasya", "of the word ma"; which is to stand?}; the root is ⟦क्षुध बुभुक्षायां⟧, "kṣudh in the sense of hunger"; the suffix is kvip. They are those greatly hungered for, hungered for exceedingly, desired to be eaten. The ⟦वीरुधो⟧ ⟦रोहति⟧ (⟦आ⟧) in the ⟦दंसु⟧, in the midst of the teeth; that is, they mount; the meaning is that they enter. ⟦यत्⟧, because, ⟦उभा⟧, the two, the Adhvaryu and the sacrificer, or the two araṇis, ⟦इन्वतः⟧, pervade, the ⟦जनुषं⟧, the birth, the origin, of this (⟦अस्य⟧) Agni; the meaning is that they accept it. The root is ⟦इवि व्याप्तौ⟧, "iv in the sense of pervading"; the augment num is because the root has an indicatory i. ⟦आदिदत⟧: for that very reason, ⟦शुचिः⟧, pure, this Agni, by ⟦घृणा⟧, by favour towards the sacrificers — or else ⟦फृणा⟧ is one who is radiant; the possessive sense is elided; or it is "by radiance" — ⟦अभवत्⟧, became, ⟦यविष्ठः⟧, the youngest. The meaning is that he became capable of accepting.

## Ṛk 5

This Agni, being ⟦शुचिः⟧ (⟦आ⟧), radiant, ⟦आदित्⟧ — since it was said in the preceding mantra that he "became the youngest", immediately after that — ⟦आविशत्⟧ the ⟦मात्याः⟧, the ten quarters, which bring him forth and stand in the place of mothers; that is, he enters them on all sides. That the quarters are mothers is because it was said earlier ⟦दशप्रमतिं जनयंत योषण⟧ **NOTE**{Ṛk 5: the reference text adds here the reference "ṛ. saṃ. 1. 141. 2", which the supplied text does not have.}. The meaning is that he pervaded the quarters with light. The mothers are further described. Those mothers ⟦यासु⟧, in which, ⟦अहिंस्यमानः⟧, not oppressed by others, ⟦उर्विया⟧, widely, very abundantly, he ⟦वि ववृधे⟧, became grown in various ways — those he entered. As in the world some man of lustre, well nourished by his mother with the giving of the breast, close embraces and the like, having shone in the world, comes back again and embraces his mother, in that way: this is the idea. At what time? To this he says: ⟦यत्⟧, when, the ⟦सनाजुवो⟧, those long separated from him, thrown on at the very time of his establishing, and for that very reason ⟦पूर्वाः⟧, the plants that come first, in due order (⟦अनु⟧) he ⟦अरुहत्⟧, mounts. It is the Vedic luṅ of the root ruh; aṅ is substituted for cli by ⟦कृमृद्यरुहिभ्यश्चंदसीति⟧, "after kṛ, mṛ, dṛ and ruh, in the Veda". He blazes on top of them. Likewise in the ⟦नव्यसीषु⟧, the newer ones, and for that very reason ⟦अवरासु⟧, for that very reason inferior in comparison with the earlier ones, the plants that come afterwards, he ⟦नि धावते⟧; that is, he goes deeply, or low; the meaning is that he enters inside. When he blazes, having entered below and above the pieces of wood, then he became illumined in the quarters: this is the meaning.

## Ṛk 6

⟦होतारं⟧ is the one who accomplishes the offering, or the invoker of the gods. Such an Agni, at the ⟦दिविष्टिषु⟧, the seekings of the day, which are the occasion, they ⟦वृणते⟧; that is, they resort to him. ⟦आदिदनंतरमेव⟧: immediately afterwards, likewise, the ⟦पपृचानासो⟧, the priests, the sacrificers, who unite him with oblation and praise — the suffix kānac stands for the Vedic liṭ of the root pṛc — ⟦ऋंजते⟧; that is, they adorn him. Like what? ⟦भगमिव⟧: as people adorn him, in that way. Or else: like a great king; the meaning is: worthy of worship like the radiance of the Sun. ⟦ऋंजतिः प्रसाधनकर्मेति⟧, "ṛñjati has the sense of adorning", says Yāska. ⟦यत्⟧, because, this Agni, ⟦पुरुष्टुतो⟧, praised by many, or in many ways, the ⟦देवान्⟧, the priests who carry on the dealings, endowed with ⟦क्रत्वा⟧, that is, with action in the form of sacrifice and the like, and with ⟦मज्मना⟧, bodily strength — or, the gods who partake of the sacrifice, to join them with action and with strength; likewise the ⟦मर्तं⟧, the sacrificer, and the ⟦शंसं⟧, the praise, or him as one worthy of praise; ⟦विश्वधा⟧, having all wealth, or else this one, having the nature of all, ⟦वेति⟧; that is, he desires those mentioned above. For what purpose? For ⟦धायसे⟧, for food characterised as the oblation. Therefore they choose Agni as hotṛ and adorn him.

## Ṛk 7

⟦यत्⟧, when, this Agni, ⟦वातचोदितो⟧, impelled by the wind — the first member keeps its original accent by ⟦तृतीया कर्मणीति⟧, "a word in the third case, before a participle in the sense of the object" — being ⟦यजतः⟧, to be worshipped by all, fit to be worshipped, ⟦व्यस्थात्⟧; that is, he spreads out; he becomes pervading on all sides. For this there is an example. ⟦अनाकृतो⟧, unrestrained, of unimpeded advance, ⟦वक्वा⟧, one who talks much — ⟦वचेरन्येभ्योऽपि दृश्यंत इति वनिप्⟧: the suffix vanip is added to the root vac by the rule "It is seen after others also"; the k is because of the mention of "seen" in that rule — ⟦ह्वारो न⟧, like a crooked man, like a jester and the like — from the causative of the root hvṛ there is the suffix ac of the group beginning with pac — as he ⟦जरणाः⟧, praises, spreads out, that is, goes on making them in various ways, so this one also, ⟦अनाकृतो⟧, of unhindered advance, has taken his stand. Then on the ⟦पत्मन्⟧, the flying, the path, in the form of the sacrifice, of ⟦तस्य⟧, of that Agni, ⟦रजो⟧, the world, whose nature is attachment, ⟦आस्थात्⟧; that is, it takes its stand; it resorts to it on all sides. The meaning is that it sacrifices in the fire in many ways. Of what kind is he? He is ⟦धक्षुषो⟧, burning — the suffix kvasu stands for the liṭ of the root dah — and ⟦कृष्णजंहसः⟧, having a black path: what is gone over again and again is ⟦जंहा⟧, a path; from the root han ending in the elision of yaṅ there is the suffix asun in the sense of the object; the absence of the guttural is a Vedic usage. He is ⟦शुचिजन्मनः⟧, because he is brought forth from the two araṇis accompanied by mantras; and ⟦व्यध्वनो⟧, having various flames, having a path that is not fixed.

## Ṛk 8

This Agni is ⟦रथो न⟧, like a chariot, that is ⟦कृतो⟧, bound, with ⟦शिक्वभी⟧, with ropes. As that moves by means of its own parts, the wheels and the rest, so he, ⟦यातः⟧, when he has been established, with his ⟦अरुषेभिः⟧, those given to going, his ⟦अंगेभिः⟧, his limbs in the form of flames and the like, being gladdened with ghee, ⟦ईयते⟧, goes, to ⟦द्यां⟧, the atmosphere. The root is ⟦ईज् गत्⟧, "ī in the sense of going". ⟦आत्⟧, after he has grown, ⟦ते⟧, those ⟦सूरयः⟧, courses, paths, of this (⟦अस्य⟧) Agni become ⟦कृष्णासः⟧, of black colour. Since they become so, for that purpose he ⟦दक्षि⟧; that is, he burns the pieces of wood. There is a transposition of the person; the elision of śap is Vedic. Moreover from the ⟦त्वेषथात्⟧, from the blazing lustre, of the kindled Agni, the ⟦वयः⟧, those that go, birds, beasts and the like, ⟦ईषते⟧; that is, they go. The root is ⟦ईष गत्यादौ⟧, "īṣ in the sense of going and the rest". The elision of śap is by ⟦बहुलं छंदसीति⟧, "variously in the Veda". They flee. For this there is an example: like those that go from the blazing lustre of a ⟦शूरस्य⟧; as if fleeing in fear from the blazing lustre of some valiant man. Thus, touching the atmosphere, he burns on all sides; so he is praised throughout in the form of the forest fire.

## Ṛk 10

O Agni, ⟦यविष्ठ⟧, youngest one, you, for ⟦शशमानाय⟧, for him who praises you — ⟦शशमानः शंसमान⟧, "śaśamāna is one who praises", says Yāska (⟦नि. ६-८⟧) — for ⟦सुन्वते⟧, for the sacrificer who performs the pressing for your sake, for the sake of his sacrifice, ⟦इन्वसि⟧, pervade, the ⟦रत्नं⟧, the delightful oblation, which is ⟦देवतातिं⟧, being spread, being extended, among the gods. The meaning is that you make the oblation given by us reach the gods. The verb inv has the sense of pervading, because it is read among the names of that action: ⟦इन्वति⟧ ⟦ननक्षेति⟧, "invati, nanakṣa" **NOTE**{Ṛk 10: the reference text adds here the reference "ni. 2. 18. 1", which the supplied text does not have.}. O ⟦सहसो युवन्⟧: by the word yuvan origin is indicated; O you who were brought forth by force at the time of the churning; or, O mixer of strength. Or else: O son of strength; ⟦युवन्⟧, O ever youthful one; ⟦महिरत्न⟧, O you whose great and delightful wealth is to be given to us; ⟦तं⟧, such a one, the eater of the oblation, who is ⟦नव्यं⟧, newer, or worthy of praise, ⟦त्वा⟧, you, ⟦वयं⟧, we the sacrificers, in the ⟦कारे⟧, in the sound characterised as praise, ⟦नु⟧, quickly, ⟦धीमहि⟧; that is, we place. The meaning is that at the time of giving the oblation we praise you very quickly. For this there is an example: ⟦भगं⟧ ⟦न⟧, like the Sun. Or else: like a great king. As people praise him, in that way.

## Ṛk 11

O Agni, in ⟦अस्मे⟧, in us, you — as you ⟦पपृचासि⟧ ⟦रयिं न⟧, as you unite wealth with us, which is ⟦स्वर्थं⟧, well worth going to, and ⟦दमूनसं⟧, to be subdued, to be acquired — the suffix ūnasi is by ⟦दमेरूनसिः⟧, "ūnasi after dam" (⟦उ. ४-२३४⟧) **NOTE**{Ṛk 11: the supplied text gives this reference as 4-234, the reference text as "u. sū. 4. 235"; which number is to stand?} — so also, as you ⟦पपृचासि⟧, unite with us, a son who is ⟦भगं⟧, to be resorted to by all, ⟦दक्षं⟧, energetic, or awakened, and ⟦धर्णसिं⟧, skilled in holding learning and the like. The meaning is that you give both. The root is ⟦पृची संपर्के⟧, "pṛc in the sense of contact". In leṭ there is the augment āṭ. The elision called ślu of the conjugational suffix is by ⟦बहुलं छंदसीति⟧, "variously in the Veda". Now he speaks in the third person. The Agni who (⟦यः⟧), ⟦रश्मीरिव⟧, as he ⟦यमति⟧, controls, spreads out, his own rays, in that way he ⟦यमति⟧, restrains, ⟦उभे⟧ ⟦जन्मनी⟧, both the worlds that are the support of birth, this world and the other world. Likewise he, ⟦सुक्रतुः⟧, one of good actions, in the ⟦ऋते⟧, in our sacrifice, ⟦यमति⟧ (⟦आ⟧) also the ⟦शंसं⟧, the praise, that is ⟦देवानां⟧, connected with the gods; that is, he holds it out; he gives it always. Or else: in the first half also ⟦पपृचासीत्यपि⟧, the word papṛcāsi, is to be construed as a third person, for the sake of the indirect statement.

## Ṛk 12

The word ⟦उत⟧ has the sense of api, and that is in the sense of expectation. That Agni ⟦उत⟧ ⟦शृणवत्⟧, may he indeed hear, ⟦नो⟧, our, invocation. Or else: ⟦नो⟧, us, may he make heard; may he make us known among the gods. In the leṭ of the root śru there is the augment aṭ. Of what kind is he? He is ⟦सुद्योत्मा⟧, shining well — the root is ⟦द्युत दीप्तौ⟧, "dyut in the sense of shining"; the suffix manin is by ⟦अन्वेभ्योऽपि दृश्यंत⟧, "It is seen after others also" — ⟦जीराश्वः⟧, having horses of swift going; ⟦होता⟧, the invoker of the gods; ⟦मंद्रो⟧, given to gladness; ⟦चंद्ररथः⟧, provided with a chariot made of gold. Moreover he, ⟦अमूरः⟧, of unbewildered strength, ⟦वस्यो⟧, fit to be dwelt with, ⟦सः⟧, that well-known ⟦अग्निः⟧, by ⟦नेषतमैः⟧, by actions, or praises, that lead exceedingly, ⟦नेषत्⟧ us also (⟦उत⟧); that is, may he indeed lead us; may he make us reach the far end of the sacrifice. In the leṭ of the root nī there is the augment aṭ; the suffix sip is by ⟦सिब्बहुलमिति⟧, "sip variously". For what purpose? May he lead us, to reach, ⟦अच्छ⟧, facing it, heaven, which is ⟦वामं⟧, desirable, and ⟦सुवितं⟧, well to be reached, longed for by all.

## Ṛk 13

This ⟦अग्निः⟧ ⟦अस्तावि⟧, has been praised by us the sacrificers, with ⟦अर्कैः⟧, with mantras that are the means of worship, which are ⟦शिमावत्भिः⟧, possessed of actions such as the giving of the oblation. ⟦शिमाति⟧: śimī is a name of action, because it is read among its names: ⟦शिमा शक्तिरिति⟧, "śimī, śakti" **NOTE**{Ṛk 13: the reference text adds here the reference "ni. 2. 1. 24", which the supplied text does not have.}. Of what kind is he? ⟦साम्राज्याय⟧: the fourth case stands in place of the sixth case in the sense of the object, "of sovereignty"; he is ⟦दधानः⟧, bearing, the state of shining fully, ⟦प्रतरं⟧, more eminently, exceedingly. The suffix amu is by ⟦अमु च च्छंदसीत्यमुप्रत्ययः⟧, "and amu in the Veda". Moreover, to this Agni ⟦अमी च⟧, both these, the priests who are present before us, or the sons and others, and ⟦ये⟧ ⟦वयं च⟧, we who were formerly well known as praisers, we also, ⟦निष्टतन्युः⟧, exceedingly; that is, let them call aloud. The root stan preceded by nis has the sense of sounding. The meaning is: hereafter also let our sons, grandsons and the rest, and we, praise Agni. For this there is an example: ⟦मिहं न सूरः⟧. As the ⟦सूरः⟧, the Sun, calls aloud to the ⟦मिहं⟧, the cloud that is given to raining, for the sake of rain, in that way. In liṅ there is the Vedic elision called ślu of the conjugational suffix. The ṣ is by ⟦अभिनिस: स्तनः शब्दसंज्ञायां⟧, "of stan after abhi and nis, when it is a name for a sound" (⟦पा. ८-३-८६⟧).
