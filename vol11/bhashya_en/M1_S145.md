## Introduction

The hymn beginning ⟦तं पृच्छत⟧, of five Ṛks, is the fifth hymn. On it the Anukramaṇikā says: "⟦तं पृच्छत⟧: five (Ṛks); the last is a triṣṭubh." Dīrghatamas is the seer. Since it was said earlier "but that is addressed to Agni", this hymn too is addressed to Agni. The last Ṛk is a triṣṭubh. The remaining ones, by the convention about hymns that end in a triṣṭubh, are jagatīs. Its ritual use in the Prātaranuvāka and in the Āśvina-śastra has been stated.

## Ṛk 1

O sacrificers, ask that Agni; whatever there is to be asked concerning heaven and the like, ask that. He states the pre-eminence that lies in his alone being the one to be asked: ⟦स जगाम⟧, he goes everywhere. For that very reason ⟦स वेद⟧: and he knows what is to be asked. Is this in a way common to others? No, he says: ⟦स चिकित्वान्⟧, he alone is possessed of awareness; the meaning is that he is one who knows the particulars. He alone ⟦ईयते⟧, goes to the presence of what is to be known. The going itself is specified once more: ⟦सः⟧, that Agni alone, ⟦नु⟧, quickly, ⟦ईयते⟧, goes. The meaning is: whatever the sacrificer has to know, he goes very speedily to know that matter. Or else: ⟦ईयते⟧, he is resorted to for the sake of knowledge; and in that way he is speedily attended upon. Since he is of the nature described, ask him alone: that is the meaning.

He says that the desired objects of wish are there in him: in that Agni there are ⟦प्रशिषः⟧, commands, powers of controlling. The meaning is that he is able to accomplish even what cannot be accomplished, by controlling it. Moreover, in that Agni alone there are ⟦इष्टयः⟧, enjoyments that are to be wished for, or sacrifices that bring about all fruits. Moreover, that Agni is the ⟦पतिः⟧, the protector, the giver, of ⟦वाजस्य⟧, food, and of ⟦शवसः⟧, strength. Moreover, he is the ⟦पतिः⟧, the master, even of the ⟦शुष्मिणः⟧, the strong one, a king and the like. Since he is of great majesty, therefore, having asked him alone, accomplish the aim of man.

## Ṛk 2

In the preceding mantra it was said "ask Agni". Here, however, he says that he is to be asked with conciliation. ⟦तमित्⟧: that Agni alone they ask; but he does not ask another, even at the time of the question. ⟦सिमः⟧, all people, do not ask (⟦पृच्छति⟧) contrariwise. Even when asked, ⟦धीरः⟧, this wise one, ⟦स्वेनेव मनसा⟧ (the word ⟦इव⟧ has the sense of "only"), by his own understanding alone, ⟦यत्⟧, whatever matter, ⟦अग्रभीत्⟧, he takes up with the thought "I say this", that alone he answers, and not what suits the question. What is said thereby is that he is an independent lord. Or else: the asker, being wise, having considered with his own mind what is to be said by speech, whatever ⟦अग्रभीत्⟧, whatever statement he would accept as the one to be spoken, that alone he asks Agni, and he does not speak much: that is the meaning.

He states that Agni cannot bear the speech of another. This Agni ⟦न मृष्यते⟧, does not endure, ⟦प्रथमं वचः⟧, a speech that comes before his own statement. Likewise ⟦अपरं⟧ ⟦वचः⟧, a reply that comes after his own utterance, he does not endure. Since he is of this kind, therefore ⟦अप्रदृपितः⟧, one who is not arrogant, or else one who has been taught by him and is not insolent, the whole world, ⟦सचते⟧, is united, with the ⟦क्रत्वा⟧, the action, the work of protecting and the like, ⟦अस्य⟧, of this Agni of great majesty; the meaning is that it lives.

## Ṛk 3

⟦जुह्वः⟧: our juhū, upabhṛt and the other ladles, ⟦तमित्⟧, aiming at him alone, being full of ghee, ⟦गच्छंति⟧, go to gratify him. Or else: "they are offered", hence ⟦जुह्वः⟧, the oblations, which consist of Soma and the like. Or else the portions of ghee placed in the juhū and the other ladles are also called ⟦जुह्व⟧, by treating the support and what is supported as not different, as in the expression ⟦मंचाः⟧ and so on, "the platforms cry out". They too go to him alone, because he has all the deities as his nature. The purport is that what is being offered for the sake of another deity too is offered right here. Moreover ⟦अर्वतीः⟧, the praises, which are possessed of reaching, go to ⟦तं⟧ ⟦इत्⟧, that Agni alone, precisely because he has all the deities as his nature. He alone, this Agni, ⟦एकः⟧, ⟦शृणवत्⟧, hears, ⟦विश्वानि⟧, all, ⟦मे⟧, my, ⟦वचांसि⟧, words, which have the form of praise. That Agni has all the deities as his nature the Taittirīyas say: ⟦ते देवा बिभ्यतोऽग्निं प्राविशन् तस्मादाहुरग्निः सर्वा देवता⟧, "Those gods, being afraid, entered Agni; therefore they say: Agni is all the deities" (⟦तै. सं. ६-२-२-६⟧). Likewise it will be recited further on in this very text: ⟦त्वमग्ने वरुणो जायसे यत्त्वं मित्रः⟧, "You, O Agni, are Varuṇa when you are born; you are Mitra" (⟦ऋ. ५-३-१⟧), and so on.

What is his distinction? It is told. ⟦पुरुप्रैषो⟧: he who has a share in many praiṣa directions. Or else: he whose sendings are many; the meaning is that he is the one who commands everyone. ⟦ततुरिः⟧: he who causes to cross. **NOTE**{The supplied text glosses taturiḥ by tarayitā; the reference text has tārayitā.} From the root tṝ, with the sense of the causative included in it, the suffix kin comes by the rule beginning ādṛgamahana; by the rule bahulaṃ chandasi there is the substitution of u. ⟦यज्ञसाधनो⟧: the accomplisher of the sacrifice, because the sacrifice depends on Agni. ⟦अच्छिद्रऊतिः⟧: he whose protection is unbroken. ⟦शिशुः⟧: he who does what is pleasing, like a child; or he who makes the enemies grieve. Agni, being such, ⟦सं रभो⟧, being possessed of zeal for the sacrifice and the like, ⟦आ⟧ ⟦अदत्त⟧, takes: he accepts the oblation and the rest.

## Ṛk 4

⟦यत्⟧, when, the adhvaryu ⟦उपस्थायं⟧ ⟦चरति⟧, approaching again and again performs, that is, carries out the activity that favours Agni's arising. When? He says: ⟦यत् समारत⟧, when he came together, when he became manifest through the churning. The middle voice of the root ṛ here is by the rule on the roots gam and ṛcch after sam. ⟦सद्यः⟧, at that very time, ⟦जातः⟧, being born, ⟦युज्येभिः⟧, being endowed with fruits that are able to be yoked, to be connected, or with lustres that are fit to be mingled, ⟦तत्सार⟧: being hidden in the two fire-sticks, he moved. Or else: with ⟦युज्येभिः⟧, horses, as soon as he was born he moved about everywhere. The root is tsar, in the sense of stealthy going; this is its form in liṭ, the perfect, with the ending ṇal.

Having thus grown, he ⟦अभि⟧ ⟦मृशते⟧, touches, the ⟦श्वांतं⟧, the wearied, or the tranquil, sacrificer, ⟦नांद्ये⟧, the gladdening rite being the occasion, ⟦मुदे⟧, for his contentment; he performs a touching. The purport is: by bestowing the fruit. Just as in the world a teacher or the like touches a wearied pupil or the like, so it is. If it is asked when, it is told: ⟦यत्⟧, when, the streams of ghee, or the praises, ⟦उशतीः⟧, desiring, ⟦गच्छंति⟧, reach, ⟦ईं⟧, this Agni, who is ⟦अपिस्थितं⟧, abiding and pervading, then he touches.

## Ṛk 5

⟦स ईं⟧: that very Agni is ⟦उप⟧ ⟦नि धायि⟧, set down near, for enjoyment afterwards, ⟦उपमस्यां⟧, in that which is a comparison, which is the seat of comparison, ⟦त्वचि⟧, in the altar that is covered with plants and the like. Of what kind is he? ⟦मृगो⟧: he who cleanses, or he whose habit is searching. ⟦मृगो मार्ष्टेर्गतिकर्मणः⟧, "mṛga is from the root mṛj, which has the sense of going" (⟦नि. १-२०⟧): so says Yāska. ⟦अप्यः⟧: he who is to be reached, to be gone to. Or else ⟦अपः⟧ is work, and he is good at that. ⟦वनर्गु⟧: he who goes to the forest. This form is from the roots ṛcch and gam. Or else it is from gam alone, and the augment ruṭ of the preceding word is Vedic. Agni, being such, ⟦व्यब्रवीत्⟧, tells in a special way, teaches, to ⟦मर्त्येभ्यः⟧, to those whose nature is to die, who are the sacrificers and the like, the ⟦वयुनानि⟧, the knowledges, the knowledges of what is to be performed. Vayuna is a name of knowledge, because of the reading ⟦वयुनमभिख्यॆ⟧, "vayuna, abhikhyā", among the names of that. **NOTE**{After this quotation the reference text gives a reference (Nighaṇṭu 3.9.10) which the supplied text does not have.}

He shows that there is such a capability: this Agni is ⟦विद्वान्⟧, all-knowing; ⟦ऋतचित्⟧, the one who is aware of, who knows, the sacrifice, or water; ⟦सत्यः⟧: ⟦सत्⟧ is the rite that is being performed, and he is good at that. Or he is one who exists among the good. Or ⟦सत्⟧ is the fruit, and he is worthy of that. The meaning is that he is the giver of the proper fruit. Since he is endowed with the capability described, therefore the teaching of knowledge is fitting.
