# Rigveda Samhita — Volume 11 (Kannada → English translation)

## Preface (Pīṭhike)

This volume, the eleventh part of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā, contains in all 21 sūktas of the first Maṇḍala, from 137 to 157. Of these, in the first three sūktas, 137 to 139, seen by Paruchchhepa Daivodāsi, Mitrāvaruṇa, Pūṣan and the Viśvedevas are praised; in the remaining sūktas, seen by Dīrghatamas Aucathya, the deities praised are Jātavedas, Agni, Mitrāvaruṇa, Aditi, Viṣṇu, Indrāviṣṇu and the Aśvins. Like the works of Kutsa Āṅgirasa, the works of Paruchchhepa and Dīrghatamas too contain lofty sentiments and are marked by a beautiful style. Both are very famous for their personal power and their skill in composition.

दध्यज् ह मे जनुषं पूर्वे अंगिराः प्रियमेधः कण्वो अत्रिर्मनुर्वि दुस्ते मे पूर्वे मनुर्विदुः । (*dadhyaj ha me januṣaṃ pūrve aṃgirāḥ priyamedhaḥ kaṇvo atrirmanurvi duste me pūrve manurviduḥ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-139-9)

Paruchchhepa here proclaims his own and his lineage's renown: the ancient sages Dadhīci, Aṅgiras, Priyamedha, Kaṇva, Atri, Manu and others know well my father, grandfather and the rest, and the son of Divodāsa. His personal power is praised in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā as supernatural. An occasion showing that he was an excellent knower of mantras is described thus:

नृमेधश्च परुच्छेपश्च ब्रह्मवाद्यमवदेतामस्मिन्नारावाद्र्रेऽग्निं जनयाव यतरो नौ ब्रह्मयानिति नृमेधोभ्यवदत्स धूममजनयत्परुच्छेपोभ्यवदत्तोऽग्निमजनयवृष इत्यब्रवीत्समावद्विद्व कथा त्वमग्निमजीजनो नाहमितिसामिधेनीनामेवाहं वर्ण० वेदेत्यब्रवीत् ॥ (*nṛmedhaśca parucchepaśca brahmavādyamavadetāmasminnārāvādrre 'gniṃ janayāva yataro nau brahmayāniti nṛmedhobhyavadatsa dhūmamajanayatparucchepobhyavadatto 'gnimajanayavṛṣa ityabravītsamāvadvidva kathā tvamagnimajījano nāhamitisāmidhenīnāmevāhaṃ varṇa0 vedetyabravīt ‖*) (Tai. Saṃ. 2-5-8-3 to 4)

When a discussion arose between Nṛmedha and Paruchchhepa as to which of the two knew the secret of the śāstra and who was the true knower of the truth, it was decided that whoever could produce fire from green wood should be regarded as the knower of the śāstra. Then, when Nṛmedha uttered the mantra, only smoke appeared. When Paruchchhepa uttered it, the fire blazed up. Then Nṛmedha said: "Since our knowledge is of one kind, how was it possible for you to kindle the fire that I could not?" Paruchchhepa gave the answer: "I know the secret of this Sāmidhenī; therefore this deed became possible." Thus the Taittirīya Saṃhitā praises him.

### Printed p. ii

Not only through his knowledge of the secret of mantras in this way, but also in the composition of mantras, Paruchchhepa has become famous by displaying his skill in construction. In the making of abhyāsa-vākyas (repeated phrases), which, while expressing an excellent sentiment, give that sentiment a certain force, strength and beauty, Paruchchhepa's distinction is exceptional. Praising this power, the author of the Nirukta says तत्परुच्छेपस्य शीलं (*tatparucchepasya śīlaṃ*) (Ni. 10-42), "that is Paruchchhepa's character", and then, citing the repeated phrase in the Ṛk

प्र तद्वोचेयं भव्यायेंदवे हव्यो न य इषवान्मन्म रेजति रक्षोहा मन्म रेजति ॥ (*pra tadvoceyaṃ bhavyāyeṃdave havyo na ya iṣavānmanma rejati rakṣohā manma rejati ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-129-6)

he has praised Paruchchhepa's style. The Aitareya Brāhmaṇa too describes the style of the seer Paruchchhepa, the nature of his subjects, the application of his mantras and so on (Ai. Brā. 5-10 to 13, khaṇḍas). All this we have explained at length in the introduction to sūkta 127 in part 10 of the Ṛ. Saṃ.

The birth of Dīrghatamas, his series of hardships after birth, his devotion to God even in the extremity of misfortune, how through his great faith and power he obtained the favour of the Aśvins and other deities, and similar matters, are explained in the special topics on the Ṛks 1-147-3 and 1-152-6. Because he was the foremost among praisers and steadfast in ritual action, the epithet "dear to Brahman" is well known for him. Besides this,

दीर्घतमा मामतेयो जुजुर्वान् दशमे युगे । अपामर्थं यतीनां ब्रह्मा भवति सारथिः ॥ (*dīrghatamā māmateyo jujurvān daśame yuge | apāmarthaṃ yatīnāṃ brahmā bhavati sārathiḥ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-158-6)

here he himself describes the nature of the excellent position he obtained. How, after receiving the favour of Agni and the other deities, he praised them is explained in the Bṛhaddevatā by these verses:

स वेदिषद इत्यस्तौच्च तुर्भिर्जातवेदसं । समिद्ध आप्रियोऽ०त्यैंद्री तमित्यग्नेः पराणि षट् । (*sa vediṣada ityastaucca turbhirjātavedasaṃ | samiddha āpriyo '0tyaiṃdrī tamityagneḥ parāṇi ṣaṭ |*) (Bṛ. De. 4-16)**NOTE**

तुष्टाव चैव सूक्ताभ्यां अबोधीत्यश्विनावृषिः । प्रेति द्यावापृथिव्यौ तु पराभ्यामेतदुत्तरं ॥ (*tuṣṭāva caiva sūktābhyāṃ abodhītyaśvināvṛṣiḥ | preti dyāvāpṛthivyau tu parābhyāmetaduttaraṃ ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 4-32)

Since this seer's works are also prominent in the next chapter, and since the maturity of his philosophical knowledge and his power of exposition are manifested there in a special manner, his great qualities will be described there.

### Printed p. iii

In the Mitrāvaruṇa sūkta (1-137) seen by Paruchchhepa there is no description of any special qualities of those deities; there is only an invocation, with praise, to drink the soma.

In sūkta 138 there is a description of Pūṣan's special qualities. In it the epithet आफृणि (*āphṛṇi*) has become a cause of disagreement, between those of the Vedic tradition and modern scholars, in determining Pūṣan's nature. The opinions of Yāska and others, the following of the same by scholars such as Bloomfield, and the giving of differing opinions by some other scholars, have also been set out in the special topics on the fourth Ṛk of sūkta 138 (pp. 33-34).

Not all the epithets that Yāska and others take up to establish that Pūṣan is a solar (Āditya) deity need refer to the Āditya alone; some argue that they can apply to all deities. In putting forward such opposing views too, recent scholars show certain grounds and maintain their side. For example—

यास्ते पूषन्नावो अंतः समुद्रे हिरण्ययीरंतरिक्षे चरंति । ताभिर्यासि दूत्यां सूर्यस्य कामेन कृत श्रव इच्छमानः ॥ (*yāste pūṣannāvo aṃtaḥ samudre hiraṇyayīraṃtarikṣe caraṃti | tābhiryāsi dūtyāṃ sūryasya kāmena kṛta śrava icchamānaḥ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-58-3)

In this Ṛk Pūṣan is regarded as the messenger of the Sun, and from other examples likewise they have shown that very different features are seen in Āditya and Pūṣan. Besides, they argue that in sūkta 8-29 of the Ṛ. Saṃ., when the nature of the Viśvedevas is described, there is no description of a solar quality in respect of Pūṣan; and so on. Arguing thus that Pūṣan is not an Āditya deity as traditionally held, they have shown that he is a deity of herdsmen, who has acquired a distinct character of his own.

The main body of the hymns to Pūṣan is contained in the sixth Maṇḍala of the Ṛgveda, in sūktas 53 to 58. It is known that the seers of the Bhāradvāja lineage made Pūṣan their principal deity. Some scholars call this portion "Pūṣa-Saṃhitā". In other parts of the Saṃhitā too Pūṣan is praised, along with other deities such as Indra and Soma. In the Ṛks Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-55-2, 6-53-9, 6-56-1, 6-55-3 and others, the descriptions कपर्दि (*kapardi*), पशुसाधनी (*paśusādhanī*), अष्टा (*aṣṭā*), करुंभाद् (*karuṃbhād*), अजाश्व (*ajāśva*) and so on all give us a definite picture of Pūṣan. Along with the common qualities shared with other deities, in the sixth Maṇḍala one special aspect of Pūṣan seems to be prominent. It is stated clearly that he is the deity of herdsmen, that through this deity's favour the worshippers' wealth of cattle should increase, and that he is the protector of cattle and the like that have lost their way.

पूषा गा अन्वेतु नः पूषा रक्षत्वर्वतः । पूषा वाजं सनोतु नः ॥ (*pūṣā gā anvetu naḥ pūṣā rakṣatvarvataḥ | pūṣā vājaṃ sanotu naḥ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-54-5)

माकिर्नेशन्माकीं रिषन्माकीं सं शारि केवटे । आधारिष्टाभिरा गहि ॥ (*mākirneśanmākīṃ riṣanmākīṃ saṃ śāri kevaṭe | ādhāriṣṭābhirā gahi ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-54-7)

In these and other ways they pray in various manners that he increase their wealth of cattle. Modern critics conjecture that these descendants of Bharadvāja did not stay settled in one place but moved about again and again, and that in their wanderings they regarded Pūṣan as guide and protector.

In sūkta 139, addressed to Mitrāvaruṇa, some descriptions are profound ideas from the philosophical and spiritual point of view. For example, in Ṛk 139-2, the sentence ऋतादनृतमाददाथे (*ṛtādanṛtamādadāthe*), "you take the anṛta from the ṛta", cannot be explained from an ordinary point of view. There must be a supernatural and profound meaning. Speaking crudely, one can say: ṛtāt—from the Āditya; anṛtam—(the perishable) water; ādadāthe—you take. But it is not easy to say which quality the name ṛta describes in Āditya. Even in the description that ṛta is the eternal path of Āditya, and that ṛta is his source and support (Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-54-6; 7-60-5), the nature of ṛta cannot be explained. Such an inseparable relation, and sometimes identity, is deeper than a merely physical meaning.

In the fifth Ṛk of the same sūkta, मा वां रातिरुपदसत्कदा चनास्मद्रातिः कदा चन। (*mā vāṃ rātirupadasatkadā canāsmadrātiḥ kadā cana|*) shows that between man's gift in the form of oblation and the gift of wealth such as food by the deities there is a relation of mutual dependence. These two, like warp and weft, are the causes of all the business of the world. It teaches that the source of the deities' gift is sacrifice, and the basis of the growth of sacrifice is the deities' favour; and that if the mutual relation of gods and men is to grow continuously and bring welfare to the world, the prescribed ritual action must be performed—this is the universal law.

In conveying the same idea, Dīrghatamas compares the sacrifice to a boat and sets out the doctrine in an ornate style, which is a most excellent matter from the poetic point of view (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-140-12). Stating that the boat of ritual action is the chief means both for gaining worldly wealth and for gaining welfare in the next world, and speaking in one place of the need to increase worldly prosperity and also of the way to escape the sorrow of saṃsāra, shows the greatness of the Vedic teaching. In the prayer अस्माकं वीरान् मघोनः (*asmākaṃ vīrān maghonaḥ*) there is the interest in increasing worldly life,

### Printed p. v

and in the faith expressed in उत नः जनांश्च पारयात् (*uta naḥ janāṃśca pārayāt*) the need of renouncing worldly life, and in the hope expressed in च, या शर्म नावं (*ca, yā śarma nāvaṃ*) the idea of the bliss of Brahmaloka; it is explained how the boat in the form of sacrifice can help both these mutually opposed pursuits, and so it teaches the need of performing sacrifice and other ritual acts. By comparing the officiants such as the ṛtvijs, the performers of the sacrifice, to the oars of a boat, it states their weighty responsibility as the means of the world's welfare.

Sūkta 142, addressed to the Āprī deities, is marked by a very mature style and profound ideas. It is one of the most excellent works of Dīrghatamas. The importance of the Āprī sūkta, the nature of the particular deities, and the lofty mind of the seer have been explained fully in the exposition of the sūkta (pages 222 to 286).

In sūktas 154, 155 and 156, the description of the all-pervading Viṣṇu gives occasion for disagreement in understanding Viṣṇu's nature. Sentences such as पार्थिवानि विममे रजांसि (*pārthivāni vimame rajāṃsi*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-54-1) and यस्कोरुषु त्रिषु विक्रमणेष्टधिक्षियंति भुवनानि विश्वा। (*yaskoruṣu triṣu vikramaṇeṣṭadhikṣiyaṃti bhuvanāni viśvā|*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-154-2) do not give a precise meaning. The author of the Nirukta has stated, with examples, the view of Aurṇavābha and others, who explain त्रिषु विक्रमणेषु (*triṣu vikramaṇeṣu*) as the three states of the Sun: rising, being at the zenith, and setting (page 525-6). To support this view some sentences of the Ṛk Saṃhitā itself can also be taken as authority (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-155-6, 1-164-4). In the Purāṇic descriptions too there are some epithets that favour this view. One may say that the Sudarśana discus reflects the solar orb, the lotus the power of light of the heavenly world that gives life to the world, and the yellow garment masses of radiance (cf. P. O. S. No. 75, P. 99). In the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa also, the description that when Viṣṇu's head was cut off it was transformed into the form of the Sun makes this point clear.

If Viṣṇu's description is interpreted in this way as referring solely to the Āditya deity, then the sentence विष्णोः पदे परमे मध्व उत्सः (*viṣṇoḥ pade parame madhva utsaḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-154-5) does not receive its proper import. This sentence describes the Satyaloka spoken of in the Upaniṣads and elsewhere. It is the Upaniṣads' view that the Āditya's excellent path is only a means to this Satyaloka. In sentences such as सूर्यद्वारेण ते विरजाः प्रयांति यत्रामृतः स पुरुषो ह्यव्ययात्मा । (*sūryadvāreṇa te virajāḥ prayāṃti yatrāmṛtaḥ sa puruṣo hyavyayātmā |*) (Mu. U. 2-11) this highest abode is described as beyond the world of the Sun. Therefore, in the view of some, it must be the abode of the Supreme Person in the form of Viṣṇu. In their opinion there is no doubt that Viṣṇu held a most exalted position. The number of sūktas addressed to this deity being small does not alone justify saying that his rank was lower than that of Indra and other deities. If one examines his descriptions and the like, he is the deity of the Satyaloka, the best of all worlds, and

### Printed p. vi

some have maintained that the same position that is described in the Purāṇas and elsewhere for Viṣṇu, the Puruṣottama, is described in the Ṛgveda too.

Macdonell brings forth other traces of Hindu Vishnu's general character as the 'Preserver in the Veda. Vishnu is beneficent (I-156-5), is innocuous and bountiful (VIII. 25. 12.) and a generous protector (I. 155. 4.) but only such features of an ordinary sun-god cannot adequately account for Vishnu's sudden rise to prominence in Hindu Mytho-logy. Vishnu must have already been a great God among 'extra-Vedic' circles and his later eminence was merely a legitimately recognised come-back, [P. O. S. No. 75. P. 104-5]. Citing the sentences that describe Viṣṇu's hospitality, they argue that these qualities are not possible for the Sun deity alone; therefore these sentences praise the nature of Viṣṇu as the description of the Supreme Person, who is the best among the deities and is described in the Purāṇas and elsewhere as Nārāyaṇa.

The description of Viṣṇu in the sūktas of Dīrghatamas gives room for both these views. But since the sentences of this seer, of supernatural greatness, suggest a very profound meaning, it is not easy to know their precise meaning. Just as Viṣṇu's nature cannot be settled as "this and no other", so too it is laborious to understand rightly the relation of Indra and Viṣṇu. These matters will be discussed later with scriptural authorities.

To publish such a Ṛgveda Saṃhitā—most ancient, most profound, most vast, and the foundation of all the śāstras—in simple Kannada with critical comment is not an easy task. Nevertheless, with the weighty aim that through such a great work all people, scholars and laymen alike, may gain increase of knowledge and become worthy of God's grace, His Highness Śrī Jayachāmarājendra Oḍeyar Bahadur, G.C.B., G.C.S.I., Lord of the throne of Karṇāṭaka, lover of Vedic learning, himself a scholar, beloved of his subjects and devoted to God, is encouraging with his generous patronage the publication of this costly work; this must be called the merit of the whole multitude of the people of Karṇāṭaka. For such a generous-minded Lord, for his family, for the princess (bearing the name of the mantra Gāyatrī, regarded as the foremost of Vedic mantras), and for the whole body of subjects, I pray that the Lord of all, the Veda-Puruṣa, bestow abundantly, beyond even this, all prosperity—long life, health, wealth and the rest, the fruits of this world—and the spiritual knowledge that leads to the highest good.

## Sūkta 137 (Maṇḍala 1)

Om. Śrī. Homage to the Lord of the Gaṇas, Mahāgaṇādhipati. The prefatory verses salute Gajāsana (Gaṇeśa), at whose worship Vāgīśa (Brahmā) and the other gods become successful in all undertakings. They salute Vidyātīrtha-Maheśvara, whose breath is the Vedas and who from the Vedas made the whole world. They state that the Veda is Śiva and Śiva is the Veda, that Sadāśiva is the student of the Veda, and that therefore one should always recite the Veda itself with all effort.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The second adhyāya of the second aṣṭaka now begins. The sūkta "suṣumā yātam" consists of three ṛks and is the fourth sūkta (of this anuvāka). Its seer is Pārucchepa (Paruchchepa). Because each ṛk has sixty syllables it is of the Atiśakvarī metre; the rule of the atyaṣṭi is not applied here, since atiśakvarī is expressly stated. Because the word "tu" is used in the earlier sūkta, its deity, Maitrāvaruṇa (Mitra and Varuṇa), carries over here. Sāyaṇa quotes the Anukramaṇikā: "suṣumā tṛcam atiśākvaram". The text then begins to state the use in the Daśarātrā rite, on the sixth day, at the morning pressing, in the Praüga-śastra; the sentence continues on the next page.

### Printed p. 2

> शस्त्र इदं सूक्तं मित्रावरुणदेवत्यं तृतीयतृचं । सूत्रितं च । स्तीर्णं बर्हिरिति
> तृचौ सुषुमा यातमद्रिभिः । आ. ८-१ । इति ॥ तथास्मिन्नेव मैत्रावरुणस्य प्रस्थितयाज्यायाः पुरस्तादाद्या प्रक्षेपणीया । षष्ठस्य प्रातःसवन इति खंडे सूत्रितं ।
> सुषुमा यातमद्रिभिः । आ. ८-१ । इति ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

This sūkta, addressed to Mitra and Varuṇa, is used as the third tṛca of the Mitrāvaruṇa recitation. It is prescribed by the sūtra "stīrṇaṃ barhiḥ iti tṛcau suṣumā yātam adribhiḥ" (Ā. 8-1). In the same rite its first ṛk is to be inserted before the Maitrāvaruṇa's prasthita-yājyā; this is prescribed in the section "ṣaṣṭhasya prātaḥsavane" by "suṣumā yātam adribhiḥ" (Ā. 8-1).

##### Anuvāda

Since the first adhyāya of the second aṣṭaka has ended, we now begin the second adhyāya. This sūkta "suṣumā yātaṃ" is the fourth sūkta in the twentieth anuvāka of the first maṇḍala. It has three ṛks. Its seer is Paruchchepa, son of Divodāsa. Since each ṛk has sixty syllables, the ṛks of this sūkta are of the Atiśakvarī metre. Since the Anukramaṇikā uses the word "tu" when naming the deity, Mitra and Varuṇa are the deity here too, as in the preceding sūkta. Since the Anukramaṇikā says "suṣumā tṛcam atiśākvaram", this sūkta of three ṛks is to be known as of the Atiśakvarī metre. In the sacrifice of ten days called Daśarātra, on the sixth day at the morning pressing, when the Praüga-śastra mantras are recited, this sūkta is applied to the recitation of the third tṛca (a tṛca is three ṛks) addressed to Mitra and Varuṇa; this is explained by the sūtra of the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra "stīrṇaṃ barhiḥ iti tṛcau suṣumāyātam adribhiḥ" (Ā. 8. 1). And in this same sacrifice the first ṛk of this sūkta is applied to the prasthita-yājyā mantras relating to Maitrāvaruṇa; this is shown by the sūtra "suṣumā yātam adribhiḥ" in the section "ṣaṣṭhasya prātassavane" of the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra (Ā. 8-1).

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

"O Mitra and Varuṇa, come: come to our sacrifice." If asked what reason there is for coming, he answers: "adribhiḥ", with the pressing instruments, the stones (grāvan), "suṣuma", we have pressed Soma (a perfect-tense form of su). Hence the Soma is "gośrītāḥ": the word prakṛti stands for the modification, so "mixed with milk"; the Brāhmaṇa says "tasmān maitrāvaruṇaṃ payasā śrīṇāti". "Matsarāḥ": Soma, causes of exhilaration; Yāska says "matsaraḥ somo mandateḥ tṛptikarmaṇaḥ" (Ni. 2-5)**NOTE**. "Ime": these are taken up and stand before us; therefore "come". They are again qualified: "ime somāso matsarāḥ", causes of intoxication, or "possessed of rivalry" in comparison with the Soma of other sacrifices**NOTE**. Of what kind are you two? "Rājānā": shining by nature; "divispṛśā": dwellers in heaven. Among us ("asmatrā") or as our protectors, you two come to "naḥ", our sacrifice. Further, O Mitra and Varuṇa, for your sake these Soma juices are "gavāśiraḥ": as before, the prakṛti stands for the modification, mixed with "go", milk; and again "gavāśiraḥ", mixed with "go", waters; or else mixed with mantras of praise (gobhiḥ). Hence "śukrāḥ", bright. Śruti: "āśiram ava nayati saśukratvāya" (Tai. Saṃ. 6-1-5)**NOTE**.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

अद्रिभिः—अभिषवसाधनैर्ग्रावभिः (*adribhiḥ—abhiṣavasādhanairgrāvabhiḥ*): it means "with the stones that are the instruments for pressing the Soma juice". Although the two words adri and grāva both have in common use the general meaning "stone", it may be said from their descriptions and epithets that the adri and the grāva that serve in pressing the Soma must differ in their very form. Whenever the word adri occurs, Sāyaṇa generally explains it as grāva, and whenever the word grāva occurs, as pāṣāṇa ("stone"). But this difference in meaning is not the basis for showing their difference. From the epithets that describe their form, from the comparisons, and from the sound-indicating words they make while pressing the Soma juice, it is understood that these two instruments are different.

### Printed p. 5
Babṛhāṇa (5-41-12); parvata (10-94-1); aśramaṇa, aśṛthita, amṛtyu, anātura, ajara, amaviṣṇu (10-94-11), dhruva, kṣemakāma (10-94-12): all these are epithets showing the distinctive quality of the adri. In the same way epithets such as pṛthu and pṛthubudhna show the distinctive quality of the grāva alone. The grāva is generally compared to a bull and the adri to a horse. It was said earlier, with the Nirukta explanations, that the word adri is read both among the names of cloud and among the names of the thunderbolt. In ṛks such as शुष्म इयर्ति प्रभृतो मे अद्रिः (*śuṣma iyarti prabhṛto me adriḥ*) (1-165-4) it is understood as the thunderbolt that devours enemies, and in ṛks such as उत पद्मा रथानामद्रिं (*uta padmā rathānāmadriṃ*) (5-52-9) as cloud or mountain.

गोश्रीताः—पयोभिर्मिश्रिताः (*gośrītāḥ—payobhirmiśritāḥ*): Soma juice mixed with milk. Sentences such as तस्मान्मैत्रावरुणं पयसा श्रीणाति (*tasmānmaitrāvaruṇaṃ payasā śrīṇāti*) (Tai. Saṃ. 6-4-8-1) say that mixing milk into the Soma juice to be offered to Mitra and Varuṇa is a rule of the śāstra. The occasion on which the offering of milk-mixed Soma juice to Mitra and Varuṇa, the presiding deities of day and night, was ordained, and the account of these deities, are told in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā:

मित्रं देवा अब्रुवन्सोमग्ं राजानग्ं हनामेति सोऽब्रवीन्नाहग्ं सर्वस्य वा आहं मित्रमस्मीति तमब्रुवन् हनामैवेति सोऽब्रवीद्वरं वृणै पयसैव मे सोमग्ग् श्रीणन्नि ति तस्मान्मैत्रावरुणं पयसा श्रीणंति । (*mitraṃ devā abruvansomagṃ rājānagṃ hanāmeti so 'bravīnnāhagṃ sarvasya vā āhaṃ mitramasmīti tamabruvan hanāmaiveti so 'bravīdvaraṃ vṛṇai payasaiva me somagg śrīṇanni ti tasmānmaitrāvaruṇaṃ payasā śrīṇaṃti |*) (Tai. Saṃ. 6-4-8)

The gods said to Mitra, "Let us slay Soma, the king." Mitra said that he was a friend to all, and therefore he alone would not agree. But when the gods insisted that they must slay him, Mitra asked as a boon that milk be mixed with the Soma and offered; so the gods mixed milk into the Soma offered to Mitra and Varuṇa. Such is the occasion that is told.

गवाशिरः (*gavāśiraḥ*): the details and use of the āśira substances to be added to the Soma juice are told at length in Ṛgveda Saṃhitā Part 1, page 676, and in Part 2, pages 172 to 174. In this ṛk the word is repeated. For the first word Sāyaṇa explains "gobhiḥ kṣīraiḥ miśritāḥ" (mixed with cows' milk), and for the second "gobhiḥ udakaiḥ miśritāḥ" (mixed with waters), or else "stutilakṣaṇair mantraiḥ" (with mantras of the nature of praise).

### Printed p. 6
मत्सराः (*matsarāḥ*): this word too is repeated. He has explained it once as "causing intoxication" (madahetu) and the other time as "possessed of rivalry" (mātsaryavantaḥ). The Nirukta authors also give both meanings of this word. मत्सरः सोमो मंदतेस्तृप्तिकर्मणः (*matsaraḥ somo maṃdatestṛptikarmaṇaḥ*) (Ni. 2-25): derived from the root mand, which gives the sense "to cause satisfaction", it is used as a synonym of Soma juice. And the Nirukta authors' explanation of the word is: मत्सर इति लोभनाम (*matsara iti lobhanāma*), "the name matsara, since it shows excessive desire and self-assertion". In the present ṛk the second word matsara means "excellent, vying with the Soma juices of other sacrifices".

> गृहीता वर्तंते आत आ यातं ! किंच दध्याशिरो धीयमानाशिरः सोमाः सुतासो

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Sāyaṇa addresses Mitra and Varuṇa: "come." For you these Soma juices are ready, made to moisten and flowing, mixed with curds (दध्याशिरः (*dadhyāśiraḥ*), "having curd as their admixture") and held before you in the cup (graha); therefore come. The Soma juices are also pressed (सुतासः (*sutāsaḥ*)) with the pressing stones, to the accompaniment of the mantra. "Come" is to be supplied. The word "mixed with curds" is used with the Āditya-graha in view, where curds are added. "Also, for your protection, at the awakening of the dawn" (बुधि (*budhi*), the locative of बुध् (*budh*), awakening, belonging to the dawn), and not only at dawn's awakening but also "together with the rays of the sun", that is, when the light has appeared along with the sun's rays, for the giving of the oblation, the Soma is pressed. He then adds the connection: it is pressed for Mitra, the god presiding over the day, benevolent to all, and for Varuṇa, the "encloser", the god presiding over the night. "Pressed" is to be supplied. Further, for ऋताय (*ṛtāya*), that is, for the sacrifice, for its accomplishment, it is pressed for drinking (पीतये (*pītaye*)); for the gods' Soma-drinking is for the completion of the sacrifice. Or else, पीतये (*pītaye*) is taken as the agent form: for the one who drinks, the priest and the like, for the sacrifice, for its accomplishment. चारुः (*cāruḥ*) is that which is to be partaken of, the Soma that is pressed.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

इंदवः (*iṃdavaḥ*)—उंदनशीलाः, सर्वं जगदमृतमयेन क्लेदयितारः । (*uṃdanaśīlāḥ, sarvaṃ jagadamṛtamayena kledayitāraḥ |*) The meaning is: Soma juices that satisfy the whole world, that is, gods, men and the rest, with the juice that is of the nature of nectar.
दध्याशिरः (*dadhyāśiraḥ*)—दधाश्रयणवंतः । (*dadhāśrayaṇavaṃtaḥ |*) Soma juice mixed with curds. The matter of दध्याशिर (*dadhyāśira*) has also been explained in detail in the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā, Part 1, page 676, and Part 2, pages 172 to 174.
ऋताय (*ṛtāya*)—Here the word ऋत (*ṛta*) means sacrifice. ऋताय (*ṛtāya*) means यज्ञाय (*yajñāya*) and पीतये (*pītaye*) means तन्निर्वाहकाय पानाय । (*tannirvāhakāya pānāya |*): the prayer is that the Soma juice, which accomplishes the sacrifice, has been pressed and made ready for drinking, therefore come. The same prayer occurs in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā, which contains the Ṛk Saṃhitā verse Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-41-4, in the passage
अयं नां मित्रावरुणा सुतस्सोम ऋतावृधा। (*ayaṃ nāṃ mitrāvaruṇā sutassoma ṛtāvṛdhā|*) ममेदिह श्रुतं ग्ं हवं । (*mamediha śrutaṃ gṃ havaṃ |*)
उपयाम गृहीतोऽसि मित्रावरुणाभ्यां त्वैष ते योनिर्यतायुभ्यां त्वा । (*upayāma gṛhīto 'si mitrāvaruṇābhyāṃ tvaiṣa te yoniryatāyubhyāṃ tvā |*) (Tai. Saṃ. 1-4-5)
and there too the commentary explains the word ऋतायुभ्यां (*ṛtāyubhyāṃ*) as ऋतं सत्यं यज्ञं वा आत्मन यजमानानां ना इच्छतीति ऋतायुः । (*ṛtaṃ satyaṃ yajñaṃ vā ātmana yajamānānāṃ nā icchatīti ṛtāyuḥ |*), that is, one who desires the sacrifice to be an accessory, whether for himself or for the sacrificers; hence "Ṛtāyu".

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

वां (*vāṃ*) means "belonging to you two". The adhvaryus prepare the Soma, which is the अंशु (*aṃśu*), of the form of a creeper. **NOTE**. "Like what?" Like that well-known वासरी (*vāsarī*) (cow) meant for the Agnihotra and the rest, whose milk is covered over every limb, that is, which has abundant milk. Or else, वासरी (*vāsarī*) is one who covers over with milk and the like: from the root वस (*vasa*) in the sense of covering, with the Uṇādi affix अरि (*ari*), and, because it ends in a kṛt-ending i other than ktin, the feminine suffix जीष् (*jīṣ*). Or else, simply the Uṇādi affix अर (*ara*), with अण् (*aṇ*) added in the same sense. धेनुं न (*dhenuṃ na*) means "like a cow, which gratifies". "By what means?" He says: "with the stones" (अद्रिभिः (*adribhiḥ*)), the pressing implements. And further, with those very implements, they milk the Soma for you alone. They milk out the juice, drenching with water the Soma of creeper form, for you alone. Further, अर्वांचा (*arvāṃcā*): "facing us"; for the drinking of Soma, you two, our protectors (अस्मत्रा (*asmatrā*), "those who protect us"), come (गंतं (*gaṃtaṃ*)) to our (नः (*naḥ*)) sacrifice (उप (*upa*)). Further, this Soma has been pressed for you by the leaders (नृभिः (*nṛbhiḥ*)), the priests who carry the sacrifice through. Further, आ पीतये (*ā pītaye*): it has been pressed for complete drinking. Therefore come; that is the meaning.

Thus, although the principal meaning of the word अंशु (*aṃśu*) is Soma juice, in some places it also means the Soma creeper.
अस्मत्र (*asmatra*)—"you who are our protectors": the protection given by Mitra and Varuṇa is praised. Just as epithets such as विश्वस्य भुवनस्य गोपाः (*viśvasya bhuvanasya gopāḥ*) (2-27-4, 1-62-9) describe the protection of the world that Mitra and Varuṇa carry out by day and night, so this epithet too states that aspect.

## Sūkta 138 (Maṇḍala 1)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> प्रप्र पूष्ण इति चतुर्ऋचं पंचमं सूक्तं सारुच्छेपमात्यष्टं पूषदेव
> ताकं । प्रप्र चतुष्कं पौष्णमित्यनुक्रांतं ॥ विनियोगो लैंगिकः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

This is the fifth sūkta, of four ṛks, addressed to Pūṣan as deity. Its seer is given in the printed Sanskrit as सारुच्छेपमात्यष्टं (*sārucchepamātyaṣṭaṃ*) **NOTE**. The Anukramaṇī says प्रप्र चतुष्कं पौष्णं (*prapra catuṣkaṃ pauṣṇaṃ*) ("the four beginning prapra are Pūṣan's"). The use (viniyoga) is लैंगिकः (*laiṃgikaḥ*), i.e. according to the indicative character of the hymn.

##### Anuvāda

This sūkta beginning "prapra pūṣṇaḥ" is the fifth sūkta in the twentieth anuvāka; it has four ṛks. Its seer is Paruchchhepa, son of Divodāsa; the deity is Pūṣan; the metre is Atyaṣṭi. In the Anukramaṇī it is stated: "the four beginning prapra are Pūṣan's". The use of the sūkta is laiṅgika.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

…it does not cease; every person praises him, because he nourishes the world. For this reason I, the sacrificer, desiring happiness (सुम्नयन् (*sumnayan*)), praise him. How is he? अंत्यूतिं (*aṃtyūtiṃ*) is "one whose protection is near", that is, one who gives a boon immediately after the praise. मयोभुवं (*mayobhuvaṃ*) is the producer, or the one who brings about, happiness. He, Pūṣan, is मखः (*makhaḥ*), "possessing sacrifice" (the possessive suffix is elided). Of all, of the praising ṛtvik, he mixes together (आयुयुवे (*āyuyuve*)) the mind completely, since he quickly grants boons. Further, this shining god mixes the sacrifice (मखं (*makhaṃ*)) up to its completion. Or else: if it is asked what the mixing of everyone's mind has to do with the sacrificer, the answer is that this Pūṣan, who brings the sacrifice to its conclusion, joins the mind of the sacrificer completely; by the sense, "the sacrificer's mind" is understood. The form आयुयुवे (*āyuyuve*) is a Vedic perfect.

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

The greatness of the strength of this god Pūṣan, born for many sacrificers, is praised exceedingly. No one, without faith, stops this praise; all are bound to praise. Therefore I too, desiring happiness, worship and praise Pūṣan, the very near protector and bringer of happiness. Pūṣan, as one who has the sacrifice, joins well together the minds of all the ṛtviks by the grace of his boon. The shining Pūṣan himself remains united until the sacrifice is complete

##### Printed English

The greatness of the strength of the many-worshipped Pushan is universally lauded: no one detracts (from his praise); his praise displeases no one. Desirous of happiness, I adore him, whose protection is ever nigh; who is the source of felicity: who, when devoutly worshipped, blends with the thoughts of all (his worshippers): who, (though) a deity, is united with the sacrifice.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

पूष्णः (*pūṣṇaḥ*)—पूषा यद्रश्मिपोषं पुष्यति तत् पूषा भवति (*pūṣā yadraśmipoṣaṃ puṣyati tat pūṣā bhavati*) (Ni. 12-16). The Nirukta's etymology is that he is called Pūṣan because he nourishes with his rays. In the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa also:
इयं वै पूषा पूषेयं हीवं सत्वं पुष्यति यदिदं किंच । (*iyaṃ vai pūṣā pūṣeyaṃ hīvaṃ satvaṃ puṣyati yadidaṃ kiṃca |*) (Śa. Brā. 14-4-2-25) **NOTE**
It is said that this deity is called Pūṣan because he nourishes everything. This kind of meaning supports the view that Pūṣan is one of the twelve Ādityas.
पुष्यन् क्षितिं पोषयति प्रणुदन् रश्मिभिस्तमः । तेनैनमस्तौत्रूषेति भरद्याजस्तु पंचभिः ॥ (*puṣyan kṣitiṃ poṣayati praṇudan raśmibhistamaḥ | tenainamastautrūṣeti bharadyājastu paṃcabhiḥ ‖*) (Pu. 96-97) **NOTE**
The Bṛhaddevatā statement is that the name Pūṣan arises because, destroying the darkness with his rays, he nourishes the earth so that it grows; the seer Bharadvāja praised this god in five sūktas of the sixth maṇḍala, 53, 54, 55, 56 and 58.

तुविजातस्य (*tuvijātasya*)—The word तुवि (*tuvi*) is read among the words for "many" (Ni. 3-17). Sāyaṇa has explained this word as बहुयजमानार्थमुत्पन्नस्य (*bahuyajamānārthamutpannasya*): born for the sake of many sacrificers who perform sacrifice (said of Pūṣan). The epithet tuvijāta is an epithet also of other deities such as Indra, Agni and Varuṇa. In many contexts the meaning also differs. Since two or three kinds of analysis of the compound are possible, the meaning suited to each context has to be stated. For example:
अहन्नहिं परिशयानमर्ण ओजायमानं तुविजात तव्यान् । (*ahannahiṃ pariśayānamarṇa ojāyamānaṃ tuvijāta tavyān |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-32-11)

### Printed p. 18
In this ṛk it is an epithet of Indra. बहूनि जातानि पृथिव्यादीनि यस्मात् सोऽयं तुविजातः । (*bahūni jātāni pṛthivyādīni yasmāt so 'yaṃ tuvijātaḥ |*): he has explained it as the Indra from whom many beings, the earth and the rest, were born.
नमः पुरा ते वरुणोत नूनमुतापरं तुविजात ब्रनाम । (*namaḥ purā te varuṇota nūnamutāparaṃ tuvijāta branāma |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-28-8)
प्र मित्रयोर्वरुणयोः स्तोमो न एतु शूष्यः । नमस्वान्तु विजातयोः । (*pra mitrayorvaruṇayoḥ stomo na etu śūṣyaḥ | namasvāntu vijātayoḥ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-66-1) **NOTE**
In such ṛks it is an epithet of Mitra and Varuṇa, explained as बहुभावमापन्नयोः (*bahubhāvamāpannayoḥ*): those who have manifested having taken many kinds of forms.
वि षाह्यग्ने गृणते मनीषां खं वेपसा तुविजात स्तवानः । (*vi ṣāhyagne gṛṇate manīṣāṃ khaṃ vepasā tuvijāta stavānaḥ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-11-2)
Here, describing the nature of Agni and addressing him, it is explained as बहुजन्मन् अग्ने (*bahujanman agne*): O Agni who arises in different forms such as lightning. In the same way, in many places it describes the nature of Indra and other deities. The authors of the Bṛhaddevatā have indicated by the word tuvijāta a separate deity. They, keeping in view the ṛk
शृणोतु मित्रो अर्यमा भगो न स्तुविजातो वरुणो दक्षो अंशः । (*śṛṇotu mitro aryamā bhago na stuvijāto varuṇo dakṣo aṃśaḥ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-27-1)
have included the deity called Tuvijāta in the group of Mitra and others.
Sāyaṇa has explained this word as बहुषु प्रदेशेषु अनुग्रहार्थं प्रादुर्भूतः (*bahuṣu pradeśeṣu anugrahārthaṃ prādurbhūtaḥ*), "one who has appeared in many places for the sake of favour", and says that it is an epithet of Varuṇa. But the authors of the Bṛhaddevatā, following the meaning of the Nirukta authors, बहुजातश्च धाता (*bahujātaśca dhātā*), "Dhātṛ, born in many ways" (Ni. 12-36),
मित्रावरुणदक्षांशतुविजातभगार्यण्णां । अदित्यानामिमाः सूक्तमिदं नारुणमुच्यते ॥ (*mitrāvaruṇadakṣāṃśatuvijātabhagāryaṇṇāṃ | adityānāmimāḥ sūktamidaṃ nāruṇamucyate ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 4-82, 83)
have indicated by this statement that he is Dhātṛ, one of the seven Ādityas.
Like tuvijāta there are also uses of the words tuviṣmān, tuvirādhaḥ, tuvimanyu, tuvidyumna and tuvinṛmṇa.
अहं ह्युंग्रस्तविषस्तुविष्णान्विश्वस्य शत्रोरनमं वधस्मैः । (*ahaṃ hyuṃgrastaviṣastuviṣṇānviśvasya śatroranamaṃ vadhasmaiḥ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-165-6)

Here tuviṣmān means exceedingly strong. In the same way, tuvidyumna (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-88-3) means abundant wealth; tuvinṛmṇa (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-43-7) means one endowed with abundant strength; tuviṣṭaṇaḥ (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-166-1) means one possessing abundant sound. In the present ṛk, as said above, it states the quality of Pūṣan as one who has manifested in many ways in order to favour the sacrificers.
अंत्यूतिं (*aṃtyūtiṃ*)—आसन्नरक्षणं (*āsannarakṣaṇaṃ*): the meaning is a protector very near. Like the epithets पशुपाः (*paśupāḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-58-2) and puṣṭiṃbhara, the epithet antyūti also tells of Pūṣan's way of protecting.
For the sentence मखः (*makhaḥ*) … विश्वस्य मनः आयुयुवे (*viśvasya manaḥ āyuyuve*), two constructions and corresponding meanings are possible, as was shown when the word-meanings were given.
आयुयुवे (*āyuyuve*)—समंतात् संपूर्तिपर्यंतं मिश्रयति (*samaṃtāt saṃpūrtiparyaṃtaṃ miśrayati*): he joins together until the work is completely accomplished without obstacle; this is the sense with reference to the sacrifice. And यजमानस्य मनः समंतात् आयुयुवे (*yajamānasya manaḥ samaṃtāt āyuyuve*): he makes the sacrificer's mind not wander elsewhere but remain fixed on the sacrifice; thus with reference to the word "mind" also two meanings are possible.
सुन्मयन् (*sunmayan*)—the word sumna is read among the words for happiness (3-13). सुखमिच्छन् (*sukhamicchan*): the meaning is, desiring happiness.
मन आयुयुवे मखो देव आयुयुवे मखः (*mana āyuyuve makho deva āyuyuve makhaḥ*)—here there is a repetition. This is a kind of style. In this repetition an intensity of feeling is seen; the Nirukta authors have thus supported the excellence of such a style:
अभ्यासे भूयांसमर्थं मन्यंते यथाहो दर्श नीयाहो दर्शनीयेति । (*abhyāse bhūyāṃsamarthaṃ manyaṃte yathāho darśa nīyāho darśanīyeti |*) (Ni. 10-42)
By repetition a kind of force and power is shown in the development of the sense. In the example "she is beautiful, she is most beautiful", an exceeding regard is seen in describing her beauty. In the same way there are many occasions where, when expressing an excellent feeling, sentences are recited showing exceeding regard for it. Among them, the Nirukta authors have praised the style of the seer Paruchchhepa, तत्परुच्छेपस्य शीलं (*tatparucchepasya śīlaṃ*) (Ni. 10-42), as a distinctive quality of Paruchchhepa; and
प्र तद्वोचेयं भव्यायेंदवे हव्यो न य इषवान्मन्म रेजति रक्षोहा मन्म रेजति । (*pra tadvoceyaṃ bhavyāyeṃdave havyo na ya iṣavānmanma rejati rakṣohā manma rejati |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-129-6)

### Printed p. 20
Citing the repetition found in the Ṛk (quoted above), (the Nirukta authors) have shown it. In the same way here too, in the repetition आयुयुवे मखो देव आयुयुवे मखः । (*āyuyuve makho deva āyuyuve makhaḥ |*) there is a kind of poetic beauty in the style of the repetition.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Sāyaṇa addresses Pūṣan: "O पूषन् (*pūṣan*), you who move swiftly (अजिरं (*ajiraṃ*)) towards the place of sacrifice — when your coming (यामनि (*yāmani*)) is the occasion, I (कृण्वै (*kṛṇvai*), "I make") exalt you with praises (स्तोमेभिः (*stomebhiḥ*))." The illustration: as a swift-going horse is praised in its swift course, so is he. Further, "as you go (ऋणवः (*ṛṇavaḥ*)) towards battles (मृधः (*mṛdhaḥ*)), so I exalt you"; or else, "as you come to our sacrifice, so I praise you" (ऋण (*ṛṇa*) is the root of going, of the Tanādi class). Further, "you carry us across (पीपरः (*pīparaḥ*)) the battle against the harming warriors" — the illustration being उष्टो न (*uṣṭo na*): as a camel carries a load across, so you bring us to the far shore of the battle. For this reason I, a mortal, a man subject to death, call (हुवे (*huve*)) you, मयोभुवं (*mayobhuvaṃ*) (the causer of happiness) and देवं (*devaṃ*) (one of shining nature), Pūṣan, for friendship (सख्याय (*sakhyāya*)), that is, for the sake of making (you) dear and of common renown. Thus invoked, make our आंगूषान् (*āṃgūṣān*), our loudly recited praises, द्युम्मिनः (*dyumminaḥ*), "possessed of brilliance" — or else, make our praises possessed of fame and food: being pleased with our (Soma) pressing, do so. Further, in the battles (वाजेषु (*vājeṣu*)) make us possessed of food: conquering our enemies, put their food in me. He quotes Yāska: अंगूषः स्तोम आघोष इति यास्कः । (*aṃgūṣaḥ stoma āghoṣa iti yāskaḥ |*) **NOTE**

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O god Pūṣan, as one praises a horse going swiftly towards battle, I proclaim and praise you, who come towards the sacrifice, with hymns on account of your coming. As a camel carries a heavy load and reaches the end of the road, so you too, destroying our enemies, bring us safely out of battle. Therefore I, a man, call you, the causer of happiness and of divine nature, for the sake of making (you) dear. Accept our hymns and make us possessed of food. In the battles let us obtain the food of our enemies.

##### Printed English

I exalt you, Pushan, with praises, that you may hasten ( to the sacrifice), like a rapid ( courser ) to the battle : that you may bear us across the combat, like a camel ; therefore do I a mortal, invoke you, the divine bestower of happiness, for your friendship ; and do thou render our invocations productive (of benefit) ; render them productive (of success) in battles.

कया नो अग्ने वि वसः सुवृक्तिं कामु स्वधामृणवः शस्यमानः । (*kayā no agne vi vasaḥ suvṛktiṃ kāmu svadhāmṛṇavaḥ śasyamānaḥ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-8-3)
the word ऋणवः (*ṛṇavaḥ*) has the sense "you attain"; so here too यथा मृधः ऋणवः (*yathā mṛdhaḥ ṛṇavaḥ*), गच्छेः (*gaccheḥ*); or यज्ञं प्रति गच्छेः (*yajñaṃ prati gaccheḥ*): the meaning is "become one who comes and reaches the place of sacrifice".
अजिरं (*ajiraṃ*)—The word अजिर (*ajira*) is read among the words for "quick" (Ni. 3-9). It means one who moves swiftly. Since Agni spreads quickly, his brilliance increases with that speed. Thus in the phrase गिरा वाजिरशोचिषं (*girā vājiraśociṣaṃ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-19-13) Agni is described as अजिरशोचिष्क (*ajiraśociṣka*), "of swift brilliance", that is, as shining because he goes swiftly. Likewise it is used as an epithet of a swift-moving enemy, of a swift-moving horse, and of a deity who goes swiftly towards the sacrifice. In the present Ṛk it is an epithet of Pūṣan, who comes swiftly towards the sacrifice. Giving a simile for his going, अजिरं न (*ajiraṃ na*)—शीघ्रगामिनमश्वमिव (*śīghragāminamaśvamiva*), "like a swift-going horse"—

मृधः (*mṛdhaḥ*)—The word मृध (*mṛdha*) is read among the words for battle (Ni. 3-9). It means war. But in some places it also means the enemy who causes battle. The Nirukta author, citing the Ṛk

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Pūṣan, you are famed as protector. When there is friendship with you, that is, benefit done, क्रत्वा चित् (*kratvā cit*) means "by the excellent rite alone". By the rule जसादिषु छंदसि वावचनम् (*jasādiṣu chaṃdasi vāvacanam*) the form with nā-ending is optional here. संतः (*saṃtaḥ*) are those who please you, and विपन्यवः (*vipanyavaḥ*) are those who are especially given to praise.
Or else: the wise sacrificers, protected (अवसा (*avasā*)) by the protection you give, enjoy (बुभुज्रिरे (*bubhujrire*)) enjoyments. इति (*iti*) means "just so, always": by the rite they enjoy; or else all are protected. Having followed that new (or praiseworthy) protection, after the protection we ask you for riches of this great number (नियुतं (*niyutaṃ*)).
Further, O Uruśaṃsa, praised in many ways, being unangry and well-disposed towards us, be सरी (*sarī*), one to be approached by us. सरी (*sarī*) is formed from the root sṛ with the Uṇādi affix ini, and has initial acute accent. Further, in every battle be सरी (*sarī*), one who goes, that is, be the one who goes in front.

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Pūṣan, because of your beneficial friendship, you who are the protector, wise sacrificers who worship you by excellent sacrificial rites alone and praise you with devotion enjoy all enjoyments through your protection. After obtaining this protection, which is the fruit of new praise, we pray for countless riches. O Pūṣan, worthy of praise of many kinds, not angry with us but well-disposed, let us approach you without fear. In all battles be our foremost one and our leader.

Through your praise, and assiduous in your worship they enjoy (abundance), through your protection ; by (assiduous) worship they enjoy (abundance) ; as consequent upon your recent favour, we solicit infinite riches. Free from anger, and entitled to ample praise, be ever accessseble to us : be our leader in every encounter.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

विपन्यवः (*vipanyavaḥ*)—This word is read among the names of the wise, विप्रः (*vipraḥ*), विग्रः (*vigraḥ*) and so on (Ni. 3-19). In the Ṛksaṃhitā the word is used in the sense of praisers who are wise and devout: तद्विप्रासो विपन्यवः (*tadviprāso vipanyavaḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-23-21), अवसा विपन्यवः (*avasā vipanyavaḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-102-5), विपन्यवोदीध्यतो मनीषा (*vipanyavodīdhyato manīṣā*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-20-1) and so on. As is understood from these Ṛks, it means विशेषेण स्तोतारः (*viśeṣeṇa stotāraḥ*), those who praise exceedingly.
नियुतं (*niyutaṃ*)—A countless number. For this word one number is stated in one place, another elsewhere. In तक्षद्वज्रं नियुतं (*takṣadvajraṃ niyutaṃ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-12-3) the word नियुत (*niyuta*) is also understood as नितरां युक्तं (*nitarāṃ yuktaṃ*), firmly attached. In this Ṛk it denotes a number.
अहेळमानः (*aheḷamānaḥ*)—Since रेळते (*reḷate*), हेळते (*heḷate*) and so on are read among the names of anger (Ni. 3-9), हेळमानः (*heḷamānaḥ*) means "being angry" and आहेळमानः (*āheḷamānaḥ*) means "not being angry".
अहेळमानो वरुणेह बोध्युरुशंस मा न आयुः प्र मोषीः (*aheḷamāno varuṇeha bodhyuruśaṃsa mā na āyuḥ pra moṣīḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-24-11)

### Printed p. 29
अहेळमान उप याहि यज्ञं तुभ्यं पवंत इंदवः सुतासः (*aheḷamāna upa yāhi yajñaṃ tubhyaṃ pavaṃta iṃdavaḥ sutāsaḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-41-1)
In these and other Ṛks, and likewise in places such as 10-32-8, there is this kind of prayer. Because it was believed that gods such as Agni and Varuṇa are severe in punishing wrongdoers, and that such people do not obtain the favour of those gods, all sacrificers, full of great fear of sin, pray that the gods not be angry, that they not neglect them, and that they bestow favour on them and do them good.
सरी भव (*sarī bhava*)—The word सरी (*sarī*), formed from the root sṛ, which has the sense of going, is used here in two senses. First, सरीभव (*sarībhava*) means अस्माभिर्गंतव्यो भव (*asmābhirgaṃtavyo bhava*), "let us approach you without fear". Second, गमनशीलो भव (*gamanaśīlo bhava*), पुरतो गत्वा भव (*purato gatvā bhava*): "go as our foremost one".
In the same way the words सरीमणि (*sarīmaṇi*), सरीसृप (*sarīsṛpa*) and so on are formed. In present usage सरीसृप (*sarīsṛpa*) means a snake that moves swiftly. But the word सरीसृप (*sarīsṛpa*) is used in the Ṛk यस्ते हंति पतयंतं निषत्सुं यः सरीसृपं (*yaste haṃti patayaṃtaṃ niṣatsuṃ yaḥ sarīsṛpaṃ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-162-3) in the sense of a moving embryo. Here सरीभव (*sarībhava*) means "be our foremost one, our leader".

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Ajāśva Pūṣan. "Ajāśva" is said of Pūṣan, says Yāska (Ni. 4-25). नोऽस्माकमस्या (*no 'smākamasyā*): "of us, of this", the genitive standing for the dative. सातये (*sātaye*) means "for gain". Being unangry, not showing disregard, and a giver (ररिवान् (*rarivān*)), be (भुवः (*bhuvaḥ*)) well near us. ररिवान् (*rarivān*) is the Vedic perfect participle in -kvasu of the root rā. Even where the condition of being a pada is absent, a Vedic division is seen in *papivāṃsaṃ tasthivāṃsaṃ* and the like. ऊ (*ū*) is a verse-filler.
Further, O Ajāśva, you whose horses are goats (अजा एवाश्वस्थानीया (*ajā evāśvasthānīyā*))—this is a name of Pūṣan—be also a giver towards us who desire food (श्रवस्यतां (*śravasyatāṃ*)). "Goats belong to Pūṣan," says Yāska. ओ (*o*) is one particle made up of two particles.
O Dasma, destroyer of enemies, Pūṣan, we turn you (ववृतीमहि (*vavṛtīmahi*)) wholly towards us, completely. The root vṛt has the causative sense inside it. The sense is: we turn you back from going to another sacrifice. By what means? That is said: *sādhubhiḥ stomebhiḥ*, by hymns able to please you.
Further, O Pūṣan, आघृणे (*āghṛṇe*), you who let rain pour on all sides; or else, you who bring and accept offerings: I do not disregard (अतिमन्ये (*atimanye*)) you, I do not show contempt. Not merely do I not transgress; I do not take away (अपह्नुवे (*apahnuve*)), do not abandon, your friendship, your benefit-doing; I proclaim it everywhere. This is the sense.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

अजाश्व (*ajāśva*)—हरी इंद्रस्य । रोहितोऽग्नेः । अजाः पूषः (*harī iṃdrasya | rohito 'gneḥ | ajāḥ pūṣaḥ*) (Ni. 2-28): thus the Nirukta authors state in order the vehicles of Indra and the others. The goat is Pūṣan's vehicle.
Explaining this Ṛk, the Nirukta authors say अस्यॆ नः सातय उपभव, आहेळमानोऽक्रुध्यन्रिवानातिरभ्यस्तोऽजाश्वेति पूषणमाहाजाश्वाजा अजनाः (*asyè naḥ sātaya upabhava, āheḷamāno 'krudhyanrivānātirabhyasto 'jāśveti pūṣaṇamāhājāśvājā ajanāḥ*). They indicate that the verse prays: "O Pūṣan, whose horses are in the form of goats, not angry, bestowing your liberal gift, be right near us", and that the seer of the sūkta addressed Pūṣan as Ajāśva. Showing the derivation of the word ररिवान् (*rarivān*), they say रातिरभ्यस्तः (*rātirabhyastaḥ*): the root rā, of giving, reduplicated, that is doubled, has become ररिवान् (*rarivān*); they also show that it means one who gives liberally (Ni. 4-25).
दस्म (*dasma*)—शत्रूणामुपक्षपयितः (*śatrūṇāmupakṣapayitaḥ*). Pūṣan is addressed as one who destroys enemies. The word दस्म (*dasma*) has two kinds of meaning: one meaning is destroyer of enemies, and beautiful

The Kannada continues Sāyaṇa's discussion of the word दस्म (*dasma*) as an epithet of Pūṣan. The word has two meanings: "destroyer of enemies", and also "one of beautiful form". If it is formed from the root दसि दर्शनदंशनयोः (*dasi darśanadaṃśanayoḥ*) it means "worth seeing, charming"; if from the root दसु-उपक्षये (*dasu-upakṣaye*) it means "destroyer (of enemies)". Since Indra and the other deities possess both kinds of power, either meaning may be given to दस्म (*dasma*). Examples:

जुजोषदिंद्रो वस्यवर्चा नासत्येव सुग्यो रथेष्ठाः (*jujoṣadiṃdro vasyavarcā nāsatyeva sugyo ratheṣṭhāḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-133-4)

त्वं भगो न आ हि रत्नमिषे परिज्जीव क्षयसि वस्यवर्चाः (*tvaṃ bhago na ā hi ratnamiṣe parijjīva kṣayasi vasyavarcāḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-13-2)

पूषा सुबंधुर्दिन आ प्रथिद्या इळस्पतिर्मघना दस्यवर्चाः (*pūṣā subaṃdhurdina ā prathidyā iḷaspatirmaghanā dasyavarcāḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-58-4)

In such Ṛks the first is shown to mean शत्रूपक्षयणबलः (*śatrūpakṣayaṇabalaḥ*), "one whose power destroys enemies"; the second, वर्च इति रूपनाम । दस्मं दर्शनीयं वर्चः यस्य तादृशः (*varca iti rūpanāma | dasmaṃ darśanīyaṃ varcaḥ yasya tādṛśaḥ*), means "one of charming form". As the epithet दस्म (*dasma*) is applied in many places in the Ṛksaṃhitā to Indra and other deities, so in the present Ṛk, as an epithet of Pūṣan, it describes his power of destroying enemies and his beautiful form.

आघ्रणे (*āghraṇe*): सर्वतो वृष्टेः क्षारयितः यद्वा हविषामाहर्तः स्वीकर्तः (*sarvato vṛṣṭeḥ kṣārayitaḥ yadvā haviṣāmāhartaḥ svīkartaḥ*) — Sāyaṇa explains that Pūṣan is addressed as one who pours rain all around, or as one who receives the oblations. That Pūṣan has this epithet is a cause of debate among critics. His qualities are of many kinds and show different aspects of him, so it is hard to say definitely what his original nature is; for recent critics it is a knotty question. Yāska counts Pūṣan among the twelve Ādityas. The scholar Bloomfield, following Yāska, holds on the basis of the epithet आघ्रणि (*āghraṇi*) in Ṛks such as Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-48-16, 6-53-3, 6-55-1 that Pūṣan is an Āditya deity who makes the light shine. Some oppose this view: the word आघ्रणि (*āghraṇi*) relates merely to radiance, the epithet may be used of any radiant deity, there is no rule that it must be the Āditya; for example it is also an epithet of Agni (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-60-20), so Pūṣan cannot be fixed as an Āditya deity by this one epithet. Yāska, the author of the Bṛhaddevatā and other followers of the Vedic tradition have shown clearly that आघ्रणि (*āghraṇi*) is an epithet showing only Pūṣan's distinctive character.

Yāska, when giving the derivation of this word in the Nirukta, explains आफृणिरागतहृणिः (*āphṛṇirāgatahṛṇiḥ*) and cites the word as it occurs in the Ṛk

एहि नां विमुचो नपादाघृणे सं सचावहै । रथीर्यतस्य नो भव ॥ (*ehi nāṃ vimuco napādāghṛṇe saṃ sacāvahai | rathīryatasya no bhava ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-55-1)

But in saying आगतहृणिः (*āgatahṛṇiḥ*) he gives no exact meaning of the word हृणि (*hṛṇi*). It is read among words for radiance (Ni. 2-28) and also among names of anger (Ni. 3-9). Therefore Durgācārya, commenting, says आफृणिशब्दो दीप्तिवाचकः । दीप्तिकर्मसु च पठित एवायं हृणिः इति (*āphṛṇiśabdo dīptivācakaḥ | dīptikarmasu ca paṭhita evāyaṃ hṛṇiḥ iti*): since हृणि (*hṛṇi*) is read among words of radiance, one meaning is "shining"; and क्रोधोऽपि हृणिरित्युच्यते (*krodho 'pi hṛṇirityucyate*): since हृणि (*hṛṇi*) also means anger, another meaning is possible: "let us serve that Pūṣan whose anger is directed upon us". In sum, it is an epithet showing Pūṣan's distinctive character. The author of the Bṛhaddevatā, in

तृचो विश्वेषां देवानां पूष्ण आफृणये तृचः । आसक्तो हि फृणिस्तस्य दध्नः पूर्णो दृती रथे ॥ आफृणिस्तत्सुतः पूषा कीरिभी रिभ्यते ततः । (*tṛco viśveṣāṃ devānāṃ pūṣṇa āphṛṇaye tṛcaḥ | āsakto hi phṛṇistasya dadhnaḥ pūrṇo dṛtī rathe ‖ āphṛṇistatsutaḥ pūṣā kīribhī ribhyate tataḥ |*) (Bṛ. De. 3-95-96)

shows that, because a leather bag filled with curds (an intoxicating substance) is tied to his chariot, he is called आफृणि (*āphṛṇi*), and that for this reason the poet-singers praised Pūṣan as आफृणि (*āphṛṇi*). Sāyaṇa gives the meaning वृष्टेः क्षारयितः (*vṛṣṭeḥ kṣārayitaḥ*) or हविषामाहर्तः (*haviṣāmāhartaḥ*) only in the present Ṛk; in all the Ṛks where the word occurs (3-62-7; 6-48-16; 6-55-3; 7-40-6; 8-4-17; 9-67-12; 10-17-5) he explains it as आगत दीप्ते पूषन् (*āgata dīpte pūṣan*). Only at one place, Ṛk 8-60-20, is the epithet applied to Agni; everywhere else it is an epithet of Pūṣan and shows his distinctive character.

## Sūkta 139 (Maṇḍala 1)
Anuvāda: This sūkta beginning अस्तु शौषट् (*astu śauṣaṭ*) is the sixth sūkta in the twentieth anuvāka; it has eleven Ṛks. The Anukramaṇikā says: अस्तु शौषळेकादश वैश्वदेवी मैत्रावरुण्याश्विन्यस्तिस्र ऐंद्राग्नेयो मारुत्यैंद्राग्नी बार्हस्पत्या वैश्वदेव्यंत्या त्रिष्टुप्पंचमी बृहती वैश्वदेवमेतदेवमन्यासामपि सूक्तप्रयोगे वैश्वदेवत्वं सूक्तभेदप्रयोगे यल्लिंगं सा देवता (*astu śauṣaḷekādaśa vaiśvadevī maitrāvaruṇyāśvinyastisra aiṃdrāgneyo mārutyaiṃdrāgnī bārhaspatyā vaiśvadevyaṃtyā triṣṭuppaṃcamī bṛhatī vaiśvadevametadevamanyāsāmapi sūktaprayoge vaiśvadevatvaṃ sūktabhedaprayoge yalliṃgaṃ sā devatā*). The seer of this sūkta is Paruchchepa. The last Ṛk is in Triṣṭubh, the fifth is Bṛhatī, the remaining Ṛks are in the metre Atyaṣṭi.

### Printed p. 37
Taking the whole sūkta together, the Viśve Devāḥ are the deity. Taken separately: the first Ṛk has the Viśve Devāḥ; the second, Mitra and Varuṇa; the next three Ṛks are addressed to the Aśvins; the sixth to Indra; the seventh to Agni; the eighth to the Maruts; the ninth to Indra and Agni; the tenth to Bṛhaspati; the eleventh to the Viśve Devāḥ. Although the whole sūkta is addressed to the Viśve Devāḥ, separate applications may be stated for the separate Ṛks in relation to their respective deities. In the sacrifice called Daśarātra, performed over ten days, on the sixth day when the Praüga-śastra mantras are recited, the first Ṛk of this sūkta is applied in reciting the triplet addressed to the Viśve Devāḥ; this is stated in the Āśvalāyana Śrautasūtra, in the section beginning षष्ठस्य (*ṣaṣṭhasya*), by the sūtra अस्तु शौषळो शू णो अग्ने (*astu śauṣaḷo śū ṇo agne*).

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

"I have placed in front, on the northern altar, the fire called Āhavanīya, with the rite of leading forward and the like (धिया (*dhiyā*)). I choose quickly, facing it, that mighty (fire), or else the Maruts' own divine power, born in heaven (तच्छर्धः (*tacchardhaḥ*) either way). And we also choose Indra and Vāyu. Because (यत् ह (*yat ha*)) a newer, praise-form speech, making its own meaning shine, is composed at the navel of the earth, the altar-place of the gods' sacrifice, in this radiant (rite) — or at the navel, the sacrifice that links all fruits, by the Śruti यज्ञमाहुर्भुवनस्य नाभिमिति (*yajñamāhurbhuvanasya nābhimiti*) — it is applied by us (संदायि (*saṃdāyi*) is aorist passive, Vedic). Since praise is made, let there be hearing (अस्तु शौषट् (*astu śauṣaṭ*)) of this praise; or let the host of Maruts, or Agni, be the hearer. On the Indra–Vāyu view the singular is used with regard to each singly. After the placing, may our acts (धीतयः (*dhītayaḥ*)) of praise and the like go forth to you completely and well; and like (those wishing) to reach the gods Agni and the rest face to face, may our acts go to them and reach their vicinity."
