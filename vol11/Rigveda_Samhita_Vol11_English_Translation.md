# Rigveda Samhita — Volume 11 (Kannada → English translation)

## Preface (Pīṭhike)

This volume, the eleventh part of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā, contains in all 21 sūktas of the first Maṇḍala, from 137 to 157. Of these, in the first three sūktas, 137 to 139, seen by Paruchchhepa Daivodāsi, Mitrāvaruṇa, Pūṣan and the Viśvedevas are praised; in the remaining sūktas, seen by Dīrghatamas Aucathya, the deities praised are Jātavedas, Agni, Mitrāvaruṇa, Aditi, Viṣṇu, Indrāviṣṇu and the Aśvins. Like the works of Kutsa Āṅgirasa, the works of Paruchchhepa and Dīrghatamas too contain lofty sentiments and are marked by a beautiful style. Both are very famous for their personal power and their skill in composition.

दध्यज् ह मे जनुषं पूर्वे अंगिराः प्रियमेधः कण्वो अत्रिर्मनुर्वि दुस्ते मे पूर्वे मनुर्विदुः । (*dadhyaj ha me januṣaṃ pūrve aṃgirāḥ priyamedhaḥ kaṇvo atrirmanurvi duste me pūrve manurviduḥ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-139-9)

Paruchchhepa here proclaims his own and his lineage's renown: the ancient sages Dadhīci, Aṅgiras, Priyamedha, Kaṇva, Atri, Manu and others know well my father, grandfather and the rest, and the son of Divodāsa. His personal power is praised in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā as supernatural. An occasion showing that he was an excellent knower of mantras is described thus:

नृमेधश्च परुच्छेपश्च ब्रह्मवाद्यमवदेतामस्मिन्नारावाद्र्रेऽग्निं जनयाव यतरो नौ ब्रह्मयानिति नृमेधोभ्यवदत्स धूममजनयत्परुच्छेपोभ्यवदत्तोऽग्निमजनयवृष इत्यब्रवीत्समावद्विद्व कथा त्वमग्निमजीजनो नाहमितिसामिधेनीनामेवाहं वर्ण० वेदेत्यब्रवीत् ॥ (*nṛmedhaśca parucchepaśca brahmavādyamavadetāmasminnārāvādrre 'gniṃ janayāva yataro nau brahmayāniti nṛmedhobhyavadatsa dhūmamajanayatparucchepobhyavadatto 'gnimajanayavṛṣa ityabravītsamāvadvidva kathā tvamagnimajījano nāhamitisāmidhenīnāmevāhaṃ varṇa0 vedetyabravīt ‖*) (Tai. Saṃ. 2-5-8-3 to 4)

When a discussion arose between Nṛmedha and Paruchchhepa as to which of the two knew the secret of the śāstra and who was the true knower of the truth, it was decided that whoever could produce fire from green wood should be regarded as the knower of the śāstra. Then, when Nṛmedha uttered the mantra, only smoke appeared. When Paruchchhepa uttered it, the fire blazed up. Then Nṛmedha said: "Since our knowledge is of one kind, how was it possible for you to kindle the fire that I could not?" Paruchchhepa gave the answer: "I know the secret of this Sāmidhenī; therefore this deed became possible." Thus the Taittirīya Saṃhitā praises him.

Not only through his knowledge of the secret of mantras in this way, but also in the composition of mantras, Paruchchhepa has become famous by displaying his skill in construction. In the making of abhyāsa-vākyas (repeated phrases), which, while expressing an excellent sentiment, give that sentiment a certain force, strength and beauty, Paruchchhepa's distinction is exceptional. Praising this power, the author of the Nirukta says तत्परुच्छेपस्य शीलं (*tatparucchepasya śīlaṃ*) (Ni. 10-42), "that is Paruchchhepa's character", and then, citing the repeated phrase in the Ṛk

प्र तद्वोचेयं भव्यायेंदवे हव्यो न य इषवान्मन्म रेजति रक्षोहा मन्म रेजति ॥ (*pra tadvoceyaṃ bhavyāyeṃdave havyo na ya iṣavānmanma rejati rakṣohā manma rejati ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-129-6)

he has praised Paruchchhepa's style. The Aitareya Brāhmaṇa too describes the style of the seer Paruchchhepa, the nature of his subjects, the application of his mantras and so on (Ai. Brā. 5-10 to 13, khaṇḍas). All this we have explained at length in the introduction to sūkta 127 in part 10 of the Ṛ. Saṃ.

The birth of Dīrghatamas, his series of hardships after birth, his devotion to God even in the extremity of misfortune, how through his great faith and power he obtained the favour of the Aśvins and other deities, and similar matters, are explained in the special topics on the Ṛks 1-147-3 and 1-152-6. Because he was the foremost among praisers and steadfast in ritual action, the epithet "dear to Brahman" is well known for him. Besides this,

दीर्घतमा मामतेयो जुजुर्वान् दशमे युगे । अपामर्थं यतीनां ब्रह्मा भवति सारथिः ॥ (*dīrghatamā māmateyo jujurvān daśame yuge | apāmarthaṃ yatīnāṃ brahmā bhavati sārathiḥ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-158-6)

here he himself describes the nature of the excellent position he obtained. How, after receiving the favour of Agni and the other deities, he praised them is explained in the Bṛhaddevatā by these verses:

स वेदिषद इत्यस्तौच्च तुर्भिर्जातवेदसं । समिद्ध आप्रियोऽ०त्यैंद्री तमित्यग्नेः पराणि षट् । (*sa vediṣada ityastaucca turbhirjātavedasaṃ | samiddha āpriyo '0tyaiṃdrī tamityagneḥ parāṇi ṣaṭ |*) (Bṛ. De. 4-16)**NOTE**

तुष्टाव चैव सूक्ताभ्यां अबोधीत्यश्विनावृषिः । प्रेति द्यावापृथिव्यौ तु पराभ्यामेतदुत्तरं ॥ (*tuṣṭāva caiva sūktābhyāṃ abodhītyaśvināvṛṣiḥ | preti dyāvāpṛthivyau tu parābhyāmetaduttaraṃ ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 4-32)

Since this seer's works are also prominent in the next chapter, and since the maturity of his philosophical knowledge and his power of exposition are manifested there in a special manner, his great qualities will be described there.

### Printed p. iii

In the Mitrāvaruṇa sūkta (1-137) seen by Paruchchhepa there is no description of any special qualities of those deities; there is only an invocation, with praise, to drink the soma.

In sūkta 138 there is a description of Pūṣan's special qualities. In it the epithet आफृणि (*āphṛṇi*) has become a cause of disagreement, between those of the Vedic tradition and modern scholars, in determining Pūṣan's nature. The opinions of Yāska and others, the following of the same by scholars such as Bloomfield, and the giving of differing opinions by some other scholars, have also been set out in the special topics on the fourth Ṛk of sūkta 138 (pp. 33-34).

Not all the epithets that Yāska and others take up to establish that Pūṣan is a solar (Āditya) deity need refer to the Āditya alone; some argue that they can apply to all deities. In putting forward such opposing views too, recent scholars show certain grounds and maintain their side. For example—

यास्ते पूषन्नावो अंतः समुद्रे हिरण्ययीरंतरिक्षे चरंति । ताभिर्यासि दूत्यां सूर्यस्य कामेन कृत श्रव इच्छमानः ॥ (*yāste pūṣannāvo aṃtaḥ samudre hiraṇyayīraṃtarikṣe caraṃti | tābhiryāsi dūtyāṃ sūryasya kāmena kṛta śrava icchamānaḥ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-58-3)

In this Ṛk Pūṣan is regarded as the messenger of the Sun, and from other examples likewise they have shown that very different features are seen in Āditya and Pūṣan. Besides, they argue that in sūkta 8-29 of the Ṛ. Saṃ., when the nature of the Viśvedevas is described, there is no description of a solar quality in respect of Pūṣan; and so on. Arguing thus that Pūṣan is not an Āditya deity as traditionally held, they have shown that he is a deity of herdsmen, who has acquired a distinct character of his own.

The main body of the hymns to Pūṣan is contained in the sixth Maṇḍala of the Ṛgveda, in sūktas 53 to 58. It is known that the seers of the Bhāradvāja lineage made Pūṣan their principal deity. Some scholars call this portion "Pūṣa-Saṃhitā". In other parts of the Saṃhitā too Pūṣan is praised, along with other deities such as Indra and Soma. In the Ṛks Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-55-2, 6-53-9, 6-56-1, 6-55-3 and others, the descriptions कपर्दि (*kapardi*), पशुसाधनी (*paśusādhanī*), अष्टा (*aṣṭā*), करुंभाद् (*karuṃbhād*), अजाश्व (*ajāśva*) and so on all give us a definite picture of Pūṣan. Along with the common qualities shared with other deities, in the sixth Maṇḍala one special aspect of Pūṣan seems to be prominent. It is stated clearly that he is the deity of herdsmen, that through this deity's favour the worshippers' wealth of cattle should increase, and that he is the protector of cattle and the like that have lost their way.

पूषा गा अन्वेतु नः पूषा रक्षत्वर्वतः । पूषा वाजं सनोतु नः ॥ (*pūṣā gā anvetu naḥ pūṣā rakṣatvarvataḥ | pūṣā vājaṃ sanotu naḥ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-54-5)

माकिर्नेशन्माकीं रिषन्माकीं सं शारि केवटे । आधारिष्टाभिरा गहि ॥ (*mākirneśanmākīṃ riṣanmākīṃ saṃ śāri kevaṭe | ādhāriṣṭābhirā gahi ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-54-7)

In these and other ways they pray in various manners that he increase their wealth of cattle. Modern critics conjecture that these descendants of Bharadvāja did not stay settled in one place but moved about again and again, and that in their wanderings they regarded Pūṣan as guide and protector.

In sūkta 139, addressed to Mitrāvaruṇa, some descriptions are profound ideas from the philosophical and spiritual point of view. For example, in Ṛk 139-2, the sentence ऋतादनृतमाददाथे (*ṛtādanṛtamādadāthe*), "you take the anṛta from the ṛta", cannot be explained from an ordinary point of view. There must be a supernatural and profound meaning. Speaking crudely, one can say: ṛtāt—from the Āditya; anṛtam—(the perishable) water; ādadāthe—you take. But it is not easy to say which quality the name ṛta describes in Āditya. Even in the description that ṛta is the eternal path of Āditya, and that ṛta is his source and support (Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-54-6; 7-60-5), the nature of ṛta cannot be explained. Such an inseparable relation, and sometimes identity, is deeper than a merely physical meaning.

In the fifth Ṛk of the same sūkta, मा वां रातिरुपदसत्कदा चनास्मद्रातिः कदा चन। (*mā vāṃ rātirupadasatkadā canāsmadrātiḥ kadā cana|*) shows that between man's gift in the form of oblation and the gift of wealth such as food by the deities there is a relation of mutual dependence. These two, like warp and weft, are the causes of all the business of the world. It teaches that the source of the deities' gift is sacrifice, and the basis of the growth of sacrifice is the deities' favour; and that if the mutual relation of gods and men is to grow continuously and bring welfare to the world, the prescribed ritual action must be performed—this is the universal law.

In conveying the same idea, Dīrghatamas compares the sacrifice to a boat and sets out the doctrine in an ornate style, which is a most excellent matter from the poetic point of view (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-140-12). Stating that the boat of ritual action is the chief means both for gaining worldly wealth and for gaining welfare in the next world, and speaking in one place of the need to increase worldly prosperity and also of the way to escape the sorrow of saṃsāra, shows the greatness of the Vedic teaching. In the prayer अस्माकं वीरान् मघोनः (*asmākaṃ vīrān maghonaḥ*) there is the interest in increasing worldly life,

### Printed p. v

and in the faith expressed in उत नः जनांश्च पारयात् (*uta naḥ janāṃśca pārayāt*) the need of renouncing worldly life, and in the hope expressed in च, या शर्म नावं (*ca, yā śarma nāvaṃ*) the idea of the bliss of Brahmaloka; it is explained how the boat in the form of sacrifice can help both these mutually opposed pursuits, and so it teaches the need of performing sacrifice and other ritual acts. By comparing the officiants such as the ṛtvijs, the performers of the sacrifice, to the oars of a boat, it states their weighty responsibility as the means of the world's welfare.

Sūkta 142, addressed to the Āprī deities, is marked by a very mature style and profound ideas. It is one of the most excellent works of Dīrghatamas. The importance of the Āprī sūkta, the nature of the particular deities, and the lofty mind of the seer have been explained fully in the exposition of the sūkta (pages 222 to 286).

In sūktas 154, 155 and 156, the description of the all-pervading Viṣṇu gives occasion for disagreement in understanding Viṣṇu's nature. Sentences such as पार्थिवानि विममे रजांसि (*pārthivāni vimame rajāṃsi*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-54-1) and यस्कोरुषु त्रिषु विक्रमणेष्टधिक्षियंति भुवनानि विश्वा। (*yaskoruṣu triṣu vikramaṇeṣṭadhikṣiyaṃti bhuvanāni viśvā|*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-154-2) do not give a precise meaning. The author of the Nirukta has stated, with examples, the view of Aurṇavābha and others, who explain त्रिषु विक्रमणेषु (*triṣu vikramaṇeṣu*) as the three states of the Sun: rising, being at the zenith, and setting (page 525-6). To support this view some sentences of the Ṛk Saṃhitā itself can also be taken as authority (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-155-6, 1-164-4). In the Purāṇic descriptions too there are some epithets that favour this view. One may say that the Sudarśana discus reflects the solar orb, the lotus the power of light of the heavenly world that gives life to the world, and the yellow garment masses of radiance (cf. P. O. S. No. 75, P. 99). In the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa also, the description that when Viṣṇu's head was cut off it was transformed into the form of the Sun makes this point clear.

If Viṣṇu's description is interpreted in this way as referring solely to the Āditya deity, then the sentence विष्णोः पदे परमे मध्व उत्सः (*viṣṇoḥ pade parame madhva utsaḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-154-5) does not receive its proper import. This sentence describes the Satyaloka spoken of in the Upaniṣads and elsewhere. It is the Upaniṣads' view that the Āditya's excellent path is only a means to this Satyaloka. In sentences such as सूर्यद्वारेण ते विरजाः प्रयांति यत्रामृतः स पुरुषो ह्यव्ययात्मा । (*sūryadvāreṇa te virajāḥ prayāṃti yatrāmṛtaḥ sa puruṣo hyavyayātmā |*) (Mu. U. 2-11) this highest abode is described as beyond the world of the Sun. Therefore, in the view of some, it must be the abode of the Supreme Person in the form of Viṣṇu. In their opinion there is no doubt that Viṣṇu held a most exalted position. The number of sūktas addressed to this deity being small does not alone justify saying that his rank was lower than that of Indra and other deities. If one examines his descriptions and the like, he is the deity of the Satyaloka, the best of all worlds, and

### Printed p. vi

some have maintained that the same position that is described in the Purāṇas and elsewhere for Viṣṇu, the Puruṣottama, is described in the Ṛgveda too.

Macdonell brings forth other traces of Hindu Vishnu's general character as the 'Preserver in the Veda. Vishnu is beneficent (I-156-5), is innocuous and bountiful (VIII. 25. 12.) and a generous protector (I. 155. 4.) but only such features of an ordinary sun-god cannot adequately account for Vishnu's sudden rise to prominence in Hindu Mytho-logy. Vishnu must have already been a great God among 'extra-Vedic' circles and his later eminence was merely a legitimately recognised come-back, [P. O. S. No. 75. P. 104-5]. Citing the sentences that describe Viṣṇu's hospitality, they argue that these qualities are not possible for the Sun deity alone; therefore these sentences praise the nature of Viṣṇu as the description of the Supreme Person, who is the best among the deities and is described in the Purāṇas and elsewhere as Nārāyaṇa.

The description of Viṣṇu in the sūktas of Dīrghatamas gives room for both these views. But since the sentences of this seer, of supernatural greatness, suggest a very profound meaning, it is not easy to know their precise meaning. Just as Viṣṇu's nature cannot be settled as "this and no other", so too it is laborious to understand rightly the relation of Indra and Viṣṇu. These matters will be discussed later with scriptural authorities.

To publish such a Ṛgveda Saṃhitā—most ancient, most profound, most vast, and the foundation of all the śāstras—in simple Kannada with critical comment is not an easy task. Nevertheless, with the weighty aim that through such a great work all people, scholars and laymen alike, may gain increase of knowledge and become worthy of God's grace, His Highness Śrī Jayachāmarājendra Oḍeyar Bahadur, G.C.B., G.C.S.I., Lord of the throne of Karṇāṭaka, lover of Vedic learning, himself a scholar, beloved of his subjects and devoted to God, is encouraging with his generous patronage the publication of this costly work; this must be called the merit of the whole multitude of the people of Karṇāṭaka. For such a generous-minded Lord, for his family, for the princess (bearing the name of the mantra Gāyatrī, regarded as the foremost of Vedic mantras), and for the whole body of subjects, I pray that the Lord of all, the Veda-Puruṣa, bestow abundantly, beyond even this, all prosperity—long life, health, wealth and the rest, the fruits of this world—and the spiritual knowledge that leads to the highest good.
