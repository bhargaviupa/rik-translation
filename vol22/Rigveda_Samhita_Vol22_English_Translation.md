**Fifth Aṣṭaka, Third Adhyāya: Sūktas 20–25 of the Seventh Maṇḍala**

**Introduction**

In the Seventh Maṇḍala, the twenty-five sūktas from the seventh sūkta to the thirty-first are addressed to Indra. Of these, sūktas 7–19 are included in the previous adhyāya, that is, in Ṛ. Saṃ. Part 21. Therefore in this part the six sūktas from 20 to 25 are in praise of Indra. Since we have described Indra at length in Ṛ. Saṃ. Part 54, pp. 569-582, and Part 16, pp. 631-683, and elsewhere, from here onwards particulars will be given only where the need appears.

## Sūkta 20 (Maṇḍala 2)

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Now the third adhyāya begins. The sūkta beginning उग्रो जज्ञॆ (*ugro jajñè*) has ten verses and is the third sūkta; its seer is Vasiṣṭha and its deity is Indra. The Anukramaṇikā says the same.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

On the fourth day of the Abhiplava sacrifice, in the niṣkevalya recitation, this sūkta serves as the nivid-dhāna, as the Sūtra states (Ā. 7-2). In the Mahāvrata too the sūkta is used in the niṣkevalya; likewise the Fifth Āraṇyaka cites its verse (Ai. Ā. 5-2-9). In the Soma Cāturmāsya sacrifices, at the place of the Vaiśvadeva, in the first Praśyamaha (?), this sūkta is again the nivid-dhāna in the niṣkevalya, as the Sūtra states (Ā. 9-9).**NOTE**

As the explanation of the second adhyāya of the fifth aṣṭaka was completed in the previous part, the explanation of the third adhyāya is now begun. This sūkta beginning उग्रोग्रॆ जज्ञॆ (*ugrogrè jajñè*) is the third sūkta in the second anuvāka of the seventh maṇḍala. It has ten Ṛks. Vasiṣṭha is the seer of this sūkta; Indra is the deity; the metre is Triṣṭup. In the Anukramaṇikā it is stated with the words उग्रोग्रॆ जत (*ugrogrè jata*). In the sacrifices connected with the Abhiplava, when the niṣkevalya-śastra mantras are recited on the fourth day, this sūkta is to be recited as the nivid-dhāna sūkta; this is set forth by the sūtra of the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra चतुर्थसूर्यो ग्रे जज्ञॆ इति निष्केवल्यं । (*caturthasūryo gre jajñè iti niṣkevalyaṃ |*) (Ā. 7-2). In the Mahāvrata sacrifice too this sūkta is to be recited among the niṣkevalya-śastra mantras; this is indicated in the Fifth Āraṇyaka of the Aitareya Āraṇyaka by the sentence beginning उग्रोग्रॆ जज्ञॆ वीळ्यायु स्थथावानुदु ब्रह्माण्युरत श्रवस्वा । (*ugrogrè jajñè vīḷyāyu sthathāvānudu brahmāṇyurata śravasvā |*) (Ai. Ā. 5-2-9). And in the sacrifices called Cāturmāsya, connected with Soma, when the work of the first day of the Praśyamaha sacrifice is performed at the place of the Vaiśvadeva, this sūkta is to be recited in the middle of the niṣkevalya-śastra mantras as the nivid-dhāna sūkta; this is explained by the sūtra of the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra जनिष्ठा उग्र उग्रोग्रॆ जज्ञॆ इति मध्यंदिनः । (*janiṣṭhā ugra ugrogrè jajñè iti madhyaṃdinaḥ |*) (Ā. 9-9).

> स्पधावान्‌ बलवानग्रः ओजस्सुद्भूलोके नेंद्रो वीर्याय वीर्यं कर्तुं जज्ञॆ । बभूव । नर्यो नरहितः सन्‌ यत्कर्म करिष्यन्‌ भவதி तदपः कर्म चक्रिः कर्तृव ॥ चक्रिरिति किन्स्‌प्रत्ययस्य लिड्डद्भावान्म लोकाम्युनिषामूलधत्त्वनामिति षष्ठीप्रतिषेधः ॥ अपि च नृषदनं यज्ञगृहं युवानित्येतरुणः सन्‌ अवोभि रक्षण्णैः साधिं जग्लिर्गंता महच्छिन्यहतोत्पेनसेः पापानोण्यस्साकं त्राता रक्षिता च भवेति ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Indra, powerful and fierce, strong in might, has been born in the world for heroic deed. Being a benefactor of men, whatever act he intends to perform, that act he accomplishes. Further, being ever young, he goes to the sacrificial house together with his protections, and from great sin he is the protector and guardian of us all.**NOTE**

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

Indra, powerful and of marvellous prowess, has been born to perform heroic deeds. Being beneficial to man, he certainly accomplishes whatever act he resolves to do. Ever young Indra, entering the sacrificial house with his protections, releases us from very great sin and protects us.

##### English translation
The fierce and powerful (Indra) has been born for heroic (deeds); friendly to man he is the accomplisher of whatever act he undertakes to perform; ever youthful, he invests the (Sacrificial) hall with defences (against interruption); be our preserver, Indra, from heinous sin.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Indra, growing in strength, becomes the slayer of Vṛtra, the asura. The hero, being a hero, protects his worshipper (the praiser) quickly with his protections, and protected him. He is also the maker of a dwelling (loka, a country) for King Sudās; or else, he makes room (loka) for the sacrificer Sudās, who gives well. The particles अह वा उ (*aha vā u*) are three expletives. And he repeatedly, again and again, gives wealth to the sacrificer who offers oblations. The particle आ (*ā*) is also used in a sense of its own.**NOTE**

> हंति ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Yuddhi (the warrior) is a fighter; anarvā is one without turning away, who does not turn back from battle; khajakṛt is the maker of battle, for khaja stands among the names of battle. Samadvā: samat is battle, and he is possessed of it. Śūra is one endowed with heroism. Januṣā means by birth, by nature itself; satrāṣāṭ is the one who overcomes many; apālnaḥ is the one himself never overcome by anyone; skocāḥ is of good strength. This Indra scatters the hosts (pṛtanāḥ) of enemies. And moreover he slays all who behave as an enemy, in whatever way.**NOTE**

- समद्वा (*samadvā*)—one who is eager for battle
- शूरः (*śūraḥ*)—the hero
- जनुषा (*januṣā*)—from birth itself
- सत्राषाट् (*satrāṣāṭ*)—who defeats many
- आपाल्नः (*āpālnaḥ*)—unobstructed

- तुं इन्द्रः (*tuṃ indraḥ*)—this same Indra

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

Indra, the best of warriors, who does not turn his face away, skilled in battle, eager for battle, a hero, a defeater of many from birth itself, unobstructed and possessed of excellent strength, scatters the armies of enemies. And he destroys all the enemies who act against him.

##### English translation
A warrior who turns not back in battle, a combatant, one engaged in tumults, a hero, victorious over (his) foes from birth, invincible, of great vigor this Indra, scatters (hostile) hosts and slays all (his) adversaries.

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> हे तुविष्यो बहुधनेंद्र महिता महत्त्वेनॆ तविषीभिश्चलैःशोभे उभुते अपि रोदसी द्यावापृथिव्यावा स्पप्रथ । अपूरितनासे । अथ परोक्षस्तुतिः । हरिवान्‌कृ- वानिंद्रो वज्रं नि मिमिक्षुन्‌ शत्रुषु प्रसारयन्‌ मदेष्टु यज्ञेषु निविष्टु वांधसा सोमेन समुवोचे । संसेव्यते संगच्छते वा ॥ उच समवाय इति धातुः । वा इति पूरणः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Indra, rich in much wealth, by your greatness and by your powers you have pervaded both heaven and earth. Then follows praise in the third person: Indra, drawn by bay horses, hurling his thunderbolt among enemies, is associated in the joyous sacrifices with the Soma food. The root उच (*uca*) is used in the sense of union, and the particle वा (*vā*) is an expletive.**NOTE**

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- इंद्र (*iṃdra*)—O Indra
- महिता (*mahitā*)—of great majesty
- तविषीभिः (*taviṣībhiḥ*)—with your powers
- उभे जित् रोदसी (*ubhe jit rodasī*)—both heaven and earth also
- आ स्पप्रथ (*ā spapratha*)—you have pervaded**NOTE**
- हरिवान् (*harivān*)—Indra, possessed of horses
- इंद्रः (*iṃdraḥ*)—Indra
- वज्र (*vajra*)—the thunderbolt
- नि मिमिक्षुन् (*ni mimikṣun*)—hurling it upon the enemies
