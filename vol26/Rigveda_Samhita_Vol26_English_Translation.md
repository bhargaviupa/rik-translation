**The first chapter in the seventh Aṣṭaka**

**The Sūktas of the ninth Maṇḍala**

**Introduction to the Maṇḍala**

**On the Soma Plant**

We have given explanations about the Soma plant many times. In all those places, many descriptions have been stated: that Soma is the juice drawn from a kind of creeper called Soma; that it brings zeal and joy to those who drink it; and that, when the gods are invited, they should come to the sacrificial ground and drink the Soma juice. Sūktas that praise Soma as a deity are very rare in the other Maṇḍalas, except the ninth Maṇḍala. All the sūktas in the ninth Maṇḍala praise the deity Soma alone. Although a detailed explanation about Soma would be necessary on this occasion, a brief explanation here will not be out of place.

### Printed p. 2

Since the Soma sacrifices are very important among the rites of the Ṛgveda, it is natural that the deity Soma too is one of the principal deities. The 114 sūktas in the ninth Maṇḍala, together with 6 other sūktas, in all 120 sūktas, praise the deity Soma. Besides these, 4–5 sūkta-portions, and 5–7 sūktas in company with Indra, Agni, Pūṣan or the Rudras, praise the deity Soma. The name सोम (*soma*) occurs, alone or in compounds, about 120 times. From the point of view of number, the deity Soma stands third. Personification is very sparing. Since the Soma plant and its juices are mentioned again and again, it is impossible to forget them altogether. For that very reason, references to the form of that deity, to his limbs, or to his movements and the like are rare. Even where feats of valour are mentioned, they are the same as those of other deities, and are weak or of secondary importance. Like the other deities, he too is invited, under the name Indu or Soma, to come to the sacrifice, to sit on the seat of darbha grass and to receive the offerings. The whole of the ninth Maṇḍala deals with the subject of the Soma juice: pressing the juice out with stones, filtering it through a woollen sieve into wooden vessels, offering it through Agni as a drink for the gods (1-94-14; 5-51-1; 8-43-11 and so on), or the drinking of it by the priests, and similar matters, are what is stated.

The Soma juice is mixed in three ways (त्राशिरः (*trāśiraḥ*) 5-27-5): mixed with milk (गवाशिर (*gavāśira*)), mixed with curds (दध्याशिर (*dadhyāśira*)) and mixed with barley (यवाशिर (*yavāśira*)); this mixture is called the garment-wearing (वस्त्र (*vastra*), वासः (*vāsaḥ*), आत्म (*ātma*)). It is also called “निर्णिक् (*nirṇik*)” (a shining garment, 9-14-5). This word is also applied to the sieve (9-30-7). For this very reason Soma is described as beautiful (9-34-4 and so on) and as abundantly adorned with ornaments (9-81-2). Rarely, mixing with ghee (9-82-2) or with water is also mentioned; but for these the word आशिर (*āśira*) has not been used.

In the sacrifices there is a rite called अप्यायन (*apyāyana*) (soaking again in water the Soma plants that have been half pressed) (Mai. Saṃ. 4-5-5). In the Ṛgveda this root (अप्या (*apyā*)) is used in connection with Soma. But here the root is used to suggest that Soma is like the moon. In one place (9-31-4) alone this ritual rite may be indicated; it is said that the Soma juice swells like a river or the sea (9-64-8, 9-107-12). Soma is pressed three times a day. The Ṛbhu gods are invited to the evening pressing (4-33-11 and so on), Indra alone (4-36-7) to the midday pressing (3-21-1 and 2; 8-37-1), and Indra, to drink first, to the morning pressing (10-112-1).

### Printed p. 3

The Soma juice has an inexpressible power that is not found in ordinary food or drink. By this power it enlivens men, or whoever drinks it, and makes them accomplish even deeds that are not possible for men. For that very reason it has given room to the idea that it is a divine drink, equal to ambrosia, indeed ambrosia itself. It would not be an exaggeration to say that those who drink it become immortal. The intoxicating drink dear to the gods (9-85-2) and bestowing immortality (1-84-4), pressed by men and mixed with milk, this juice is drunk by all the gods (9-109-15); from it they quickly feel exhilaration (8-2-18) and, having drunk, become gladdened (8-58-11). Soma is eternal (1-43-9; 8-88-12; 9-3-1 and so on); the gods drank it to attain immortality (9-106-8). He (the deity Soma) grants immortality to the gods (1-91-6; 9-108-3) and to men (1-91-1; 8-89-3). There are descriptions such as that he places his worshipper, made immortal, in the world that is the seat of everlasting splendour and fame, imperishable, the dwelling place of Vivasvat (9-113-7 and 8), and so on.

By drinking the Soma juice a man's zeal increases. But the Soma juice is chiefly offered to the gods. Therefore the joy and zeal born of Soma arise in the gods. The effect of the intoxication born of Soma falls mostly upon Indra. It helps Indra to subdue the opposing powers in the atmosphere. It is stated many times that the special strength and capacity needed for fighting Vṛtra came from the Soma juice (8-81-17 and so on). In the intoxication produced by the Soma juice, Indra defeats all enemies (9-1-10), and no one can withstand in battle Indra who has drunk Soma (6-47-1). Soma is the very self of Indra (9-85-3); the benevolent friend (10-25-9) increases Indra's prowess (9-76-2) and helps in the slaying of Vṛtra (9-61-22). Accompanied by Soma, Indra made the waters flow for men and slew Ahi (4-28-1). Hence at times Soma himself is said to be Indra's thunderbolt (9-72-7; 9-77-1; 9-111-3). This Soma juice, belonging to Indra, becomes the thunderbolt that wins a thousand victories (9-47-3). The Soma juice is the intoxicating drink that can destroy hundreds of fortresses (9-48-2), and it is the plant that slays Vṛtra and causes intoxication (6-17-11). In this way the deity Soma, like Indra, is called the slayer of Vṛtra and the breaker of fortresses (9-88-4). The personal epithet of Indra, वृत्रहा (*vṛtrahā*), is applied to Soma five or six times.

Because it is the most important plant, the Soma plant is also called the lord of all plants (9-114-2) and king (9-97-18 and 19), the lord of the forest (वनस्पति (*vanaspati*)) (1-91-6; 9-12-7), and for all plants

### Printed p. 4

the root (1-91-22). In the Brāhmaṇas all trees have the name सौम्य (*saumya*) (belonging to Soma) (Śa. Brā. 12-1-1-2). Moreover, like the other principal deities, Soma too has the name of king. He is king of the rivers (9-89-2) and of the whole world (9-97-58). He is king or father of the gods (9-86-10; 9-87-2; 9-109-4); king of the god-men (9-97-24), and of the Brāhmaṇas (Vā. Saṃ. 9-40; Tai. Saṃ. 1-8-10; Mai. Saṃ. 2-6-9). That he is a deity is stated many times; only in one place is it said that he is a deity pressed (made the one to be pressed) for the sake of a deity (9-3-6 and 7).

In later literature सोम (*soma*) is a name of the moon. When he has waned because the gods have drunk him, the sun fills him up. In the Chāndogya Upaniṣad (5-10-1) it is said that the moon is Soma, that he is the food of the gods, and that they drink him up. In the Brāhmaṇas the identity of the moon and Soma is a common matter. King Soma is the food of the gods, and he is the moon (Śa. Brā. 1-6-4-5). That plant used in the sacrifice, or its juice, indicates the moon deity (Kau. Brā. 7-10; 4-4)**NOTE**. The very opinion of the Brāhmaṇas is that the moon wanes day by day because the Fathers and the gods eat him, who is of the nature of ambrosia. In the Yajurveda it is said that the daughters of Prajāpati are the wives of the moon, and that they are the cluster of stars. In the Atharvaveda (Ā. Va. 7-81-3 and 4; 11-6-7 and so on)**NOTE**, wherever Soma is mentioned, in general the moon is meant. Among the passages that speak of the identity of the moon and Soma, the 85th sūkta of the tenth Maṇḍala, which describes the marriage of Soma and Sūryā, is the chief; here Soma is in the lap of the stars. It is said that no one consumes the Soma known to the priests, and that there is a difference between this Soma and what they must press out. Its secret is known only to the Brāhmaṇas. Descriptions are indeed there of Soma as heavenly, radiant, dispelling darkness and so on; it has the name इंदु (*iṃdu*) (6-44-21). It is compared to the moon reflected in the water in the vessels (8-71-8; compare 1-105-1). The Soma described as a drop (द्रप्प (*drappa*): drop, bindu) and going to the sea (10-123-8) must also be the moon.

In the opinion of some, Soma is the moon alone and not another deity at all. The whole of the ninth Maṇḍala praises this moon. Wherever this word occurs in the Ṛgveda, it indicates only these two, the Soma plant or the moon. They argue that the juice that comes from that plant is itself ambrosia, that the moon is the abode of that ambrosia, that the moon is a more important deity than the sun, and that Indra comes after him. Others again say that there is not enough basis, or none, for the above opinion in the Ṛgveda. However much support the identity of the moon and Soma may find in later works, in the Ṛgveda, among the hundreds of ṛks concerning the deity Soma

this is hinted at here and there only rarely, and nowhere is it stated clearly. Moreover, none of the commentators has said that this point has a basis in the Veda.

**Derivation of the word Soma**

The Nirukta-writers, ओषधिस्सोमः सुनोतेर्यदेनमभिषुण्वंति (*oṣadhissomaḥ sunoteryadenamabhiṣuṇvaṃti*): a herb well known by the name Soma, because they press it; this**NOTE**

आदाय श्येनो अभरत्सोमं सहस्रं सना आयुतं च साकं । अत्रा पुरंधिरजहादरातीर्मदे सोमस्य मूरा अमूरः ॥ (*ādāya śyeno abharatsomaṃ sahasraṃ sanā āyutaṃ ca sākaṃ | atrā puraṃdhirajahādarātīrmade somasya mūrā amūraḥ ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-26-7)

They have explained the greatness of Soma: that the Gāyatrī metre, assuming the form of the falcon, brought it from heaven to the earth, and that, on occasions when Indra drinks this Soma, the sacrificers perform sacrifices with a thousand pressings and give away gifts of ten thousand in number. And बहुलमस्य नैघंटुकं वृत्तं आश्चर्यमिव प्राधान्येन (*bahulamasya naighaṃṭukaṃ vṛttaṃ āścaryamiva prādhānyena*): the praise of this Soma also indicates its importance, and it is marvellous. This is recited in the ninth Maṇḍala. And the glory of this Soma is stated in the form of praise in the ṛk

स्वादिष्ठया मदिष्टया पवस्व सोम धारया । इंद्राय पातने सुतः । (*svādiṣṭhayā madiṣṭayā pavasva soma dhārayā | iṃdrāya pātane sutaḥ |*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-1-1) **GLOSS**

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-1-1)**NOTE**

सोमं मन्यते पपिवान् यत्संपिषंत्योषधिं । सोमं यं ब्रह्माणो विदुर्न तस्याश्नाति कश्चन ॥ (*somaṃ manyate papivān yatsaṃpiṣaṃtyoṣadhiṃ | somaṃ yaṃ brahmāṇo vidurna tasyāśnāti kaścana ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-85-3)

The word सोमं (*somaṃ*) here is understood to mean the moon. In this ṛk both the Soma plant and the moon are praised. But Yāska has described in this way the greater importance of this Soma over the well-known moon.

सोमं मन्यते पपिवान् यत्संपिषंत्योषधिमिति वृथासुतमसोममाह सोमं यं ब्रह्माणो विदुरिति न तस्याश्नाति कश्चनायजैत्यधियज्ञं । अथाधिदैवतं सोमं मन्यते पपिवान् यत्संपिषंत्योषधिमिति यजुस्सुतमसोममाह सोमं यं ब्राहणो विदुश्चंद्रमसं न तस्याश्नाति कश्चनादेव इति । (*somaṃ manyate papivān yatsaṃpiṣaṃtyoṣadhimiti vṛthāsutamasomamāha somaṃ yaṃ brahmāṇo viduriti na tasyāśnāti kaścanāyajaityadhiyajñaṃ | athādhidaivataṃ somaṃ manyate papivān yatsaṃpiṣaṃtyoṣadhimiti yajussutamasomamāha somaṃ yaṃ brāhaṇo viduścaṃdramasaṃ na tasyāśnāti kaścanādeva iti |*) (Ni. 11-4)

Although those who prepare rasāyanas and the like bring Soma, press out the juice and drink it, they do not become famous as Soma-drinkers at the sacrifice; therefore, since it is used as a limb of the sacrifice

### Printed p. 6

(for the sacrifice), Soma is praised as producing, only for the sacrificer, the fame of being a Soma-drinker in the prescribed manner. In the adhidaiva view also (where the word Soma is explained as meaning the moon), the Soma that is a limb of the sacrifice is indicated by the word Soma. By the śruti sentence “सोमो नूनमेष यद्देवानामन्नं (*somo nūnameṣa yaddevānāmannaṃ*)”, even though the moon is denoted by the word Soma, he is food for the gods alone. Therefore, although by the mantra सोमं मन्यते (*somaṃ manyate*) two kinds of meaning, adhiyajña and adhidaiva, are given for the word Soma, the sacrificial authorities have assigned the primacy in the sacrifice to the Soma in the form of the herb.

यत्त्वा देव प्रपिबंति तत आ स्यायसे पुनः । वायुस्सोमस्य रक्षिता समानां मास अकृतिः ॥ (*yattvā deva prapibaṃti tata ā syāyase punaḥ | vāyussomasya rakṣitā samānāṃ māsa akṛtiḥ ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-85-5)

यत्वा देव प्रपिबंति तत आप्यायसे पुनरिति नाराशंसानभिप्रेत्य पूर्वपक्षापरपक्षाविति ना वायुसोमस्य रक्षिता वायुमस्य रक्षितारमाह साहचर्यात् रसहरणाद्वा ! समानां संवत्सराणां मास आकृतिः सोमो रूपविशेषैरोषधिश्चंद्रमा वा । (*yatvā deva prapibaṃti tata āpyāyase punariti nārāśaṃsānabhipretya pūrvapakṣāparapakṣāviti nā vāyusomasya rakṣitā vāyumasya rakṣitāramāha sāhacaryāt rasaharaṇādvā ! samānāṃ saṃvatsarāṇāṃ māsa ākṛtiḥ somo rūpaviśeṣairoṣadhiścaṃdramā vā |*)

In the sense that concerns the sacrifice, the priests, the sacrificer and others drink Soma at the three pressings. Hence this Soma is the one who makes (the gods) swell. Although Vāyu sucks the juice of the herbs, he does not suck the Soma juice, so he is its protector. Those who make rasāyanas and know the Āyurveda say that this Soma obtains growth of leaves (pṛṇa) in accordance with the tithis of the bright fortnights of each month, and waning (apacita) of leaves in accordance with the tithis of the dark fortnights. In the adhidaiva view, the moon, denoted by the word Soma, becomes by his digits the one who refreshes the gods in accordance with the respective tithis. The gods also drink the digits of the moon. The moon obtains waxing and waning in the respective fortnights through his digits. Thus, in both ways, adhiyajña and adhidaiva, a likeness (equal state) of the moon is stated for the Soma plant, its identity with Soma is established, and the Soma plant is praised as full of abundant greatness.

**The Story of the Fetching of Soma**

सोमो वै राजामुष्मिंल्लोक आसीत्तं देवाश्च ऋषयश्चाभ्यध्यायन् कथमयमस्मात्सोमो राजागच्छेदिति तेऽब्रुवंश्वंदांसि यूयं न इमं सोमं राजानमाहरतेति तथेति ते सुपर्णा भूत्वोदपतंस्ते यत्सुपर्णा भूत्वोदपतंस्तदेतत्सौपर्णमित्याख्यानविद आचक्षते ॥ (*somo vai rājāmuṣmiṃlloka āsīttaṃ devāśca ṛṣayaścābhyadhyāyan kathamayamasmātsomo rājāgacchediti te 'bruvaṃśvaṃdāṃsi yūyaṃ na imaṃ somaṃ rājānamāharateti tatheti te suparṇā bhūtvodapataṃste yatsuparṇā bhūtvodapataṃstadetatsauparṇamityākhyānavida ācakṣate ‖*)

(Ai. Brā. 13-25) **GLOSS**

### Printed p. 7

In the beginning King Soma (the Soma plant; there is a custom of calling the Soma plant King Soma out of respect) was in heaven. He was not in this world. Knowing this, the gods and the seers considered how to bring this Soma here. Then they discussed the matter, and, understanding that the metres have the power to bring Soma from heaven to the earth, they addressed the metres and prayed: O metres, for our sake, that is, to be used in our sacrifice, you should bring to the earth the Soma that is in heaven. Accepting their prayer, the metres assumed the forms of certain birds well known in the world and flew off to heaven. Since winged birds are called सुपर्ण (*suparṇa*), and since these birds engaged in the fetching of Soma, this episode of the fetching of Soma is called the Sauparṇa story in the Purāṇas and similar works:

छंदांसि वै तत्सोमं राजानमच्छाचरंस्तानि ह तर्हि चतुरक्षराणि चतुरक्षराण्येव छंदांस्यासन्सा जगती चतुरक्षरा प्रथनोदपतत्सा पतित्वार्धमध्वनो गत्वाश्राम्यत्सा परास्य त्रीण्यक्षराण्येकाक्षरा भूत्वा दीक्षां च तपश्च हरंती पुनरभ्यवापतत्तस्मात्तस्य वित्ता दीक्षा वित्तं तपो यस्य पशवः संति जागता हि पशवो जगती हि तानाहरत् ॥ (*chaṃdāṃsi vai tatsomaṃ rājānamacchācaraṃstāni ha tarhi caturakṣarāṇi caturakṣarāṇyeva chaṃdāṃsyāsansā jagatī caturakṣarā prathanodapatatsā patitvārdhamadhvano gatvāśrāmyatsā parāsya trīṇyakṣarāṇyekākṣarā bhūtvā dīkṣāṃ ca tapaśca haraṃtī punarabhyavāpatattasmāttasya vittā dīkṣā vittaṃ tapo yasya paśavaḥ saṃti jāgatā hi paśavo jagatī hi tānāharat ‖*)

(Ai. Brā. 13-25)

Then the metres, Gāyatrī and the others, assuming the forms of birds, got ready to fly to heaven. At that time all the metres, Gāyatrī and the others, had only four syllables (in each pāda); no metre had more than four syllables. First the metre Jagatī said that it would bring Soma, and flew towards heaven, where Soma was. When it had flown half the way it was worn out with weariness, had no strength to fly further, and had to turn back. Through the weariness of having gone so far, the metre Jagatī lost three of its four syllables. It had to return with the one syllable that remained. In this way, returning without the strength to bring Soma, it brought with it the initiation (दीक्षॆ (*dīkṣè*)) connected with the Soma sacrifice, that is, the rite of initiation in the form of the दक्षिणीयॆष्टि (*dakṣiṇīyèṣṭi*), and the austerity in the form of drinking milk and the like. Therefore the initiation is undertaken with the ṛks of the metre Jagatī. The metre Jagatī has the power to increase wealth in cattle; therefore initiation and wealth come easily to a man who has wealth in cattle. Because the metre Jagatī brought cattle, it is said that the metre Jagatī has the power to give cattle. This is stated in the Yajurveda:

सा पशुभिश्च दीक्षया चागच्छत्तस्माज्जगतीछंदसां पशव्यतमा तस्मात्पशुमंतं दीक्षोपनमति (*sā paśubhiśca dīkṣayā cāgacchattasmājjagatīchaṃdasāṃ paśavyatamā tasmātpaśumaṃtaṃ dīkṣopanamati*) (Tai. Saṃ. 6-1-6-2).

Then the metre Triṣṭubh said that it would bring Soma, and sprang into the atmosphere. But before it had gone a little more than half the way, it was worn out with weariness, left one of its four syllables there itself and returned with the remaining three syllables. On returning it brought with it the dakṣiṇā (the order of the fees given to the priests in the sacrifice). Because the metre Triṣṭubh brought the dakṣiṇā in this way, and because the metre Triṣṭubh is connected with the midday pressing (माध्यंदिनसवन (*mādhyaṃdinasavana*)), there is the custom of giving the dakṣiṇā (honorarium) to the priests at the time of the midday pressing performed in the sacrifice.

ते देवा अब्रुवन् गायत्रीं त्वं न इमं सोमं राजानमाहरेति सा तथेत्यब्रवीत्तां वै मा सर्वेण स्वस्त्ययनेनानुमंत्रयध्वमिति तथेति सोदपतत्तां देवाः सर्वेण स्वस्त्ययनेनान्वमंत्रयंत प्रेति चेति चेत्येतद्वै सर्वं स्वस्त्ययनं तत्प्रेति चेति तद्योऽस्य प्रियः स्यात्तमेतेनानुमंत्रयेत प्रेति चेति स्तस्येव गच्छति स्वस्ति पुनरागच्छति ॥ (*te devā abruvan gāyatrīṃ tvaṃ na imaṃ somaṃ rājānamāhareti sā tathetyabravīttāṃ vai mā sarveṇa svastyayanenānumaṃtrayadhvamiti tatheti sodapatattāṃ devāḥ sarveṇa svastyayanenānvamaṃtrayaṃta preti ceti cetyetadvai sarvaṃ svastyayanaṃ tatpreti ceti tadyo 'sya priyaḥ syāttametenānumaṃtrayeta preti ceti stasyeva gacchati svasti punarāgacchati ‖*)

(Ai. Brā. 13-26)

Seeing that the metres Jagatī and Triṣṭubh had returned without the capacity to bring Soma, the gods went to the metre Gāyatrī and prayed: O Gāyatrī, you must bring Soma for us from heaven. Gāyatrī agreed that it should be so, and said to the gods: O gods, as you say, I shall go to heaven to bring Soma. But during the time of my journey, and so that I may return safely, you must recite the blessing mantras of benediction (स्वस्त्ययन (*svastyayana*)) and pray, for those who come, that I may return safely. Having said this, it assumed the form of the falcon bird and flew off by the path of the atmosphere. The gods agreed that it should be so, and kept reciting the benediction mantras that Gāyatrī might return safely. And which is that mantra? It is प्रा च च (*prā ca ca*)**NOTE**. Here pra is one mantra; ā is the second mantra; there are two letters ca to indicate the sense of joining the two together. The meaning of these mantras is: go safely to the place where Soma is, take it, and come back again safely. There is no better benediction mantra than this. Therefore, in the world, friends, kinsmen and others recite this mantra when they have to make a journey. It is well known that through the power of reciting this mantra, those who have travelled come back safe and reach home.

सा पतित्वा सोमपालान् भीषयित्वा पद्भ्यां च मुखेन च सोमं राजानं समगृभ्णाद्यानि चेतरे छंदसी अक्षराण्यजहितां तानि चोपसमगृभ्णात् ॥ (*sā patitvā somapālān bhīṣayitvā padbhyāṃ ca mukhena ca somaṃ rājānaṃ samagṛbhṇādyāni cetare chaṃdasī akṣarāṇyajahitāṃ tāni copasamagṛbhṇāt ‖*)

Then Gāyatrī, in the form of the falcon, flew to heaven and frightened the Soma guardians, स्नान (*snāna*), भ्राज (*bhrāja*) and the others, who were guarding Soma there, with its harsh cry and with weapons such as its sharp beak and claws. Seeing it, the Soma guardians ran off a little way. Then the falcon seized the Soma (the Soma plants) there with its two feet and its beak, and tried to fly back, that is, to the earth. Who were the Soma guardians who protected Soma in heaven? What are their names? This matter is stated in the following Yajurveda mantras:

That is: स्नान (*snāna*), भ्राज (*bhrāja*), अंघारि (*aṃghāri*), बंभारि (*baṃbhāri*), हस्त (*hasta*), सुहस्त (*suhasta*) and कृशानु (*kṛśānu*) are the Soma guardians. O Soma guardians, guard carefully this Soma that is under your charge, so that no one may steal it; this is the meaning of the first mantra. The meaning of the second mantra is that स्नान (*snāna*), भ्राज (*bhrāja*) and the other Soma protectors were guarding the Soma that was in heaven. When the falcon thus carried off the Soma, which was under the protection of the Soma guardians स्नान (*snāna*), भ्राज (*bhrāja*) and the others mentioned above, and brought it to the earth, it also brought back the four syllables that the metres Jagatī and Triṣṭubh had lost. The Gandharvas who guarded Soma in heaven were seven, स्वान् (*svān*)**NOTE**, भ्राज (*bhrāja*) and the others. Among them कृशानु (*kṛśānu*) was the seventh. Seeing Gāyatrī carrying Soma away in the form of the falcon, this Kṛśānu pursued it, and, since he could not catch it, shot an arrow from the bow in his hand. It struck the claw of the falcon's left foot, and it

was cut off. That cut-off claw (nail) became a porcupine (Porcupine) about the size of a monkey. This porcupine has on the tip of its tail many sharp quills, a span in extent, hard and pointed like iron rods. Such an animal is called शल्य (*śalya*) or शल्यक (*śalyaka*) (porcupine). Because this animal arose from the claw cut off from the falcon, its quills are as very sharp as a claw. When the claw was cut off and fell by the arrow, the liquid वशा (*vaśā*) or fat that was near the claw flowed out. This fat was transformed into a she-goat, अजा (*ajā*), आवि (*āvi*) (female goat). Because this goat arose from Gāyatrī in the form of the falcon, it was fixed as a fit offering in sacrifices and the like, suitable for the deities. This matter is indicated in the Yajurveda and other texts by mantras such as

एतामेव वशा मादित्येभ्यः कामायालभेत (*etāmeva vaśā mādityebhyaḥ kāmāyālabheta*) **GLOSS**

When the arrow shot at the falcon by the Gandharva named Kṛśānu cut its claw, it struck the hard claw and the sharp point of the arrow was blunted. The shaft or stick of the arrow became a snake that had no power to bite. It is called दुंदुभ (*duṃdubha*). This snake called दुंदुभ (*duṃdubha*), a snake that always moves in water, has no venom, and so is not capable of biting and killing any creature. The pointed metal (iron) tip of the arrow lost its sharpness, became blunt and became a snake with two heads called स्वज (*svaja*). The bird's feathers tied at the base of the arrow, so that it would go fast, became the hanging roots of banyan and other trees; the hanging shoots that come down from the branches of the banyan tree, touch the earth and become roots are called मंथावला (*maṃthāvalā*). The feathers tied on the arrow themselves became these मंथावल (*maṃthāvala*). The sinews (nerves), like threads, that had been used to bind the feathers to the arrow became worms of a special kind called गंडूपद (*gaṃḍūpada*). The wooden stick used for making that arrow became a snake without sight, without eyes, called अंधाहि (*aṃdhāhi*). The arrow shot by the Gandharva named Kṛśānu became the many kinds of creatures mentioned above.

सा यद्दक्षिणेन पदा समगृभ्णात्तत्प्रातःसवनमभवत्तद्गायत्री स्वमायतनमकुरुत तस्मात्तत्समृद्धतमं मन्यंते सर्वॆषां सवनानामग्रियो मुख्यो भवति श्रेष्ठतामश्नुते य एवं वेदाथ यत्सव्येन पदा समगृभ्णात्तन्माद्धंदिनं सवनमभवत्तद्विस्रंसत तद्विस्रस्तं नान्वाप्पोत्तूर्वं सवनं ते देवाः प्राजिज्ञानंत तस्मिंस्किष्टुभं छंदसामदधुरिंद्रं देवतानां तेन तत्समावद्वीर्यमभवत्तूर्वेण सवनेनोभाभ्यां सवनाभ्यां समावद्वीर्याभ्यां समावज्जामिभ्यां राध्योति य एवं वेदाथ यन्मुखेन समगृभ्णात्तृतीयसवनमभवत् ॥ (*sā yaddakṣiṇena padā samagṛbhṇāttatprātaḥsavanamabhavattadgāyatrī svamāyatanamakuruta tasmāttatsamṛddhatamaṃ manyaṃte sarvèṣāṃ savanānāmagriyo mukhyo bhavati śreṣṭhatāmaśnute ya evaṃ vedātha yatsavyena padā samagṛbhṇāttanmāddhaṃdinaṃ savanamabhavattadvisraṃsata tadvisrastaṃ nānvāppottūrvaṃ savanaṃ te devāḥ prājijñānaṃta tasmiṃskiṣṭubhaṃ chaṃdasāmadadhuriṃdraṃ devatānāṃ tena tatsamāvadvīryamabhavattūrveṇa savanenobhābhyāṃ savanābhyāṃ samāvadvīryābhyāṃ samāvajjāmibhyāṃ rādhyoti ya evaṃ vedātha yanmukhena samagṛbhṇāttṛtīyasavanamabhavat ‖*)

It has been said already that when the falcon brought Soma from heaven, it brought the Soma plants holding them with its two feet and its beak. While bringing them thus, the Soma (or Soma plant) that the bird held with its right foot became the morning pressing (प्रातःसवन (*prātaḥsavana*)). Gāyatrī made that, that is, the morning pressing, its own place. Therefore, among all the pressings the morning pressing is the best of all; this is the view of the sacrificial authorities. At the time of that morning pressing the use of mantras and the like is also special. Therefore the man who knows this matter well is regarded as the best and the chief among his equals. The portion of Soma that Gāyatrī brought holding it with the left foot became known as the midday pressing. Since, in holding and bringing the Soma plant with the left foot, the left foot did not have as much strength as the right, the plants kept slipping a little from the foot again and again. For this reason this midday pressing did not become equal to the morning pressing in strength and power. Seeing this, the gods deliberated and established, for the metres used in the midday pressing rite, the metre Triṣṭubh, and Indra, the best among the gods. Through the companionship of Indra, the best among the gods, and of the metre Triṣṭubh, dear to him, the midday pressing obtained a position equal to the morning pressing in strength, power and excellence. The man who knows this equality of the morning pressing and the midday pressing, and their strength, power and excellence, obtains abundance and well-being in wealth and the like, and lives happily. Then the portion of Soma that Gāyatrī, in the falcon form, brought holding it in her beak became known as the third pressing (तृतीयसवन (*tṛtīyasavana*)).

तस्य पतंती रसमधयत्तद्वीतरसं नान्वाप्नोत्पूर्वे सवने ते देवाः प्राजिज्ञासंत तत्पशुष्पपश्यंस्तद्यदाशिरमवनयंत्याज्येन पशुना चरंति तेन तत्समावद्वीर्यमभवत्पूर्वाभ्यां सवनाभ्यां सर्वैः सवनैः समावद्वीर्यैः समावज्जामिभी राध्योति य एवं वेद ॥ (*tasya pataṃtī rasamadhayattadvītarasaṃ nānvāpnotpūrve savane te devāḥ prājijñāsaṃta tatpaśuṣpapaśyaṃstadyadāśiramavanayaṃtyājyena paśunā caraṃti tena tatsamāvadvīryamabhavatpūrvābhyāṃ savanābhyāṃ sarvaiḥ savanaiḥ samāvadvīryaiḥ samāvajjāmibhī rādhyoti ya evaṃ veda ‖*)

While Gāyatrī (the falcon) was coming, holding the Soma plant in her beak, she kept drinking the juice in that plant a little at a time. Therefore, that is, because the juice in the Soma plants brought in the beak was not abundant but little, this third pressing did not become equal to the morning pressing and the midday pressing in strength, power and the like. So the gods, seeing this, deliberated and found that the suitable means for it lay in cattle. From cattle come milk and the offering materials obtained from it, such as curds and ājya (ghee). If milk is mixed with the Soma juice, which is little in the plants, that juice becomes strong. If oblations and the like are offered with ājya and with parts of the cattle, the pressing rite becomes complete. Therefore those who know the order of the sacrifice

The same meaning is stated in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā. The man who knows this matter, that is, that the three pressings, the morning, the midday and the third, have obtained a position equal in strength, power and excellence, lives happily, endowed with all abundance.

ते ना इमे इतरे छंदसी गायत्रीमभ्यवदेतां वित्तं नावक्षराण्यनुपर्यागुरिति नेत्यब्रवीद्गायत्री यथावित्तमेव न इति ते देवेषु प्रश्नमैतां ते देवा अब्रुवन्यथावित्तमेव न इति तस्माद्वाप्येतर्हि वित्यां व्याहुर्यथावित्तमेव न इति ततो वा अष्टाक्षरा गायत्र्यभवत्प्रक्षरा त्रिष्टुबेकाक्षरा जगती ॥ (*te nā ime itare chaṃdasī gāyatrīmabhyavadetāṃ vittaṃ nāvakṣarāṇyanuparyāguriti netyabravīdgāyatrī yathāvittameva na iti te deveṣu praśnamaitāṃ te devā abruvanyathāvittameva na iti tasmādvāpyetarhi vityāṃ vyāhuryathāvittameva na iti tato vā aṣṭākṣarā gāyatryabhavatprakṣarā triṣṭubekākṣarā jagatī ‖*)

(Ai. Brā. 13-28)

Earlier, when the metres Triṣṭubh and Jagatī flew to heaven to bring Soma, they did not reach heaven but grew weary half way and had to return; it was said already that for that reason, of their four syllables, the metre Jagatī lost three syllables and the metre Triṣṭubh lost one syllable. It was also said earlier that when Gāyatrī brought Soma, she brought back the four syllables that Triṣṭubh and Jagatī had lost, added them to her own four syllables, and made for herself eight syllables in all. Seeing this, the metres Triṣṭubh and Jagatī came to Gāyatrī and said: O Gāyatrī, it is well that you brought back the four syllables that we left behind in heaven. But you have joined them with your four syllables

### Printed p. 13

alone is not right. Since those syllables belong to us, you must give them back to us. So they asked. Gāyatrī refused their words and spoke thus: Whatever object is in our possession, that is, whatever we have acquired, it is right that it should belong to us. Even in worldly dealings, those who know the law say that if a thing is in the possession and enjoyment of someone, it belongs to him. Having gone to the world of heaven and accomplished the bringing of Soma, it is I alone, who brought back the syllables you had lost, who should have those syllables. I cannot give them to you. Hearing this, the Triṣṭubh and Jagatī went to the gods and made their complaint to them. The gods too upheld the very rule current in worldly dealings, that a thing belongs to the one in whose possession it is, and, supporting it, declared: What Gāyatrī says is right; since she brought those syllables, they should belong to her. How so? In battles and the like, whatever each of the victors gets from the things left behind by the defeated enemy forces fleeing, all agree that it is his own. So here too it should be. Thus the gods set out the rule. Therefore Gāyatrī, besides her own four syllables, had the four syllables of Triṣṭubh and Jagatī brought from heaven, and so came to have eight syllables in all. The metre Triṣṭubh was left with three syllables. The metre Jagatī was left with only one syllable.

साष्टाक्षरा गायत्री प्रातस्सवनमुदयच्छन्नाशक्कोत्रिष्टुप् त्र्यक्षरा माध्यंदिनं सवनमुद्यंतुं तां गायत्र्यब्रवीदायान्यपि मेऽत्रास्त्विति सा तथेत्यब्रवीत्रिष्टुप्तां वै मैतैरष्टाभिरक्षरैरुपसंधेहीति तथेति तामुपसमदधादेतद्वै तद्दायत्रै मध्यंदिने यन्मरुत्वतीयस्कोत्तरे प्रतिपदो यश्चानुचरः सैकादशाक्षरा भूत्वा माध्यंदिनं सवनमुदयच्छत् ॥ (*sāṣṭākṣarā gāyatrī prātassavanamudayacchannāśakkotriṣṭup tryakṣarā mādhyaṃdinaṃ savanamudyaṃtuṃ tāṃ gāyatryabravīdāyānyapi me 'trāstviti sā tathetyabravītriṣṭuptāṃ vai maitairaṣṭābhirakṣarairupasaṃdhehīti tatheti tāmupasamadadhādetadvai taddāyatrai madhyaṃdine yanmarutvatīyaskottare pratipado yaścānucaraḥ saikādaśākṣarā bhūtvā mādhyaṃdinaṃ savanamudayacchat ‖*)

It has been explained earlier that there are three pressings, the morning pressing (प्रातःसवन (*prātaḥsavana*)), the midday pressing (माध्यंदिनसवन (*mādhyaṃdinasavana*)) and the third pressing (तृतीयसवन (*tṛtīyasavana*)). There is the rule that the proceedings of the morning pressing are to be carried out with mantras of the Gāyatrī metre, the proceedings of the midday pressing with mantras of the Triṣṭubh metre, and the proceedings of the third pressing with mantras of the Jagatī metre.

Since the Gāyatrī metre had come to have eight syllables, it became complete, as it were, and capable of carrying out the proceedings of the morning pressing. But the metre Triṣṭubh, having lost one syllable and having only three syllables, was left without the capacity to carry out the proceedings of the midday pressing. Seeing the metre Triṣṭubh thus incapable, Gāyatrī said: O metre Triṣṭubh, since you have no power to carry out the work, I shall come to help you in the proceedings of the midday pressing.

### Printed p. 14

But in return for that help, I too must have a share in the midday pressing. If you agree to that, I shall help you. Hearing this, the metre Triṣṭubh agreed that it should be so, and said: O Gāyatrī, I now have only three syllables. If your eight syllables are joined with them, I shall have eleven syllables, and so I shall have the strength to carry out the work. Accordingly Gāyatrī joined her eight syllables with the three syllables of the metre Triṣṭubh. Because this was done, the metre Triṣṭubh too became capable of carrying out the work at the midday pressing. Triṣṭubh agreed, saying that it should be so, to Gāyatrī's asking that she too should have a share in the midday pressing; this is right. Now what is Gāyatrī's share in the midday pressing? In the last part of the Marutvatīya and third śastra mantras recited at the time of the midday pressing, in the tṛca (a tṛca is 3 ṛks) beginning आ त्वा रथं यथोतये (*ā tvā rathaṃ yathotaye*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-68-1)**NOTE**, and in the tṛca after it, विश्वासानरस्य वस्पतिः (*viśvāsānarasya vaspatiḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-68-4), two ṛks each are in the Gāyatrī metre. That is, in the tṛca आ त्वा रथं यथोतये (*ā tvā rathaṃ yathotaye*) the two ṛks तुविशुष्क तुविक्रतो (*tuviśuṣka tuvikrato*) and यस्य शे महिना महः (*yasya śe mahinā mahaḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-68-2, the 3rd ṛks), and in the second tṛca विश्वासानरस्य वस्पतिः (*viśvāsānarasya vaspatiḥ*) the two ṛks अभिष्टये सदावृधं (*abhiṣṭaye sadāvṛdhaṃ*) and परोमात्रम्यचेषं (*paromātramyaceṣaṃ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-68-, the 6th ṛks)**NOTE**, together make four mantras in the Gāyatrī metre in the pratipat tṛcas. Pratipat tṛca means the ṛks recited at the beginning. Further, the first ṛk of the tṛca recited at the end, called anucara, the mantra इदं वसो सुतमंधः (*idaṃ vaso sutamaṃdhaḥ*), is also in the Gāyatrī metre. Therefore the 4 ṛks of the Gāyatrī metre in the pratipat tṛcas and the 1 ṛk of the Gāyatrī metre in the anucara tṛca, in all 5 ṛks in the Gāyatrī metre, are in the midday pressing. These 5 ṛks are assigned as the share that should go to Gāyatrī in the midday pressing.

नाशक्कोज्जगत्येकाक्षरा तृतीयसवनमुद्यंतुं तां गायत्र्यब्रवीदायान्यपि मेऽत्रास्त्विति सा तथेत्यब्रवीज्जगती तां वै मैतैरेकादशभिरक्षरैरुपसंधे हीति तथेति तामुपसमदधादेतद्वै तद्गायत्र्यै तृतीयसवने यद्वैश्वदेवस्योत्तरे प्रतिपदो यश्चानुचरः सा द्वादशाक्षरा भूत्वा तृतीयसवनमुदयच्छत् ॥ (*nāśakkojjagatyekākṣarā tṛtīyasavanamudyaṃtuṃ tāṃ gāyatryabravīdāyānyapi me 'trāstviti sā tathetyabravījjagatī tāṃ vai maitairekādaśabhirakṣarairupasaṃdhe hīti tatheti tāmupasamadadhādetadvai tadgāyatryai tṛtīyasavane yadvaiśvadevasyottare pratipado yaścānucaraḥ sā dvādaśākṣarā bhūtvā tṛtīyasavanamudayacchat ‖*)

The metre Jagatī too, having lost three syllables, had only one syllable. Therefore it too became unable to carry out the proceedings of the third pressing. Then too Gāyatrī addressed Jagatī: O Jagatī, I shall help you in the proceedings of the third pressing. But I too must have a share in it. Jagatī agreed and said: Do so. That is, the eight syllables of Gāyatrī and the three syllables of Triṣṭubh together became eleven syllables and became capable of carrying out the work in the midday

### Printed p. 15

pressing. Those eleven syllables now joined the one syllable of Jagatī, became twelve syllables, and became capable of carrying out the proceedings of the third pressing. How so? At the end of the Vaiśvadeva śastra mantras recited at the time of the third pressing, the ṛks recited as pratipat tṛcas and the ṛks recited as anucara are also mantras of the Gāyatrī metre. In this way these mantras, as Jagatī-metre mantras of twelve syllables in all, became capable of accomplishing the work of the third pressing; this is the meaning.

ततो ना अष्टाक्षरा गायत्र्यभवदेकादशाक्षरा त्रिष्टुब् द्वादशाक्षरा जगती । सर्वैश्चंदोभिः समावद्वीर्यैः समावज्जामिभी राध्योति य एवं वेद । एकं वै सत्तत्रेधाभवत्तस्मादाहुर्दातव्यमेवं विदुष इत्येकं हि सत्तत्रेधाभवत् ॥ (*tato nā aṣṭākṣarā gāyatryabhavadekādaśākṣarā triṣṭub dvādaśākṣarā jagatī | sarvaiścaṃdobhiḥ samāvadvīryaiḥ samāvajjāmibhī rādhyoti ya evaṃ veda | ekaṃ vai sattatredhābhavattasmādāhurdātavyamevaṃ viduṣa ityekaṃ hi sattatredhābhavat ‖*)

(Ai. Brā. 13-28)

Therefore the metre Gāyatrī came to have eight syllables, the metre Triṣṭubh eleven syllables, and the metre Jagatī twelve syllables. These three metres obtained a position and standing equal to all the other metres, and excellence. The man who knows this obtains excellence and an honoured position. Because Gāyatrī's form, which earlier had only four syllables, afterwards acquired four syllables more and entered also into the metres Triṣṭubh and Jagatī (that is, because her eight syllables were mingled with the syllables of the metres Triṣṭubh and Jagatī), her form, that is, her greatness, became greater threefold. Therefore the wise who know the secret of dharma say that gold and the like should be given as a gift to the man who knows this greatness of Gāyatrī.

**The Episode of the Fetching of Soma (the Sauparṇa Story) is ended.**

**Some ṛks in the Ṛgveda and other texts on this subject, on the occasion of the fetching of Soma, and so on**

**The occasion of the fetching of Soma. The effort of the falcon.**

First: by सोमाहरणात् (*somāharaṇāt*), that is, because she brought the Soma that is the chief thing in the sacrifice, the special power of Gāyatrī has been indicated. This account of the fetching of Soma is described in all the most important places in the Saṃhitās and the Brāhmaṇas. First of all it is told in the form of a story in the Ṛk-saṃhitā itself. In the 26th and 27th sūktas of Ṛ. Saṃ. 4

### Printed p. 16

Indra, describing his own prowess, explains how that prowess increased through drinking Soma, and how Gāyatrī brought that Soma.

[Whether this description is the speech of the seer Vāmadeva or of Indra, and whether the deity of these sūktas is Indra or the Supreme Self, are also matters to be noted. On the present occasion that discussion is unnecessary.]

प्र सु ष विभ्यो मरुतो विरस्तु प्र श्येनः श्येनेभ्य आशुपत्ता । अचक्रया यत्त्वधया सुपर्णो हव्यं भरन्मनवे देवजुष्टं ॥ (*pra su ṣa vibhyo maruto virastu pra śyenaḥ śyenebhya āśupattā | acakrayā yattvadhayā suparṇo havyaṃ bharanmanave devajuṣṭaṃ ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-26-4)

O Maruts, may this Suparṇa, which brought swiftly to Manu, in a chariot without wheels, this Soma desired by the gods, be superior in valour to the other falcons and obtain greater honour.

भरद्यदि विरतो वेविजानः पथोरुणा मनोजवा असर्जि । तूयं ययौ मधुना सोम्येनोत श्रवो विविदे श्येनो अत्र ॥ (*bharadyadi virato vevijānaḥ pathoruṇā manojavā asarji | tūyaṃ yayau madhunā somyenota śravo vivide śyeno atra ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-26-5)

Because this falcon, having rebuked the Soma guardians, carried off the Soma and came flying through the atmosphere with the speed of thought and did a service, the whole company of falcons obtained honour on the earth.

ऋजीषी श्येनो ददमानो अंशुं परावतः शकुनो मंद्रं मदं । सोमं भरद्दादृहाणो देवानान्दिवो अमुष्मादुत्तरादादाय ॥ (*ṛjīṣī śyeno dadamāno aṃśuṃ parāvataḥ śakuno maṃdraṃ madaṃ | somaṃ bharaddādṛhāṇo devānāndivo amuṣmāduttarādādāya ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-26-6)

This bird, flying straight through the atmosphere at the speed of thought, followed by the gods, brought the Soma and increased in them the power of resolve and faith. By the fetching of Soma it did a service to the world.

आदाय श्येनो अभरत्सोमं सहस्रं सना अयुतं च साकं । अत्रा पुरंधिरजहादरातीर्मदे सोमस्य मूरा अमूरः ॥ (*ādāya śyeno abharatsomaṃ sahasraṃ sanā ayutaṃ ca sākaṃ | atrā puraṃdhirajahādarātīrmade somasya mūrā amūraḥ ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-26-7)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-26-7)**NOTE**

This falcon brought Soma from the world of heaven and helped in the performance of thousands of sacrifices. Satisfied by drinking this Soma, Indra's prowess increased and he beat down all his enemies.

Having fetched the Soma, as the falcon was coming down from the atmosphere, expressing joy, Kṛśānu, one of the Soma guardians and an archer, also came down with the speed of thought, pursued it and shot an arrow at it with his bow.

ऋजिष्य ईमिंद्रावतो न भुज्यं श्येनो जभार बृहतो अधि ष्ठोः । अंतः पतत्पतत्र्यस्य पर्णमध यामनि प्रसितस्य तद्वेः ॥ (*ṛjiṣya īmiṃdrāvato na bhujyaṃ śyeno jabhāra bṛhato adhi ṣṭhoḥ | aṃtaḥ patatpatatryasya parṇamadha yāmani prasitasya tadveḥ ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-27-4)

As this falcon was coming down from the atmosphere, which is under Indra's lordship, it was struck by Kṛśānu's arrow, lost one feather of its body in its struggle, and was wounded.

When the story is thus, it becomes laborious to find out what the essential meaning is here. Even so, in the Aitareya Āraṇyaka, introducing the seer Vāmadeva, the author of this sūkta, this same occasion is explained from the point of view of the principle. There, quoting from the Ṛk-saṃhitā the ṛk

गर्भे नु सन्नन्वेषामवेदमहं देवानां जनिमानि विश्वा । शतं मा पुर आयसीररक्षन्नध श्येनो जवसा निरदीयं ॥ (*garbhe nu sannanveṣāmavedamahaṃ devānāṃ janimāni viśvā | śataṃ mā pura āyasīrarakṣannadha śyeno javasā niradīyaṃ ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-27-1)

it says गर्भ एव एतच्छयानो वामदेव एवमुवाच (*garbha eva etacchayāno vāmadeva evamuvāca*) (Ai. Ā. 2-5), that Vāmadeva, while still in the womb, understood and declared this principle. The meaning of the above mantra is that, while still in the womb, Vāmadeva came to know the origin and nature of all the gods, and that although there was a bondage made of hundreds of iron bars, he escaped it like a falcon and flew towards the earth. The purport of this part is not easy. Śaṅkarācārya, Ānandatīrtha and Sāyaṇa too, in explaining this occasion, hold that the purport is that Vāmadeva entered hundreds of bodies in the form of bonds, finally became a knower, was freed from them and attained liberation. But if one examines the context of the Aitareya Āraṇyaka, it says स एवं विद्वान् अस्माच्चरीरभेदादूर्व्व उत्क्रम्यामुष्मिन् स्वर्गे लोके सर्वान्यामानाप्तामृतः समभवत्समभवत् । (*sa evaṃ vidvān asmāccarīrabhedādūrvva utkramyāmuṣmin svarge loke sarvānyāmānāptāmṛtaḥ samabhavatsamabhavat |*) (Ai. A. 2-5)**NOTE**. It says that Vāmadeva, who knew this principle while still in the womb, gave up the earthly body and obtained liberation. Therefore the sentence of the mantra Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-27-1 is understood to apply to Vāmadeva himself.

**2. Gāyatrī, of the form of a bird, is the falcon.**

Sāyaṇa alone says:

श्येनभावं समास्थाय गर्भाद्योगेन निस्सृतः । ऋषिगर्भे शयानःसन् ब्रूते गर्भे नु सन्निति ॥ (*śyenabhāvaṃ samāsthāya garbhādyogena nissṛtaḥ | ṛṣigarbhe śayānaḥsan brūte garbhe nu sanniti ‖*)

(Introduction to the commentary on Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-27-1)

that Vāmadeva himself, freed from the womb through the greatness of his yoga, recites this sentence. English critics such as Ludwig have not examined what the reason and the basis are for this falcon being described in the form of Gāyatrī. In describing this same occasion the falcon is referred to by the word सुपर्ण (*suparṇa*) and also by the word Gāyatrī. In some places, in explaining the word falcon, Sāyaṇa has explained शैनः शंसनीयगतिमान्पक्षी पक्ष्याकारा गायत्री (*śainaḥ śaṃsanīyagatimānpakṣī pakṣyākārā gāyatrī*). Since in this way different words, falcon, Suparṇa and Gāyatrī, are used in these contexts, an exact meaning cannot be given here without knowing the narration of this story. Therefore, after knowing this part of the story told in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā, the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa and the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa, one can examine what the essential meaning is in the matter of Gāyatrī's bringing Soma.
