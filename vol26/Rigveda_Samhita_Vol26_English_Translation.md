**The first chapter in the seventh Aṣṭaka**

**The Sūktas of the ninth Maṇḍala**

**Introduction to the Maṇḍala**

**On the Soma Plant**

We have given explanations about the Soma plant many times. In all those places, many descriptions have been stated: that Soma is the juice drawn from a kind of creeper called Soma; that it brings zeal and joy to those who drink it; and that, when the gods are invited, they should come to the sacrificial ground and drink the Soma juice. Sūktas that praise Soma as a deity are very rare in the other Maṇḍalas, except the ninth Maṇḍala. All the sūktas in the ninth Maṇḍala praise the deity Soma alone. Although a detailed explanation about Soma would be necessary on this occasion, a brief explanation here will not be out of place.

### Printed p. 2

Since the Soma sacrifices are very important among the rites of the Ṛgveda, it is natural that the deity Soma too is one of the principal deities. The 114 sūktas in the ninth Maṇḍala, together with 6 other sūktas, in all 120 sūktas, praise the deity Soma. Besides these, 4–5 sūkta-portions, and 5–7 sūktas in company with Indra, Agni, Pūṣan or the Rudras, praise the deity Soma. The name सोम (*soma*) occurs, alone or in compounds, about 120 times. From the point of view of number, the deity Soma stands third. Personification is very sparing. Since the Soma plant and its juices are mentioned again and again, it is impossible to forget them altogether. For that very reason, references to the form of that deity, to his limbs, or to his movements and the like are rare. Even where feats of valour are mentioned, they are the same as those of other deities, and are weak or of secondary importance. Like the other deities, he too is invited, under the name Indu or Soma, to come to the sacrifice, to sit on the seat of darbha grass and to receive the offerings. The whole of the ninth Maṇḍala deals with the subject of the Soma juice: pressing the juice out with stones, filtering it through a woollen sieve into wooden vessels, offering it through Agni as a drink for the gods (1-94-14; 5-51-1; 8-43-11 and so on), or the drinking of it by the priests, and similar matters, are what is stated.

The Soma juice is mixed in three ways (त्राशिरः (*trāśiraḥ*) 5-27-5): mixed with milk (गवाशिर (*gavāśira*)), mixed with curds (दध्याशिर (*dadhyāśira*)) and mixed with barley (यवाशिर (*yavāśira*)); this mixture is called the garment-wearing (वस्त्र (*vastra*), वासः (*vāsaḥ*), आत्म (*ātma*)). It is also called “निर्णिक् (*nirṇik*)” (a shining garment, 9-14-5). This word is also applied to the sieve (9-30-7). For this very reason Soma is described as beautiful (9-34-4 and so on) and as abundantly adorned with ornaments (9-81-2). Rarely, mixing with ghee (9-82-2) or with water is also mentioned; but for these the word आशिर (*āśira*) has not been used.

In the sacrifices there is a rite called अप्यायन (*apyāyana*) (soaking again in water the Soma plants that have been half pressed) (Mai. Saṃ. 4-5-5). In the Ṛgveda this root (अप्या (*apyā*)) is used in connection with Soma. But here the root is used to suggest that Soma is like the moon. In one place (9-31-4) alone this ritual rite may be indicated; it is said that the Soma juice swells like a river or the sea (9-64-8, 9-107-12). Soma is pressed three times a day. The Ṛbhu gods are invited to the evening pressing (4-33-11 and so on), Indra alone (4-36-7) to the midday pressing (3-21-1 and 2; 8-37-1), and Indra, to drink first, to the morning pressing (10-112-1).

### Printed p. 3

The Soma juice has an inexpressible power that is not found in ordinary food or drink. By this power it enlivens men, or whoever drinks it, and makes them accomplish even deeds that are not possible for men. For that very reason it has given room to the idea that it is a divine drink, equal to ambrosia, indeed ambrosia itself. It would not be an exaggeration to say that those who drink it become immortal. The intoxicating drink dear to the gods (9-85-2) and bestowing immortality (1-84-4), pressed by men and mixed with milk, this juice is drunk by all the gods (9-109-15); from it they quickly feel exhilaration (8-2-18) and, having drunk, become gladdened (8-58-11). Soma is eternal (1-43-9; 8-88-12; 9-3-1 and so on); the gods drank it to attain immortality (9-106-8). He (the deity Soma) grants immortality to the gods (1-91-6; 9-108-3) and to men (1-91-1; 8-89-3). There are descriptions such as that he places his worshipper, made immortal, in the world that is the seat of everlasting splendour and fame, imperishable, the dwelling place of Vivasvat (9-113-7 and 8), and so on.

By drinking the Soma juice a man's zeal increases. But the Soma juice is chiefly offered to the gods. Therefore the joy and zeal born of Soma arise in the gods. The effect of the intoxication born of Soma falls mostly upon Indra. It helps Indra to subdue the opposing powers in the atmosphere. It is stated many times that the special strength and capacity needed for fighting Vṛtra came from the Soma juice (8-81-17 and so on). In the intoxication produced by the Soma juice, Indra defeats all enemies (9-1-10), and no one can withstand in battle Indra who has drunk Soma (6-47-1). Soma is the very self of Indra (9-85-3); the benevolent friend (10-25-9) increases Indra's prowess (9-76-2) and helps in the slaying of Vṛtra (9-61-22). Accompanied by Soma, Indra made the waters flow for men and slew Ahi (4-28-1). Hence at times Soma himself is said to be Indra's thunderbolt (9-72-7; 9-77-1; 9-111-3). This Soma juice, belonging to Indra, becomes the thunderbolt that wins a thousand victories (9-47-3). The Soma juice is the intoxicating drink that can destroy hundreds of fortresses (9-48-2), and it is the plant that slays Vṛtra and causes intoxication (6-17-11). In this way the deity Soma, like Indra, is called the slayer of Vṛtra and the breaker of fortresses (9-88-4). The personal epithet of Indra, वृत्रहा (*vṛtrahā*), is applied to Soma five or six times.

Because it is the most important plant, the Soma plant is also called the lord of all plants (9-114-2) and king (9-97-18 and 19), the lord of the forest (वनस्पति (*vanaspati*)) (1-91-6; 9-12-7), and for all plants

### Printed p. 4

the root (1-91-22). In the Brāhmaṇas all trees have the name सौम्य (*saumya*) (belonging to Soma) (Śa. Brā. 12-1-1-2). Moreover, like the other principal deities, Soma too has the name of king. He is king of the rivers (9-89-2) and of the whole world (9-97-58). He is king or father of the gods (9-86-10; 9-87-2; 9-109-4); king of the god-men (9-97-24), and of the Brāhmaṇas (Vā. Saṃ. 9-40; Tai. Saṃ. 1-8-10; Mai. Saṃ. 2-6-9). That he is a deity is stated many times; only in one place is it said that he is a deity pressed (made the one to be pressed) for the sake of a deity (9-3-6 and 7).

In later literature सोम (*soma*) is a name of the moon. When he has waned because the gods have drunk him, the sun fills him up. In the Chāndogya Upaniṣad (5-10-1) it is said that the moon is Soma, that he is the food of the gods, and that they drink him up. In the Brāhmaṇas the identity of the moon and Soma is a common matter. King Soma is the food of the gods, and he is the moon (Śa. Brā. 1-6-4-5). That plant used in the sacrifice, or its juice, indicates the moon deity (Kau. Brā. 7-10; 4-4)**NOTE**. The very opinion of the Brāhmaṇas is that the moon wanes day by day because the Fathers and the gods eat him, who is of the nature of ambrosia. In the Yajurveda it is said that the daughters of Prajāpati are the wives of the moon, and that they are the cluster of stars. In the Atharvaveda (Ā. Va. 7-81-3 and 4; 11-6-7 and so on)**NOTE**, wherever Soma is mentioned, in general the moon is meant. Among the passages that speak of the identity of the moon and Soma, the 85th sūkta of the tenth Maṇḍala, which describes the marriage of Soma and Sūryā, is the chief; here Soma is in the lap of the stars. It is said that no one consumes the Soma known to the priests, and that there is a difference between this Soma and what they must press out. Its secret is known only to the Brāhmaṇas. Descriptions are indeed there of Soma as heavenly, radiant, dispelling darkness and so on; it has the name इंदु (*iṃdu*) (6-44-21). It is compared to the moon reflected in the water in the vessels (8-71-8; compare 1-105-1). The Soma described as a drop (द्रप्प (*drappa*): drop, bindu) and going to the sea (10-123-8) must also be the moon.

In the opinion of some, Soma is the moon alone and not another deity at all. The whole of the ninth Maṇḍala praises this moon. Wherever this word occurs in the Ṛgveda, it indicates only these two, the Soma plant or the moon. They argue that the juice that comes from that plant is itself ambrosia, that the moon is the abode of that ambrosia, that the moon is a more important deity than the sun, and that Indra comes after him. Others again say that there is not enough basis, or none, for the above opinion in the Ṛgveda. However much support the identity of the moon and Soma may find in later works, in the Ṛgveda, among the hundreds of ṛks concerning the deity Soma

this is hinted at here and there only rarely, and nowhere is it stated clearly. Moreover, none of the commentators has said that this point has a basis in the Veda.

**Derivation of the word Soma**

The Nirukta-writers, ओषधिस्सोमः सुनोतेर्यदेनमभिषुण्वंति (*oṣadhissomaḥ sunoteryadenamabhiṣuṇvaṃti*): a herb well known by the name Soma, because they press it; this**NOTE**

आदाय श्येनो अभरत्सोमं सहस्रं सना आयुतं च साकं । अत्रा पुरंधिरजहादरातीर्मदे सोमस्य मूरा अमूरः ॥ (*ādāya śyeno abharatsomaṃ sahasraṃ sanā āyutaṃ ca sākaṃ | atrā puraṃdhirajahādarātīrmade somasya mūrā amūraḥ ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-26-7)

They have explained the greatness of Soma: that the Gāyatrī metre, assuming the form of the falcon, brought it from heaven to the earth, and that, on occasions when Indra drinks this Soma, the sacrificers perform sacrifices with a thousand pressings and give away gifts of ten thousand in number. And बहुलमस्य नैघंटुकं वृत्तं आश्चर्यमिव प्राधान्येन (*bahulamasya naighaṃṭukaṃ vṛttaṃ āścaryamiva prādhānyena*): the praise of this Soma also indicates its importance, and it is marvellous. This is recited in the ninth Maṇḍala. And the glory of this Soma is stated in the form of praise in the ṛk

स्वादिष्ठया मदिष्टया पवस्व सोम धारया । इंद्राय पातने सुतः । (*svādiṣṭhayā madiṣṭayā pavasva soma dhārayā | iṃdrāya pātane sutaḥ |*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-1-1) **GLOSS**

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-1-1)**NOTE**

सोमं मन्यते पपिवान् यत्संपिषंत्योषधिं । सोमं यं ब्रह्माणो विदुर्न तस्याश्नाति कश्चन ॥ (*somaṃ manyate papivān yatsaṃpiṣaṃtyoṣadhiṃ | somaṃ yaṃ brahmāṇo vidurna tasyāśnāti kaścana ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-85-3)

The word सोमं (*somaṃ*) here is understood to mean the moon. In this ṛk both the Soma plant and the moon are praised. But Yāska has described in this way the greater importance of this Soma over the well-known moon.

सोमं मन्यते पपिवान् यत्संपिषंत्योषधिमिति वृथासुतमसोममाह सोमं यं ब्रह्माणो विदुरिति न तस्याश्नाति कश्चनायजैत्यधियज्ञं । अथाधिदैवतं सोमं मन्यते पपिवान् यत्संपिषंत्योषधिमिति यजुस्सुतमसोममाह सोमं यं ब्राहणो विदुश्चंद्रमसं न तस्याश्नाति कश्चनादेव इति । (*somaṃ manyate papivān yatsaṃpiṣaṃtyoṣadhimiti vṛthāsutamasomamāha somaṃ yaṃ brahmāṇo viduriti na tasyāśnāti kaścanāyajaityadhiyajñaṃ | athādhidaivataṃ somaṃ manyate papivān yatsaṃpiṣaṃtyoṣadhimiti yajussutamasomamāha somaṃ yaṃ brāhaṇo viduścaṃdramasaṃ na tasyāśnāti kaścanādeva iti |*) (Ni. 11-4)

Although those who prepare rasāyanas and the like bring Soma, press out the juice and drink it, they do not become famous as Soma-drinkers at the sacrifice; therefore, since it is used as a limb of the sacrifice

### Printed p. 6

(for the sacrifice), Soma is praised as producing, only for the sacrificer, the fame of being a Soma-drinker in the prescribed manner. In the adhidaiva view also (where the word Soma is explained as meaning the moon), the Soma that is a limb of the sacrifice is indicated by the word Soma. By the śruti sentence “सोमो नूनमेष यद्देवानामन्नं (*somo nūnameṣa yaddevānāmannaṃ*)”, even though the moon is denoted by the word Soma, he is food for the gods alone. Therefore, although by the mantra सोमं मन्यते (*somaṃ manyate*) two kinds of meaning, adhiyajña and adhidaiva, are given for the word Soma, the sacrificial authorities have assigned the primacy in the sacrifice to the Soma in the form of the herb.

यत्त्वा देव प्रपिबंति तत आ स्यायसे पुनः । वायुस्सोमस्य रक्षिता समानां मास अकृतिः ॥ (*yattvā deva prapibaṃti tata ā syāyase punaḥ | vāyussomasya rakṣitā samānāṃ māsa akṛtiḥ ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-85-5)

यत्वा देव प्रपिबंति तत आप्यायसे पुनरिति नाराशंसानभिप्रेत्य पूर्वपक्षापरपक्षाविति ना वायुसोमस्य रक्षिता वायुमस्य रक्षितारमाह साहचर्यात् रसहरणाद्वा ! समानां संवत्सराणां मास आकृतिः सोमो रूपविशेषैरोषधिश्चंद्रमा वा । (*yatvā deva prapibaṃti tata āpyāyase punariti nārāśaṃsānabhipretya pūrvapakṣāparapakṣāviti nā vāyusomasya rakṣitā vāyumasya rakṣitāramāha sāhacaryāt rasaharaṇādvā ! samānāṃ saṃvatsarāṇāṃ māsa ākṛtiḥ somo rūpaviśeṣairoṣadhiścaṃdramā vā |*)

In the sense that concerns the sacrifice, the priests, the sacrificer and others drink Soma at the three pressings. Hence this Soma is the one who makes (the gods) swell. Although Vāyu sucks the juice of the herbs, he does not suck the Soma juice, so he is its protector. Those who make rasāyanas and know the Āyurveda say that this Soma obtains growth of leaves (pṛṇa) in accordance with the tithis of the bright fortnights of each month, and waning (apacita) of leaves in accordance with the tithis of the dark fortnights. In the adhidaiva view, the moon, denoted by the word Soma, becomes by his digits the one who refreshes the gods in accordance with the respective tithis. The gods also drink the digits of the moon. The moon obtains waxing and waning in the respective fortnights through his digits. Thus, in both ways, adhiyajña and adhidaiva, a likeness (equal state) of the moon is stated for the Soma plant, its identity with Soma is established, and the Soma plant is praised as full of abundant greatness.

**The Story of the Fetching of Soma**

सोमो वै राजामुष्मिंल्लोक आसीत्तं देवाश्च ऋषयश्चाभ्यध्यायन् कथमयमस्मात्सोमो राजागच्छेदिति तेऽब्रुवंश्वंदांसि यूयं न इमं सोमं राजानमाहरतेति तथेति ते सुपर्णा भूत्वोदपतंस्ते यत्सुपर्णा भूत्वोदपतंस्तदेतत्सौपर्णमित्याख्यानविद आचक्षते ॥ (*somo vai rājāmuṣmiṃlloka āsīttaṃ devāśca ṛṣayaścābhyadhyāyan kathamayamasmātsomo rājāgacchediti te 'bruvaṃśvaṃdāṃsi yūyaṃ na imaṃ somaṃ rājānamāharateti tatheti te suparṇā bhūtvodapataṃste yatsuparṇā bhūtvodapataṃstadetatsauparṇamityākhyānavida ācakṣate ‖*)

(Ai. Brā. 13-25) **GLOSS**

### Printed p. 7

In the beginning King Soma (the Soma plant; there is a custom of calling the Soma plant King Soma out of respect) was in heaven. He was not in this world. Knowing this, the gods and the seers considered how to bring this Soma here. Then they discussed the matter, and, understanding that the metres have the power to bring Soma from heaven to the earth, they addressed the metres and prayed: O metres, for our sake, that is, to be used in our sacrifice, you should bring to the earth the Soma that is in heaven. Accepting their prayer, the metres assumed the forms of certain birds well known in the world and flew off to heaven. Since winged birds are called सुपर्ण (*suparṇa*), and since these birds engaged in the fetching of Soma, this episode of the fetching of Soma is called the Sauparṇa story in the Purāṇas and similar works:

छंदांसि वै तत्सोमं राजानमच्छाचरंस्तानि ह तर्हि चतुरक्षराणि चतुरक्षराण्येव छंदांस्यासन्सा जगती चतुरक्षरा प्रथनोदपतत्सा पतित्वार्धमध्वनो गत्वाश्राम्यत्सा परास्य त्रीण्यक्षराण्येकाक्षरा भूत्वा दीक्षां च तपश्च हरंती पुनरभ्यवापतत्तस्मात्तस्य वित्ता दीक्षा वित्तं तपो यस्य पशवः संति जागता हि पशवो जगती हि तानाहरत् ॥ (*chaṃdāṃsi vai tatsomaṃ rājānamacchācaraṃstāni ha tarhi caturakṣarāṇi caturakṣarāṇyeva chaṃdāṃsyāsansā jagatī caturakṣarā prathanodapatatsā patitvārdhamadhvano gatvāśrāmyatsā parāsya trīṇyakṣarāṇyekākṣarā bhūtvā dīkṣāṃ ca tapaśca haraṃtī punarabhyavāpatattasmāttasya vittā dīkṣā vittaṃ tapo yasya paśavaḥ saṃti jāgatā hi paśavo jagatī hi tānāharat ‖*)

(Ai. Brā. 13-25)

Then the metres, Gāyatrī and the others, assuming the forms of birds, got ready to fly to heaven. At that time all the metres, Gāyatrī and the others, had only four syllables (in each pāda); no metre had more than four syllables. First the metre Jagatī said that it would bring Soma, and flew towards heaven, where Soma was. When it had flown half the way it was worn out with weariness, had no strength to fly further, and had to turn back. Through the weariness of having gone so far, the metre Jagatī lost three of its four syllables. It had to return with the one syllable that remained. In this way, returning without the strength to bring Soma, it brought with it the initiation (दीक्षॆ (*dīkṣè*)) connected with the Soma sacrifice, that is, the rite of initiation in the form of the दक्षिणीयॆष्टि (*dakṣiṇīyèṣṭi*), and the austerity in the form of drinking milk and the like. Therefore the initiation is undertaken with the ṛks of the metre Jagatī. The metre Jagatī has the power to increase wealth in cattle; therefore initiation and wealth come easily to a man who has wealth in cattle. Because the metre Jagatī brought cattle, it is said that the metre Jagatī has the power to give cattle. This is stated in the Yajurveda:

सा पशुभिश्च दीक्षया चागच्छत्तस्माज्जगतीछंदसां पशव्यतमा तस्मात्पशुमंतं दीक्षोपनमति (*sā paśubhiśca dīkṣayā cāgacchattasmājjagatīchaṃdasāṃ paśavyatamā tasmātpaśumaṃtaṃ dīkṣopanamati*) (Tai. Saṃ. 6-1-6-2).

Then the metre Triṣṭubh said that it would bring Soma, and sprang into the atmosphere. But before it had gone a little more than half the way, it was worn out with weariness, left one of its four syllables there itself and returned with the remaining three syllables. On returning it brought with it the dakṣiṇā (the order of the fees given to the priests in the sacrifice). Because the metre Triṣṭubh brought the dakṣiṇā in this way, and because the metre Triṣṭubh is connected with the midday pressing (माध्यंदिनसवन (*mādhyaṃdinasavana*)), there is the custom of giving the dakṣiṇā (honorarium) to the priests at the time of the midday pressing performed in the sacrifice.

ते देवा अब्रुवन् गायत्रीं त्वं न इमं सोमं राजानमाहरेति सा तथेत्यब्रवीत्तां वै मा सर्वेण स्वस्त्ययनेनानुमंत्रयध्वमिति तथेति सोदपतत्तां देवाः सर्वेण स्वस्त्ययनेनान्वमंत्रयंत प्रेति चेति चेत्येतद्वै सर्वं स्वस्त्ययनं तत्प्रेति चेति तद्योऽस्य प्रियः स्यात्तमेतेनानुमंत्रयेत प्रेति चेति स्तस्येव गच्छति स्वस्ति पुनरागच्छति ॥ (*te devā abruvan gāyatrīṃ tvaṃ na imaṃ somaṃ rājānamāhareti sā tathetyabravīttāṃ vai mā sarveṇa svastyayanenānumaṃtrayadhvamiti tatheti sodapatattāṃ devāḥ sarveṇa svastyayanenānvamaṃtrayaṃta preti ceti cetyetadvai sarvaṃ svastyayanaṃ tatpreti ceti tadyo 'sya priyaḥ syāttametenānumaṃtrayeta preti ceti stasyeva gacchati svasti punarāgacchati ‖*)

(Ai. Brā. 13-26)

Seeing that the metres Jagatī and Triṣṭubh had returned without the capacity to bring Soma, the gods went to the metre Gāyatrī and prayed: O Gāyatrī, you must bring Soma for us from heaven. Gāyatrī agreed that it should be so, and said to the gods: O gods, as you say, I shall go to heaven to bring Soma. But during the time of my journey, and so that I may return safely, you must recite the blessing mantras of benediction (स्वस्त्ययन (*svastyayana*)) and pray, for those who come, that I may return safely. Having said this, it assumed the form of the falcon bird and flew off by the path of the atmosphere. The gods agreed that it should be so, and kept reciting the benediction mantras that Gāyatrī might return safely. And which is that mantra? It is प्रा च च (*prā ca ca*)**NOTE**. Here pra is one mantra; ā is the second mantra; there are two letters ca to indicate the sense of joining the two together. The meaning of these mantras is: go safely to the place where Soma is, take it, and come back again safely. There is no better benediction mantra than this. Therefore, in the world, friends, kinsmen and others recite this mantra when they have to make a journey. It is well known that through the power of reciting this mantra, those who have travelled come back safe and reach home.

सा पतित्वा सोमपालान् भीषयित्वा पद्भ्यां च मुखेन च सोमं राजानं समगृभ्णाद्यानि चेतरे छंदसी अक्षराण्यजहितां तानि चोपसमगृभ्णात् ॥ (*sā patitvā somapālān bhīṣayitvā padbhyāṃ ca mukhena ca somaṃ rājānaṃ samagṛbhṇādyāni cetare chaṃdasī akṣarāṇyajahitāṃ tāni copasamagṛbhṇāt ‖*)

Then Gāyatrī, in the form of the falcon, flew to heaven and frightened the Soma guardians, स्नान (*snāna*), भ्राज (*bhrāja*) and the others, who were guarding Soma there, with its harsh cry and with weapons such as its sharp beak and claws. Seeing it, the Soma guardians ran off a little way. Then the falcon seized the Soma (the Soma plants) there with its two feet and its beak, and tried to fly back, that is, to the earth. Who were the Soma guardians who protected Soma in heaven? What are their names? This matter is stated in the following Yajurveda mantras:

That is: स्नान (*snāna*), भ्राज (*bhrāja*), अंघारि (*aṃghāri*), बंभारि (*baṃbhāri*), हस्त (*hasta*), सुहस्त (*suhasta*) and कृशानु (*kṛśānu*) are the Soma guardians. O Soma guardians, guard carefully this Soma that is under your charge, so that no one may steal it; this is the meaning of the first mantra. The meaning of the second mantra is that स्नान (*snāna*), भ्राज (*bhrāja*) and the other Soma protectors were guarding the Soma that was in heaven. When the falcon thus carried off the Soma, which was under the protection of the Soma guardians स्नान (*snāna*), भ्राज (*bhrāja*) and the others mentioned above, and brought it to the earth, it also brought back the four syllables that the metres Jagatī and Triṣṭubh had lost. The Gandharvas who guarded Soma in heaven were seven, स्वान् (*svān*)**NOTE**, भ्राज (*bhrāja*) and the others. Among them कृशानु (*kṛśānu*) was the seventh. Seeing Gāyatrī carrying Soma away in the form of the falcon, this Kṛśānu pursued it, and, since he could not catch it, shot an arrow from the bow in his hand. It struck the claw of the falcon's left foot, and it

was cut off. That cut-off claw (nail) became a porcupine (Porcupine) about the size of a monkey. This porcupine has on the tip of its tail many sharp quills, a span in extent, hard and pointed like iron rods. Such an animal is called शल्य (*śalya*) or शल्यक (*śalyaka*) (porcupine). Because this animal arose from the claw cut off from the falcon, its quills are as very sharp as a claw. When the claw was cut off and fell by the arrow, the liquid वशा (*vaśā*) or fat that was near the claw flowed out. This fat was transformed into a she-goat, अजा (*ajā*), आवि (*āvi*) (female goat). Because this goat arose from Gāyatrī in the form of the falcon, it was fixed as a fit offering in sacrifices and the like, suitable for the deities. This matter is indicated in the Yajurveda and other texts by mantras such as

एतामेव वशा मादित्येभ्यः कामायालभेत (*etāmeva vaśā mādityebhyaḥ kāmāyālabheta*) **GLOSS**

When the arrow shot at the falcon by the Gandharva named Kṛśānu cut its claw, it struck the hard claw and the sharp point of the arrow was blunted. The shaft or stick of the arrow became a snake that had no power to bite. It is called दुंदुभ (*duṃdubha*). This snake called दुंदुभ (*duṃdubha*), a snake that always moves in water, has no venom, and so is not capable of biting and killing any creature. The pointed metal (iron) tip of the arrow lost its sharpness, became blunt and became a snake with two heads called स्वज (*svaja*). The bird's feathers tied at the base of the arrow, so that it would go fast, became the hanging roots of banyan and other trees; the hanging shoots that come down from the branches of the banyan tree, touch the earth and become roots are called मंथावला (*maṃthāvalā*). The feathers tied on the arrow themselves became these मंथावल (*maṃthāvala*). The sinews (nerves), like threads, that had been used to bind the feathers to the arrow became worms of a special kind called गंडूपद (*gaṃḍūpada*). The wooden stick used for making that arrow became a snake without sight, without eyes, called अंधाहि (*aṃdhāhi*). The arrow shot by the Gandharva named Kṛśānu became the many kinds of creatures mentioned above.

सा यद्दक्षिणेन पदा समगृभ्णात्तत्प्रातःसवनमभवत्तद्गायत्री स्वमायतनमकुरुत तस्मात्तत्समृद्धतमं मन्यंते सर्वॆषां सवनानामग्रियो मुख्यो भवति श्रेष्ठतामश्नुते य एवं वेदाथ यत्सव्येन पदा समगृभ्णात्तन्माद्धंदिनं सवनमभवत्तद्विस्रंसत तद्विस्रस्तं नान्वाप्पोत्तूर्वं सवनं ते देवाः प्राजिज्ञानंत तस्मिंस्किष्टुभं छंदसामदधुरिंद्रं देवतानां तेन तत्समावद्वीर्यमभवत्तूर्वेण सवनेनोभाभ्यां सवनाभ्यां समावद्वीर्याभ्यां समावज्जामिभ्यां राध्योति य एवं वेदाथ यन्मुखेन समगृभ्णात्तृतीयसवनमभवत् ॥ (*sā yaddakṣiṇena padā samagṛbhṇāttatprātaḥsavanamabhavattadgāyatrī svamāyatanamakuruta tasmāttatsamṛddhatamaṃ manyaṃte sarvèṣāṃ savanānāmagriyo mukhyo bhavati śreṣṭhatāmaśnute ya evaṃ vedātha yatsavyena padā samagṛbhṇāttanmāddhaṃdinaṃ savanamabhavattadvisraṃsata tadvisrastaṃ nānvāppottūrvaṃ savanaṃ te devāḥ prājijñānaṃta tasmiṃskiṣṭubhaṃ chaṃdasāmadadhuriṃdraṃ devatānāṃ tena tatsamāvadvīryamabhavattūrveṇa savanenobhābhyāṃ savanābhyāṃ samāvadvīryābhyāṃ samāvajjāmibhyāṃ rādhyoti ya evaṃ vedātha yanmukhena samagṛbhṇāttṛtīyasavanamabhavat ‖*)

It has been said already that when the falcon brought Soma from heaven, it brought the Soma plants holding them with its two feet and its beak. While bringing them thus, the Soma (or Soma plant) that the bird held with its right foot became the morning pressing (प्रातःसवन (*prātaḥsavana*)). Gāyatrī made that, that is, the morning pressing, its own place. Therefore, among all the pressings the morning pressing is the best of all; this is the view of the sacrificial authorities. At the time of that morning pressing the use of mantras and the like is also special. Therefore the man who knows this matter well is regarded as the best and the chief among his equals. The portion of Soma that Gāyatrī brought holding it with the left foot became known as the midday pressing. Since, in holding and bringing the Soma plant with the left foot, the left foot did not have as much strength as the right, the plants kept slipping a little from the foot again and again. For this reason this midday pressing did not become equal to the morning pressing in strength and power. Seeing this, the gods deliberated and established, for the metres used in the midday pressing rite, the metre Triṣṭubh, and Indra, the best among the gods. Through the companionship of Indra, the best among the gods, and of the metre Triṣṭubh, dear to him, the midday pressing obtained a position equal to the morning pressing in strength, power and excellence. The man who knows this equality of the morning pressing and the midday pressing, and their strength, power and excellence, obtains abundance and well-being in wealth and the like, and lives happily. Then the portion of Soma that Gāyatrī, in the falcon form, brought holding it in her beak became known as the third pressing (तृतीयसवन (*tṛtīyasavana*)).

तस्य पतंती रसमधयत्तद्वीतरसं नान्वाप्नोत्पूर्वे सवने ते देवाः प्राजिज्ञासंत तत्पशुष्पपश्यंस्तद्यदाशिरमवनयंत्याज्येन पशुना चरंति तेन तत्समावद्वीर्यमभवत्पूर्वाभ्यां सवनाभ्यां सर्वैः सवनैः समावद्वीर्यैः समावज्जामिभी राध्योति य एवं वेद ॥ (*tasya pataṃtī rasamadhayattadvītarasaṃ nānvāpnotpūrve savane te devāḥ prājijñāsaṃta tatpaśuṣpapaśyaṃstadyadāśiramavanayaṃtyājyena paśunā caraṃti tena tatsamāvadvīryamabhavatpūrvābhyāṃ savanābhyāṃ sarvaiḥ savanaiḥ samāvadvīryaiḥ samāvajjāmibhī rādhyoti ya evaṃ veda ‖*)

While Gāyatrī (the falcon) was coming, holding the Soma plant in her beak, she kept drinking the juice in that plant a little at a time. Therefore, that is, because the juice in the Soma plants brought in the beak was not abundant but little, this third pressing did not become equal to the morning pressing and the midday pressing in strength, power and the like. So the gods, seeing this, deliberated and found that the suitable means for it lay in cattle. From cattle come milk and the offering materials obtained from it, such as curds and ājya (ghee). If milk is mixed with the Soma juice, which is little in the plants, that juice becomes strong. If oblations and the like are offered with ājya and with parts of the cattle, the pressing rite becomes complete. Therefore those who know the order of the sacrifice

The same meaning is stated in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā. The man who knows this matter, that is, that the three pressings, the morning, the midday and the third, have obtained a position equal in strength, power and excellence, lives happily, endowed with all abundance.

ते ना इमे इतरे छंदसी गायत्रीमभ्यवदेतां वित्तं नावक्षराण्यनुपर्यागुरिति नेत्यब्रवीद्गायत्री यथावित्तमेव न इति ते देवेषु प्रश्नमैतां ते देवा अब्रुवन्यथावित्तमेव न इति तस्माद्वाप्येतर्हि वित्यां व्याहुर्यथावित्तमेव न इति ततो वा अष्टाक्षरा गायत्र्यभवत्प्रक्षरा त्रिष्टुबेकाक्षरा जगती ॥ (*te nā ime itare chaṃdasī gāyatrīmabhyavadetāṃ vittaṃ nāvakṣarāṇyanuparyāguriti netyabravīdgāyatrī yathāvittameva na iti te deveṣu praśnamaitāṃ te devā abruvanyathāvittameva na iti tasmādvāpyetarhi vityāṃ vyāhuryathāvittameva na iti tato vā aṣṭākṣarā gāyatryabhavatprakṣarā triṣṭubekākṣarā jagatī ‖*)

(Ai. Brā. 13-28)

Earlier, when the metres Triṣṭubh and Jagatī flew to heaven to bring Soma, they did not reach heaven but grew weary half way and had to return; it was said already that for that reason, of their four syllables, the metre Jagatī lost three syllables and the metre Triṣṭubh lost one syllable. It was also said earlier that when Gāyatrī brought Soma, she brought back the four syllables that Triṣṭubh and Jagatī had lost, added them to her own four syllables, and made for herself eight syllables in all. Seeing this, the metres Triṣṭubh and Jagatī came to Gāyatrī and said: O Gāyatrī, it is well that you brought back the four syllables that we left behind in heaven. But you have joined them with your four syllables

### Printed p. 13

alone is not right. Since those syllables belong to us, you must give them back to us. So they asked. Gāyatrī refused their words and spoke thus: Whatever object is in our possession, that is, whatever we have acquired, it is right that it should belong to us. Even in worldly dealings, those who know the law say that if a thing is in the possession and enjoyment of someone, it belongs to him. Having gone to the world of heaven and accomplished the bringing of Soma, it is I alone, who brought back the syllables you had lost, who should have those syllables. I cannot give them to you. Hearing this, the Triṣṭubh and Jagatī went to the gods and made their complaint to them. The gods too upheld the very rule current in worldly dealings, that a thing belongs to the one in whose possession it is, and, supporting it, declared: What Gāyatrī says is right; since she brought those syllables, they should belong to her. How so? In battles and the like, whatever each of the victors gets from the things left behind by the defeated enemy forces fleeing, all agree that it is his own. So here too it should be. Thus the gods set out the rule. Therefore Gāyatrī, besides her own four syllables, had the four syllables of Triṣṭubh and Jagatī brought from heaven, and so came to have eight syllables in all. The metre Triṣṭubh was left with three syllables. The metre Jagatī was left with only one syllable.

साष्टाक्षरा गायत्री प्रातस्सवनमुदयच्छन्नाशक्कोत्रिष्टुप् त्र्यक्षरा माध्यंदिनं सवनमुद्यंतुं तां गायत्र्यब्रवीदायान्यपि मेऽत्रास्त्विति सा तथेत्यब्रवीत्रिष्टुप्तां वै मैतैरष्टाभिरक्षरैरुपसंधेहीति तथेति तामुपसमदधादेतद्वै तद्दायत्रै मध्यंदिने यन्मरुत्वतीयस्कोत्तरे प्रतिपदो यश्चानुचरः सैकादशाक्षरा भूत्वा माध्यंदिनं सवनमुदयच्छत् ॥ (*sāṣṭākṣarā gāyatrī prātassavanamudayacchannāśakkotriṣṭup tryakṣarā mādhyaṃdinaṃ savanamudyaṃtuṃ tāṃ gāyatryabravīdāyānyapi me 'trāstviti sā tathetyabravītriṣṭuptāṃ vai maitairaṣṭābhirakṣarairupasaṃdhehīti tatheti tāmupasamadadhādetadvai taddāyatrai madhyaṃdine yanmarutvatīyaskottare pratipado yaścānucaraḥ saikādaśākṣarā bhūtvā mādhyaṃdinaṃ savanamudayacchat ‖*)

It has been explained earlier that there are three pressings, the morning pressing (प्रातःसवन (*prātaḥsavana*)), the midday pressing (माध्यंदिनसवन (*mādhyaṃdinasavana*)) and the third pressing (तृतीयसवन (*tṛtīyasavana*)). There is the rule that the proceedings of the morning pressing are to be carried out with mantras of the Gāyatrī metre, the proceedings of the midday pressing with mantras of the Triṣṭubh metre, and the proceedings of the third pressing with mantras of the Jagatī metre.

Since the Gāyatrī metre had come to have eight syllables, it became complete, as it were, and capable of carrying out the proceedings of the morning pressing. But the metre Triṣṭubh, having lost one syllable and having only three syllables, was left without the capacity to carry out the proceedings of the midday pressing. Seeing the metre Triṣṭubh thus incapable, Gāyatrī said: O metre Triṣṭubh, since you have no power to carry out the work, I shall come to help you in the proceedings of the midday pressing.

But in return for that help, I too must have a share in the midday pressing. If you agree to that, I shall help you. Hearing this, the metre Triṣṭubh agreed that it should be so, and said: O Gāyatrī, I now have only three syllables. If your eight syllables are joined with them, I shall have eleven syllables, and so I shall have the strength to carry out the work. Accordingly Gāyatrī joined her eight syllables with the three syllables of the metre Triṣṭubh. Because this was done, the metre Triṣṭubh too became capable of carrying out the work at the midday pressing. Triṣṭubh agreed, saying that it should be so, to Gāyatrī's asking that she too should have a share in the midday pressing; this is right. Now what is Gāyatrī's share in the midday pressing? In the last part of the Marutvatīya and third śastra mantras recited at the time of the midday pressing, in the tṛca (a tṛca is 3 ṛks) beginning आ त्वा रथं यथोतये (*ā tvā rathaṃ yathotaye*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-68-1)**NOTE**, and in the tṛca after it, विश्वासानरस्य वस्पतिः (*viśvāsānarasya vaspatiḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-68-4), two ṛks each are in the Gāyatrī metre. Saṃ. 6-68-2, the 3rd ṛks), and in the second tṛca विश्वासानरस्य वस्पतिः (*viśvāsānarasya vaspatiḥ*) the two ṛks अभिष्टये सदावृधं (*abhiṣṭaye sadāvṛdhaṃ*) and परोमात्रम्यचेषं (*paromātramyaceṣaṃ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-68-, the 6th ṛks)**NOTE**, together make four mantras in the Gāyatrī metre in the pratipat tṛcas. Pratipat tṛca means the ṛks recited at the beginning. Further, the first ṛk of the tṛca recited at the end, called anucara, the mantra इदं वसो सुतमंधः (*idaṃ vaso sutamaṃdhaḥ*), is also in the Gāyatrī metre. Therefore the 4 ṛks of the Gāyatrī metre in the pratipat tṛcas and the 1 ṛk of the Gāyatrī metre in the anucara tṛca, in all 5 ṛks in the Gāyatrī metre, are in the midday pressing. These 5 ṛks are assigned as the share that should go to Gāyatrī in the midday pressing.

The metre Jagatī too, having lost three syllables, had only one syllable. Therefore it too became unable to carry out the proceedings of the third pressing. Then too Gāyatrī addressed Jagatī: O Jagatī, I shall help you in the proceedings of the third pressing. But I too must have a share in it. Jagatī agreed and said: Do so. That is, the eight syllables of Gāyatrī and the three syllables of Triṣṭubh together became eleven syllables and became capable of carrying out the work in the midday

pressing. Those eleven syllables now joined the one syllable of Jagatī, became twelve syllables, and became capable of carrying out the proceedings of the third pressing. How so? At the end of the Vaiśvadeva śastra mantras recited at the time of the third pressing, the ṛks recited as pratipat tṛcas and the ṛks recited as anucara are also mantras of the Gāyatrī metre. In this way these mantras, as Jagatī-metre mantras of twelve syllables in all, became capable of accomplishing the work of the third pressing; this is the meaning.

ततो ना अष्टाक्षरा गायत्र्यभवदेकादशाक्षरा त्रिष्टुब् द्वादशाक्षरा जगती । सर्वैश्चंदोभिः समावद्वीर्यैः समावज्जामिभी राध्योति य एवं वेद । एकं वै सत्तत्रेधाभवत्तस्मादाहुर्दातव्यमेवं विदुष इत्येकं हि सत्तत्रेधाभवत् ॥ (*tato nā aṣṭākṣarā gāyatryabhavadekādaśākṣarā triṣṭub dvādaśākṣarā jagatī | sarvaiścaṃdobhiḥ samāvadvīryaiḥ samāvajjāmibhī rādhyoti ya evaṃ veda | ekaṃ vai sattatredhābhavattasmādāhurdātavyamevaṃ viduṣa ityekaṃ hi sattatredhābhavat ‖*)

(Ai. Brā. 13-28)

Therefore the metre Gāyatrī came to have eight syllables, the metre Triṣṭubh eleven syllables, and the metre Jagatī twelve syllables. These three metres obtained a position and standing equal to all the other metres, and excellence. The man who knows this obtains excellence and an honoured position. Because Gāyatrī's form, which earlier had only four syllables, afterwards acquired four syllables more and entered also into the metres Triṣṭubh and Jagatī (that is, because her eight syllables were mingled with the syllables of the metres Triṣṭubh and Jagatī), her form, that is, her greatness, became greater threefold. Therefore the wise who know the secret of dharma say that gold and the like should be given as a gift to the man who knows this greatness of Gāyatrī.

**2. Gāyatrī, of the form of a bird, is the falcon.**

Sāyaṇa alone says:

श्येनभावं समास्थाय गर्भाद्योगेन निस्सृतः । ऋषिगर्भे शयानःसन् ब्रूते गर्भे नु सन्निति ॥ (*śyenabhāvaṃ samāsthāya garbhādyogena nissṛtaḥ | ṛṣigarbhe śayānaḥsan brūte garbhe nu sanniti ‖*)

(Introduction to the commentary on Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-27-1)

that Vāmadeva himself, freed from the womb through the greatness of his yoga, recites this sentence. English critics such as Ludwig have not examined what the reason and the basis are for this falcon being described in the form of Gāyatrī. In describing this same occasion the falcon is referred to by the word सुपर्ण (*suparṇa*) and also by the word Gāyatrī. In some places, in explaining the word falcon, Sāyaṇa has explained शैनः शंसनीयगतिमान्पक्षी पक्ष्याकारा गायत्री (*śainaḥ śaṃsanīyagatimānpakṣī pakṣyākārā gāyatrī*). Since in this way different words, falcon, Suparṇa and Gāyatrī, are used in these contexts, an exact meaning cannot be given here without knowing the narration of this story. Therefore, after knowing this part of the story told in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā, the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa and the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa, one can examine what the essential meaning is in the matter of Gāyatrī's bringing Soma.

Kadrū and Suparṇī, the two of them, came to compete about which of them excelled in her own form. Kadrū won, defeated Suparṇī and kept her in her power. Then she said to the defeated Suparṇī: "In the third region of heaven there is Soma. Bring it and give it, and you may obtain your release." Kadrū is this earth; Suparṇī is the atmosphere. The metres are the offspring of Suparṇī. "With the help of children who are reared for the very service of their mother, you can have the Soma fetched and win your freedom," Kadrū told Suparṇī. Then Suparṇī in the same way told her children of this situation. Then the Jagatī, consisting of fourteen syllables, flew up into the atmosphere; but it could not obtain the Soma and turned back. Of the fourteen it lost two syllables, and it returned together with sacrificial animals and with the consecration. For this reason the Jagatī metre is rich in the wealth of cattle, and a man rich in cattle becomes fit to be consecrated.

Next the Triṣṭubh metre, with thirteen syllables, flew up into the atmosphere. It too lost two syllables and did not obtain the Soma. But on its way back it returned with the sacrificial fee that is useful for the sacrifice and with the materials of austerity. For this reason, in common usage, the sacrificial fee is offered at the midday pressing. The giving of one's wealth as a gift is itself what is called austerity. After that the Gāyatrī, consisting of four syllables, set out for the atmosphere together with a goat, which was of the nature of light. That goat caused the Gāyatrī to obtain the Soma. Then, as the Gāyatrī was bringing the Soma, it also brought back the four syllables that the Triṣṭubh and the Jagatī had left behind there; these four syllables joined the four syllables it had in its former state, and so it became of eight syllables. Thus, although it is the smallest of all the metres, because it brought the Soma, which is the most important thing for the sacrificial work, the sacrifice

holds the most prominent place in the rite, according to the view of the Brahmavādins; and therefore it is the most brilliant of all the metres. It (the Gāyatrī in the form of a bird) brought two pressings with its two feet and one with its mouth. The pressing taken with the mouth it drank. For this reason the morning pressing and the midday pressing contain bright Soma, while the third pressing has the Ṛjīṣa (the pressed residue). This is the account given in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā. In the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa the account differs somewhat. It is told in two passages. First:

सोमो वै राजा गंधर्वेष्वासीत्तं देवाश्च ऋषयश्चाभ्यध्यायन्कथमयमस्मान्त्सोमो राजागच्छेदिति सा वागब्रवीत् स्त्रीकामा वै गंधर्वा मयैव स्त्रिया भूतया पणद्वमिति नेति देवा अब्रुवन्कथं वयं त्वदृते स्यामेति साब्रवीत्क्रीणीतैव यर्हि वाव वो मयार्थो भविता तर्ह्येव वोऽहं पुनरागंतास्मीति तथेति तया महानग्न्या भूतया सोमं राजानमक्रीणन्. (*somo vai rājā gaṃdharveṣvāsīttaṃ devāśca ṛṣayaścābhyadhyāyankathamayamasmāntsomo rājāgacchediti sā vāgabravīt strīkāmā vai gaṃdharvā mayaiva striyā bhūtayā paṇadvamiti neti devā abruvankathaṃ vayaṃ tvadṛte syāmeti sābravītkrīṇītaiva yarhi vāva vo mayārtho bhavitā tarhyeva vo 'haṃ punarāgaṃtāsmīti tatheti tayā mahānagnyā bhūtayā somaṃ rājānamakrīṇan.*) (Ai. Brā. 5-27)

When King Soma was living among the Gandharvas, the gods and the sages too wished to obtain him and fell into meditation upon him. While they were reflecting on how to obtain him, the goddess Vāk came before them and said: "The Gandharvas are fond of women. Send me, and I shall bring Soma and hand him over to you." The gods answered: "We cannot be without you. That cannot be." Then the goddess Vāk gave them an assurance: "For the present send me and obtain Soma; afterwards, as soon as you desire me, I shall come to you." After that, with her help, they obtained Soma. The same occasion is described elsewhere in this way.

**4. The Metres' Attempt to Bring the Soma, and Their Defeat**

ओं सोमो वै राजामुष्मिल्लोक आसीत्तं देवाश्च ऋषयश्चाभ्यध्यायन्कथमयमस्मान्सोमो राजागच्छेदिति तेऽब्रुवंशद्वांसि यूयं न इमं सोमं राजानमाहरतेति तथेति ते सुपर्णा भूत्योदपतंस्ते यत्सुपर्णा भूत्योदपतंस्तदेतत् सौपर्णमित्याख्यानविद आचक्षते । (*oṃ somo vai rājāmuṣmilloka āsīttaṃ devāśca ṛṣayaścābhyadhyāyankathamayamasmānsomo rājāgacchediti te 'bruvaṃśadvāṃsi yūyaṃ na imaṃ somaṃ rājānamāharateti tatheti te suparṇā bhūtyodapataṃste yatsuparṇā bhūtyodapataṃstadetat sauparṇamityākhyānavida ācakṣate |*)

छंदांसि वै तत्सोमं राजानमच्छाचरंस्तानि ह तर्हि चतुरक्षराणि चतुरक्षराण्येव छंदांस्यासन् सा जगती चतुरक्षरा प्रथमोदपतत्सा पतित्वार्धमध्वनो गत्वाश्रामत्सा परास्य त्रीण्यक्षराण्येकाक्षरा भूत्वा (*chaṃdāṃsi vai tatsomaṃ rājānamacchācaraṃstāni ha tarhi caturakṣarāṇi caturakṣarāṇyeva chaṃdāṃsyāsan sā jagatī caturakṣarā prathamodapatatsā patitvārdhamadhvano gatvāśrāmatsā parāsya trīṇyakṣarāṇyekākṣarā bhūtvā*)

दीक्षां च तपश्च हरंती पुनरभ्यवापतत्तस्मात्तस्य वित्ता दीक्षा वित्तं तपो यस्य परवः संति जागता हि पशवो जगती हि तानाहरदथ त्रिष्टुबुदपतत्सा पतित्वा भूयोर्धादध्वनो गत्वाश्राम्यत्सा परास्यैकमक्षरं त्रैक्षरा भूत्वा दक्षिणा हरंती पुनरभ्यवापतत्तस्मान्माध्यंदिने दक्षिणा नीयंते त्रिष्टुभो लोके त्रिष्टुब्बि ता आहरत् । (*dīkṣāṃ ca tapaśca haraṃtī punarabhyavāpatattasmāttasya vittā dīkṣā vittaṃ tapo yasya paravaḥ saṃti jāgatā hi paśavo jagatī hi tānāharadatha triṣṭubudapatatsā patitvā bhūyordhādadhvano gatvāśrāmyatsā parāsyaikamakṣaraṃ traikṣarā bhūtvā dakṣiṇā haraṃtī punarabhyavāpatattasmānmādhyaṃdine dakṣiṇā nīyaṃte triṣṭubho loke triṣṭubbi tā āharat |*) (Ai. The gods thought for a long time how the Soma that stood in the atmosphere might be obtained; then they approached the metres and begged: "Bring us the Soma that is in the atmosphere." The metres promised, "So be it," took the form of birds and flew up into the atmosphere. Because they were transformed into the form of Suparṇa birds and flew to fetch the Soma, this episode became famous as the Suparṇopākhyāna. When these metres in bird form flew to Soma, each of them had only four syllables. The Jagatī, of four syllables, flew up into the atmosphere first, and got tired half-way. Leaving three of its syllables there, it returned with only the one remaining syllable. But on the way back it brought the sacrificial consecration and austerity. Since the Jagatī and the wealth of cattle are closely connected, one who has cattle obtains faith and the power of austerity. Next the Triṣṭubh too flew, went beyond half of the way and a little further, and got tired. Leaving one syllable of its own there, it returned with its remaining three syllables. On its return it brought the sacrificial fee, an article of the sacrifice. For this reason the fee is given prominence at the time of the midday pressing.

**5. The Victory of the Gāyatrī**

ते देवा अब्रुवन्गायत्रीं त्वं न इमं सोमं राजानमाहरेति सा तथे त्यब्रवीत्तां वै मा सर्वेण स्वस्ययॆनेनानुमंत्रयध्वमिति तथेति सोदपतत्तां देवाः सर्वेण स्वस्ययनेनान्वमंत्रयंत प्रेति चेति चेत्येतद्वै सर्वं स्वस्ययनं यत्प्रेति चेति चेति तद्योऽस्य प्रियः स्यात्तमेतेनानुमंत्रयेत प्रेति चेति तद्योऽस्य प्रियः स्यात्तमेतेनानुमंत्रयेत प्रेति चेति चेति स्वस्त्येव गच्छति स्वस्ति पुनरागच्छसा (*te devā abruvangāyatrīṃ tvaṃ na imaṃ somaṃ rājānamāhareti sā tathe tyabravīttāṃ vai mā sarveṇa svasyayènenānumaṃtrayadhvamiti tatheti sodapatattāṃ devāḥ sarveṇa svasyayanenānvamaṃtrayaṃta preti ceti cetyetadvai sarvaṃ svasyayanaṃ yatpreti ceti ceti tadyo 'sya priyaḥ syāttametenānumaṃtrayeta preti ceti tadyo 'sya priyaḥ syāttametenānumaṃtrayeta preti ceti ceti svastyeva gacchati svasti punarāgacchasā*)

पतित्वा सोमपालान् भीषयित्वा पद्भ्यां च मुखेन च सोमं राजानं समगृभ्नाड्यानि चेतरे छंदसी अक्षराण्यजहितां तानि चोपसमगृभ्नात्तस्या अनु विसृज्य कृशानुः सोमपालः सव्यस्य पदो नखमच्छिदत्तच्छल्यकोऽभवत्तस्मात्सनखमिव यद्वशमस्रवत्सा वशाभवत्तस्मात्सा हविरिवाथ यः शल्यो यदनीकमासीत्स सर्पो निर्दंश्यभवत्सहसस्सजो यानि (*patitvā somapālān bhīṣayitvā padbhyāṃ ca mukhena ca somaṃ rājānaṃ samagṛbhnāḍyāni cetare chaṃdasī akṣarāṇyajahitāṃ tāni copasamagṛbhnāttasyā anu visṛjya kṛśānuḥ somapālaḥ savyasya pado nakhamacchidattacchalyako 'bhavattasmātsanakhamiva yadvaśamasravatsā vaśābhavattasmātsā havirivātha yaḥ śalyo yadanīkamāsītsa sarpo nirdaṃśyabhavatsahasassajo yāni*)

### Printed p. 22

पर्णानि ते मंथावला यानि स्नानानि ते गंडूपदा यत्तेजनं सोऽंधाहिः सो सा तथेषुरभवत् । (*parṇāni te maṃthāvalā yāni snānāni te gaṃḍūpadā yattejanaṃ so 'ṃdhāhiḥ so sā tatheṣurabhavat |*) (Ai. Brā. 13-26)

Then the gods approached the Gāyatrī and prayed: "Bring us King Soma." The Gāyatrī agreed, "So be it," and said that the gods should follow it reciting a svastyayana mantra. The gods gladly accepted this and set out reciting the svastyayana mantra **NOTE**. Because by reciting this mantra the journey is completed without obstacle and the work is accomplished, the gods recited it. The Gāyatrī reached the place where the Soma was without hindrance, lifted the Soma with its feet and with its mouth, and returned also taking the four syllables (3+1) which the Triṣṭubh and the Jagatī had left behind there. But Kṛśānu, the guardian of the Soma, cut off the nail of its left foot. It was transformed into the form of a porcupine and turned into a quill. The blood that flowed from it became a barren cow, and the sinew became a snake; but it did not become one that spits venom and bites. In the same way the feather became a bat and the tendon an earthworm, and thus the form of the arrow came to be what it is.

**6. The Prominence of the Gāyatrī in the Times of Pressing**

सा यद्दक्षिणेन पदा समग्रभ्वात्तत्प्रातःसवनमभवत्तद्गायत्री स्वमायतनमकुरुत तस्मात्तत्समृद्धतमं मन्यंते सर्वॆषां सवनानामग्रियो मुख्यो भवति श्रेष्ठतामश्नुते (*sā yaddakṣiṇena padā samagrabhvāttatprātaḥsavanamabhavattadgāyatrī svamāyatanamakuruta tasmāttatsamṛddhatamaṃ manyaṃte sarvèṣāṃ savanānāmagriyo mukhyo bhavati śreṣṭhatāmaśnute*)

य एवं वेदाथ यत्सव्येन पदा समग्रभ्वात्तन्माध्यंदिनं सवनमभवत्तद्विस्स्रंसत तद्विस्स्रस्तं नान्वाप्पोत्पूर्वं सवनं ते देवाः प्राजिज्ञासंत तस्मिंस्त्रिष्टुभं छंदसामदधुरिंद्रं देवतानां तेन तत्समावद्वीर्यमभवत्तूर्वेण सवनेनोभाभ्यां सवनाभ्यां समावद्वीर्याभ्यां समावज्जामिभ्यां राध्योति (*ya evaṃ vedātha yatsavyena padā samagrabhvāttanmādhyaṃdinaṃ savanamabhavattadvissraṃsata tadvissrastaṃ nānvāppotpūrvaṃ savanaṃ te devāḥ prājijñāsaṃta tasmiṃstriṣṭubhaṃ chaṃdasāmadadhuriṃdraṃ devatānāṃ tena tatsamāvadvīryamabhavattūrveṇa savanenobhābhyāṃ savanābhyāṃ samāvadvīryābhyāṃ samāvajjāmibhyāṃ rādhyoti*)

य एवं वेदाथ यन्मुखेन समग्रभ्वात्तत्तृतीयसवनमभवत्तस्य पतंती रसमधयत्तद्धीतरसं नान्वाप्पोत्पूर्वे सवने ते देवाः प्राजिज्ञासंत तत्पशुष्पपश्यंस्तद्यदाशिरमवनयन्त्याजेन पशुना चरंति तेन तत्समावद्वीर्यमभवत्तूर्वाभ्यां सवनाभ्यां सर्वैः सवनैः समावद्वीर्यैः समावज्जामिभी राध्योति य एवं वेद । (*ya evaṃ vedātha yanmukhena samagrabhvāttattṛtīyasavanamabhavattasya pataṃtī rasamadhayattaddhītarasaṃ nānvāppotpūrve savane te devāḥ prājijñāsaṃta tatpaśuṣpapaśyaṃstadyadāśiramavanayantyājena paśunā caraṃti tena tatsamāvadvīryamabhavattūrvābhyāṃ savanābhyāṃ sarvaiḥ savanaiḥ samāvadvīryaiḥ samāvajjāmibhī rādhyoti ya evaṃ veda |*) (Ai. Brā. 13-26)

The part of the Soma that the Gāyatrī seized with its right foot became the morning pressing. This morning pressing became the dwelling-place of the Gāyatrī. For this reason the morning pressing is regarded as the most prosperous of all the pressings. One who knows this truth becomes the foremost and the most prominent among sacrificers.

### Printed p. 23

The part of the Soma that the Gāyatrī seized with its left foot became the midday pressing. Because it slipped away, it did not obtain the excellence of the morning pressing. The gods tried to remedy this deficiency, and established in it the Triṣṭubh among the metres and Indra among the deities. After that it too obtained the vigour of the morning pressing. A man who knows the equal power and wealth of these two pressings attains prosperity. The part seized with the mouth became the third pressing. While it was flying, the falcon in the form of the Gāyatrī drank it. For this reason it did not obtain the equality in power of the first two pressings. The gods realised that this deficiency had to be remedied. They then looked for it among the animals, and then worshipped with the āśira and with the offering of ghee. After that it too obtained the power of the first two pressings. One who knows this truth attains prosperity.

**7. The Division of the Syllables of the Metres**

ते वा इमे इतरे छंदसी गायत्रीमभ्यवदेतां वित्तं नावक्षराण्यनुपरागुरिति नेत्यब्रवीद्गायत्री यथा वित्तमेव न इति तेऽदेवेषु प्रश्नमैतां ते देवा अब्रुवन्यथावित्तमेव व इति तस्माद्दाप्येतर्हि वित्त्यां व्याहु र्यथा वित्तमेव न इति ततो ना अष्टाक्षरा गायत्र्यभवत्र्यक्षरा त्रिष्टुबेकाक्षरा जगती साष्टाक्षरा गायत्री प्रातःसवनमुदयुच्छन्नाशक्कोत्रिष्टुप् त्यक्षरा माध्यंदिनं सवनमुद्यंतुं तां गायत्र्यब्रवीदायान्यपि नोत्रास्त्वितिसा तथेत्यब्रवीत् त्रिष्टुप् तांवै मैतैरष्टाभिरक्षरैरुपसंधेहीति तथेति .... (*te vā ime itare chaṃdasī gāyatrīmabhyavadetāṃ vittaṃ nāvakṣarāṇyanuparāguriti netyabravīdgāyatrī yathā vittameva na iti te 'deveṣu praśnamaitāṃ te devā abruvanyathāvittameva va iti tasmāddāpyetarhi vittyāṃ vyāhu ryathā vittameva na iti tato nā aṣṭākṣarā gāyatryabhavatryakṣarā triṣṭubekākṣarā jagatī sāṣṭākṣarā gāyatrī prātaḥsavanamudayucchannāśakkotriṣṭup tyakṣarā mādhyaṃdinaṃ savanamudyaṃtuṃ tāṃ gāyatryabravīdāyānyapi notrāstvitisā tathetyabravīt triṣṭup tāṃvai maitairaṣṭābhirakṣarairupasaṃdhehīti tatheti ....*) (Ai. Brā. 13-28)

After the Gāyatrī had returned taking the three syllables of the Triṣṭubh and the one syllable of the Jagatī, the two approached it and prayed: "The syllables that are our wealth have come along with you. Give them back." The Gāyatrī said: "No, they are not yours; since I brought them, they have become mine." Then the two approached the gods and told them of their complaint. The gods took the side of the Gāyatrī and said: "Since you brought them, those four syllables have become yours. Just as in worldly dealings there is the custom that whatever property we have obtained becomes ours, so you have the right to make them your own"

### Printed p. 24

Then the Gāyatrī became one of eight syllables, the Triṣṭubh one of three syllables, and the Jagatī one of a single syllable. The Gāyatrī, with its eight syllables, became able to take part in the morning pressing. But the Triṣṭubh, with its three syllables, could not take part in the midday pressing. Then the Gāyatrī said: "I too shall take part in the midday pressing; let me also have a share of its sacrificial portion." "So be it; join me with your eight syllables," said the Triṣṭubh. Together, as eleven syllables, they became helpful to the midday pressing. The Jagatī, with its single syllable, was unable to carry out the third pressing. Then the Gāyatrī again asked that it might join with it and have a share in the third pressing also. The Jagatī agreed, "So be it," and at the time of the third pressing the Gāyatrī joined it. Then, as twelve syllables, it became helpful to the third pressing. Thus the Gāyatrī became eight syllables, the Triṣṭubh eleven syllables and the Jagatī twelve syllables. One who knows this truth obtains all wealth through the favour of these metres, which have obtained equal power and splendour. This is the praise of the Gāyatrī.

**The Preliminary Account of the Soma-carrying Rite**

In former times the Soma was in heaven. When no one was able to bring it, the Gāyatrī went to heaven in the form of a falcon, disregarded the resistance of those who were there, and brought it to the earth. While it was being brought, on the way a Gandharva named Viśvāsu stole that Soma and kept it with himself. The gods considered how to get it back from him, and, since the Gandharvas are especially fond of women and full of desire, they resolved to show them a desire for women and so get the Soma back. For this purpose they went to the goddess Vāk, told her the whole matter, and decided that Vāk should become a beautiful woman, that they should sell her to the Gandharvas, and in exchange bring back the Soma. Accordingly, when the goddess Vāk took the form of a beautiful woman, they gave her to the Gandharvas and brought the Soma back. After the Soma had come into the power of the gods, the goddess Vāk, who was in the form of a beautiful woman, took the form of a deer, escaped from the Gandharvas, ran away and came and joined the gods. From that time it became the custom to buy Soma plants for use in sacrifices. The rite called the purchase of Soma is still performed in the sacrifice. Moreover, since the best Soma plants grow in high mountain regions (the mountain called Mūjavata is famous for the Soma plant), it is very laborious to fetch them. Therefore it seems to have become the custom to buy them, paying a fitting price to those who endured the toil for the sake of wealth, went to the mountains and brought the Soma. In former times, when the Gandharva stole the Soma, it remained with him for three nights, and so for the purchase

### Printed p. 25

the Soma that is taken in purchase must likewise remain for three nights, such is the rule. (All this is stated in the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa and in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā.) After the purchase of the Soma it is brought in two carts called havirdhāna and left at the edge of the sacrificial hall or of the prāgvaṃśa (the ancient hall). The ritual act of bringing that Soma with mantras to the neighbourhood of the Uttaravedi is called Somapravāhaṇa; therefore the procedure is stated here as it stands in the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa (Ai. Brā. 3-12).

प्राच्यां वै दिशि देवाः सोमं राजानमक्रीणंस्तस्मात्प्राच्यां दिशि क्रीयते । (*prācyāṃ vai diśi devāḥ somaṃ rājānamakrīṇaṃstasmātprācyāṃ diśi krīyate |*)

In former times, when the gods performed a sacrifice, they bought the Soma from the region to the east of the prāgvaṃśa (the sacrificial altar). Therefore even now the Soma must be bought and brought from the east.

तं त्रयोदशान्मासादक्रीणंस्तस्मात्रयोदशो मासो नानुविद्यते न वै सोमविक्रय्यनुविद्यते पापो हि सोमविक्रया । (*taṃ trayodaśānmāsādakrīṇaṃstasmātrayodaśo māso nānuvidyate na vai somavikrayyanuvidyate pāpo hi somavikrayā |*)

In former times a year had thirteen months. The gods bought the Soma from the presiding deity of the thirteenth month. Now there is no thirteenth month in the year; there are only twelve months. Although at certain times there is a thirteenth month called the intercalary month, since it is of no use for any auspicious rites and the like, there is no custom of buying in it. Since selling Soma is regarded as a very sinful act, the man who sells Soma is also most sinful. No courteous dealings of any kind may be carried on with him. On this matter the Taittirīya Śruti says:

अस्मे ज्योतिः सोमविक्रयणि तम इत्याह । ज्योतिरेव यजमाने दधाति । तमः सोमविक्रयिणमर्पयति । (*asme jyotiḥ somavikrayaṇi tama ityāha | jyotireva yajamāne dadhāti | tamaḥ somavikrayiṇamarpayati |*)

Here तमः (*tamaḥ*) means sin. The luminosity of the seller of Soma, that is, his power and capacity, leaves him and joins the sacrificer who buys the Soma and performs the sacrifice. The meaning of the Śruti sentence is that only sin befalls the seller of Soma. After the purchase of the Soma, there is the injunction that eight Ṛks must be recited when it is brought to the neighbourhood of the prāgvaṃśa. There is also a reason for this, and it is stated here:

तस्य क्रीतस्य मनुष्यानभ्युपावर्तमानस्य दिशो वीर्याणींद्रियाणि व्युदसीदंस्तान्येकयर्चावारुरुत्संत तानि नाशक्कुवंस्तानि द्वाभ्यां तानि तिसृभिस्तानि चतसृभिस्तानि पंचभिस्तानि षड्भिस्तानि सप्तभिर्नैवावरुंधत तान्यष्टाभिरवारुंधताष्टाभिराश्नुवत यदष्टाभिरवारुंधताष्टाभिराश्नुवत तदष्टानामष्टत्वमश्नुते यद्यत्कामयते य एवं वेद । (*tasya krītasya manuṣyānabhyupāvartamānasya diśo vīryāṇīṃdriyāṇi vyudasīdaṃstānyekayarcāvārurutsaṃta tāni nāśakkuvaṃstāni dvābhyāṃ tāni tisṛbhistāni catasṛbhistāni paṃcabhistāni ṣaḍbhistāni saptabhirnaivāvaruṃdhata tānyaṣṭābhiravāruṃdhatāṣṭābhirāśnuvata yadaṣṭābhiravāruṃdhatāṣṭābhirāśnuvata tadaṣṭānāmaṣṭatvamaśnute yadyatkāmayate ya evaṃ veda |*)

After the Soma was bought and brought, when it came near the men (the sacrificers), its powers and capacities shrank. That is, whatever the Soma ought to have (what those who drink Soma may obtain)

### Printed p. 26

the bestowing of strength and the capacity to make the senses, the eye and the others, keen were all lost. Then those sacrificers, wishing to prevent those powers and capacities from going away, tried reciting one Ṛk for the purpose of binding the directions. It was not possible. Then they tried reciting two Ṛks, and afterwards three, four, five, six and up to seven Ṛks, but it was not possible. Then, when they recited eight Ṛks, it became possible to bind. Therefore, because the work was accomplished by reciting eight Ṛks, the use of the word अष्ट (*aṣṭa*) (from अश् (*aś*), to attain) is fitting here. The man who knows this obtains what he desires.

तस्मादेतेषु कर्मस्वष्टावनूच्यंत इंद्रियाणां वीर्याणामवरुध्यॆ । (*tasmādeteṣu karmasvaṣṭāvanūcyaṃta iṃdriyāṇāṃ vīryāṇāmavarudhyè |*) (Ai. Brā. 3-12)

Therefore in these Somapravāhaṇa rites the Hotṛ must recite eight Ṛks separately. By reciting in this way it is possible to prevent the senses and the vigour (the powers and capacities) from being destroyed.

Here the preliminary account ends.

**The Rite Called Somapravāhaṇa**

(The rite of bringing the Soma from the neighbourhood of the prāgvaṃśa or sacrificial altar to the neighbourhood of the Uttaravedi, which lies in the west)

So far the number of Ṛks to be used in this Somapravāhaṇa rite has been made clear. Now the Adhvaryu recites the preṣa mantra addressed to the Hotṛ:

सोमाय क्रीताय प्रोह्यमाणायानुब्रू ३ हीत्याहाध्वर्युः (*somāya krītāya prohyamāṇāyānubrū 3 hītyāhādhvaryuḥ*)

Now the Adhvaryu addresses the Hotṛ, saying: "O Hotṛ, सोमाय क्रीताय प्रोह्यमाणायानुब्रू ३ हि (*somāya krītāya prohyamāṇāyānubrū 3 hi*)", that is, recite the mantra required in this rite called Somapravāhaṇa, which is performed in order to carry the Soma, taken in purchase, to the Uttaravedi.

भद्रादभि श्रेयः प्रेहीत्यन्वाह (*bhadrādabhi śreyaḥ prehītyanvāha*)

Hearing this, the Hotṛ recites the mantra भद्रादभि श्रेयः प्रेहि (*bhadrādabhi śreyaḥ prehi*). This mantra is not in the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā. It is stated in the Taittirīya recension, that is, in the Yajurveda. This mantra is

### Printed p. 27

भद्रादभि श्रेयः प्रेहि बृहस्पतिः पुर एता ते अस्तु । अथेमवस्य वर अ पृथिव्या आरे शत्रून् कृणुहि सर्ववीरः ॥ (*bhadrādabhi śreyaḥ prehi bṛhaspatiḥ pura etā te astu | athemavasya vara a pṛthivyā āre śatrūn kṛṇuhi sarvavīraḥ ‖*) (Tai. Saṃ. 1-2-3-3)

Its meaning is as follows: O Soma, set out from this earth, which is auspicious and is the place of purchase, towards the region of the prāgvaṃśa, which is equal to the place of heaven. As you go thus, Bṛhaspati will go before you as your guide. When you have gone there, fix on the earth, in the place of the divine sacrifice, a fitting and excellent seat for yourself to sit upon. Then, you who are the bravest of all, cause all the enemies, the sinful ones who hate the sacrifice, to be kept far away; that is, make them depart far away. This is the meaning of the mantra. The explanation of the first quarter-verse is given by describing the result of reciting this mantra.

अयं नाव लोको भद्रस्तस्मादसानेव लोकः श्रेयान् स्वर्गमेव तल्लोकं यजमानं गमयति । (*ayaṃ nāva loko bhadrastasmādasāneva lokaḥ śreyān svargameva tallokaṃ yajamānaṃ gamayati |*)

As said in the first quarter-verse, this earth is auspicious. But the heavenly world is far more beneficial than this world. The recitation of this mantra brings the sacrificer who performs the sacrifice to such a heavenly world.

बृहस्पतिः पुर एता ते अस्त्विति ब्रह्म वै बृहस्पतिर्ब्रह्मैनासा एतत्पुरोगमकर्ण वै ब्रह्मण्वद्रिष्यति ॥ (*bṛhaspatiḥ pura etā te astviti brahma vai bṛhaspatirbrahmaināsā etatpurogamakarṇa vai brahmaṇvadriṣyati ‖*)

Next the Hotṛ recites the second quarter-verse, बृहस्पतिः पुरा एता ते अस्तु (*bṛhaspatiḥ purā etā te astu*). For the gods Bṛhaspati is the priest called Brahman. Since it is said that the Brahman priest, the foremost of Brāhmaṇas, will be the guide, a sacrificial rite performed under the leadership of a Brāhmaṇa is never spoiled; it reaches its completion in auspiciousness.

अथेमवस्य वर आ पृथिव्या इति देवयजनं वै वरं पृथिव्यै देवयजन एनैनं तदवसाययत्यारे शत्रुन्हणुहि सर्ववीर इति द्विषंतमेवास्मै तत्पाप्मानं भ्रातृव्यमपभाधतेऽधरं पादयति । ई (*athemavasya vara ā pṛthivyā iti devayajanaṃ vai varaṃ pṛthivyai devayajana enainaṃ tadavasāyayatyāre śatrunhaṇuhi sarvavīra iti dviṣaṃtamevāsmai tatpāpmānaṃ bhrātṛvyamapabhādhate 'dharaṃ pādayati | ī*)

Now they explain the third quarter-verse: अथेमवस्य वर आ पृथिव्याः (*athemavasya vara ā pṛthivyāḥ*) is the third quarter-verse. The most excellent region on the earth is the region of the divine sacrifice. By reciting this third quarter-verse the Hotṛ makes the Soma settle in the excellent place connected with the earth, that is, in the region of the divine sacrifice.

### Printed p. 28

Next, by reciting the fourth quarter-verse, आरे शत्रुन्हणुहि सर्ववीरः (*āre śatrunhaṇuhi sarvavīraḥ*), there is help in driving away the enemy in the form of sin that hates the sacrificer. Therefore the Hotṛ recites this fourth quarter-verse, drives away the enemy who would do harm to the sacrificer, and makes that enemy go to a lowly (very base) region.

सोम यास्ते मयोभुव इति तृचं सौम्यं गायत्रमन्वाह सोमे राजनि प्रोह्यमाणे स्वयैवैनं तद्देवतया स्टेन छंदसा समर्धयति । (*soma yāste mayobhuva iti tṛcaṃ saumyaṃ gāyatramanvāha some rājani prohyamāṇe svayaivainaṃ taddevatayā sṭena chaṃdasā samardhayati |*)

Then the Hotṛ recites the three Ṛks:

सोम यास्ते मयोभुव ऊतयः संति दाशुषे । ताभिर्नोऽविता भव ॥ इमं यज्ञमिदं वचो जुजुषाण उपागहि । सोम त्वं नो वृधे भव ॥ सोम गीर्भिष्ट्वा वयं वर्धयानो वचोविदः । सुमृळीको न आ विश ॥ (*soma yāste mayobhuva ūtayaḥ saṃti dāśuṣe | tābhirno 'vitā bhava ‖ imaṃ yajñamidaṃ vaco jujuṣāṇa upāgahi | soma tvaṃ no vṛdhe bhava ‖ soma gīrbhiṣṭvā vayaṃ vardhayāno vacovidaḥ | sumṛḷīko na ā viśa ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-90-9, 20, 21) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-91-9, 10, 11) **NOTE**

These three Ṛks have Soma as their deity and are in the Gāyatrī metre. They are to be recited at the time of bringing the Soma. By reciting Ṛks in the Gāyatrī metre that praise the deity Soma himself and are dear to the deity Soma, it is as if the deity Soma were praised with his own mantras and with his own Gāyatrī metre, and so the deity Soma becomes prosperous, that is, becomes glad at heart. Here the substance that is brought is Soma, and the mantras that praise are those of which Soma is the deity. Therefore the mantras are said to be Soma's own. Since it was the Gāyatrī that brought the Soma, which was in heaven, to the earth, the Gāyatrī metre is regarded as Soma's own. This matter is stated in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā in the anuvāka beginning कद्रूश्च वै (*kadrūśca vai*) (Tai. Saṃ. 6-1-6). Therefore it is explained here that the three Ṛks mentioned above, and the Gāyatrī metre as well, are Soma's own.

सर्वे नंदंति यशसागतेनेत्यन्नाह (*sarve naṃdaṃti yaśasāgatenetyannāha*)

Thus the Hotṛ recites the fifth Ṛk:

सर्वे नंदंति यशसागतेन सभासाहेन सख्या सखायः । किल्पिषस्पृत्पितुषणिर्हैषामरं हितो भवति वाजिनाय ॥ (*sarve naṃdaṃti yaśasāgatena sabhāsāhena sakhyā sakhāyaḥ | kilpiṣaspṛtpituṣaṇirhaiṣāmaraṃ hito bhavati vājināya ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-72-18)

### Printed p. 29

This Ṛk is in the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā itself. Its main purport is as follows. The people who take part in the sacrifice, the sacrificer, the priests and the rest, are glad on seeing this god Soma, who like a friend is dear to all, come to the sacrificial ground. This Soma is the giver of fame; just as a scholar who defeats everyone in an assembly of the learned by his extraordinary learning enters the assembly and brings joy to all, so all rejoice at the arrival of this god Soma; and this Soma destroys the kilbiṣas, that is, the sins and faults, of the priests and the sacrificer, and protects them; he is the giver of food to them, that is, all are satisfied by drinking the Soma. Such a Soma, endowed with these great qualities, has been brought to this sacrificial altar and placed here in order to favour all with food, strength of the senses and vigour. This matter is well known. This is the purport of the Ṛk.

यशो वै सोमो राजा सर्वो ह वा एतेन क्रियमाणेन नंदंति यश्च यज्ञे लप्स्यमानो भवति यश्च न । (*yaśo vai somo rājā sarvo ha vā etena kriyamāṇena naṃdaṃti yaśca yajñe lapsyamāno bhavati yaśca na |*)

The first quarter-verse of this Ṛk is explained. The god Soma is the cause of fame. Indra and the others drank Soma, performed many heroic deeds and became famous. Therefore Soma is said to be of the very nature of fame. Some, by performing the priestly duties in the sacrifice, gain wealth; some are glad merely by watching the sacrifice. Therefore all rejoice on seeing this Soma, which has been bought and brought to the sacrificial ground.

सभासाहेन सख्या सखाय इत्येष वै ब्राह्मणानां सभासहः सखा यत्सोमो राजा । (*sabhāsāhena sakhyā sakhāya ityeṣa vai brāhmaṇānāṃ sabhāsahaḥ sakhā yatsomo rājā |*)

The second quarter-verse is explained: this radiant, shining Soma conquers all in the learned assemblies of the Brāhmaṇas and shines with his learning. Since this god brings benefit to the Brāhmaṇas and to the priests and sacrificer, he is their friend.

किल्पिषस्पृदित्येष उ एव किल्पिषस्पृत् यो वै भवति यः श्रेष्ठतामश्नुते स किल्पिषं भवति । तस्मादाहुर्मानुवोचो मा प्रचारीः किल्पिषं नु मा यातयन्निति । (*kilpiṣaspṛdityeṣa u eva kilpiṣaspṛt yo vai bhavati yaḥ śreṣṭhatāmaśnute sa kilpiṣaṃ bhavati | tasmādāhurmānuvoco mā pracārīḥ kilpiṣaṃ nu mā yātayanniti |*)

The third quarter-verse is explained: this god Soma removes kilbiṣa, that is, sin, and protects. In the Soma sacrifice performed to obtain all one's desired ends, there is the likelihood that kilbiṣas, that is, omissions, faults, deficiencies, sins and the like, may arise. In such circumstances

### Printed p. 30

the deity Soma removes those omissions, faults and sins and protects the sacrificer. As to what the occasion is for kilbiṣa to befall the priests and the sacrificer while a sacrifice is being performed: when a man engaged in the sacrificial work recites mantras and the like in an advanced sacrifice that must be conducted with great discernment, with a wish to finish it quickly, or with the conceit that he himself is the most capable in the use of mantras and that there is no one equal to him, then, through the recitation of mantras, there arise in the pronunciation of the mantras and in their order such faults as the omission of a syllable or the omission of a rite; the rite becomes deficient and sin results. And the sacrificer will address the priests thus: "O Hotṛ, at the time of reciting the mantras let your mind be fixed on the recitation and meaning of the mantras; with a steady mind, not letting it wander elsewhere, recite the puronuvākyā and the other mantra texts. O Adhvaryu, so that the duties to be done in the haste of the work do not go awry, and so that, with your attention elsewhere, the work is not spoiled, give up the hurry of getting the work done quickly and perform the duties carefully. Take care that no kilbiṣa, that is, sin, befalls me, the sacrificer, through the omissions and faults you commit." The sacrificer will have to say this. It is said that the deity Soma should protect the priests and the sacrificer from such sins.

पितुषणिरित्यन्नं वै पितु दक्षिणा वै पितु तामेनेन सनोत्यन्नसनिमेवैनं तत्करोति । (*pituṣaṇirityannaṃ vai pitu dakṣiṇā vai pitu tāmenena sanotyannasanimevainaṃ tatkaroti |*)

Explaining the fourth quarter-verse, they explain the meaning of the word पितुषणि (*pituṣaṇi*): the word पितु (*pitu*) means food, and besides it means also the fee (dakṣiṇā) given to the priests in sacrifices and the like. In this Soma sacrifice Soma gives the fees and food to the priests who take part.

अरं हितो भवति वाजिनायेतींद्रियं वै वीर्यं वाजिनं अजरसं हास्यॆ वाजिनं नापच्छिद्यते य एवं वेद । (*araṃ hito bhavati vājināyetīṃdriyaṃ vai vīryaṃ vājinaṃ ajarasaṃ hāsyè vājinaṃ nāpacchidyate ya evaṃ veda |*)

And Soma gives the priests and the sacrificer keenness of the senses, vigour and strength up to old age. For the one who knows this, the god Soma grants that keenness of the senses and the like is not destroyed midway. Seeing such a Soma brought to the sacrificial altar and placed in his own seat, all the people rejoice; thus the main purport of this Ṛk is described.

आगस्त्येव इत्यन्वाह (*āgastyeva ityanvāha*) **NOTE**

Now the Ṛk beginning **NOTE**

आगन्नेव ऋतुभिर्वर्धतु क्षयं दधातु नः सविता सुप्रजामिषं । स नः क्षपाभिरहभिश्च जिन्वतु प्रजावंतं रयिमस्मे समिन्वतु ॥ (*āganneva ṛtubhirvardhatu kṣayaṃ dadhātu naḥ savitā suprajāmiṣaṃ | sa naḥ kṣapābhirahabhiśca jinvatu prajāvaṃtaṃ rayimasme saminvatu ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-53-7)

He recites this Ṛk. This mantra is in the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā itself. Its main purport is this: May this god Soma graciously come to our sacrificial ground; having come here, accompanied by the seasons, may he increase our dwelling; and may this god Soma, as our impeller, provide us with good offspring, sons, grandsons and the rest, and with food; and may that god delight us through days and nights; may he obtain for us progeny (sons and the rest) and wealth.

आगतो हि स तर्हि भवति ऋतुभिर्वर्धतु क्षयमित्यृतवो वै सोमस्य राज्यो राजभ्रातरो यथा मनुष्यस्यतैरेवैनं तत्सहागमयति । (*āgato hi sa tarhi bhavati ṛtubhirvardhatu kṣayamityṛtavo vai somasya rājyo rājabhrātaro yathā manuṣyasyatairevainaṃ tatsahāgamayati |*)

The meaning of the first quarter-verse of this Ṛk is explained: the word आगन् (*āgan*) at the beginning of this Ṛk indicates that after the purchase of the Soma, Soma has come to the sacrificial ground. In worldly life, just as men have brothers with them, so kings also have brothers. Here Soma is the king. The seasons are his brothers. This King Soma has graciously come here together with these brothers, the seasons. The Hotṛ recites the first quarter-verse, which has this meaning, and so wishes a welcome to King Soma together with his brothers.

दधातु नः सविता सुप्रजामिषमित्याशिषमाशास्ते । स नः क्षपाभिरहभिश्च जिन्वत्वित्यहानि वा अहानि रात्रयः क्षपा अहोरात्तैरेवास्मा एतामाशिषमाशास्ते प्रजावंतं रयिमस्मे समिन्वत्वित्याशिषमेवाशास्ते । प (*dadhātu naḥ savitā suprajāmiṣamityāśiṣamāśāste | sa naḥ kṣapābhirahabhiśca jinvatvityahāni vā ahāni rātrayaḥ kṣapā ahorāttairevāsmā etāmāśiṣamāśāste prajāvaṃtaṃ rayimasme saminvatvityāśiṣamevāśāste | pa*)

Having explained the first quarter-verse, they explain the meaning of the following quarter-verses. May this god Soma, the impeller of all, protect us by giving us good sons and food. And may that god, delighting us through days and nights, protect us by giving us the line of offspring, sons, grandsons and the rest, and wealth. Such is the wish that is expressed.

(The Ṛk beginning आगन् (*āgan*) quoted above has Savitṛ as its deity. This whole sūkta of seven Ṛks, seen by the seer Vāmadeva, is addressed to the deity Savitṛ. Nevertheless it has been interpreted and used there with reference to the deity Soma. Since the matters stated in this Ṛk are appropriate to the occasion of bringing the Soma, it is natural that this Ṛk is used with reference to the deity Soma. In sacrificial rites and the like,

### Printed p. 32

एतद्वै यज्ञस्य समृद्धं यद्रूपसमृद्धं यत्कर्म क्रियमाणमृगभिवदति (*etadvai yajñasya samṛddhaṃ yadrūpasamṛddhaṃ yatkarma kriyamāṇamṛgabhivadati*) (Ai. Brā. 3-13)

according to this Śruti text it is said that in each rite the Ṛks that set forth the matters of that rite and explain the meaning appropriate to the occasion should be used; and since here, at the time of bringing the Soma, all the matters in which the priests and the sacrificer pray to the deity Soma are found in this Ṛk, this Ṛk, though it belongs to another deity, has been recited here in the belief that it is in favour of the deity Soma. No fault is seen in this.)

After that the Hotṛ recites the seventh Ṛk in this rite.

या ते धामानि हविषा यजंतीत्यन्नाह (*yā te dhāmāni haviṣā yajaṃtītyannāha*)

The seventh Ṛk that the Hotṛ recites is in the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā itself:

या ते धामानि हविषा यजंति ता ते विश्वा परिभूरस्तु यज्ञं । गयस्थानः प्रतरणः सुवीरोऽवीरहा प्रचरा सोम दुर्यान् ॥ (*yā te dhāmāni haviṣā yajaṃti tā te viśvā paribhūrastu yajñaṃ | gayasthānaḥ prataraṇaḥ suvīro 'vīrahā pracarā soma duryān ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-91-4)

The main purport of this Ṛk is: O Soma, the sacrificers who perform the sacrifice worship your places, the Uttaravedi and the rest, with oblations and the like. Since those places of yours pervade all places, you dwell surrounding the sacrificial ground; and you are the one who nourishes and increases our cattle; the one who brings us across all troubles; the one who gives us good sons, grandsons and the rest; and moreover, O god Soma, you are not a slayer of the excellent heroes among us. Such as you are, now graciously turn towards our house.

ता ते विश्वा परिभूरस्तु यज्ञं गयस्थानः प्रतरणः सुवीर इति गवां नत स्थावयिता प्रतारयित्यैधीत्येव तदाह ॥ (*tā te viśvā paribhūrastu yajñaṃ gayasthānaḥ prataraṇaḥ suvīra iti gavāṃ nata sthāvayitā pratārayityaidhītyeva tadāha ‖*)

Since the meaning of the first quarter-verse is clear, the second and third quarter-verses are explained. Since their meaning too has been stated above, there is no need to say it again.

अवीरहा प्रचरा सोम दुर्यानिति गृहा वै मर्या बिभ्यति वै सोमाद्राज्ञ आयतो यजमानस्य गृहाः स यदेतामन्वाह शांत्यैवैनं तच्छमयति सोऽस्य शांतो न प्रजां न पशून् हिनस्ति । (*avīrahā pracarā soma duryāniti gṛhā vai maryā bibhyati vai somādrājña āyato yajamānasya gṛhāḥ sa yadetāmanvāha śāṃtyaivainaṃ tacchamayati so 'sya śāṃto na prajāṃ na paśūn hinasti |*)

Now the meaning of the fourth quarter-verse is explained: seeing this King Soma coming towards the sacrificer's house, the heroic men in the sacrificer's house, thinking that through excess or deficiency in the service we render to this King Soma

### Printed p. 33

this god will become angry, the heroic men and the others in the house are afraid. Then the Hotṛ, in order to remove that fear, recites the fourth quarter-verse, which has the word अवीरहा (*avīrahā*), that is, this god Soma is not a slayer of heroes, and thus pacifies King Soma with words of peace. King Soma too, satisfied by those words of peace, does not harm the sacrificer's sons and the rest, his cattle and his household.

इमां धियं शिक्षमाणस्य देवेति नारुण्या परिदधाति (*imāṃ dhiyaṃ śikṣamāṇasya deveti nāruṇyā paridadhāti*)

Now the Hotṛ recites the eighth Ṛk, इमां धियं (*imāṃ dhiyaṃ*), which has Varuṇa as its deity, and so brings this rite called Somapravāhaṇa to its close.

इमां धियं शिक्षमाणस्य देव क्रतुं दक्षं वरुण संशिशाधि । ययाति विश्वा दुरिता तरेम सुतर्माणमधि नावं रुहेम ॥ (*imāṃ dhiyaṃ śikṣamāṇasya deva kratuṃ dakṣaṃ varuṇa saṃśiśādhi | yayāti viśvā duritā tarema sutarmāṇamadhi nāvaṃ ruhema ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-42-3)

This Ṛk too is in the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā itself. The main purport of this Ṛk is: O god Varuṇa, to this sacrificer, who is devoted to the intention set on the performance of this sacrifice (that is, who wishes to perform the sacrifice in the prescribed manner), teach well the capacity, skill and knowledge relating to the sacrifice. Climbing into the boat of instruction, which is your speech, may we cross over all misfortunes and sins and be saved.

वरुणदेवत्यो ना एष तावद्यावदुपनद्धो यावत्परिश्रितानि प्रपद्यते स्वय्यैवैनं तद्देवतया स्वेन छंदसा समर्धयति । (*varuṇadevatyo nā eṣa tāvadyāvadupanaddho yāvatpariśritāni prapadyate svayyaivainaṃ taddevatayā svena chaṃdasā samardhayati |*)

The reason is stated why a Ṛk addressed to Varuṇa should be recited to bring this Somapravāhaṇa rite to a close. Until now the Soma that was bought and brought was tied with bonds such as rope, covered with cloth and the like, and brought to places such as the prāgvaṃśa. Whatever object it may be, the bond that ties it or the covering of cloth and the like laid over it is subject to the noose of the god Varuṇa. By the rule that a Ṛk praying to Varuṇa must be recited in order to untie it, the Hotṛ recites the mantra that has Varuṇa as its deity. Earlier, when Soma was prayed to, he was praised with Ṛks in the Gāyatrī metre, which is dear to the god Soma or his own. Now the mantra to Varuṇa, which is recited at the time of the completion of the rite, is in the Triṣṭubh metre. That too is Soma's own metre. How? There is an earlier history in this matter. Formerly the Soma was in the world of the gods. The gods thought that it must be brought to the earth, and when they considered who was able to bring it, it was decided that the metres, the Gāyatrī and the others, were capable of bringing the Soma.

### Printed p. 34

जगत्युदपतच्चतुर्दशाक्षरा सती साप्राप्य न्यवर्तत तस्यै द्वे अक्षरे अमीयेताग्ं सा पशुभिश्च दीक्षया चागच्छत्तस्माज्जगती छंदसां पशव्यतमा तस्मात्पशुमंतं दीक्षोपनमति त्रिष्टुगुदपतत्रयोदशाक्षरा सती साप्राप्य न्यवर्तत तस्यै द्वे अक्षरे अमीयेताग्ं सा दक्षिणाभिश्च तपसा जागच्छत्तस्मात्रिष्टुभो लोके माध्यंदिने सवने दक्षिणा नीयंत एतत्पलु वाव शप (*jagatyudapataccaturdaśākṣarā satī sāprāpya nyavartata tasyai dve akṣare amīyetāgṃ sā paśubhiśca dīkṣayā cāgacchattasmājjagatī chaṃdasāṃ paśavyatamā tasmātpaśumaṃtaṃ dīkṣopanamati triṣṭugudapatatrayodaśākṣarā satī sāprāpya nyavartata tasyai dve akṣare amīyetāgṃ sā dakṣiṇābhiśca tapasā jāgacchattasmātriṣṭubho loke mādhyaṃdine savane dakṣiṇā nīyaṃta etatpalu vāva śapa*)

इत्याहुर्यः स्वं ददातीति गायत्र्युदपतच्चतुरक्षरा सत्यजया ज्योतिषा तमस्या अजाभ्यरुंधत तदजाया अजत्वग्ं सा सोमं चाहरच्चत्वरि चाक्षराणि साष्टाक्षरा समपद्यत ब्रह्मवादिनो वदंति कस्मात्सत्याद्गायश्री कनिष्ठा छंदसाग्ं सती यज्ञमुखं परीयायेति यदेवादः सोममाहरत्तस्माद्यज्ञमुखं पर्यैत्तस्मात्तेजस्विनीतमा पद्व्यां द्वे सवने समगृह्णान्मुखेनैकं यन्मुखेन समगृह्णात्तदधयुत्तस्माद्वे सवने शुक्रवती प्रातःसवनं च माध्यदिनं च तस्मात्तृतीयसवनमृजीषमभिषुण्णंति धीतमिव हि मन्यंते आशिरमवनयति सशुक्रत्वायाथो संभरत्येवैनत् । (*ityāhuryaḥ svaṃ dadātīti gāyatryudapataccaturakṣarā satyajayā jyotiṣā tamasyā ajābhyaruṃdhata tadajāyā ajatvagṃ sā somaṃ cāharaccatvari cākṣarāṇi sāṣṭākṣarā samapadyata brahmavādino vadaṃti kasmātsatyādgāyaśrī kaniṣṭhā chaṃdasāgṃ satī yajñamukhaṃ parīyāyeti yadevādaḥ somamāharattasmādyajñamukhaṃ paryaittasmāttejasvinītamā padvyāṃ dve savane samagṛhṇānmukhenaikaṃ yanmukhena samagṛhṇāttadadhayuttasmādve savane śukravatī prātaḥsavanaṃ ca mādhyadinaṃ ca tasmāttṛtīyasavanamṛjīṣamabhiṣuṇṇaṃti dhītamiva hi manyaṃte āśiramavanayati saśukratvāyātho saṃbharatyevainat |*) (Tai. Saṃ. 6-1-6-2, 3, 4, 5)

Then first the Jagatī metre set out, saying that it would bring the Soma, and leapt into the atmosphere. At that time each quarter of the Jagatī metre had fourteen syllables. But unable to bring the Soma, it grew weary and came back exhausted. Then it lost two syllables of the fourteen; but on its return it brought with it the sacrificial animals that were there and the order of consecration (the rule of undertaking the consecration in a sacrifice). Therefore in the world the Jagatī metre became famous as rich in cattle. And the consecration follows a sacrificer who is endowed with the wealth of cattle. Then the Triṣṭubh metre, saying that it would bring the Soma, leapt into the atmosphere. At that time each quarter of the Triṣṭubh metre had thirteen syllables. The Triṣṭubh metre too, unable to bring the Soma, grew weary and spent, lost two of its thirteen syllables and came back. On its return it brought with it the sacrificial fee and austerity; therefore, when a sacrifice is performed, fees are brought and given at the midday pressing (to the priest). The giving away of what one has is called austerity. Then the Gāyatrī metre, saying that it would bring the Soma, set out and flew into the atmosphere in the form of a falcon. At that time each quarter of the Gāyatrī had four syllables. When setting out, the Gāyatrī took with it, to be its companion and help, a shining she-goat. This goat helped the Gāyatrī when it brought the Soma. Therefore the goat became famous by the name अजा (*ajā*). Then the Gāyatrī brought back the Soma that was in heaven and the four syllables that the Jagatī and Triṣṭubh metres had lost.

### Printed p. 35

The four syllables it brought back and the four syllables it had before joined, and so each quarter of the Gāyatrī came to have eight syllables. (The Jagatī metre came to have twelve syllables and the Triṣṭubh metre eleven syllables.) As the Brahmavādins say, a doubt arises here: although the Gāyatrī metre is much smaller than all the metres, how did it obtain prominence in the sacrifice? The answer: this Gāyatrī brought the Soma; therefore the Gāyatrī has come to have precedence in sacrificial rites, and it also shines with great brilliance. When this Gāyatrī brought the Soma from heaven, it took the form of a falcon and brought the two kinds of pressing, the morning pressing and the midday pressing, holding them with its two feet (of the three quarters of eight syllables of the Gāyatrī, two are said to be the feet and the third the mouth), and the third, the third pressing, holding it in its mouth (its beak). Because the morning and midday pressings held in the feet remained just as they were without any alteration, in both those pressings the Soma plants are crushed and the bright, pure juice is pressed out. But since it is thought that, in bringing the third pressing in its beak, it bit the Soma plant a little, pressed out the juice and drank it, at the time of the third pressing fresh Soma plants are not used; the juice is pressed out once at the morning and midday pressings, and from the crushed plants that remain, like husks (the Ṛjīṣa), the remaining juice is pressed out well once more. Since the juice drawn out in this way is not abundant, āśira substances (milk, curds, flour) are added to it to make it fit for drinking.

As told in this history, the Triṣṭubh metre too made an effort to bring the Soma from heaven. Therefore here the Triṣṭubh metre too is said to be Soma's own.

शिक्षमाणस्य देवेति शिक्षते ना एष यो यजते क्रतुं दक्षं वरुण संशिशाधीति वीर्यं प्रज्ञानं वरुण संशिशाधीत्येव तदाह । (*śikṣamāṇasya deveti śikṣate nā eṣa yo yajate kratuṃ dakṣaṃ varuṇa saṃśiśādhīti vīryaṃ prajñānaṃ varuṇa saṃśiśādhītyeva tadāha |*)

As long as the Soma plants are tied with rope and covered with cloth, they are considered to be in the possession of Varuṇa. Now Varuṇa is prayed to, in order to remove the bond and take off the cloth. In so praying, the Hotṛ, by reciting this mantra, prays to Varuṇa: O Varuṇa, give right understanding to the sacrificer who is performing this sacrifice; give keenness of the senses, the capacity to perform the sacrifice, and knowledge.

ययाति विश्वा दुरिता तरेम सुतर्माणमधि नावं रुहेमेति यज्ञो वै सुतर्मा नौः कृष्णाजिनं वै सुतर्मा नौर्वाग्वै सुतर्मा नार्वाचमेव तदारुह्य तया स्वर्गं लोकमभि संतरति । (*yayāti viśvā duritā tarema sutarmāṇamadhi nāvaṃ ruhemeti yajño vai sutarmā nauḥ kṛṣṇājinaṃ vai sutarmā naurvāgvai sutarmā nārvācameva tadāruhya tayā svargaṃ lokamabhi saṃtarati |*)

### Printed p. 36

Here the boat means the sacrifice; the black antelope skin used in the sacrifice for the consecration is the boat; the sentences in the form of mantras are the boat. The meaning is: climbing into such a boat, that is, beginning the sacrifice, receiving the consecration in it, reciting the mantras suited to the time and performing the sacrifice, may we shed all sins and obtain the heavenly world. By reciting a Ṛk with such a meaning the sacrificer who performs the sacrifice will obtain heaven.

ता एता अष्टावन्नाह रूपसमृद्धा एतद्वै यज्ञस्य समृद्धं यद्रूपसमृद्धं यत्कर्म क्रियमाणमृगभिवदति ॥ (*tā etā aṣṭāvannāha rūpasamṛddhā etadvai yajñasya samṛddhaṃ yadrūpasamṛddhaṃ yatkarma kriyamāṇamṛgabhivadati ‖*)

These eight Ṛks together complete the rite called Somapravāhaṇa. By reciting Ṛks that bring the rite to completion, the sacrifice is as if performed with all its parts; and it is by reciting Ṛks that relate to the rite concerned and contain that very meaning that the respective rites are completed and the sacrifice is properly accomplished.

तासां त्रिः प्रथमामन्वाह त्रिरुत्तमां ता द्वादश संपद्यंते द्वादश नै मासाः संवत्सरः संवत्सरः प्रजापतिः । प्रजापत्यायतनाभिरेवाभी राध्योति य एवं वेद । त्रिः प्रथमां त्रिरुत्तमामन्वाह यज्ञस्यैव तद्वर्सौ नह्यति स्थेन्ने बलायाविस्रंसाय ॥ (*tāsāṃ triḥ prathamāmanvāha triruttamāṃ tā dvādaśa saṃpadyaṃte dvādaśa nai māsāḥ saṃvatsaraḥ saṃvatsaraḥ prajāpatiḥ | prajāpatyāyatanābhirevābhī rādhyoti ya evaṃ veda | triḥ prathamāṃ triruttamāmanvāha yajñasyaiva tadvarsau nahyati sthenne balāyāvisraṃsāya ‖*) (Ai. Brā. 3-14)

In reciting these eight Ṛks, he recites the first Ṛk and the last Ṛk three times each. Thus twelve Ṛks in all are recited. There are twelve months in a year. The year is of the nature of Prajāpati. The Ṛks mentioned above also rest in Prajāpati. One who knows this becomes victorious in the respective undertakings through reciting these Ṛks. By reciting the first and last Ṛks three times each at the beginning and at the end of the rite, the rite called the sacrifice is firmly bound with ropes in the form of mantras and becomes firm, so that it does not slip away.

Here the rite called Somapravāhaṇa ends.

**The Subsequent Introduction**

So far the Ṛks connected with the Somapravāhaṇa have been explained; now the procedure to be followed on the occasion of unloading the Soma from the cart is explained.

### Printed p. 37

अन्यतरोऽनड्वान्युक्तः स्यादन्यतरो विमुक्तोऽथ राजानमुपावहरेयुः । (*anyataro 'naḍvānyuktaḥ syādanyataro vimukto 'tha rājānamupāvahareyuḥ |*)

Of the two oxen yoked to the cart that has brought King Soma from the place of purchase to the Prācīnavaṃśa, only one is to be unyoked and the other left as it is, yoked; the Ṛtvijs then bring Soma down from the cart and carry him to the Uttaravedi. This is the rule.

यदुभयोर्विमुक्तयोरुपावहरेयुः पितृदेवत्यं राजानं कुर्युः । (*yadubhayorvimuktayorupāvahareyuḥ pitṛdevatyaṃ rājānaṃ kuryuḥ |*)

If, not acting according to the rule stated above, they unyoke both the oxen of the cart and then carry Soma to the Uttaravedi, then the Pitṛ-deities take Soma to themselves. Soma does not become fit for the gods and worthy of the sacrifice.

यद्युक्तयोरयोगक्षेमः प्रजा विंदेत्ताः प्रजाः परिप्लवेरन्। योऽनड्वान्विमुक्तस्तच्छालासदां प्रजानां रूपं यो युक्तस्तच्चक्रियाणां ते ये युक्तेऽन्ये विमुक्तेऽन्य उपावहरंत्युभावेव ते क्षेमयोगौ कल्पयंति । (*yadyuktayorayogakṣemaḥ prajā viṃdettāḥ prajāḥ pariplaveran| yo 'naḍvānvimuktastacchālāsadāṃ prajānāṃ rūpaṃ yo yuktastaccakriyāṇāṃ te ye yukte 'nye vimukte 'nya upāvaharaṃtyubhāveva te kṣemayogau kalpayaṃti |*)

If Soma is brought down and carried off while both oxen are left yoked to the cart, then the yoga (good fortune) of acquiring wealth and the yoga of preserving what has been acquired, so that it is not lost, are both destroyed; moreover there is the danger that the sacrificer's sons and others will go away in various directions. Of the two oxen yoked to the cart, the one that has been unyoked from the cart is the form of the sons and others who are in the sacrificial hall and the house. The ox that has not been unyoked from the cart is the form of the worldly and Vedic rites that go on in the sacrificial hall. Here the word चक्रि (*cakri*) means the cart that has wheels. The sons, grandsons and others, who are the progeny of the sacrificer travelling in that cart, are called चक्रियाः (*cakriyāḥ*). The wise among the sacrificers unyoke one ox at the time of the lowering of Soma, leave the other as it is, and perform the lowering of Soma; thereby they obtain both the acquisition of wealth and its safekeeping, as said before. What the Taittirīya Saṃhitā says on this very matter deserves notice.

यदुभौ विमुच्यातिथ्यं गृह्णीयाद्यज्ञं विच्छिंद्याद्यदुभावविमुच्य यथानाग ताय तिथ्यं क्रियते तादृगेव तद्विमुक्तोऽन्योऽनड्वान्भवत्यविमुक्तोऽ न्यूऽथातिथ्यं गृह्णाति यज्ञस्य संतत्यै । (*yadubhau vimucyātithyaṃ gṛhṇīyādyajñaṃ vicchiṃdyādyadubhāvavimucya yathānāga tāya tithyaṃ kriyate tādṛgeva tadvimukto 'nyo 'naḍvānbhavatyavimukto ' nyū 'thātithyaṃ gṛhṇāti yajñasya saṃtatyai |*) (Kṛ. Saṃ. 6-2-1-1) **NOTE**

If one unyokes both the oxen of the cart that has brought the Soma and lowers the Soma, King Soma does not accept the hospitality; and by doing so the sacrifice is cut off and is not completed, but stops in the middle. If both oxen are left yoked to the cart and made to stand so, it is

### Printed p. 38

as though King Soma had not come to the house to be received as a guest. It is as though he had simply gone away. Therefore it is not right to leave both oxen yoked either. Then what should be done so that the sacrifice is completed in full? The answer is that one ox is to be unyoked from the cart, and the other is to remain yoked to the cart without being unyoked. If this is done, King Soma comes to the sacrificial ground and accepts the sacrificer's hospitality, and the sacrifice also reaches its completion without any interruption. This is the meaning.

An ancient story is cited and explained in support of the rule that the rite of lowering Soma (bringing Soma down from the cart) is to be performed in the north-eastern quarter:

देवासुरा वा एषु लोकेषु समयतंत त एतस्यां प्राच्यां दिश्ययतंत तांस्ततोऽसुरा अजयंस्ते दक्षिणस्यां दिश्ययतंत तांस्ततोऽसुरा अजयंस्ते प्रतीच्यां दिश्ययतंत तांस्ततोऽसुरा अजयंस्त उदीच्यां दिश्ययतंत तांस्ततोऽसुरा अजयंस्त उदीच्यां प्राच्यां दिश्ययतंत ते ततो न पराजयंत सैषा दिगपराजिता तस्मादेतस्यां दिशि यतेत वा यातयेद्वेश्वरो हानृणाकर्तोः । (*devāsurā vā eṣu lokeṣu samayataṃta ta etasyāṃ prācyāṃ diśyayataṃta tāṃstato 'surā ajayaṃste dakṣiṇasyāṃ diśyayataṃta tāṃstato 'surā ajayaṃste pratīcyāṃ diśyayataṃta tāṃstato 'surā ajayaṃsta udīcyāṃ diśyayataṃta tāṃstato 'surā ajayaṃsta udīcyāṃ prācyāṃ diśyayataṃta te tato na parājayaṃta saiṣā digaparājitā tasmādetasyāṃ diśi yateta vā yātayedveśvaro hānṛṇākartoḥ |*)

Long ago, in this world, the gods and the Asuras were fighting. They fought in the eastern quarter. In that battle the Asuras defeated the gods; then they fought in the southern quarter. There too the Asuras won; then they fought in the western quarter. There too victory went to the Asuras; then they fought in the northern quarter. There too the Asuras defeated the gods and became victorious; afterwards they fought in the north-eastern quarter, which lies between the northern and the eastern quarters. There the gods were not defeated; victory was theirs. Therefore (because the gods were not defeated) the north-eastern quarter came to be named अपराजिता (*aparājitā*). Therefore, whatever good work is to be done, it should be done in that quarter, that is, in the north-eastern quarter, or one should strive to do it there. By so doing the sacrificer discharges his debt and becomes freed from it.

Here, as to what a debt is and what its discharge is, the Taittirīya Saṃhitā has a clear description:

जायमानो वै ब्राह्मणस्त्रिभिर्यणवा जायते ब्रह्मचर्येण ऋषिभ्यो यज्ञेन देवेभ्यः प्रजया पितृभ्य एष वा अनृणो यः पुत्री यज्वा ब्रह्मचारिवासी । (*jāyamāno vai brāhmaṇastribhiryaṇavā jāyate brahmacaryeṇa ṛṣibhyo yajñena devebhyaḥ prajayā pitṛbhya eṣa vā anṛṇo yaḥ putrī yajvā brahmacārivāsī |*) (Tai. Saṃ. 6-3-10-5)

### Printed p. 39

That is, a twice-born man (Brāhmaṇa, Kṣatriya, Vaiśya) is born, at the very time of his birth, together with three kinds of debts, called the debt to the Ṛṣis, the debt to the gods and the debt to the Pitṛs. For these debts to be paid: after the Upanayana, if he studies the Veda in the Brahmacarya stage in the teacher's house, the debt to the Ṛṣis is paid; afterwards, when he marries, takes up the householder's stage, performs sacrifices and the like and worships the gods, the debt to the gods is paid; by begetting sons in his married wife for the continuance of lawful progeny, the debt to the Pitṛs is paid. It is said here that the sacrificer becomes free from these three kinds of debts.

Now the cause of King Soma's victory is stated:

ते देवा अब्रुवन्नराजतया वै नो जयंति राजानं करवामहा इति तथेति ते सोमं राजानमकुर्वंस्ते सोमेन राज्ञा सर्वा दिशोऽजयन्नेष वै सोमराजा यो यजते प्राचि तिष्ठत्याददति तेन प्राचीं दिशं जयति तं दक्षिणा परिवहंति तेन दक्षिणां दिशं जयति तं प्रत्यंचमावर्तयंति ते प्रतीचीं दिशं जयति तमुदीचस्तिष्ठत उपावहरंति तेनोदीचीं दिशं जयति सोमेन राज्ञा सर्वा दिशो जयति एवं वेद ॥ (*te devā abruvannarājatayā vai no jayaṃti rājānaṃ karavāmahā iti tatheti te somaṃ rājānamakurvaṃste somena rājñā sarvā diśo 'jayanneṣa vai somarājā yo yajate prāci tiṣṭhatyādadati tena prācīṃ diśaṃ jayati taṃ dakṣiṇā parivahaṃti tena dakṣiṇāṃ diśaṃ jayati taṃ pratyaṃcamāvartayaṃti te pratīcīṃ diśaṃ jayati tamudīcastiṣṭhata upāvaharaṃti tenodīcīṃ diśaṃ jayati somena rājñā sarvā diśo jayati evaṃ veda ‖*) (Ai. Brā. 3-14)

The gods spoke among themselves thus: We have no king who can take the lead in battles and the like and win victory for us; therefore we met with defeat in the eastern and the other quarters. So let us settle on a king. Having decided thus, they chose the god Soma to be king over them. Through that, the gods obtained victory in the eastern and the other quarters. Since this is so, for whichever sacrificer performs a Soma sacrifice, Soma is the king. By the power of this King Soma the sacrificer gains victory everywhere. How is that? When Soma is being brought in the cart, the Ṛtvijs make the cart stand facing the east and place Soma in it. Because Soma is in a cart facing east, the sacrificer gains victory in the eastern quarter. Afterwards, while Soma is being brought, they turn the cart in which Soma is, to face the south. Since Soma then faces south, victory is gained for the sacrificer in the southern quarter also. Afterwards, by turning that cart to the western quarter, victory comes in the western quarter also. Then, by turning that cart to the northern quarter, victory is gained in the northern quarter also. After that, when the cart has come and stands between the northern and the eastern quarters, from there (from the north-eastern quarter) they bring Soma down from the cart and carry him to the Uttaravedi. That is to say, the cart that was left on the west side of the Prācīnavaṃśa is turned to face the east in order to be brought to the Uttara-

### Printed p. 40

vedi, which lies to the east of the sacrificial hall; then, as it goes round the sacrificial hall, it is brought to the south, then to the west, then to the north, and when it arrives at the north-eastern quarter they bring Soma down from the cart. This is the meaning. The great sage Āpastamba explains this matter by the sentence संजग्राह प्रत्यवस्तनुवस्यत इति प्रांचोऽभिप्रवाय दक्षिणमावर्तत इत्यग्रेण प्राग्वंशं प्रागीषमुदगीषं वा शकटमवस्थाप्य (*saṃjagrāha pratyavastanuvasyata iti prāṃco 'bhipravāya dakṣiṇamāvartata ityagreṇa prāgvaṃśaṃ prāgīṣamudagīṣaṃ vā śakaṭamavasthāpya*) **GLOSS**. One who knows this matter conquers all the quarters.

Here the explanation of the Uttarapīṭhikā ends.

The explanation of the rite called Somapravāhaṇa ends.

**The Nature of the Three Savanas**

सूयते सोमः अत्रेति सवनानि सोमयागाः । (*sūyate somaḥ atreti savanāni somayāgāḥ |*) The word सवन (*savana*) is stated among the names of sacrifice (Ni. 3-19).

एता विश्वा सवना तूतुमाकृषे स्वयं सूनो सहसो यानि दधिषे । वराय ते पात्रं धर्मणे तना यज्ञो मंत्रो ब्रहोद्यतं वचः । (*etā viśvā savanā tūtumākṛṣe svayaṃ sūno sahaso yāni dadhiṣe | varāya te pātraṃ dharmaṇe tanā yajño maṃtro brahodyataṃ vacaḥ |*) **GLOSS** (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-50-6)

In this Ṛk the meaning स्थानानि (*sthānāni*) is given for the word सवना (*savanā*). The Soma sacrifices, which are the occasions of pressing out the Soma juice, are described here as the meaning of the word सवन (*savana*).

Here, through the form of the savana, there is indicated the importance of the sacrifice, which establishes a mutual relation between the earth and the atmosphere, and also the relation between the sacrifice and Indra, who is the lord of this sacrifice, who gains satisfaction from this sacrifice and who, through it, bestows his favour on the world. The point is also indicated that the savana is the chief means of the well-being and peace of both earth and atmosphere.

The meaning of the word savana is very profound. It mirrors the principle of the universe. Just as the sacrifice has the division called the three savanas, so the Śruti passages describe that in worldly affairs too there are distinctions that follow this division; that in every individual life too there are three states corresponding to the three savanas; and that the creation of the Brāhmaṇa and the other classes also follows this very division. Moreover, in all the Śrutis there is the account that the deities headed by Indra, who are the presiding deities of the three savanas, regulate and mirror the divisions of the universal principle. And each savana also indicates its presiding deity, the metre connected with it, and a creative act. Only in regard to the deity of the third savana is there room for doubt even in the Śruti itself. Concerning the greatness of the savana the Taittirīya Saṃhitā says as follows.

प्रजापतिर्वाव ज्येष्ठस्स हैते नाग्रेयजत प्रजापतिरकामयत प्रजायेयेति स मुखतस्त्रिवृतं निरमिमित तमग्निर्देवतान्नसृज्यत गायत्री छंदो रथंतरग्‌ंसाम ब्राह्मणो मनुष्याणामजः पशूनां तस्मात्ते मुख्या मुखतो ह्यसृज्यंतोरसो बाहुभ्यां पंचदशं निरमिमित तमिंद्रो देवतान्नसृज्यत त्रिष्टुप् छंदो बृहत्साम राजन्यो मनुष्याणामविः पशूनां तस्मात्ते वीर्यावंतो वीर्याद्दस्यृज्यंत मध्यतस्सप्तदशं निरमिमित तं विश्वे देवा देवता अन्वसृज्यंत जगतीछंदो वैरूपग्‌ं साम वैश्यो मनुष्याणां गावः पशूनां तस्मात्त आद्या अन्नधानाद्व्यसृज्यंत तस्माद्भूयाग्ंसोन्येभ्यो भूयिष्ठा हि देवता अन्वसृज्यंत पत्त एकविग्‌ंशं निरमिमित ॥ (*prajāpatirvāva jyeṣṭhassa haite nāgreyajata prajāpatirakāmayata prajāyeyeti sa mukhatastrivṛtaṃ niramimita tamagnirdevatānnasṛjyata gāyatrī chaṃdo rathaṃtaragṃsāma brāhmaṇo manuṣyāṇāmajaḥ paśūnāṃ tasmātte mukhyā mukhato hyasṛjyaṃtoraso bāhubhyāṃ paṃcadaśaṃ niramimita tamiṃdro devatānnasṛjyata triṣṭup chaṃdo bṛhatsāma rājanyo manuṣyāṇāmaviḥ paśūnāṃ tasmātte vīryāvaṃto vīryāddasyṛjyaṃta madhyatassaptadaśaṃ niramimita taṃ viśve devā devatā anvasṛjyaṃta jagatīchaṃdo vairūpagṃ sāma vaiśyo manuṣyāṇāṃ gāvaḥ paśūnāṃ tasmātta ādyā annadhānādvyasṛjyaṃta tasmādbhūyāgṃsonyebhyo bhūyiṣṭhā hi devatā anvasṛjyaṃta patta ekavigṃśaṃ niramimita ‖*) (Tai. Saṃ. 7-1-1-4-5)

Prajāpati is the eldest, since he performed the sacrifice at the beginning. Wishing to carry out creation, he poured forth the Trivṛt stoma from his mouth. From it arose, among the gods, Agni; among the metres, Gāyatrī; among the Sāmans, Rathantara; among men, the Brāhmaṇa; among animals, the goat. When he poured forth the Pañcadaśa stoma from his arms, there arose, among the gods, Indra; among the metres, Triṣṭup; among the Sāmans, the Bṛhat Sāman; among men, the king; among animals, the sheep. When he poured forth the Saptadaśa stoma from his middle part, there arose, among the gods, the Viśvedevas; among the metres, Jagatī; among the Sāmans, the cow.

Following this order, in the Soma sacrifice the offering of the morning savana is directed to Agni, the offering of the midday savana to Indra, and the offering of the third savana to the Viśvedevas. As said in the Atharvaveda, the morning savana is assigned to the Aśvins, the midday savana to Indra and Agni, and the third savana to the Ṛbhus (Ā. Ve. 9-1-2) **NOTE**. As said in the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa, the third savana belongs to the Ṛbhus. It is called the Ārbhava Pavamāna. But there is a legend about what right the Ṛbhus have to receive the oblation in the sacrifice along with the gods. The Aitareya Brāhmaṇa describes it thus:

ऋभवो नै देवेषु तपसा सोमपीथमभ्यजयंस्तेभ्यः प्रातःसवने वाचि कल्पयिषंस्तानग्निर्वसुभिः प्रातस्सवनादनुदत । (*ṛbhavo nai deveṣu tapasā somapīthamabhyajayaṃstebhyaḥ prātaḥsavane vāci kalpayiṣaṃstānagnirvasubhiḥ prātassavanādanudata |*) (Ai. Brā. 13-6)

By their superhuman power of action the Ṛbhus attained the position of the gods. When the gods then made arrangements to include them with themselves in the drinking of Soma, Agni, together with the Vasus, drove them away

तेभ्यो माध्यंदिने सवने वाचि कल्पयिषंस्तानिंद्रो रुद्रैर्माध्यंदिनात्सवनादनुदत । (*tebhyo mādhyaṃdine savane vāci kalpayiṣaṃstāniṃdro rudrairmādhyaṃdinātsavanādanudata |*) (Ai. Brā. 13-6)

Afterwards, when the gods made arrangements to provide them a place in the midday savana, Indra, together with the Rudras, drove them away.

तेभ्यस्मृतीयसवने वाचि कल्पयिषंस्ताश्वेदेवाः अनोनुद्यंत नेह पास्यंति नेहेति स प्रजापतिरब्रवीत्सवितारं तव ना इमेऽंतेवासास्त्वमेवैभिः संपिबस्वीति स तथेत्यब्रवीत्सविता तान्वै त्वमुभयतः परिपिबेति तान्प्रजापतिरुभयतः पर्यपिबत् ॥ (*tebhyasmṛtīyasavane vāci kalpayiṣaṃstāśvedevāḥ anonudyaṃta neha pāsyaṃti neheti sa prajāpatirabravītsavitāraṃ tava nā ime 'ṃtevāsāstvamevaibhiḥ saṃpibasvīti sa tathetyabravītsavitā tānvai tvamubhayataḥ paripibeti tānprajāpatirubhayataḥ paryapibat ‖*) (Ai. Brā. 6-13)

When the Viśvedevas did not admit them at the time of the third savana, Prajāpati said to Savitṛ: These are your pupils; do you drink with them. Then Savitṛ and Prajāpati drank together with the Ṛbhus. Thus the place the Ṛbhus have in the third savana is shown. As said in another place of the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa,

प्रजापतिर्वै यज्ञं छंदांसि देवोभ्यो भागधेयानि व्यभजत्स गायत्रीमेवाग्नये वसुभ्यः प्रातःसवनेऽभजत्रिष्टुभमिंद्राय रुद्रेभ्यो मध्यंदिने जगतीं विश्वेभ्यो देवेभ्य आदित्येभ्यस्तृतीयसवने ॥ (*prajāpatirvai yajñaṃ chaṃdāṃsi devobhyo bhāgadheyāni vyabhajatsa gāyatrīmevāgnaye vasubhyaḥ prātaḥsavane 'bhajatriṣṭubhamiṃdrāya rudrebhyo madhyaṃdine jagatīṃ viśvebhyo devebhya ādityebhyastṛtīyasavane ‖*) (Ai. Brā. 12-2)

Prajāpati divided the shares of the sacrifice and the metres among the gods. In the morning savana he gave the Gāyatrī to Agni and the Vasus; in the midday savana the Triṣṭup to Indra and the Rudras (the Maruts?); and in the third savana the Jagatī to the Viśvedevas and the Ādityas.

Since Agni mirrors Brahmanical brilliance, Indra Kṣatriya power, and the Viśvedevas the Vaiśya's way of life, it is understood from the Taittirīya Saṃhitā passages cited above that the differences of the classes in creation also follow these divisions of the savanas. The Chāndogya Upaniṣad explains this same division from the spiritual point of view and says as follows:

ब्रह्मवादिनो वदंति यद्वसूनां प्रातःसवनं रुद्राणां माध्यंदिनं सवनमादित्यानां च विश्वेषां च देवानां तृतीयसवनं ॥ (*brahmavādino vadaṃti yadvasūnāṃ prātaḥsavanaṃ rudrāṇāṃ mādhyaṃdinaṃ savanamādityānāṃ ca viśveṣāṃ ca devānāṃ tṛtīyasavanaṃ ‖*) (Bhā. U. 2-24-1) **NOTE**

It states that the morning savana is allotted to the Vasus, the midday savana to the Rudras, and the third savana to the Ādityas and the Viśvedevas, and it tells the special mantras to be uttered at the time of these savanas.

From these mantras it is understood that the best places in the earth, in the atmosphere and in the heavenly world are obtained through the offering made in these savanas.

लोकद्वारमपावार्णा पश्येम त्वा वयं ......... अथ जुहोति नमोऽग्नये पृथिविक्षिते लोकक्षिते लोकं मे यजमानाय विंदैष वै यजमानस्य लोक एतास्मि ॥ (*lokadvāramapāvārṇā paśyema tvā vayaṃ ......... atha juhoti namo 'gnaye pṛthivikṣite lokakṣite lokaṃ me yajamānāya viṃdaiṣa vai yajamānasya loka etāsmi ‖*) (Chā. U. 2-24)

Before the morning savana, the sacrificer addresses the Vasus: "Open the door to the entrance of your world. May we also obtain a sight of you"; having prayed thus, he makes an offering directed to Agni and becomes fit to obtain the best place in the world of the earth. In the same way, praying to the Rudras and the Ādityas, and making offerings to them in the midday savana and the third savana, he becomes fit for the best place in the world of the atmosphere and in the heavenly world. Thus the greatness of the three savanas and the fruit of serving them are described. Moreover, it is described, as stated below, that the whole consciousness of man is the mirror of the Soma sacrifice:

पुरुषो नाव यज्ञस्तस्य यानि चतुर्विंशतिवर्षाणि तत्प्रातःसवनं चतुर्विंशत्यक्षरा गायत्री गायत्रं प्रातःसवनं तदस्य वसवोऽन्वायत्ताः प्राणा नाव वसव एते हीदं सर्वं वासयंति ॥ (*puruṣo nāva yajñastasya yāni caturviṃśativarṣāṇi tatprātaḥsavanaṃ caturviṃśatyakṣarā gāyatrī gāyatraṃ prātaḥsavanaṃ tadasya vasavo 'nvāyattāḥ prāṇā nāva vasava ete hīdaṃ sarvaṃ vāsayaṃti ‖*) (Chā. U. 3-16-1)

The Puruṣa is the sacrifice. Because the Gāyatrī metre, consisting of twenty-four syllables, is the metre of the morning savana, the first twenty-four years of man are the morning savana. The Vasus are the presiding deities of this offering. The vital breaths are the Vasus. Those very breaths are the means of dwelling on the earth.

तं चेदेतस्मिन्वयसि किंचिदुपतपेत्स ब्रूयात्प्राणा वसव इदं मे प्रातःसवनं माध्यंदिनं सवनमनुसंतनुतेति माहं प्राणानां वसूनां मध्यॆ यज्ञो विलोप्सीयेत्युद्धैव तत एत्यगदो ह भवति ॥ (*taṃ cedetasminvayasi kiṃcidupatapetsa brūyātprāṇā vasava idaṃ me prātaḥsavanaṃ mādhyaṃdinaṃ savanamanusaṃtanuteti māhaṃ prāṇānāṃ vasūnāṃ madhyè yajño vilopsīyetyuddhaiva tata etyagado ha bhavati ‖*) (Chā. U. 3-16-2)

If during this period of twenty-four years sluggishness comes upon the body, then, if he prays, addressing the Vasus: "O you vital breaths (Vasus), the means of dwelling, may this morning savana of mine not be broken off in the middle, but carry me on to the midday savana as well; may this sacrifice of my life not be cut off", the man is freed from the sluggishness.

अथ यानि चतुर्विंशद्वर्षाणि तन्माध्यंदिनं सवनं चतुश्चत्वारिंशदक्षरा त्रिष्टुप् त्रैष्टुभं माध्यंदिनं सवनं तदस्य रुद्रा अन्वायत्ताः प्राणा वाव रुद्रा एते हीदं सर्वं रोदयंति । (*atha yāni caturviṃśadvarṣāṇi tanmādhyaṃdinaṃ savanaṃ catuścatvāriṃśadakṣarā triṣṭup traiṣṭubhaṃ mādhyaṃdinaṃ savanaṃ tadasya rudrā anvāyattāḥ prāṇā vāva rudrā ete hīdaṃ sarvaṃ rodayaṃti |*) (Chā. 3-16-3) **NOTE**

### Printed p. 44

The Triṣṭup metre consists of forty-four syllables, and the Triṣṭup is the metre of the midday savana. Therefore the next forty-four years, the second stage of man, are of the nature of the midday savana in the sacrifice of life. In this the Rudras are dependent. Because even the departure of the breath makes everyone weep, these breaths themselves are the Rudras.

तं चेहेतस्मिन्वयसि किंचिदुपतपेत्स ब्रूयात् प्राणा रुद्रा इदं मे माध्यंदिनं सवनं तृतीयसवनमनुसंतनुतेति माहं प्राणानां रुद्राणां मध्यॆ यज्ञो विलोप्सीयेत्युद्धैव तत एत्यगदो ह भवति । (*taṃ cehetasminvayasi kiṃcidupatapetsa brūyāt prāṇā rudrā idaṃ me mādhyaṃdinaṃ savanaṃ tṛtīyasavanamanusaṃtanuteti māhaṃ prāṇānāṃ rudrāṇāṃ madhyè yajño vilopsīyetyuddhaiva tata etyagado ha bhavati |*) (Bhā. 3-16-4) **NOTE**

If during these forty-four years, which are of the nature of the midday savana, there comes any sluggishness of the body, then, addressing the Rudras: "O Rudras, who are of the nature of breaths, may this midday savana of mine carry me up to the third savana. May my sacrifice of life not be cut off in the middle", if one prays thus, the man becomes freed from sluggishness **NOTE**.

अथ यान्यष्टाचत्वारिंशद्वर्षाणि तत्तृतीयसवनमष्टाचत्वारिंशदक्षरा जगती जागतं तृतीयसवनं तदस्यादित्या अन्वायत्ताः प्राणा नावादित्या एते हीदं सर्वमाददते । (*atha yānyaṣṭācatvāriṃśadvarṣāṇi tattṛtīyasavanamaṣṭācatvāriṃśadakṣarā jagatī jāgataṃ tṛtīyasavanaṃ tadasyādityā anvāyattāḥ prāṇā nāvādityā ete hīdaṃ sarvamādadate |*) (Chā. U. 3-16-5)

The Jagatī, which consists of forty-eight syllables, is the metre of the third savana; therefore the last forty-eight years of man are also of the nature of the evening savana. Because at the time of the departure of the breath they draw everything into themselves, the breaths are of the form of the Ādityas **NOTE**. And the breaths, being of the nature of the Ādityas, are the presiding deities of this third savana.

तं चेदेतस्मिन्वयसि किंचिदुपतपेत्स ब्रूयात्प्राणा आदित्या इदं मे तृतीयसवनमायुरनु संतनोतीति माहं प्राणानामादित्यानां मध्ये यज्ञो विलोप्सीयेत्युद्धैव तत एत्यगदो हैव भवति. (*taṃ cedetasminvayasi kiṃcidupatapetsa brūyātprāṇā ādityā idaṃ me tṛtīyasavanamāyuranu saṃtanotīti māhaṃ prāṇānāmādityānāṃ madhye yajño vilopsīyetyuddhaiva tata etyagado haiva bhavati.*) (Bhā. U. 3-16-6) **NOTE**

If sluggishness comes at this time, then, addressing the Ādityas: "May my sacrifice not be cut off in the middle. May my span of life be lived out in full", if one prays thus, the sacrifice of life is not cut off but is carried out completely.

एतद्दस्मै वै तद्विद्वानाह महिदास ऐतरेयः स किं म एतदुपतपसि योऽहमनेन न प्रेष्यामीति स ह षोडशं वर्षशतमजीवत्प्र ह षोडशं वर्षशतं जीवति य एवं वेद । (*etaddasmai vai tadvidvānāha mahidāsa aitareyaḥ sa kiṃ ma etadupatapasi yo 'hamanena na preṣyāmīti sa ha ṣoḍaśaṃ varṣaśatamajīvatpra ha ṣoḍaśaṃ varṣaśataṃ jīvati ya evaṃ veda |*) (Bhā. Because Mahidāsa Aitareya had this knowledge of the truth, he did not fear the bodily sluggishness and the like that came upon him, and lived one hundred and sixteen years, so as to bring his sacrifice of life to full completion; thus the greatness of the three savanas, which are of the nature of human life, is described. In the Ṛk at hand the relation between Indra, who is the presiding deity of all the means of sacrifice such as rain, and the savana is described. And since it is said, at the time when the Soma sacrifice that gives satisfaction to Indra has been performed, विश्वा गौः ते जोष्या भूत् (*viśvā gauḥ te joṣyā bhūt*), that all speech, of the form of stotra and śastra, becomes pleasing to you, the order of those stotras and śastras that belong to the Soma sacrifice is stated thus.

**For the Prātaḥsavana**
- Stotras: बहिष्पवमान स्तोत्र (*bahiṣpavamāna stotra*); 4 आज्य स्तोत्रगळु (*ājya stotragaḷu*)
- Śastras: होतृविन आज्य शस्त्र (*hotṛvina ājya śastra*); होतृविन प्रलुग शस्त्र (*hotṛvina praluga śastra*); 3 आज्यशस्त्रगळु (*ājyaśastragaḷu*) of the Hotṛka priests (Maitrāvaruṇa; Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin, Acchāvāka)

**For the Mādhyandina-savana**
- Stotras: माध्यंदिनपवमानस्तोत्र (*mādhyaṃdinapavamānastotra*); 4 पृष्ठ स्तोत्रगळु (*pṛṣṭha stotragaḷu*)
- Śastras: होतृविन मरुत्वतीयशस्त्र (*hotṛvina marutvatīyaśastra*); होतृविन निष्केवल्यशस्त्र (*hotṛvina niṣkevalyaśastra*); 3 निष्केवल्यशस्त्र (*niṣkevalyaśastra*) of the Hotṛka priests

**For the Tṛtīya-savana**
- Stotras: आर्भवपवनमानस्तोत्र (*ārbhavapavanamānastotra*); अग्निष्टोमसाम (*agniṣṭomasāma*)
- Śastras: होतृविन वैश्वदेवशस्त्र (*hotṛvina vaiśvadevaśastra*); होतृविन अग्निमारुतशस्त्र (*hotṛvina agnimārutaśastra*)

Thus the meaning given for गौः (*gauḥ*), speech of the nature of stotra and śastra, also indicates the close relation of Indra with the sacrifice and with the speech connected with the sacrifice. This relation is explained in the Śatapatha in this way.

The sacrifice desired to establish a relation with speech. Indra, knowing this, understood that from the union of the two a great power would certainly arise, and, in fear lest that power might become greater than his own, he himself entered into them in the form of their embryo. After a year had passed he was born in the form of a great power and himself became the performer of the sacrifice. Therefore it is well known that sacrifice and speech are sources of delight to Indra.

In the sacrifice, when oblations and the like are offered in the fire, they make the offering by adding the word स्वाहा (*svāhā*) or वषट् (*vaṣaṭ*) at the end of the mantra. Therefore it is said here that वषट्कर्तृ (*vaṣaṭkartṛ*) means the actions (the offerings) of the seven Ṛtvijs who make the offering with the utterance of vaṣaṭ. Who are these seven offering priests, the seven Hotṛs? They are the seven Ṛtvijs named Hotṛ, Maitrāvaruṇa, Brāhmaṇācchaṃsī, Potṛ, Neṣṭṛ, Acchāvāka and Agnīdhra.

In the three savanas, called the Prātaḥsavana, the Mādhyandinasavana and the Tṛtīyasavana, each of these seven Ṛtvijs recites one Ṛk and offers the Soma in the fire. At the time of the Prātaḥsavana the offering is to be made with Ṛks of the Gāyatrī metre, at the time of the Mādhyandina-savana with Ṛks of the Triṣṭup metre, and at the time of the Tṛtīyasavana with Ṛks of the Jagatī metre. These mantras, recited by the respective Ṛtvijs at the time of the respective savanas, are called the Prasthitayājyā mantras, or else the Saptahotṛ mantras. They are explained here in detail.

अमेव नः सहवा आ हि गंतन नि बर्हिषि सदतना रणिष्टन । अथा मंदस्व जुजुषाणो अंधसस्त्वष्टर्देवेभिर्जनिभिः सुमद्गणः ॥ (*ameva naḥ sahavā ā hi gaṃtana ni barhiṣi sadatanā raṇiṣṭana | athā maṃdasva jujuṣāṇo aṃdhasastvaṣṭardevebhirjanibhiḥ sumadgaṇaḥ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-36-3) **GLOSS**

6. Acchāvāka:

इंद्राविष्णू पिबतं मध्यो अस्य सोमस्य दस्रा जठरं पृणेथां । आ नामंधांसि मदिराण्यग्मनन्नुप ब्रह्माणि शृणुतं हवं मे ॥ (*iṃdrāviṣṇū pibataṃ madhyo asya somasya dasrā jaṭharaṃ pṛṇethāṃ | ā nāmaṃdhāṃsi madirāṇyagmanannupa brahmāṇi śṛṇutaṃ havaṃ me ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-69-7) **GLOSS**

7. Āgnīdhra:

इमं सोममर्हते जातवेदसे रथमिव सं महेमा मनीषया । भद्रा हि नः प्रमतिरस्य संसद्यग्ने सख्ये मा रिषामा वयं तव ॥ (*imaṃ somamarhate jātavedase rathamiva saṃ mahemā manīṣayā | bhadrā hi naḥ pramatirasya saṃsadyagne sakhye mā riṣāmā vayaṃ tava ‖*) **GLOSS**

In this way, in each savana, each of these seven Ṛtvijs recites one Ṛk each, seven Ṛks in all, and makes the offering. As said before, in the Prātaḥsavana they make the offering with mantras of the Gāyatrī metre, in the Mādhyandinasavana with mantras of the Triṣṭup metre, and in the Tṛtīyasavana with mantras of the Jagatī metre. This is the account of the mantras to be recited at the times of the savanas.

**The Explanation of the Unnīyamāna Sūktas**

Having stated the seven mantras, each in the Gāyatrī, Triṣṭup and Jagatī metres, which the seven Ṛtvijs beginning with the Hotṛ recite and offer in the three savanas, he now explains, as stated in the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa (Ai. Brā., the 27th chapter), the procedure to be followed at the time of the Prātaḥsavana, the mantras to be recited, and the like.

Before making the offering at the time of the savana, when the Ṛtvijs stand ready, having filled the Soma juice into the Camasa vessel (a wooden vessel like a ladle, used for making offerings), the Adhvaryu directs the Ṛtvij called Maitrāvaruṇa, by means of the Praiṣa mantra, to recite the Unnīyamāna sūkta. The Unnīyamāna sūkta means the sūkta that is recited while the Soma juice, filled into the Camasa vessel for the offering, is held up and lifted for the offering

. The Unnīyamāna sūkta that is recited at the time of the Prātaḥsavana must be in the Gāyatrī metre, that of the Mādhyandinasavana in the Triṣṭup metre, and that of the Tṛtīyasavana in the Jagatī metre. Besides this there is the rule that a sūkta of nine Ṛks is to be recited as the Unnīyamāna sūkta in the Prātaḥsavana, a sūkta of ten Ṛks in the Mādhyandinasavana, and a sūkta of nine Ṛks in the Tṛtīyasavana.

Besides this there is yet another special point regarding the recitation of the mantras: the mantras are to be recited at these three savana times following the course of the sun. How? In the morning the brightness of the sun is very faint, so in the Prātaḥsavana the mantras are to be recited in a soft voice. At midday the brightness of the sun is very sharp, so at the time of the Mādhyandinasavana the mantras are to be uttered in a high voice, that is, very loudly. In the evening the heat of the sun is declining and has become less, so at the time of the Tṛtīyasavana the mantras are to be recited in a soft voice only.

**The Unnīyamāna Sūkta to be Recited at the Time of the Prātaḥsavana**

As soon as the Adhvaryu directs Maitrāvaruṇa, by means of the Praiṣa mantra, to recite the Unnīyamāna sūkta at the time of the Prātaḥsavana, Maitrāvaruṇa accordingly recites the sūkta आ त्वा वहंतु हरयः (*ā tvā vahaṃtu harayaḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-16).

1. आ त्वा वहंतु हरयो वृषणं सोमपीतये । इंद्र त्वा सूरचक्षसः ॥ (*ā tvā vahaṃtu harayo vṛṣaṇaṃ somapītaye | iṃdra tvā sūracakṣasaḥ ‖*) **GLOSS**

2. इमा धाना धृतस्न्नुवो हरी इहोप वक्षतः । इंद्रं सुखतमे रथे ॥ (*imā dhānā dhṛtasnnuvo harī ihopa vakṣataḥ | iṃdraṃ sukhatame rathe ‖*) **GLOSS**

3. इंद्रं प्रातर्हनामह इंद्रं प्रयत्यध्वरे । इंद्रं सोमस्य पीतये ॥ (*iṃdraṃ prātarhanāmaha iṃdraṃ prayatyadhvare | iṃdraṃ somasya pītaye ‖*) **GLOSS**

4. उप नः सुतमा गहि हरिभिरिंद्र केशिभिः । सुते हि त्वा हनामहे ॥ (*upa naḥ sutamā gahi haribhiriṃdra keśibhiḥ | sute hi tvā hanāmahe ‖*) **GLOSS**
