## Preface (Pīṭhike)

**[p. 20]**

The ninth maṇḍala of the Ṛksaṃhitā has the distinction of praising, entirely, only one deity. Apart from this maṇḍala, only four sūktas praising the deity Soma are found in the remaining maṇḍalas; moreover, in certain contexts Soma is praised together with other deities as well. All those are incidental (ānuṣaṅgika); they are not addressed solely to the deity Soma.

All the ṛks of the ninth maṇḍala, which praise Soma, who is self-purified and the purifier of the world (the one called Pavamāna), are well known as Pāvamānī ṛks. The Sarvānukramaṇī has the sentence ⟦ನವಮಂ ಮಂಡಲಂ ಪಾವಮಾನಂ ಸೌಮ್ಯಂ⟧ **GLOSS**. The Bṛhaddevatā statement ⟦ಪವಮಾನಃ ಸ್ತುತಃ ಸೋಮೋ ನವಮೇ ತ್ವಿಹ ಮಂಡಲೇ⟧ **GLOSS** makes this known.

⟦ಸಾವಮಾನೀರ್ಯೋ ಅಧ್ಯೇತ್ಯೃಷಿಭಿಃ~~ಕ್ಷೀರಂ ಸರ್ಪಿರ್ಮಧೂದಕಂ⟧

(Ma. Saṃ. 9-67-32) **NOTE**{abbreviation ಮ. ಸಂ. printed}

The Pāvamānī sūktas, acquired by the ṛṣis who had Madhucchandas as their origin, are the essence of all the Vedas. It is in praise of the greatness of all these sūktas that it is said that Sarasvatī provides the utmost happiness to those who recite and study these Pāvamānī sūktas, the essence of all the Vedas.

"Austerity of the utmost power should be performed," Indra instructed the ṛṣis. Desiring an excellent world, they performed austerity. As a result they recited the Pāvamānī sūktas praising Soma. Those who recite these sūktas with a purified heart purify the elders and the younger ones of their lineage and become pure themselves. All sins are destroyed. "⟦ಪಾವಮಾನ್ಯಃ ಪರಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಶುಕ್ರಂ ಜ್ಯೋತಿಃ ಸನಾತನಂ⟧" (Bṛ. De. 6-14-) **NOTE**{reference ಬೃ. ದೇ. ೬-೧೪- ends with a hyphen} The Pāvamānī sūktas are of the nature of the Supreme Brahman. They are the supreme light. It is eternal. For those who recite these Pāvamānī sūktas, of the form of divine light, with a heart given to meditation, the most excellent good destination is obtained — thus, and so on, the Bṛhaddevatā statements praise.

If the whole of the ninth maṇḍala, of one hundred and fourteen sūktas, only praises the deity Soma, then what is the distinctive character of Soma? What is the nature of the deity Soma? What is the relation between the other deities and the deity Soma? It is necessary to examine such matters.

The nirvacana (etymological explanation) of the word Soma reveals two forms of Soma, the supra-mundane (alaukika) and the mundane (laukika). In a gross view, one may say that the supra-mundane form is the deity and the mundane form is of the nature of juice (rasa). But many Saṃhitā passages indicate divinity even in the substance that is of the nature of juice, and praise it; in some places of the Ṛksaṃhitā and in the Upaniṣads and the like its spiritual form too is described. This subject can be made, in all, into four divisions.

- The deity Soma.
- Soma juice, the material of the sacrifice.
- The Soma sacrifice and its varieties.
- Descriptions that establish the divinity of Soma (description of the spiritual form, etc.).

**Description of the nature of Soma as deity**

The explanation of the Niruktakāras is that, for the word Soma, derived from the root ⟦ಸು⟧ with the meaning "to squeeze", the principal meaning is the Soma plant. To show the meaning that relates to the deity, they have cited, from the Ṛksaṃhitā, a statement that establishes the divinity of Soma. The purport is that it possesses both a divine part and an earthly part. Soma, which is available on earth in the form of the plant and in the form of juice, has also a divine form of splendour in the atmosphere and in the heavenly world. In the Brāhmaṇas and the like, identity (tādātmya) of this Soma of the form of splendour with the Moon is stated. The idea too is clear that this very Soma, which is of the form of the Moon and situated in the heavenly world, descends to the earth in the form of the plant and in the form of juice.

But it is not easy to state definitively whether the Soma of the nature of splendour is of the form of the Moon, is related to the Sun, is of the form of Agni or is of the form of Indra. If we first examine the statements in which identity with the Moon is shown, and which are

**[p. iii]**

described, Soma is described in just the same way as the position, held by the Moon, of lord of all the herbs (oṣadhi). The statement that the Moon is food for the gods follows the identity of Soma and the Moon, that "Soma himself is the Moon".

"⟦ತೇ ದೇವಾ ಅಬ್ರುವನ್ |................ಸೋಮೋ ರಾಜಾ ದೇವಾನಾಮನ್ನಂ ಯಚ್ಚಂದ್ರಮಾಃ ಸ ಯತ್ರೈಷ ಏತಾಗ್ಂ ರಾತ್ರಿಂ............ ............⟧" || (Śa. Brā. 1-6-4-5)

The Soma that is food for the gods is none other than the Moon. When the Moon is not seen in the atmosphere, either in the east or in the west, then the meaning is that he has come down to the earth. Having descended to the earth, he enters the waters and the herbs in the form of an embryo (garbha). The Moon, who in the form of the Moon is food for the gods, becomes, on the earth, the cause of the nourishment of the herbs and the like, and so is both food and the cause of food for the creatures of the earth. Because on the days when he is not seen in the atmosphere he dwells with us on earth (⟦ಅಮಾ⟧—⟦ವಸ್⟧: dwelling together), those days are named Amāvāsyā; such a context describes the fortnightly movement of the Moon. This same movement is described in the Ṛksaṃhitā also in this way.

⟦ಸಂ ದಕ್ಷೇಣ ಮನಸಾ~~ಜನಿಮ ನೇಮಮುದ್ಯತಂ⟧
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-68- **NOTE**{reference ಋ. ಸಂ ೯-೬೮- breaks off after the hyphen}

In ṛks of this kind it is stated that Soma himself, in the forms of the Sun and the Moon, undergoes revolution through day and night. Sāyaṇa has explained it: ⟦ದಿವಾ ಸೂರ್ಯಃ ಪ್ರಾದುರ್ಭವತಿ ರಾತ್ರ್ಯ ಚಂದ್ರಮಾಃ⟧. Here only ⟦ಗುಹಾ ಹಿತಂ⟧ is a secret matter.

The relation of Sūrya's daughter to Soma makes still clearer the relation between Sūrya and Soma. "Sūrya's daughter purifies Soma in the daśāpavitra. Sūrya's daughter brings Soma, who has been nourished by Parjanya; Soma has chosen Sūryā as his wife" — descriptions of this kind establish the solar element in Soma. Moreover,

"At the time of Sūryā's wedding, Soma himself is the bridegroom. The Aśvin deities are on the bridegroom's side. Agni is the guide in this wedding festival. At this time of rejoicing, Savitṛ gave his daughter to the bridegroom, who is of the form of Soma, and conducted the marriage. During this festival the mind is the chariot. The Sun and the Moon themselves are those who drive the chariot." Descriptions of this kind show

**[p. iv]**

Soma's supremely divine element. But in such contexts it is also indicated that Soma is a particular deity distinct from the Sun and the Moon.

It is also clearly stated that Soma, who is of greater power than the Moon, the Sun and the like, and is the giver of radiance to the whole world of the gods, is the producer of the Moon, the Sun and the like.

⟦ಪವಮಾನೋ ಅಜೀಜನದ್ದಿವಶ್ಚಿತ್ರಂ~~ಜ್ಯೋತಿರ್ವೈಶ್ವಾನರಂ ಬೃಹತ್⟧
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-61-16)

"Soma has produced the light, of the form of Āditya, that gives radiance to all worlds, and has established it in the heavenly world." "The omniscient Pavamāna has made even the Sun shine with his own splendour. He has made all worlds full of light." "He has caused the Sun to be situated in the heavenly world. Not the Sun alone: you are the illuminator of all the orbs of splendour. (⟦ಸ ನೋ ಜ್ಯೋತೀಂಷಿ ವಿರೋಚಯ⟧) The very divine power which the Bṛhaddevatā statement has described as the supreme light above all else is described in the Ṛksaṃhitā also in this way.

⟦ತ್ವಂ ಸಮುದ್ರೋ ಅಸಿ ವಿಶ್ವವಿತ್ಕವೇ~~ತವ ಜ್ಯೋತಿಂಷಿ ಪವಮಾನ ಸೂರ್ಯಃ⟧
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-86-29)

O Soma, you are omniscient. Following your regulating law, all the worlds remain in their ordained places. You, transcending all the worlds, bear them all. The Sun and the other orbs of light, shining by your power, are carrying out their duty of protecting the world." — thus the lordship of Soma over all is praised. In the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa and the like it is praised in these words: "⟦ತಸ್ಮಾದಾಹುಃ ಸೋಮಃ ಸರ್ವಾ ದೇವತಾ ಇತಿ⟧" — Soma is of the nature of all the deities. And further, "⟦ಭುವನಾನಾಗ್ ಹೈಷ ಪತಿರ್ವಿಶ್ವಾನ್ಯಭಿಧಾಮಾನಿ⟧" (Śa. Brā. 3-3-4-14): this Soma is the lord of all the worlds, Soma is the support of all creatures; in these words and others too Soma's lordship and power are described.

Although the lordship over all is thus described, in certain contexts there is identity with deities such as Indra and Agni. The close relation between Soma and Indra is well known. The means that gives Indra bodily and mental strength, produces genuine enthusiasm in him and makes Indra full of vigour in every way is the Soma juice. What gives strength to Indra is the vajra, the thunderbolt. What gives vigour is Soma. Therefore there is identity between Soma and the vajra.

**[p. v]**

⟦ಆತ್ಸೋಮ ಇಂದ್ರಿಯೋ ರಸೋ~~ಉಕ್ತಂ ಯದಸ್ಯ ಜಾಯತೇ⟧
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-47-3)

No sooner has this ukta-mantra praising Indra been recited than Soma, which is of the form of the vajra, arises possessed of an exceedingly powerful might — descriptions of this kind are many. "This vajra, of the form of Soma, has a beauty surpassing the most beautiful thing" (⟦ವಜ್ರೋ ವಪುಷೋ ವಪುಷ್ಟರಃ⟧). The vajra is a help in the slaying of Vṛtra, and Soma too is an equal help.

In such contexts Soma cannot be said to be an earthly juice. It is divine splendour and divine power. One who drinks only the earthly juice cannot be said to have drunk Soma fully. The divine form of Soma is realised by only a few. This divine form is beyond the range of others' perception. Drinking its juice is not possible.

⟦ಸೋಮಂ ಯಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಾಣೋ ವಿದುರ್ನ ತಸ್ಯಾಶ್ನಾತಿ ಕಶ್ಚನ |⟧

(Ma. Saṃ. 10-85-3) **NOTE**{abbreviation ಮ. ಸಂ. printed}

This Soma, full of divine splendour, flows through the three worlds in three kinds of streams.

⟦ಸಂ ತ್ರೀ ಪವಿತ್ರಾ ವಿತತಾನ್ಯೇಷ್ಯನ್ವೇಕಂ~~ಮಘವದ್ಭ್ಯ ಇಂದೋ⟧
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-97-55)

Your three kinds of extended streams attain unity at the end. O Soma, you are the giver of everything desired — in this description, explaining the purport of the portion ⟦ವಿತತಾ ತ್ರೀ ಪವಿತ್ರಾಣಿ⟧, Sāyaṇa has explained in his bhāṣya: ⟦ಅಗ್ನಿ, ವಾಯು, ಸೂರ್ಯಾತ್ಮಕಾನಿ⟧; the pavitras are the three streams, of the form of the light of the three worlds, named Agni, Vāyu and Sūrya. The divine principle, which pervades the universe and is described in the Upaniṣads and the like as the supreme light, is manifest in three forms, those of Agni, Vāyu and Āditya; this is here praised as the three streams of Soma, who is of the form of divine light.

**Soma juice, the material of the sacrifice**

That Soma, of the form of the supreme light, descends to the earth as an embryo (garbha) in the waters, the herbs and the trees (vanaspati) is one way of saying it; or that the śyena (falcon) itself went and brought that same Soma to the earth is another. In all, it must be said that the earthly Soma juice is a part of the divine Soma. (How Gāyatrī, in the form of a falcon, brought Soma has been explained earlier in the Gāyatrī section and also in the preface of this maṇḍala.)

**[p. vi]**

"Parjanya is the father of this Soma of great form. He has his dwelling in the mountains, or in the hills (those related to mountains) of the sacrificial ground. The waters are his sisters, and he is the embryo of Pajrā" — in these words and others there is an account of the origin of Soma. But what is the meaning of the address ⟦ಪಜ್ರಾಯಾಃ ಗರ್ಭ⟧ is one question. Pajrā may be the earth, or may be Mādhyamikā Vāk (the speech of the middle region). The embryo of Mādhyamikā Vāk means one of the form of rain. The embryo of the earth means one of the form of herbs. Sāyaṇa has so explained. (⟦ಪಜ್ರಾ ಪೃಥಿವೀತ್ಯಾಹುಃ~~ಮಾಧ್ಯಮಿಕಾ ವಾಕ್ ಪಜ್ರಾ⟧) The earthly Soma is established in the mountains. Varuṇa has established Soma in the mountain. (⟦ದಿವಿ ಸೂರ್ಯಮದಧಾತ್ ಸೋಮಮದ್ರೌ⟧) He is described by epithets such as ⟦ಪರ್ವತಾವೃಧೆಃ⟧ and ⟦ಗಿರಿಷ್ಠಾಃ⟧. The Soma plant is indicated as a forest **NOTE**{ಆತಸಮಯವಾದ ವನ unclear; word ಆತಸಮಯವಾದ}. "⟦ವೃಕ್ಷಸ್ಯ ಶಾಖಾಮರುಣಸ್ಯ ಬಪ್ಸತಃ⟧" — feeding on the branches of the tree of reddish colour (aruṇa), the Soma plants flourish. There are descriptions of this kind.

The Soma plants are not of one species but are also of different species; or, when excellent Soma plants are lacking, other plants having the same special qualities are acceptable as their substitutes. This explanation is as follows in the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa. (Śa. Brā. 4, 5, 10. 1 to 8)

When Soma has been stolen, it is a duty to search for it and bring it. If it is not found, there are other plants, possessing the same power, that are its substitutes. Or the idea is expressed in some places that these too belong to the Soma species.

"⟦ದ್ವಯಾನಿ ವೈ ಫಾಲ್ಗುನಾನಿ~~ಚಾರುಣಪುಷ್ಪಾಣಿ ಚ⟧" Among the Phālguna plants there are two kinds: those with red flowers and those with golden-yellow (aruṇa) flowers. Among them, if the plant with golden-yellow flowers is squeezed, it gives an excellent juice just like the juice obtained when the Soma plant is squeezed. If this Phālguna plant with flowers of aruṇa colour is not found, then the plant named Śainahṛta is to be obtained. (⟦ಶೈನಹೃತಮಭಿಷುಣುಯಾದ್ಯತ್ರ ವೈ ಗಾಯತ್ರಿ ಸೋಮಮಚ್ಛಾಪತತ್⟧) While Gāyatrī was flying with Soma, a part of Soma broke off and fell. That became famous as the Śainahṛta plant. Therefore the Śainahṛta plant, which is itself a limb of Soma, may be squeezed for juice. **NOTE**{ಶೈನಹೃತ and ಶ್ಯೇನಹೃತ both printed}

If the plant named Śyenahṛta is not found, the plant named Ādāra may be brought. (⟦ಆದಾರಾನಭಿಷುಣುಯಾತ್⟧) When the head of the sacrifice was split, from its juice the plant named Ādāra arose. Therefore the Ādāra plant, which is related to the sacrifice, may be squeezed.

If this Adāra plant is not found, then the golden-yellow plant named Dūrvā may be squeezed. **NOTE**{ಆದಾರ and ಅದಾರ both printed} This Dūrvā plant is a close relation of Soma. (⟦ಏಷ ವೈ ಸೋಮಸ್ಯ ನ್ಯಂಗಃ........ತಸ್ಮಾದರುಣದೂರ್ವಾ ಅಭಿಷುಣುಯಾತ್⟧).

**[p. vii]**

If this Dūrvā plant, which is related to Soma, is also not found, yellow kuśa may be squeezed. In this way other substitutes that have just the quality of the Soma plant are also stated. Thus, because the plants that become available according to the circumstance are used, the colour of the juice too is not of one kind. In stating the colour of the Soma juice, a different colour is specified at different times.

The colour of the Soma juice is described in many places as of the colour of gold. But brown (⟦ಬಭ್ರು⟧), the colour made up of both brown and yellow (⟦ಹರಿಂ ಬಭ್ರುಂ⟧), red (⟦ಅರುಷ⟧), a mixture of yellow and red (⟦ಹರಿಂ ಅರುಷಂ⟧), or slightly yellow (⟦ಗೌರ⟧) — these and other colours are all specified. Therefore,

⟦ವಿಶ್ವಾ ರೂಪಾಣ್ಯಾವಿಶನ್ನುನಾನೋ~~ಯತ್ರಾಮೃತಾಸ ಆಸತೇ⟧
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-25-4)

The attractive Soma juice flows, taking on many forms — this description tells the colour of the Soma stream and its other qualities.

The juice produced from the pure Soma plant is compared to pure milk. But the Ṛksaṃhitā says that only when mixed with milk does it become of white colour. (⟦ಶ್ವೇತಂ ರೂಪಂ ಕೃಣುತೇ~~ಕಲಶಂ ಗೋಭಿರಕ್ತಂ⟧) How the deities such as Indra become nourished and full of vigour through drinking Soma juice has been explained earlier in many places. Here we must examine in what way this drinking of Soma juice is beneficial to the human sacrificer (yajamāna).

⟦ತಮಿದ್ವರ್ಧಂತು ನೋ ಗಿರೋ~~ಯ ಇಂದ್ರಸ್ಯ ಹೃದಂಸನಿಃ⟧
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-61-14)

"May the Soma juices that are pleasing to Indra nourish our excellent speech, which is of the form of praise, as cows rich in milk protect their calves" — thus it is prayed. This Soma is a giver of vigour to the sacrificer in every way. It makes the form of the sacrificer attractive. It helps excellent meditation. And,

⟦ದೇವೋ ದೇವಾನಾಂ ಗುಹ್ಯಾನಿ~~ಬರ್ಹಿಷಿ ಪ್ರವಾಚೇ⟧
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-95-2)

The Soma juice, which is of the nature of a deity, makes manifest the secret nature of the deities such as Indra and makes it perceptible to the sacrificer.

**[p. viii]**

The drinking of Soma juice is the most excellent of all other drinks. Soma juice is ⟦ವಯಸ್ಕತ್, ವಯೋಧಾಃ⟧: it should increase the life of the sacrificer. It gives vigour on the field of battle; increasing virility, it should be a means to progeny. All these belong to material nourishment. Besides these,

⟦ತ್ವಂ ಸೋಮ ಪ್ರ ಚಿಕಿತೋ ಮನೀಷಾ~~ದೇವೇಷು ರತ್ನಮಭಜಂತ ಧೀರಾಃ⟧

(Ma. Saṃ. 7-91-1) **NOTE**{abbreviation ಮ. ಸಂ. printed}

O divine Soma, you alone are our guide. By our understanding (prajñā) we have known your nature. You have made the whole world drenched with amṛta. You have established our forefathers, who were of excellent action, among the gods. Be a guide on the good path for us — in this way, and so on, besides praising his being a guide, it is also praised in many places that by this drinking of Soma juice spiritual discernment and supersensory knowledge are obtained. ⟦ಭದ್ರಂ ನೋ ಅಪಿ ವಾತಯ ಮನೋ ದಕ್ಷಮುತ ಕ್ರತುಂ⟧ **GLOSS** It was by this very drinking of Soma juice that the praiser became able to compose praise. (⟦ಇತ್ಥಾ ಹಿ ಸೋಮ ಇನ್ಮದೇ ಬಹ್ಮಾ ಚಕಾರ ವರ್ಧನಂ⟧) Praises of this kind are there as well.

That Soma juice is not blameworthy in any respect is an undisputed matter. Of the hundreds of statements describing the power of Soma juice, only a couple have been cited above as examples.

For the sacrifice, which upholds the universe, Soma is the chief means. To obtain this means, many ritual operations are necessary. After the Soma in plant form has been brought, it must be squeezed into the form of juice in the prescribed manner, according to the respective sacrificial rite, and afterwards used as an instrument of the sacrifice.

The implements useful for the pressing of Soma (somābhiṣava) are many. All of them must be obtained in the prescribed manner. What they are — the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa, putting them together, tells the names of these implements in order in this way. All of these are to be provided by the Adhvaryu.

"⟦ಅಧ್ವರ್ಯುಃ ಪುರಸ್ತಾದುಪಕಲ್ಪಯೇತಾಧಿಷವಣಂ~~ತದೇನಾನಿ ದ್ವೇಧಾ⟧ ⟦ವಿಗೃಹ್ಣೀ- ಯಾತ್ ||................⟧" (Ai. Brā. 7-32)

The hide needed for pressing (adhiṣavaṇa-carma), the boards needed for pressing (phalake), the droṇakalaśa, the daśāpavitra (a sieve-cloth), the adri (the pressing stone), the pūtabhṛt and the ādhavanīya (the filtering

**[p. ix]**

vessels), the sthālī, the udañcana (a pouring vessel), the camasa (a ladle for offering the oblation or for drinking the juice).

After these implements have been obtained, the Soma is to be brought, in the prescribed manner, by the havirdhāna carts (śakaṭa). (This point was stated earlier when the subject of the havirdhāna carts was explained.) Afterwards, the pressing of the Soma is according to the respective savana. (There are three divisions of this: the morning savana (prātaḥ savana), the midday savana (mādhyandina savana) and the third savana (tṛtīya savana). These divisions have been stated in the preface of the maṇḍala.) This pressing is also in accordance with the deities connected with the respective savanas.

⟦ತ್ರಯೋ ವೈ ದೇವಾಃ~~ಮಿಶ್ರಮಾದಿತ್ಯಾನಾಂ ತೃತೀಯ ಸವನಂ⟧

(Śa. Brā. 4-3-5-1.)

Among the gods there are three kinds: the Vasus, the Rudras and the Ādityas. In them the three savanas are apportioned. The morning savana belongs to the Vasus. The midday savana belongs to the Rudras. The third savana belongs to the Ādityas. The morning savana is for the Vasus alone. The midday savana is for the Rudras alone. The third savana is for the Ādityas together with the other deities. This is only a gross division. The individual deities connected with these, the graha vessels, the śastra mantras and similar matters have been explained in the preface of the maṇḍala.

The good destination that the sacrificer obtains by performing the Soma sacrifice, which consists of the three savanas, is stated in the Chāndogya in this way.

⟦ಬ್ರಹ್ಮವಾದಿನೋ ವದಂತಿ~~ವಿದ್ವಾನ್ ಕುರ್ಯಾತ್⟧
(Dhā U. 2, 24. 1-2) **NOTE**{abbreviation ಧಾ ಉ. printed}

The great ṛṣis who have attained knowledge of Brahman ask in this way. If the morning savana is allotted to the Vasus, the midday savana to the Rudras, and the third savana to the Ādityas, then what is the world of the sacrificer? If it is not known to him which world that is, how will the fruit of his performance of ritual be accomplished? For success to be attained by him, this knowledge is quite essential.

(Bhā. U. 2-24-3 to 5) **NOTE**{abbreviation ಭಾ. ಉ. printed}

Before the recitation of the prātaranuvāka, one should sit behind the Gārhapatya fire, facing north, and recite the mantra ⟦ಲೋಕದ್ವಾರಂ⟧, which is pleasing to the Vasus. Its purport is this: "O Vasus, open the door for us, for the sight of you, which is the means of obtaining kingship (rājya)." Then, "Homage to Agni, who dwells on the earth. O Agni, provide a place in this world for me, the sacrificer" — thus one should worship Agni and pray.

Before the midday savana, one should sit behind the Āgnīdhrīya fire, facing north, and recite the mantra ⟦ಲೋಕದ್ವಾರಂ⟧, which is pleasing to the Rudras. Its purport is this: "O Rudras, open the door for the sight of you, which is the means of obtaining our vairājya." Afterwards, "O Vāyus who dwell in the atmosphere, provide a place for me, the sacrificer, in your world" — thus one should pray to Vāyu and worship.

⟦ಅಥ ಜುಹೋತಿ ನಮ ಆದಿತ್ಯೇಭ್ಯಶ್ಚ~~ಲೋಕಂ ಮೇ ಯಜಮಾನಾಯ ವಿಂದತ⟧

(Dhā. U. 2-24-9 to 14) **NOTE**{abbreviation ಧಾ. ಉ printed}

Before the third savana, the sacrificer should sit behind the Āhavanīya fire, facing north, and recite the mantra ⟦ಲೋಕದ್ವಾರಂ⟧ so as to be pleasing to the Ādityas and the Viśvedevas. Its purport is this: "O Ādityas, O Viśvedevas, in order that sāmrājya (imperial sovereignty) may be obtained also by me, open the door of your world, so that I may attain the sight of you, which is the means of it."

**[p. xi]**

Afterwards, "O Ādityas and Viśvedevas, who dwell in the heavenly world, provide a place for me, the sacrificer, in your world too" — thus one should pray and worship.

In this way the fruit of performing the three savanas has been set forth. That is, the purport is that if the three savanas, which are limbs of the Soma sacrifice, are performed in the prescribed manner, an excellent place is obtained in all three worlds. It is because of this influence, which pervades all the worlds, that in the Ṛksaṃhitā too the divine power of Soma is described in many places.

Soma is the cause of wealth such as riches, which is the means of worldly happiness; of the obtaining of vigour by Indra and the other deities, who are the givers of this wealth to the sacrificer; of the obtaining of the harmony (samanvaya) of all the worlds; and of the attainment of bliss, which is of the form of liberation. Soma is the accomplisher of the success of all the dealings of gods, men and the rest, which are of the form of sacrifice; he is the support. The purport is that the sādhya (what is to be accomplished), the sādhana (the means) and the siddhi (the accomplishment) are all he himself.
