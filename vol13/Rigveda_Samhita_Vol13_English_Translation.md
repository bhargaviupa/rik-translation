## Sūkta 166 (Maṇḍala 1)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> अथ चतुर्थोऽध्याय आरभ्यते । कया शुभेत्यस्मिंस्रयोविंशेऽनुवाके
> पंचदश सूक्तानि । तत्र तन्नित्येतद्वितीयं सूक्तं: पंचदशर्चं । अत्रानुक्रमणिका ।
> तन्नगस्यो मारुतं हि द्वित्रिष्टुबंतं मित्रावरुणयोर्दिक्षितयोरुर्वशीमप्सरसं
> दृष्ट्वा वासतीवरे कुंभे रेतोऽपतत्ततोऽगस्यवसिष्ठावजायेतामिति । सूक्तसंख्यानुवर्तत आन्यस्याः सूक्तसंख्यायाः । अनु. १२-१ । इति परिभाषितत्वात्पंचदशर्चं । उक्तलक्षणेनागस्तेन दृष्टत्वात्तस्यार्षं । मारुतं हीत्युक्तत्वात्तुह्यादि
> परिभाषयेदमादिसूक्तत्रयं मरुद्देवताकं । अंत्ये द्वे त्रिष्टुभौ शिष्टास्त्रिष्टुबंत
> परिभाषया जगत्यः ॥ विनियोगो लैंगिकः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Here the fourth adhyāya begins. In the twenty-third anuvāka, which begins with कया शुभा (*kayā śubhā*), there are fifteen sūktas; of these this sūkta, तन्नु वोचाम (*tannu vocāma*), is the second, of fifteen Ṛks. The Anukramaṇikā says: तन्नगस्यो मारुतं हि द्वित्रिष्टुबंतं मित्रावरुणयोर्दिक्षितयोरुर्वशीमप्सरसं दृष्ट्वा वासतीवरे कुंभे रेतोऽपतत्ततोऽगस्यवसिष्ठावजायेतामिति । (*tannagasyo mārutaṃ hi dvitriṣṭubaṃtaṃ mitrāvaruṇayordikṣitayorurvaśīmapsarasaṃ dṛṣṭvā vāsatīvare kuṃbhe reto 'patattato 'gasyavasiṣṭhāvajāyetāmiti |*): when Mitra and Varuṇa were consecrated, they saw the apsaras Urvaśī and semen fell into a jar at the Vasatīvarī; from it Agastya and Vasiṣṭha were born. By the rule सूक्तसंख्यानुवर्तत आन्यस्याः सूक्तसंख्यायाः (*sūktasaṃkhyānuvartata ānyasyāḥ sūktasaṃkhyāyāḥ*) (Anu. 12-1) the sūkta has fifteen Ṛks. Because it is seen by Agastya, as described, the seer is Agastya. Because the Anukramaṇikā says मारुतं हि (*mārutaṃ hi*), by the rule of such words this sūkta and the next two are addressed to the Maruts. The last two Ṛks are Triṣṭubh; by the rule of the Triṣṭubh ending, the rest are Jagatī. The application (viniyoga) is by the characteristic sign (लैंगिकः (*laiṃgikaḥ*)).

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Maruts, we quickly proclaim that famous, ancient (पूर्वं (*pūrvaṃ*), "filling" or "earlier") greatness of yours. For what purpose? For your impetuous (रभसाय (*rabhasāya*)) birth, that is, your manifestation at the altar, meaning your presence there. And why that? For the knowledge (केतवे (*ketave*), प्रज्ञानाय (*prajñānāya*)) of the sacrifice, the showerer (वृषभस्य (*vṛṣabhasya*)), the rainer of fruit. The coming of clouds by the wind's impulsion is well known. O loud-roaring (तुविष्टणः (*tuviṣṭaṇaḥ*); with abundant sound from the vehemence of going) and mighty ones, who can do everything: when the journey to the sacrifice (यामन् (*yāman*)) is under way, you perform powers (तविषाणि (*taviṣāṇi*), strengths, capacities) with a greatness to be attained, like the energies belonging to fuel (*edha*); do that, is the meaning. The illustration: as you perform powers for battle, because of this or that battle, so here.

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Maruts, loud-roaring and mighty, as you display your strength in times of battle, so you display your power when you come to our sacrificial hall too. O Maruts, come without delay and bring about knowledge of the sacrifices that rain fruit; so saying, we praise your ancient greatness.

##### English translation (H. H. Wilson)
We proclaim eagerly, Maruts, your ancient greatness, for (the sake of inducing) your prompt appearance, as the indication of (the approach of) the showerer (of benfits). Loud-roaring and mighty Maruts, you exert your vigorous energies for the advance (to the sacrifice), as if it was to battle.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

तन्नु वोचाम पूर्वं महित्वं (*tannu vocāma pūrvaṃ mahitvaṃ*)—Here the word तत् (*tat*) indicates "that which was greatly praised by many seers in the past and became famous." पूर्वं (*pūrvaṃ*)—पूरकं पूर्वतनं वा (*pūrakaṃ pūrvatanaṃ vā*): very great, or ancient, earlier; the (heroic deeds) that you performed earlier, or that are famous because many seers extolled them earlier: such is the meaning. महित्वं (*mahitvaṃ*)—greatness, or heroic acts, deeds of valour, etc. That is, O Maruts, we shall now describe the many famous deeds of valour that you performed in the past and their glory: this is the main sense of the sentence.

रभसाय जन्मने (*rabhasāya janmane*)—राभस्ययुक्ताय जन्मने वेद्यां प्रादुर्भानाय (*rābhasyayuktāya janmane vedyāṃ prādurbhānāya*): "in order to come very quickly to the sacrificial altar," is Sāyaṇa's explanation. By this, रभसाय (*rabhasāya*) means swift, quickly, and जन्मने (*janmane*) means origin or arrival (For your immedaiate presence); the main sense is "for your quick coming to the sacrificial altar." Max Müller explains these words as "for the robust host," that is, the very powerful Marut troop. But the commentator's explanation is more fitting.

वृषभस्य केतवे (*vṛṣabhasya ketave*)—The meaning of these words is not clear. The commentator explains: वृषभस्य फलस्य वर्षितुर्यज्ञस्य केतवे प्रज्ञापनाय (*vṛṣabhasya phalasya varṣituryajñasya ketave prajñāpanāya*), "for understanding the sacrifice that grants the sacrificer's desired ends," that is, for gaining knowledge of the sacrificial acts. Max Müller translates these words "for the herald of powerful Indra." The words can be understood in two ways: either the sacrifice now close at hand, that is, being prepared for performance; or, according to the commentator, the coming of clouds, since the Maruts' arrival indicates rain. Yet the main sense of these words remains doubtful.

विधेव यामन् मरुतः तुविष्टणः (*vidheva yāman marutaḥ tuviṣṭaṇaḥ*)—As wood and the like thrown into fire make a sound and blaze very brightly when burning fiercely, so, O Maruts, your coming is full of great noise: such is the meaning. When wind blows with great force, sound or noise arises naturally;

### Printed p. 6
and when wood is put in a fire and burns, sound arises naturally. Likewise, here the coming of the Maruts is described as full of sound. Since the word तुवि (*tuvi*) is read among the twelve words for "much," beginning with उरु (*uru*) (Ni. 3-13), तुवि (*tuvi*) means much, and स्वनः (*svanaḥ*) means sound; so तुविस्वनः (*tuvisvanaḥ*) means "accompanied by much sound."
शक्राः (*śakrāḥ*)—शक्ताः (*śaktāḥ*): able, possessed of power.
युधेव तविषाणि कर्तन (*yudheva taviṣāṇi kartana*)—The word तविषाणि (*taviṣāṇi*) is derived from तवः (*tavaḥ*) or तविषी (*taviṣī*) (Ni. 3-9), which mean strength; it indicates strength, capacity. Since showing strength in battle is natural, this illustration is used: so also when coming to our sacrificial altar, you should come quickly, with a display of strength, that is, with great enthusiasm.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The Maruts, holding sweet, delicious oblation as one holds a dear son of one's own body (नित्यं न सूनुं (*nityaṃ na sūnuṃ*)), become घृष्टयः (*ghṛṣṭayaḥ*) at the sacrifices (विदथेषु (*vidatheṣu*)), that is, crushers of Rakṣas and the like, and being playful by nature they approach and sport, roam about (क्रीळंति (*krīḷaṃti*)). Further, the sons of Rudra, the Maruts, are themselves called Rudras, since begotten and begetter are not different. That the Maruts are Rudra's sons is well known from the Purāṇas: Pārvatī and Parameśvara, pleased by austerity, accepted the Maruts as sons. The Rudras reach, pervade (नक्षंति (*nakṣaṃti*)), by way of protection or satisfaction, the sacrificer (हविष्कृतं (*haviṣkṛtaṃ*)), the doer and giver of the oblation, who is नमस्विनं (*namasvinaṃ*), accompanied by praise marked by homage. And the Maruts, स्वतवसः (*svatavasaḥ*), whose strength is their own, do not harm (न मर्धंति (*na mardhaṃti*)) the sacrificer; that is, they do not make him fight or distress him.

- क्रीळाः (*krīḷāḥ*)—being playful by nature
- उप (*upa*)—approaching

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

The Maruts accept the sweet oblation as one accepts a son of one's own body, and sport at the sacrifices, crushing those who are hostile. The Maruts, sons of Rudra, come to protect the sacrificer, who offers homage and gives the oblation. Though extremely mighty, they do not harm the sacrificer.

##### English translation (H. H. Wilson)
Ever accepting the sweet (libation), as (they would) a son, they sport playfully at sacrifices, demolishing (all intruders): the Rudras come to the offerer of prayers and oblations, for his protection, and powerful as they are, do him no harm.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

नित्यं न सूनुं मधु (*nityaṃ na sūnuṃ madhu*)—Here the word न (*na*) denotes comparison. As a father every day gives a sweet delicacy to his dear son (child), so the Maruts give the desired fruits to the sacrificer who performs the sacrifice: the seer has given this very fitting illustration. As a father gladdens his dear child by giving it a sweet delicacy with great affection, so the Maruts grant all desired objects to the sacrificers who worship them with praises and the like; this is the main purport.

क्रीळंति (*krīḷaṃti*), क्रीळाः (*krīḷāḥ*)—Since a kind of delightful sound arises when the wind blows violently, the seer describes the Maruts as sporting for recreation, making sounds of joy. The words क्रीळंति (*krīḷaṃti*), क्रीळाः (*krīḷāḥ*) are generally used as epithets of the Maruts and show their distinctive character. Yet this word is also used of other deities. For example:

क्रीळं वः शर्धो मारुतमनर्वाणं रथेशभुं । कण्वा अभि प्र गायत ॥ (*krīḷaṃ vaḥ śardho mārutamanarvāṇaṃ ratheśabhuṃ | kaṇvā abhi pra gāyata ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-37-1)
In Ṛks like this it is used of the Maruts, and in
क्रीळंतस्वा सुमनसः सपेमाभि द्युम्ना तस्थिवांसो जनानां । (*krīḷaṃtasvā sumanasaḥ sapemābhi dyumnā tasthivāṃso janānāṃ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-4-9)
ज्येष्ठेभिर्यस्तेजिष्टैः क्रीळुमद्भिवर्षिष्ठेभिर्भानुभिर्नक्षति द्यां । (*jyeṣṭhebhiryastejiṣṭaiḥ krīḷumadbhivarṣiṣṭhebhirbhānubhirnakṣati dyāṃ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-3-5)
Ṛks like these, it is used of Agni; and in
वृथा क्रीळंत इंदवः सधस्थमभ्येकमित् । सिंधोरूर्मा व्यक्षरन् ॥ (*vṛthā krīḷaṃta iṃdavaḥ sadhasthamabhyekamit | siṃdhorūrmā vyakṣaran ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-21-3)
Ṛks like this, the word is used of the deity Soma.

विदथेषु (*vidatheṣu*)—यज्ञेषु (*yajñeṣu*): "at the sacrifices", that is, at the sacrificial places. By वेदयत्सु स्तोत्रेषु निमित्तभूतेषु (*vedayatsu stotreṣu nimittabhūteṣu*) the derivation of the word विदथ (*vidatha*) is shown. Here only one of the Nirukta authors' derivations is followed. It is read among the names of the sacrifice, यज्ञः (*yajñaḥ*), वेनः (*venaḥ*) and so on (Ni. 3-19), and विदथानि वेदनानि (*vidathāni vedanāni*). To show that the word विदथ (*vidatha*) means knowledge (Ni. 6-7),

### Printed p. 10
he quotes the Ṛk
होता देवो अमर्त्यः पुरस्तादेति मायया । विदथानि प्रचोदयन् ॥ (*hotā devo amartyaḥ purastādeti māyayā | vidathāni pracodayan ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-27-6)
and shows that there the word is formed from the root विद् (*vid*) in the sense of knowing. Here Sāyaṇa has explained विदथानि वेदितव्यानि कर्माणि (*vidathāni veditavyāni karmāṇi*) as "works that are to be known". Some scholars have tried to state a definite meaning for this word, and all have given their own individual opinions. Since each has shown a different derivation of the word and made a different meaning, it appears that all those meanings fit according to context. The scholar Max Muller, explaining the Ṛk
अमादेषां भियसा भूमिरेजति नौर्न पूर्णा क्षरति व्यथिर्यती । दूरेदृशो ये चितयंत एमभिरंतर्महे विदथे येतिरे नरः । (*amādeṣāṃ bhiyasā bhūmirejati naurna pūrṇā kṣarati vyathiryatī | dūredṛśo ye citayaṃta emabhiraṃtarmahe vidathe yetire naraḥ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 5-59-2)
has translated the word विदथे (*vidathe*) as (sacrificial) assembly (the great sacrificial assembly). Giving the explanation of it, he sets out the meanings other German scholars have given to the word and their opinions on its root, and at last: "Vidatha may have been the name of a sacred act, as Veda is of sacred knowledge. But the fact remains that it is best translated by assembly, particularly an assembly for sacrificial purposes." (Sacred Books of the East Vol. 32 p. 349-50)
Some have said that, as sacred knowledge is the meaning of the word वेद (*veda*), a sacred act is the meaning of the word विदथ (*vidatha*). But he has concluded that there is no doubt that sacrificial assembly is the main meaning of this word.

The scholar Hermann Oldenberg, in the work Vedic Hymns, when explaining the meaning of the word विदथे (*vidathe*) in Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-31-6, stated his opinion in this way. His sentences are quoted and then his opinions are stated.
"On the derivation and meaning of विदथ (*vidatha*) Various opinions have been pronounced in the last years which have been collected by prof. Max Muller in his note on R. V, V. 59·2 ( S. B. (Sacred Books of the East. Vol. 46. p. 27).

The main substance of this writing is as follows. The word विदथ (*vidatha*) is formed by adding the prefix वि (*vi*) to the root धा (*dhā*). विधा (*vidhā*) means "distribute, ordain"; it may also mean observance of rule. That the meanings of the two words विदथ (*vidatha*) and विधा (*vidhā*) are the same is known from the identical sense of the words विदथेष्वह्नां (*vidatheṣvahnāṃ*) in the Ṛk
वयमग्ने वनुयाम त्वोता वसूयवो हविषा बुध्यमानाः । वयं समर्ये विदथेष्वह्नां वयं राया सहसस्पुत्र मर्तान् ॥ (*vayamagne vanuyāma tvotā vasūyavo haviṣā budhyamānāḥ | vayaṃ samarye vidatheṣvahnāṃ vayaṃ rāyā sahasasputra martān ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 5-3-6)
and of the words वि-दधुः (*vi-dadhuḥ*) in the Ṛk
वि ये दधुः शरदं मासमादहरज्ञमक्तुं चादृचं । अनाप्यं वरुणो मित्रो अर्यमा क्षत्रं राजान आशत । (*vi ye dadhuḥ śaradaṃ māsamādaharajñamaktuṃ cādṛcaṃ | anāpyaṃ varuṇo mitro aryamā kṣatraṃ rājāna āśata |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-66-11)
And the meanings of the words विहित (*vihita*) and विदथ (*vidatha*) being the same, the sacrifice prescribed by the śāstra came to be called by the name विदथ (*vidatha*). The meaning "sacrifice" or "sacrificial assembly" remained in the end, this scholar has explained.

### Printed p. 12
The scholar Bloomfield has pointed out the faults in the meaning given by the two, Max Muller and Oldenberg, saying that विदथ (*vidatha*) never means an assembly and that the meaning "personal household" alone is correct. His opinion is as follows:
"The word विदथ (*vidatha*) contains the root वित् (*vit*) in its ordinary sense of ' get, acquire '. Instead of this, the radical idea underlying the word was generally felt to be ' come to-gether,' ' assemble '. Again, the false start from the notion of ' assemblage ' resulted in an approximation of the word to other words for assemblage, especially to सभा (*sabhā*) with which विदथ (*vidatha*) was almost identified. We shall see that there are decided points of contact between the words विदथ (*vidatha*) and सभा (*sabhā*) but the contact is that of extreme opposites in a way. विदथ (*vidatha*) refers to home matters ; सभा (*sabhā*) generally to public matters.
In the interpretation of a term that figures prominently in the mystic—hieratic sphere of the Veda it is peculiarly necessary to search for its uses outside of that sphere, if there be such uses. In X—85·26, the bride after the wedding ceremoney proper, as the newly married couple are about to travel to their new home is addressed with the words " May Pushan taking thee by the hand lead thee home, may the Asvins lead thee forth on their car. Go to your house in order that thou mayst be mistress of the house '.
Now विदथ (*vidatha*) here, can but have one meaning, household or something very like it. It certainly cannot refer to anything in the nature of public gathering similar to the सभा (*sabhā*). In the phrase बृहद्वदेम विदथे सुवीराः (*bṛhadvadema vidathe suvīrāḥ*) (R. V. 2-1-I6), the word सुवीराः (*suvīrāḥ*) refers to family relations, not to public life.
Thus far, it may not be doubted, the word appears in the language of ordinary life. But it is found to a very much greater extent in the sacredotal sphere; indeed, as all interpreters from Yaska on, have agreed, it often has a meaning very close to Yagna 'Sacrifice' itself. This is as it should be in as much as the sacrifice is a private, home affair, it is very natural विदथ (*vidatha*) the house, the premises, the establishment, should figure

### Printed p. 13
prominently in connection with the sacrifice. The substance of this note is that it is not correct when some say that the word विदथ (*vidatha*) means "assembly" (सभा (*sabhā*)); the word means a private house of one's own. One can observe the word used in this sense in many places. For example:

पूषा त्वेतो नयतु हस्तगृह्याश्विना त्वा प्रवहतां रथेन । गृहान् गच्छ गृहपत्नि यथासो वशिनी त्वं विदथवा वदासि ॥ (*pūṣā tveto nayatu hastagṛhyāśvinā tvā pravahatāṃ rathena | gṛhān gaccha gṛhapatni yathāso vaśinī tvaṃ vidathavā vadāsi ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-85-26) **NOTE**

In this Ṛk the meaning is plainly that Pūṣan and the Aśvin deities should lead you safely to your husband's house. Further:

ये स्तोतृभ्यो गोअग्रामश्वपेशसमग्ने रातिमुपसृजंति सूरयः । अस्माञ्च तांश्च प्र हि नेषि वस्य आ बृहद्वदेम विदथे सुवीराः (*ye stotṛbhyo goagrāmaśvapeśasamagne rātimupasṛjaṃti sūrayaḥ | asmāñca tāṃśca pra hi neṣi vasya ā bṛhadvadema vidathe suvīrāḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-1-16)

Here too, since सुवीराः (*suvīrāḥ*) is explained as शोभनपुत्रादिसहिताः (*śobhanaputrādisahitāḥ*) ("accompanied by fine sons and the like"), the meaning is "house". But since sacrificial work is carried out in the house, in the present context it becomes "sacrificial house". The same meaning lies in Yāska and others giving it as a synonym of "sacrifice". Many Western scholars have given their own individual opinions to this effect.

Since the different meanings — house, sacrifice, assembly — all fit, none of them can be called wrong. All these meanings occur in Sāyaṇa's own commentary. For example, in the Ṛks 10-85-26 and 27, 7-84-3 and so on he explains it as "house"; in 10-100-6, 10-100-7 and many other Ṛks as "sacrifice". It is also understood as "sacrificial house" and as "sacrificial assembly".

फृष्टयः (*phṛṣṭayaḥ*)—रक्षःप्रभृतीनां घर्षकाः (*rakṣaḥprabhṛtīnāṃ gharṣakāḥ*): those who rub down (crush) Rākṣasas and the like, that is, those who remove the troubles they cause to the sacrifice; the sense is that they inflict harm on Rākṣasas and the like.

रुद्राः (*rudrāḥ*)—रुद्रपुत्राः । मरुतोऽपि रुद्रा उच्यंते जन्यजनकयोरभेदात् (*rudraputrāḥ | maruto 'pi rudrā ucyaṃte janyajanakayorabhedāt*). Here रुद्राः (*rudrāḥ*) means sons of Rudra. Because it is the custom to call sons by the father's name, and because in usage the begotten and the begetter are not counted as different, the Marut deities, the sons of Rudra, are here described by the word रुद्र (*rudra*) itself. As to how the Maruts became sons of Rudra, Sāyaṇa tells an old story in his commentary on Ṛk 1-114-6.

That is: Once, long ago, when Indra had conquered the Asuras, Diti, the mother of the Asuras, desiring a son able to subdue Indra, performed austerity and became pregnant through the service of her husband. Indra, learning of this, under the pretext of serving his mother stayed near Diti, took a subtle form, entered Diti's womb together with his thunderbolt, and split that embryo first into seven parts and then each of those again into seven pieces, that is, into forty-nine pieces. The pieces of the embryo slipped out of the womb and fell on the ground weeping. Pārvatī and Parameśvara, who were wandering for the welfare of the world, saw them. Then Pārvatī, moved with pity for those pieces of flesh, begged her husband to make them all live, endowed with life-breath. Parameśvara made those forty-nine pieces of flesh into sons of one and the same form, ornament and age, and said to Pārvatī, "These are your children." By this story the Maruts are said in the respective hymns to be sons of Rudra, and Rudra is said to be the father of the Maruts.

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> यस्मै यजमानाय हविषा ददाशुषे सोमादिहनिर्दत्तवते हविषा प्रीता

> चशब्दात्पश्वादिपुष्टिं । अरासत प्रायच्छन् ; रासतिर्दानार्थः । अस्मै यजमानाय

> सस्याद्युत्पत्यर्थमुक्षंति । सिंचंति ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The sacrificer who has given an oblation (soma and the like) — to that sacrificer the Maruts, who are pleased by the oblation, who are protectors (ऊमासः (*ūmāsaḥ*)) and immortal, i.e. not subject to death, have given रायस्पोषं (*rāyaspoṣaṃ*), an increase of wealth; the word च (*ca*) adds an increase of cattle and the like. अरासत (*arāsata*) means "they gave"; the root रा (*rā*) has the sense of giving. To this sacrificer the Maruts, who are as it were हिताः (*hitāḥ*), acting for his good, like friends, and who are मयोभुवः (*mayobhuvaḥ*), producers of happiness, sprinkle (उक्षंति (*ukṣaṃti*), सिंचंति (*siṃcaṃti*)) the रजांसि (*rajāṃsi*), the worlds, plentifully (पुरु (*puru*) is "abundantly") with water (पयसा (*payasā*)), for the production of plants and the like.

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

The Marut deities, protectors and immortals, bring about for the sacrificer who gives oblations and the like an increase of wealth such as cattle; and these Marut deities, who are like well-wishing friends, being givers of happiness, supply water and thereby, through the growth of plants and the like, make all the worlds flourish.

##### English translation (H. H. Wilson)
To him, the presenter (of offerings), the protecting and immortal Maruts, (gratified) by the oblation, have given abundance of riches : they, the bestowers of happiness, becoming, as it were, the friends (of the worshipper), sprinkle the regions plentifully with water.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

The main purport: to the sacrificers who offer oblations addressed to the Marut deities, the deathless Maruts give wealth and the riches of cattle, sons and the like, and protect them. And, always working for the sacrificers' good, for their benefit, for the protection of the world and for the growth of plants, they bring about rain and make the whole earth soaked with water.

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> हे मरुतः युष्मत्संबंधिनो य एवास एवा अश्वविशेषास्तविषीभिः स्वकीयैर्बलैर्वृष्टिप्रदानादिरूपै रजांसि लोकानाव्यत सर्वत अवृण्वंति ते वो
> युष्माकमेवासोऽश्वाः स्वयतासः स्वयमेव सारथिना विनैव युक्ताः प्राध्रजन् ।

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

"O Maruts, those horses of yours, the एवासः (*evāsaḥ*) (a kind of horse), with their own powers (तविषीभिः (*taviṣībhiḥ*)), which take the form of bestowing rain and the like, cover the worlds (रजांसि (*rajāṃsi*)) on every side (आ अव्यत— (*ā avyata—*)). Those horses of yours, स्वयतासः (*svayatāsaḥ*), yoked of their own accord without any charioteer, प्राध्रजन् (*prādhrajan*)—

### Printed p. 20

> प्रकर्षण गच्छंति । व्याप्नुवंति । किंच युष्मदागमनाद्विश्वा भुवनानि सर्वाणि
> भूतजातानि विश्वा हर्म्या सर्वाणि हर्म्याणि च भयंते । बिभ्यति पतनात् । यस्मादेवं
> तस्माद्वो यामश्चित्रश्चायनीयः । आश्चर्यभूत इत्यर्थः । भीतेः कारणमाह ।
> ऋष्टिषु युद्धस्थाषु हेतिषु प्रयतासु प्रकर्षणोद्यतासु सतीषु । अथवा भीतौ
> दृष्टांतः । ऋष्टिषु प्रयतासु सतीषु । लुप्तोपमेयं । आयुधेषूद्यतेषु
> यथा जना बिभ्यति तद्वद्युष्मदागमनाद्बिभ्यतीत्यर्थः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

—go forth with force, pervading. Moreover, at your coming all beings (विश्वा भुवनानि (*viśvā bhuvanāni*)) and all houses (हर्म्या (*harmyā*)) fear (भयंते (*bhayaṃte*)) falling. Because this is so, your course is चित्रः (*citraḥ*), wonderful—such is the meaning. He gives the cause of the fear: when the ऋष्टिषु (*ṛṣṭiṣu*), the weapons used in battle, are प्रयतासु (*prayatāsu*), raised forcefully. Or else, an illustration of the fear: "when the ṛṣṭis are raised"; the object of comparison is left unexpressed—as men fear when weapons are raised, so they fear at your coming.

- विश्वा भुवनानि (*viśvā bhuvanāni*)—all beings

- ऋष्टिषु (*ṛṣṭiṣu*)—weapons used in battle
- प्रयतासु (*prayatāsu*)—when raised to strike (as men fear)
- भयंते (*bhayaṃte*)—fear (that they will fall)
- वः (*vaḥ*)—your
- यामः (*yāmaḥ*)—going, course
- चित्रः (*citraḥ*)—wonderful

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Maruts, your horses, which yoke themselves, move through all the worlds, endowed with the work of bestowing rain and the like; as in battle people are terrified by weapons, so at your coming palaces and the like, and all beings, are afraid that they will fall.

##### English translation (H. H. Wilson)
Those, ycur coursers, which traverse the regions in their speed,
proceed, self-guided : all worlds, all dwellings, are alarmed, for marvellous
is your coming : (such fear as is felt) when spears are thrust forth (in battle).

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

रजांसि (*rajāṃsi*)—रजो रजतेज्योती रज उच्यते । उदकं रज उच्यते । लोका रजांस्युच्यंते । (*rajo rajatejyotī raja ucyate | udakaṃ raja ucyate | lokā rajāṃsyucyaṃte |*) The word रजः (*rajaḥ*) has several meanings: light, water, worlds (Ni. 4-19). Here it means worlds.
तविषीभिः (*taviṣībhiḥ*)—The word तविषी (*taviṣī*) is read among the twenty-eight names of strength (Ni. 3-9). The Bhāṣyakāra explains this word as स्वकीयैर्बलैर्वृष्टिप्रदानादिरूपैः (*svakīyairbalairvṛṣṭipradānādirūpaiḥ*)—

### Printed p. 21

by their own power and might, which cause the rain to fall. Max Müller explains: "you have stirred up the clouds with might"—by your power you scatter the clouds and make rain fall. It is described here that, since the wind blows very violently when it rains, the Maruts have exerted their strength.
अव्यत (*avyata*)—लोकान् सर्वत आवृणंति (*lokān sarvata āvṛṇaṃti*): they pervade, cover the whole earth, is the Bhāṣyakāra's explanation. Max Müller says this word is derived from the root vī, meaning to stir up, to excite, and that here the Maruts scatter and impel the clouds. He adds that words such as "pravayaṇa—a goad" and "pravetṛ—driver" are likewise derived from that root.
एवासः (*evāsaḥ*)—This word, derived from the root इण् गत् (*iṇ gat*), denotes swift going; it means "going very fast". Since swift-going creatures are horses, they are to be understood as the horses of the Maruts. Although, according to the etymology पृषत्यो मरुतां (*pṛṣatyo marutāṃ*) (Ni. 2-28), the Maruts' horses are named spotted ones, deer, in many Ṛks words denoting horses are used.
स्वयतासः (*svayatāsaḥ*)—स्वयमेव सारथिना विनैव युक्ताः (*svayameva sārathinā vinaiva yuktāḥ*): the sense is that they yoke themselves to the chariot without a charioteer's help. In ordinary usage a charioteer or others harness the horses to the chariot; but the Maruts' horses are able of themselves, without others' urging or help, to yoke themselves to the chariot. The same idea is described of Indra's horses called Hari, and of the vehicle of the Aśvins.
भयंते विश्वा भुवनानि हर्म्या (*bhayaṃte viśvā bhuvanāni harmyā*)—All things in the world, living beings, and buildings such as houses tremble with fear at the Maruts' coming. Trees, people and houses shake from the very violent wind and rain; trees are uprooted by the blast and houses collapse. The seer describes just this with the word भयंते (*bhayaṃte*).
हर्म्या (*harmyā*)—सर्वाणि हर्म्याणि (*sarvāṇi harmyāṇi*): "all houses" is the Bhāṣyakāra's explanation. Max Müller says this word is an adjective of भुवनानि (*bhuvanāni*) and means "living in houses", and he explains the various meanings of the word. He holds that "hearth-pit, oven", then "house", came into use successively in the course of time:

### Printed p. 22

"Harmya is used here as an adjective of bhuvanāni and can only mean living in houses It does not, however, occur again in the same sense, though it occurs several times as a Substantive, meaning houses- Its original meaning is fire-pit, then, hearth, then house, a transition of meaning analogous to that of aedes. Most of the ancient nations begin their kitchen with a fire-pit. They dig a hole in the ground, take a piece of the animal's raw hide, and press it down with their hands close to the sides of the hole, which thus becomes a sort of pot or basin. This they fill with water, and they make a number of stones red-hot in a fire clse by. The meat is put into the water, and the stones dropped in till the meat is boiled. Catlin describes the process as awkward and tedious, and says that since the Assinaboins had learnt from the Mandans to make pottery, and had been supplied with vessels by the traders, they had entirely done away the custom excepting at public festivals; where they seem, like all others of the human family, to take pleasure in cherishing and perpetuating their ancient customs. This pit was called हर्म्य (*harmya*)." (Tylor's Early History of mankind. p. 262).
Besides:
ते हर्म्येषाः शिशवो न शुभ्रा वत्सासो न प्र क्रीळिनः पयोधाः । (*te harmyeṣāḥ śiśavo na śubhrā vatsāso na pra krīḷinaḥ payodhāḥ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-56-16)
In this Ṛk it is said that the Maruts stood near the hearth, shining like small boys; hence here हर्म्य (*harmya*) means hearth.
तेषां स हन्नो अक्षाणि यथेदं हर्म्यं तथा । (*teṣāṃ sa hanno akṣāṇi yathedaṃ harmyaṃ tathā |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-55-6)
In this Ṛk it is said that we close our eyes as we close this house; hence here हर्म्य (*harmya*) means house.
अभूदु केतुरुषसः पुरस्तात्पतीच्यागादधि हर्म्येभ्यः । (*abhūdu keturuṣasaḥ purastātpatīcyāgādadhi harmyebhyaḥ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-76-2)
In this Ṛk it is said that the light of dawn falls first on the houses; hence हर्म्य (*harmya*) means houses.

### Printed p. 23

स शेवृधो जात आ हर्म्येषु नाभिर्युवा भवति रोचनस्य । (*sa śevṛdho jāta ā harmyeṣu nābhiryuvā bhavati rocanasya |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-46-3)
Here Agni is born in houses (fire is kindled in houses); the word हर्म्य (*harmya*) again indicates the meaning house.
मन्योरियाय हर्म्येषु तस्था यतः प्रजज्ञ इंद्रो अस्य वेद । (*manyoriyāya harmyeṣu tasthā yataḥ prajajña iṃdro asya veda |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-73-10)
Here, since it says (Indra) came from anger and stood in the house, the meaning is again house.
Although in some places the word हर्म्य (*harmya*) may indicate "one who dwells in a house", yet since in the Ṛk Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-55-6 given above it is clearly said "house" or "building", it would not be very reasonable to assign another kind of meaning to the word. Still, the word is also seen used in the sense of pit, hollow.
यदीं सुक्षत्र प्रभृता मदस्य युयुत्संतं तमसि हर्म्ये धाः । (*yadīṃ sukṣatra prabhṛtā madasya yuyutsaṃtaṃ tamasi harmye dhāḥ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 5-32-5)
In this Ṛk ("O Indra, you cast the demon Śuṣṇa, who desired to fight, into a pit filled with pitch darkness") it is used in the sense of pit; and
युवं कण्वाय नासत्यापिरिप्ताय हर्म्ये । शश्वदूतीर्वशस्यथः । (*yuvaṃ kaṇvāya nāsatyāpiriptāya harmye | śaśvadūtīrvaśasyathaḥ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-5-23)
in this Ṛk ("O Nāsatyas, you give help to the seer Kaṇva, who was cast by the Asuras into a pit") the word हर्म्य (*harmya*) is used in the sense of pit.
श्रमस्य दायं वि भजंत्येभ्यो यदा यमो भवति हर्म्ये हितः । (*śramasya dāyaṃ vi bhajaṃtyebhyo yadā yamo bhavati harmye hitaḥ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-114-10)
In this Ṛk ("when Yama is seated in his house or in the lower world") it is used in the sense of house or lower world. Besides, the word घर्म (*gharma*) in the Ṛks Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-112-7; 1-119-6; 8-73-3; 10-80-3 also indicates that the seer Atri was cast into a pit full of flames of fire.

##### Grammar

- तविषीभिः — तव इति सौत्रधातुः । अस्मात् तनेणिद्वा (उ. सू. १-४८) इति टिषच् प्रत्ययः । टित्त्वात् टिड्डाणञ् इति स्त्रीत्वे ङीप् । व्यत्ययेन आद्युदात्तस्वरो भवति । तृतीयाबहुवचनांतरूपम् ।
- अव्यत — वी गत्यादिषु । छांदसत्वेन वर्तमानार्थे लङ् व्यत्ययो बहुलम् इति आत्मनेपदी भवति । शपि लुक् । बहुवचने झ प्रत्ययस्य आतादेशः । यणादेशः । अडागमः पूर्वं भवति । यद्योगत्वान्निघातस्वरो न भवति । आद्युदात्तो भवति ।
- एवासः — इण् गतौ धातुः । इण्‌शीभ्यां वन् (उ. सू. १-१५०) इति वन् प्रत्ययः । तन्निमित्तं धातोर्गुणः । नित्त्वादाद्युदात्तस्वरो भवति । जसि परे आज्जसेरसुक् (पा. सू. ७-१-५०) इति असुगागमः ।
- स्वयतासः — स्वः यतः येषां ते स्वयतासः । बहुव्रीहौ प्रकृत्या पूर्वपदम् इति पूर्वपदप्रकृतिस्वरो भवति ।
- अध्रजन् — धृज गतौ धातुः । छांदसत्वेन वर्तमानार्थे लङ् । प्रथमपुरुषबहुवचनांतरूपम् । तिजंतनिघातस्वरो भवति ।
- भयंते — इभी भये धातुः । व्यत्ययेन आत्मनेपदी भवति । लट् प्रथमपुरुषबहुवचने झोंतः इति अंतादेशः । व्यत्ययेन शपि श्लुआदेशो न भवति । शप्निमित्तं धातोरिको गुणः अयादेशः । पादादित्वान्निघातस्वरो न भवति । तास्यनुदात्तेत् इति सूत्रेण लसार्वधातुकस्य अनुदात्तत्वात् धातुस्वरः शिष्यते ।
- विश्वा, हर्म्या — शेश्छंदसि बहुलं इति नपुंसके शेः लोपः ।
- चित्रः — चिञ् चयने धातुः । अमिचिमिदि (उ. सू. ४-६०३) इति क्त्र प्रत्ययः । कित्त्वाद्गुणो न भवति । प्रत्ययस्वरेण अंतोदात्तो भवति ।
- प्रयतासु — गतिरनंतरः (पा. सू. ६-२-४९) इति कर्मणि क्तप्रत्ययांतस्य परत्वात् गतेः प्रकृतिस्वरो भवति । सप्तमीबहुवचनांतरूपम् ।
- यामः — या प्रापणे धातुः । अर्तिस्तुसु (उ. सू. १-१३७) इति मन् प्रत्ययः । नित्त्वादाद्युदात्तो भवति ।

> स्त्री । रथारूढेत्यर्थः । तादृशी स्त्रीव । सा यथा चलति तद्वत् !

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The Maruts are त्वेषयामाः (*tveṣayāmāḥ*), "of blazing course". When they make the mountains resound, that is, the caverns of the mountain that has joints, or else the cloud-bearing clouds, or when they traverse the back, the upper region, of the sky that consists of lightning, beneficial to men, then, at your going, the whole assemblage of forest trees, the guardian of the forest, is afraid. Likewise the herb, such as priyaṅgu or rice, moves forth vigorously, that is, stirs from its place this way and that. An illustration follows: like a woman who desires a chariot for herself, that is, one mounted on a chariot. As such a woman sways as she moves, so do they.

- यत् (*yat*)—when
- पर्वतान् (*parvatān*)—the mountain caverns or the clouds
- नदयंत (*nadayaṃta*)—they make resound
- वा (*vā*)—or
- दिवः (*divaḥ*)—of the firmament

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

त्वेषयामाः (*tveṣayāmāḥ*)—प्रदीप्तगमनाः (*pradīptagamanāḥ*). Those whose going is terrible. The meaning is that when the wind blows with force it causes fear to everyone, chiefly to trees and houses. For trees are overturned with their roots by the swiftly blowing storm; houses and their roofs are thrown far away; small plants and shrubs are thrown into confusion. Because it causes great calamity in this way, the going of the Maruts (the wind blowing with violence) is described here as very terrible. Pandit Wilson has taken this word to mean the horses of the Maruts. No proper reason for taking it so appears.

नदयंत (*nadayaṃta*)—the root नद् (*nad*) has the sense "make a sound, resound". The word पर्वत (*parvata*) used in this Ṛk can be given two meanings: a cloud (Ni. 2-21) or a hill (Ni. 1-20). If the meaning "cloud" is taken, the blast of the wind makes the cloud thunder; if "hill", it must be said that the swiftly blowing wind makes a sound in the foothills and mountain caverns. Pandit Wilson explains that when the horses of the Maruts go swiftly, they make the mountains and the like echo. But the root नद् (*nad*) at first had the meanings "to vibrate, to shake", and in course of time the sense "make a sound" seems to have come into use. From this point of view, if the meaning of this Ṛk is explained, it is suggested that at the coming of the Maruts all people, trees and the like tremble with fear. The sentence विश्वो वो अज्मन्भयते वनस्पतिः (*viśvo vo ajmanbhayate vanaspatiḥ*) in the second half of this Ṛk makes this clear.

### Printed p. 27
In the Ṛk
अच्युता चिद्वो अज्मन्ना नानदति पर्वतासो वनस्पतिः । भूमिर्यामेषु रेजते ॥ (*acyutā cidvo ajmannā nānadati parvatāso vanaspatiḥ | bhūmiryāmeṣu rejate ‖*)
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-20-5)
the very matter stated in the present Ṛk is elaborated. Here it is said that the earth also trembles. In this connection, the terrible character of the coming of the Maruts stated in the Ṛk

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-37-7)
is noteworthy.

रथीयंतीव (*rathīyaṃtīva*)—like a woman seated in a chariot; the meaning is that a woman seated in a chariot sways this way and that, or trembles with fear, when the chariot moves fast. This word is not used anywhere else in the Ṛgveda. Pandit Max Muller has said that the meaning "a swiftly moving chariot or chariot-wheel" for this word is reasonable.

जिहीत ओषधिः (*jihīta oṣadhiḥ*)—प्रियंगुव्रीह्यादि प्र जहीते । प्रकर्षण गच्छति स्वस्थानादितस्ततश्चलतीत्यर्थः । (*priyaṃguvrīhyādi pra jahīte | prakarṣaṇa gacchati svasthānāditastataścalatītyarthaḥ |*) The word वनस्पति (*vanaspati*), stated earlier in this Ṛk, indicates large trees. The word ओषधि (*oṣadhi*) indicates small plants such as priyaṅgu, rice and the like, that is, saplings, creepers and the like that do not grow very tall or firm. The meaning is that such plants are torn up and thrown by the blow of the wind, scattered in various directions. In the example above, the sentence जिहीत पर्वतो गिरिः (*jihīta parvato giriḥ*) says that even the immovable mountain made of boulders is scattered from its place by the Maruts; so the main sense is: how indeed could plants and the like, which have no power to stand up against them, withstand the force of the Maruts?

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O fierce Maruts, of uplifted strength, you, with a good mind, as hosts unharmed, fill up our good mind, our good understanding. At what time? That is told: when, at which time, your lightning, scattering in nature and biting, tears in the cloud-formation, and harms the animals. It is well known that when the wind blows, animals are shattered. An illustration of the harm: as a well-placed, well-sped weapon, an instrument of slaying, wounds, so it does.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

सुचेतुना (*sucetunā*)—शोभनचेतसा (*śobhanacetasā*). With an excellent, that is, gracious, mind. ##### Gist of the bhāṣya

प्र स्कंभदेष्णाः (*pra skaṃbhadeṣṇāḥ*): those whose gifts are firmly established, i.e. whose giving never ceases. अनवभ्रराधसः (*anavabhrarādhasaḥ*): those whose wealth, such as oblations, is unfailing; राधः (*rādhaḥ*) is a name for wealth. अलातृणासः (*alātṛṇāsaḥ*): the letter l stands in the place of n; it is अनातृणास (*anātṛṇāsa*), "free from oppression". Or else: those who sufficiently oppress enemies. Or else: those who give fruits in great measure. Such Maruts worship (honour, esteem with praise and the like) in the sacrifices Indra, the adorable, who is their friend. For what purpose? For the drinking of the exhilarating Soma, the means of joy. How do they know Indra's greatness? He answers: the Maruts know the first (chief) manly deeds of the hero Indra, who drives his enemies about in many ways, deeds such as the slaying of Vṛtra.

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

These Marut deities, unceasing givers, endowed with immense wealth such as oblations, givers of abundant fruit, especially praised in the sacrifices, knowing the chief exploits of the hero Indra such as the slaying of Vṛtra, worship and invoke the venerable Indra with praise and the like, in order that he may drink the Soma juice, the means of exhilaration.

##### English translation (H. H. Wilson)
Givers without stint, possessors of inexhaustible riches, devoid of malevolence, ever glorified at sacrifices. they worship the adorable (indra), for (the sake of) drinking the (Soma) wine; for they know the first manly exploits of the hero.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

प्र स्कंभदेष्णाः (*pra skaṃbhadeṣṇāḥ*)—प्रकर्षेण स्तंभितदानाः । अविरतदाना इत्यर्थः । (*prakarṣeṇa staṃbhitadānāḥ | aviratadānā ityarthaḥ |*) The desired objects the Marut deities give are firm: they do not last a short time and perish but remain for ever. This is the intention.

अनवभ्रराधसः (*anavabhrarādhasaḥ*)—अभ्रष्टहविरादिधनाः । (*abhraṣṭahavirādidhanāḥ |*) Since the word राधः (*rādhaḥ*) is read among the twenty-eight names of wealth, beginning with मघं (*maghaṃ*) and रेक्लः (*reklaḥ*) (Ni. 3-9), राधः (*rādhaḥ*) means wealth. अनवभ्र (*anavabhra*) means not fallen, that is, not wasted. The oblations and the like offered to the Maruts are not wasted: they accept them and bestow on the sacrificers a fitting reward, so the wealth in the form of oblations offered to the Maruts is not in vain. This is the main intention.

आलातृणासः (*ālātṛṇāsaḥ*)—This word is used in this Ṛk and also in the Ṛk
अलातृणो वल इंद्र व्रजो गोः पुरा हंतोर्भयमानो व्यार (*alātṛṇo vala iṃdra vrajo goḥ purā haṃtorbhayamāno vyāra*) ।
सुगान्सथो अकृणोन्निरजे गाः प्रावन्वाणीः पुरुहूतं धमंतीः (*sugānsatho akṛṇonniraje gāḥ prāvanvāṇīḥ puruhūtaṃ dhamaṃtīḥ*) ॥
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-30-12)
and nowhere else. For this word Sāyaṇa, in his explanation of the present Ṛk, says अनातृणास आतर्दनरहिताः अथवा अलं पर्याप्तमातर्दनाः शत्रूणां यद्वा अलमत्यर्थं दातारः फलानां। (*anātṛṇāsa ātardanarahitāḥ athavā alaṃ paryāptamātardanāḥ śatrūṇāṃ yadvā alamatyarthaṃ dātāraḥ phalānāṃ|*) and in explaining the Ṛk given as example, अलातृणो वल इंद्र व्रजः (*alātṛṇo vala iṃdra vrajaḥ*), he says बहुदकतयालमत्यर्थमातृद्यते हिंस्यत इत्यलातृणः (*bahudakatayālamatyarthamātṛdyate hiṃsyata ityalātṛṇaḥ*). The author of the Nirukta, explaining this Ṛk, says अलातृणोऽलमातर्दनो मेघः (*alātṛṇo 'lamātardano meghaḥ*) (Ni. 6-2). That is: clouds that are oppressed for the water in them, that is, forced to pour rain. The mere presence of water in clouds does not allow one to say that they pour rain; the main intention is that they are driven by the Maruts, that is, by the blow of the swiftly blowing wind, and then pour rain, thus oppressing the clouds. Or the meaning may be: oppressing enemies, or giving fruits abundantly to those who praise them. Such is Sāyaṇa's explanation.

Max Müller has cited the opinions of some Western scholars on the meaning of this word and criticised them. He gives the Nirukta's अलमतर्दनो मेघः (*alamatardano meghaḥ*) the sense "the cloud which opens easily", the cloud that easily pours water; in his view the word अतर्वन (*atarvana*) is derived from the root तृप् (*tṛp*). Sāyaṇa, on the contrary, says the word is derived from the root तृः (*tṛḥ*) meaning "kill, injure", and explains accordingly. Professor Roth holds that this word is derived from the root रा दाने (*rā dāne*) combined with the prefix तृण (*tṛṇa*), and, since अतर्वन (*atarvana*) implies negation, that it must mean one who does not give or yield anything. If this meaning is accepted, would not the generosity of the Maruts, stated in this very Ṛk in words such as स्कंभदेष्टाः (*skaṃbhadeṣṭāḥ*), be made vain? Wilson's translation, "Devoid of malevolence", does not seem very fitting either. Therefore, to explain it so that a right sense fits both this Ṛk, where it qualifies the Maruts, and Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-30-10, where it qualifies the asura Vala, one must say that the word अलातृण (*alātṛṇa*) is derived from लातृन् (*lātṛn*). This लातृन् (*lātṛn*) or लात्र (*lātra*) is

### Printed p. 35
a variant form of the word रात्र (*rātra*). Since r and l are not distinguished, one may say रात्र (*rātra*) instead of लात्र (*lātra*). If this word रात्र (*rātra*) is derived from the root रै (*rai*) or रा शब्दे (*rā śabde*) (to bark), then for the Maruts the word अलातृण (*alātṛṇa*) (अरातृण (*arātṛṇa*)) can mean "not abusing, not wishing evil", and for Vala "without making a sound" (without braking). So he has examined the meaning of the word.

विदथेषु (*vidatheṣu*)—in sacrifices and the like. For the detailed explanation we have given of the meaning of this word, see pages 9-13.

अर्कं (*arkaṃ*)—अर्चनीयमिंद्रं । (*arcanīyamiṃdraṃ |*) Indra, worthy of worship. The word अर्क (*arka*) has various meanings: food (Ni. 3-9), the thunderbolt (Ni. 3-11), deity, mantra (Ni. 5-4). Yāska says of अर्क (*arka*):
अर्कः । अर्को देवो भवति यदेनमर्चति (*arkaḥ | arko devo bhavati yadenamarcati*) ।
अर्को मंत्रो भवति यदनेनार्चंति (*arko maṃtro bhavati yadanenārcaṃti*) ।
अर्कमन्नं भवत्यर्चति भूतानि (*arkamannaṃ bhavatyarcati bhūtāni*) ।
अर्को वृक्षो भवति संवृत्तः कटुकिम्ना (*arko vṛkṣo bhavati saṃvṛttaḥ kaṭukimnā*) ॥
(Ni. 3-4-2 **NOTE**) **NOTE**
Thus he gives four kinds of meaning. Their explanation can be made as follows.
Deity: तावत्सर्व एवार्क इत्युच्यते यद्यस्मादेनं देवं अर्चंति स्तोतारः (*tāvatsarva evārka ityucyate yadyasmādenaṃ devaṃ arcaṃti stotāraḥ*)—अर्क (*arka*) means a deity, because the devotees worship by means of him the person or god whom they worship; the object (deity) the devotees worship is called अर्क (*arka*).
अर्को मंत्रो भवति। यद्यस्मादनेन मंत्रेणार्चंति स्तोतारस्तस्मादर्कः— (*arko maṃtro bhavati| yadyasmādanena maṃtreṇārcaṃti stotārastasmādarkaḥ—*) अर्क (*arka*) also means mantra, because those who praise worship the deity by this (by अर्क (*arka*) or the mantra); hence the word अर्क (*arka*) is also a name of the mantra.
अर्कमन्नं भवति—तद्धि अर्चति भूतानि यो हि पूज्यः तस्यान्नमपि संस्कारविशेषेण संस्कृतं पूजायां क्रियते, पूज्यं च सर्वस्यॆवान्नं— (*arkamannaṃ bhavati—taddhi arcati bhūtāni yo hi pūjyaḥ tasyānnamapi saṃskāraviśeṣeṇa saṃskṛtaṃ pūjāyāṃ kriyate, pūjyaṃ ca sarvasyèvānnaṃ—*) अर्क (*arka*) also means food. Since food is necessary to all creatures, everyone honours and desires it; because all, with love and respect, honour and wish for it, the word अर्क (*arka*) also means food.

अर्को वृक्षो भवति य एष प्रसिद्धः । संवृत्तः हि संगतः संव्याप्त कटुकिम्मा कटुकभावेन भवति— (*arko vṛkṣo bhavati ya eṣa prasiddhaḥ | saṃvṛttaḥ hi saṃgataḥ saṃvyāpta kaṭukimmā kaṭukabhāvena bhavati—*) The name of a particular tree is well known for the word अर्क (*arka*). The अर्क (*arka*) tree is called ऎक्क (*èkka*) in the Karnataka language. Because its twigs are used in the homas and it is customarily used in medicines and the like, the अर्क (*arka*) tree is known to all. Because it is bitter it is said कटुकिम्मा (*kaṭukimmā*). In the present Ṛk the commentator explains the word अर्क (*arka*) as अर्चनीयं (*arcanīyaṃ*), that is, worthy of worship.

प्रथमानि—प्रथमानि प्रतमानि मुख्यानि । (*prathamāni—prathamāni pratamāni mukhyāni |*) प्रथम (*prathama*) means chief. Yāska gives the explanation प्रथम इति मुख्यनाम प्रतमो भवति (*prathama iti mukhyanāma pratamo bhavati*) (Ni. 2-22).

पौंस्या—पुंस्तानि वृत्रवधादिरूपाणि । (*pauṃsyā—puṃstāni vṛtravadhādirūpāṇi |*) Daring acts and deeds of valour that men and heroes can perform; here he explains them as daring acts such as the slaying of Vṛtra.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Sāyaṇa continues: whom? The man whom you protect from अभिह्रुतेः (*abhihruteḥ*), a cause of overpowering, from crooked-natured sin (अघात् (*aghāt*)). Further, O fierce ones (उग्रा (*ugrā*), of surging splendour), तवसः (*tavasaḥ*), mighty and swift; विरप्पिनः (*virappinaḥ*): this (रप्पिनः (*rappinaḥ*)) is a name for "great", the great ones. Words worth uttering are रपाः (*rapāḥ*); those who have them are रप्पिनः (*rappinaḥ*); those who have various रप्पिनः (*rappinaḥ*), that is, praisers, are so called. Such as you, whomever you protect (पाथन (*pāthana*) = पाथ रक्षथ (*pātha rakṣatha*)). On what ground is it said? तनयस्य (*tanayasya*) is only illustrative: because of calumny (शंसात् (*śaṃsāt*)) in the matters that cause the nourishment of sons and the rest. "Him who does so, protect" is the remainder. The meaning: protect him by averting what is undesired, such as the destruction through sin, and in the attainment of the desired, in the form of nourishment of sons and others.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

"O Maruts, in your cars" (वो रथेषु (*vo ratheṣu*)), in the cars that belong to you, the instruments of swift going, "all good things" (विश्वानि भद्रा (*viśvāni bhadrā*)): all auspicious things, substances fit for enjoyment, or fruits to be given to us, "have been placed" (आहिता (*āhitā*)). Likewise अंसेषु (*aṃseṣu*), on your shoulders, that is, on the arms indicated by them; the particle आ (*ā*) has the sense of "also". "As if vying with one another" (मिथस्पृध्येव (*mithaspṛdhyeva*)): from excess of strength, the तविषाणि (*taviṣāṇi*), the strengths, which are matters of mutual rivalry, namely the weapons of the kind stated, are placed on the shoulders. Further, वः प्रपथेषु (*vaḥ prapatheṣu*): प्रपथानि (*prapathāni*) are resting places on which the paths go forward; in them खादयः (*khādayaḥ*), edibles and foods, have been placed. Or else: प्रपथेषु (*prapatheṣu*) means "at the tips of the feet", where the paths go forward, and खादयः (*khādayaḥ*) are firm special ornaments, placed there. Further, अक्षः (*akṣaḥ*), your chariot's axle, and the wheels fixed on it (चक्रा (*cakrā*)): समया (*samayā*), near them, in their vicinity, वि ववृते (*vi vavṛte*), it turns specially; the meaning is that it never becomes stuck to the wheels. His purport: since such means for going to the place of divine sacrifice are ready, therefore come quickly.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

विश्वानि भद्रा महतो रथेषु वः (*viśvāni bhadrā mahato ratheṣu vaḥ*)—In this Ṛk the seer, desiring the coming of the Maruts, describes everything as ready for their coming. O Maruts, in your car all excellent or choice things have been made ready. These things are fit for enjoyment; the main purport of this sentence is that these substances have been prepared and placed in your car in order to be given to us, who praise.

रथेषु (*ratheṣu*)—Yāska in his Nirukta says of the word रथ (*ratha*): रथो रंहतेर्गतिकर्मणः । स्थिरतेर्वा स्याद्विपरीतस्य । रममाणोऽस्मिंस्तिष्ठतीति वा रपतेर्वा रसतेर्वा (*ratho raṃhatergatikarmaṇaḥ | sthiratervā syādviparītasya | ramamāṇo 'smiṃstiṣṭhatīti vā rapatervā rasatervā*) (Ni. 9-11). That is, it may be formed from the root रंह् (*raṃh*) denoting the action of going, or from the root स्थिरति (*sthirati*) meaning "be firm". Because this car is always going (moving about), it is called रथ (*ratha*). Or else, the warrior who sits in it remains steady: whereas the body shakes when one sits on horses and the like, one can sit steadily without shaking in a car, so it is called रथ (*ratha*). Or else, because one sits in this car and moves about happily, it is called रथ (*ratha*); thus he has explained the parts of the word. **NOTE**

…made of metals, various weapons are gathered together and placed in one spot in the chariot; as the chariot moves, those weapons strike against one another and make a noise. This is the intention.

तविषाणि (*taviṣāṇi*)—The word तविष (*taviṣa*) is read among the names of strength (Ni. 3-13). But since the word is used here in the neuter plural, it is not proper to explain तविषाणि (*taviṣāṇi*) in the first Ṛk of this sūkta as "capacities, prowess, daring deeds". For capacities and daring deeds are not things that are placed in chariots; they are only names of actions. Or, even if तविषाणि (*taviṣāṇi*) is taken to mean armies, it is impossible to place armies in a chariot, however large it may be. Therefore the meaning "strong weapons" alone is proper for the word तविषाणि (*taviṣāṇi*). Hence the sentence मिथस्पृधैव तविषाण्याहिता (*mithaspṛdhaiva taviṣāṇyāhitā*) means: many excellent weapons, which clash against one another with the motion of the chariot, are placed in your chariot.

अंसेष्वा वः (*aṃseṣvā vaḥ*)—Besides the weapons in the chariot mentioned above, you bear some other weapons on your arms.

प्रपथेषु खादयः (*prapatheṣu khādayaḥ*)—वः प्रपथेषु । प्रगताः पंथानो येषु विश्रामस्थानेषु तानि । प्रपथानि । तेषु खादयः खाद्यानि भक्ष्याण्याहितानि । यद्वा प्रगतः पंथा मैस्तेषु पादाग्रेषु खादयः स्थिरा आभरणविशेषा अहिताः ॥ (*vaḥ prapatheṣu | pragatāḥ paṃthāno yeṣu viśrāmasthāneṣu tāni | prapathāni | teṣu khādayaḥ khādyāni bhakṣyāṇyāhitāni | yadvā pragataḥ paṃthā maisteṣu pādāgreṣu khādayaḥ sthirā ābharaṇaviśeṣā ahitāḥ ‖*) The commentator has given two kinds of meaning for this sentence. A प्रपथ (*prapatha*) is a place where, on a journey, one sits to rest when tired from walking for some time. It is natural to sit in such a place and eat the foods one has brought along; this matter is what is indicated here. Or else प्रपथ (*prapatha*) means the feet used for walking, and the anklets worn on them are called खादयः (*khādayaḥ*); this is the other meaning he has given.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Those Maruts, in their arms that are beneficial to men, bear abundant auspicious (भद्रा (*bhadrā*)) riches to give to the praisers; धिरे (*dhire*) is a form of धारयंति (*dhārayaṃti*), "they hold". So too on their chests they wear ornaments (रुक्माः (*rukmāḥ*)) made of gold, jewels and the like. Of what kind? रभसासः (*rabhasāsaḥ*) means full of impetuosity or radiant, and अंजयः (*aṃjayaḥ*) means manifest. On the shoulder-roots are these white garlands. On their vajra-like weapons are razor-edges (क्षुराः (*kṣurāḥ*)). अधि (*adhi*) only restates the sense of the locative. Thus the Maruts variously bear splendour (श्रियः (*śriyaḥ*)) on all their limbs in due order. The illustration: वयो न पक्षान् (*vayo na pakṣān*)—as birds bear wings. **NOTE**

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

On the arms of the Maruts, who bring benefit to men, are many auspicious things dear to men; on their chests are gleaming, clearly visible golden ornaments; on their shoulders are clean garlands, and (in their hands) sharp, vajra-like weapons. Just as birds have wings of many colours, the Maruts too are variously adorned. (Or, they spread their radiance all around.)
