# Preface

In this part of the Ṛgveda-saṃhitā the eighth Maṇḍala has come to an end and the ninth Maṇḍala has been begun. In this Maṇḍala there are in all 114 sūktas and in all 1108 ṛks. Although the seers of all these are different, the deity is one and the same: Pavamāna Soma. Of these 114 sūktas, in the first 67 sūktas there are 610 ṛks, and except for a few of these ṛks, all the remaining ṛks are in the Gāyatrī metre. In the Ṛgveda the Gāyatrī metre and the deity Soma have a very important place. Therefore it is the established practice to employ these 610 ṛks, which are famous as "Pavamāna", in Vedic rites in many different ways. Although the deity of these mantras, called Pavamāna Soma, denotes the soma plant and the soma juice, in recent times some people are in the habit of explaining the word soma in whatever way they like and using these mantras accordingly. Some explain soma as meaning Rudra and use these mantras for the Rudrābhiṣeka. Some explain pavamāna as meaning the wind, and say that these mantras praise the wind-deity. In the Ṛgveda the meaning Rudra for the word soma is not seen anywhere. Only in the Yajurveda is the meaning Rudra for the word soma (the god accompanied by the goddess Umā, that is, Rudra) stated clearly, in such mantras as "Homage to Soma and to Rudra". But for the word soma, the meaning that is found in the Ṛgveda, namely the deity Soma, is the proper one. From very ancient times those who were engaged in the soma sacrifice, the drinking of soma and the like have been regarded as eminent. There are many sacrifices that are performed for the very purpose of drinking soma. Those who have performed these soma sacrifices are called somayājins. In the sacrifice, on certain days, in the morning, at midday and in the evening, the savana rites of pressing out the soma juice, called the morning pressing, the midday pressing and the third pressing, are duly carried out. For these reasons the deity Soma, and Indra, who is the chief deity in the drinking of soma, hold an exalted place and importance. Moreover, the Veda-Puruṣa himself has declared in the Pariśiṣṭa mantra that even those who duly study these 610 ṛks of this Maṇḍala, which are called Pavamāna, will remove and be cleansed of many kinds of sins committed from the time of dwelling in the womb until death, such as the killing of mother and father, the slaying of a cow, theft, the slaying of a woman, the slaying of a Brāhmaṇa, the drinking of liquor, the theft of gold, intercourse with the guru's wife, unlawful intercourse with women, and the eating of forbidden food, and that he himself will make them pure. And further:

The wise who recite the Pāvamāna, the supreme Brahman,
will for seven births be Brāhmaṇas, rich in wealth and masters of the Veda.
These Pāvamānī ṛks are six hundred with ten added;
whoever recites and repeats this mantra will ward off the fear of dreadful death.
This is meritorious, removes sin, and takes away the fear of disease and death;
to those who recite it and to those who listen to it, it gives the highest state.
(Pariśiṣṭa mantra 18-19 to 21)

That is: the man who recites these Pāvamānī sūktas, which contain 610 ṛks in the Gāyatrī metre, will in seven births be rich in wealth and a master of the Veda. If the muttering, the offering in the fire and the like of these mantras are performed, even the fear of dreadful death that has drawn near will be removed. Those who recite this and those who listen to it attain the best state. So the Veda-Puruṣa himself has stated clearly.

To publish such a Ṛgveda-saṃhitā, which is most ancient, most profound, most vast, and the foundation of all the śāstras, in simple Kannada together with a critical translation, is not an easy task. Nevertheless, with the weighty aim that through such a great undertaking all people, learned and unlearned alike, may gain growth of knowledge and become recipients of God's grace, His Highness Śrī Jayachamarajendra Wodeyar Bahadur, G.C.B., G.C.S.I., the Lord of the throne of Karnataka, a lover of Vedic learning, himself a scholar, beloved of his subjects and devoted to God, and his Government, are encouraging with their generous patronage the publication of this work, which requires great expense; this must be called the good fortune of the multitude of people of Karnataka. I pray that the Sarveśvara, the Veda-Puruṣa, may bestow still more abundantly on our generous-minded Lord, on his family, on the princesses, on the prince bearing the auspicious name Śrīkaṇṭhadatta Narasimharaja Wodeyar, and on the whole multitude of his subjects, all worldly fruits such as long life, health and prosperity, and all wealth, as well as the spiritual knowledge that leads to the highest goal.

Mysore.
