## Sūkta 68 (Maṇḍala 8)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> ॥ श्रीगणेशाय नमः ॥
> यस्य निःश्वसितं वेदा यो वेदेभ्योऽखिलं जगत् ।
> निर्ममे तमहं वंदे विद्यातीर्थमहेश्वरम् ॥
> षष्ठे चतुर्थमध्यायं श्रीमायणसुतः सुधीः ।।
> व्याख्याय सायणाचार्यः पंचमं व्याकरोत्यथ ।।
> तत्र ‘आ त्वा रथम्' इत्येकोनविंशत्यृचं नवमं सूक्तम् । अत्रेयमनुक्रमणिका-' आ त्वैकोना प्रियमेध आदावनुष्टुम्मुखास्तृचाश्चत्वारोऽंत्याः षळृक्षाश्वमेधयोर्दानस्तुतिः' इति । आंगिरसः प्रियमेध ऋषिः। प्रथमाचतुर्थीसप्तमीदशम्योऽनुष्टुभः शिष्टाः परं गायत्रम्' इति परिभाषया गायत्र्यः । अंततः षट्स्वृक्षु ऋक्षाश्वमेधयोर्दानं स्तूयते । अतस्तास्तद्देवताकाः । शिष्टा अनुक्तपरिभाषयैंद्र्यः । सूक्तविनियोगो लैंगिकः। अद्यस्तृचो मरुत्वतीयस्य प्रतिपत्। तथा च सूत्रितम्--- ‘ आ त्वा रथं यथोतय इदं वसो सुतमंध इति मरुत्वतीयस्य प्रतिपदनुचरौ' (आश्व. श्रौ. ५. १४) इति । महाव्रतादिष्वपि यत्र तृचांतरं न विधीयते तत्र सर्वत्रायमेव प्रतिपद्भवति ।।

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

After the salutation to Gaṇeśa and the verse in praise of Vidyātīrtha-Maheśvara, from whose breath the Vedas came and from the Vedas the whole world, Sāyaṇa says that, having explained the fourth Adhyāya in the sixth division, he now expounds the fifth. Its first sūkta, the ninth of the Anuvāka, is the one beginning आ त्वा रथम् (*ā tvā ratham*) and has nineteen ṛks. The Anukramaṇikā describes it as follows: Priyamedha is the seer; the first ṛk and the other ṛks named there are in the Anuṣṭubh metre, and the remaining ones are Gāyatrī according to the general rule that the rest is Gāyatrī; in the last six ṛks the gift of Ṛkṣa and Aśvamedha is praised, so these have the praise of that gift as deity, and the remaining ṛks are Indra's by the rule for what is not stated. Priyamedha is of the Āṅgirasa family. The use of the sūkta is according to the Liṅga. Its first tṛca is the opening verse-group (pratipad) of the Marutvatīya recitation, as the sūtra states (Āśv. Śrau. 5. 14) **NOTE**. In the Mahāvrata and other rites too, wherever no other tṛca is prescribed, this one is the pratipad.

### Printed p. 2

The Anuvāda: In the eighth Maṇḍala of the Ṛgveda-saṃhitā the seventh Anuvāka contains ten sūktas in all. Of these, eight sūktas have been explained in the previous Adhyāya. Now this sūkta beginning आ त्वा रथं (*ā tvā rathaṃ*) is the ninth in this Anuvāka. In the Anukramaṇikā it is said: आ त्वैकोना प्रियमेध आदावनुष्टुप्खास्तृचाश्चत्वारोऽन्त्याः षळृक्षाश्वमेधयोर्दानस्तुतिः (*ā tvaikonā priyamedha ādāvanuṣṭupkhāstṛcāścatvāro 'ntyāḥ ṣaḷṛkṣāśvamedhayordānastutiḥ*). The seer of this sūkta is Priyamedha, the son of Aṅgiras; the first, fourth, seventh and tenth ṛks of this sūkta are in the Anuṣṭubh metre; the remaining ṛks are in the Gāyatrī metre. In the last six ṛks the praise of the gifts of the two, Ṛkṣa and Aśvamedha, is stated. Therefore the praise of their gifts is the deity of those ṛks. For the remaining ṛks Indra is the deity. The use of the sūkta is the Liṅga. The first three ṛks of this sūkta are to be recited as the pratipad-tṛca among the mantras of the Marutvatīya śastra, as is explained by the sūtra of the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra आ त्वा रथं यथोतय इदं वसो सुतमंध इति मरुत्वतीयस्य प्रतिपदनुचरौ (*ā tvā rathaṃ yathotaya idaṃ vaso sutamaṃdha iti marutvatīyasya pratipadanucarau*) (Ā. 5-14). And in the Mahāvrata and other ritual rites also, where there is no specific rule that other mantras are to be recited for the pratipad-tṛcas, there the tṛca stated above is to be recited as the pratipad-tṛca.

#### Ṛk 1

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> आ त्वा॒ रथं॒ यथो॒तये॑ सु॒म्नाय॑ वर्तयामसि ।  
> तु॒वि॒कू॒र्मिमृ॑ती॒षह॒मिंद्र॒ शवि॑ष्ठ॒ सत्प॑ते ॥१॥

### Printed p. 3

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> आ । त्वा । रथम् । यथा । ऊतये । सुम्नाय । वर्तयामसि ।  
> तुविऽकूर्मिम् । ऋतिऽसहम् । इंद्र । शविष्ठ । सत्ऽपते ॥१

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> हे इंद्र त्वा त्वाम् आ वर्तयामसि आवर्तयामः । किमर्थम् । ऊतये अस्माकं रक्षणाय सुम्नाय सुखाय च । किमिव । रथं यथा । रथं यथोतये सुखाय चावर्तयंति तद्वत् । कीदृशं त्वाम् । तुविकूर्मिं बहुकर्माणम् ऋतीषहं हिंसकानामभिभवितारम् । हे इंद्र शविष्ठ अतिशयेन बलवन् हे सत्पते सतां पालक त्वामिति समन्वयः ।।

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Indra, we turn you hither, we bring you, for our protection and for our happiness. As what? As people turn a chariot hither for protection and happiness, so we turn you. What kind of you? One of many deeds (तुविकूर्मिं (*tuvikūrmiṃ*)), and the overcomer of the violent (ऋतीषहं (*ṛtīṣahaṃ*)). O Indra, most mighty one (शविष्ठ (*śaviṣṭha*)), O protector of the good (सत्पते (*satpate*)): this is the construction with "you".

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- शविष्ठ (*śaviṣṭha*)—most powerful
- सत्पते (*satpate*)—protector of the good
- इंद्र (*iṃdra*)—O Indra
- तुविकूर्मिं (*tuvikūrmiṃ*)—doer of many kinds of deeds
- ऋतीषहं (*ṛtīṣahaṃ*)—one who overthrows the violent enemies
- त्वा (*tvā*)—you
- ऊतये (*ūtaye*)—for our protection
- सुम्नायु (*sumnāyu*)—and also for our happiness **NOTE**
- रथं यथा (*rathaṃ yathā*)—as a chariot brings you
- आ वर्तयामसि (*ā vartayāmasi*)—we bring

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, most powerful one, protector of the good, doer of many kinds of deeds, one who overthrows the violent enemies: you we bring, for our protection and also for our happiness, as a chariot brings you.

##### English translation
Most powerful Indra, protector of the good, we bring you here, rich in achievement and subduer of enemies, as a car for our protection and weal.

#### Ṛk 2

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> तुवि॑शुष्म॒ तुवि॑क्रतो॒ शची॑वो॒ विश्व॑या मते ।  
> आ प॑प्राथ महित्व॒ना ॥२॥

### Printed p. 4

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> तुविऽशुष्म । तुविक्रतो इति तुविऽक्रतो । शचीऽवः । विश्वया । मते ।  
> आ । पप्राथ । महिऽत्वना ॥२

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> हे तुविशुष्म प्रभूतबल हे तुविक्रतो बहुकर्मन् । अथवा बहुप्रज्ञ कर्मणः पृथगभिधानात् । हे शचीवः बहुकर्मोपेत मते पूजनीयेंद्र विश्वया विश्वव्याप्तेन महित्वना महत्त्वेन आ पप्राथ आपूरितवानसि । अविशेषाद्विश्वमित्यर्थः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Tuviśuṣma, of abundant strength; O Tuvikrato, of many deeds, or else of much wisdom, since deed is named separately. O Śacīvaḥ, endowed with many deeds; O Mate, adorable Indra: with your all-pervading greatness you have filled everything. Since nothing is specified, the meaning is "the whole universe".

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- तुविशुष्म (*tuviśuṣma*)—one of exceeding strength
- तुविक्रतो (*tuvikrato*)—one of manifold wisdom
- शचीवः (*śacīvaḥ*)—one endowed with many kinds of deeds
- मते (*mate*)—and worshipful, O Indra
- विश्वया (*viśvayā*)—entire
- महित्वना (*mahitvanā*)—with your greatness
- आ पप्राथ (*ā paprātha*)—you have filled everywhere with your influence

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, one of exceeding strength, of manifold wisdom, endowed with many kinds of deeds and worshipful: with your entire greatness you have filled everywhere with your influence.

##### English translation
Great in power, rich in deeds, mighty one, adorable, you have filled (all things) with your universal majesty.

#### Ṛk 3

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> यस्य॑ ते महि॒ना म॒हः परि॑ ज्मा॒यंत॑मी॒यतुः॑ ।  
> हस्ता॒ वज्रं॑ हिर॒ण्यय॑म् ॥३॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> यस्य । ते । महिना । महः । परि । ज्मायंतम् । ईयतुः ।  
> हस्ता । वज्रम् । हिरण्ययम् ॥३

### Printed p. 5

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> महः महतः यस्य ते तव । यच्छब्दः प्रकृतपरामर्शकः । प्रकृतं तूक्तमृग्द्वयम् । तत्रत्यतुविकूर्मिमृतीषहमित्याद्युक्तलक्षणस्य तवेत्यर्थः । महिना महत्त्वेन हस्ता तव हस्ता हस्तौ ज्मायंतं पृथिव्यां सर्वतो व्याप्नुवंतं हिरण्ययं हिरण्मयं वज्रम् ईयतुः परिगृह्णीतः । सर्वदास्माकं भयनिवारणायेति भावः ।।

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

महः (*mahaḥ*) means "of the great"; of you, यस्य (*yasya*) ("whose"). The word "whose" refers back to what has gone before, namely the two preceding ṛks: it means of you who have the qualities stated there, beginning with "of many deeds, subduer of enemies". By your greatness, your two hands (हस्ता (*hastā*) is the dual) hold (ईयतुः (*īyatuḥ*) means "grasp") the golden thunderbolt (हिरण्ययं (*hiraṇyayaṃ*) means "made of gold") that pervades the earth on all sides (ज्मायंतं (*jmāyaṃtaṃ*)). The sense is: always to ward off fear from us.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- (O Indra) यस्य (*yasya*)—one possessing the qualities mentioned before
- महः (*mahaḥ*)—and great-souled
- ते (*te*)—your
- महिना (*mahinā*)—by greatness itself
- हस्ता (*hastā*)—your two hands
- परि ज्मायंतं (*pari jmāyaṃtaṃ*)—that which pervades everywhere
- हिरण्ययं (*hiraṇyayaṃ*)—and is made of gold
- वज्रं (*vajraṃ*)—the thunderbolt weapon
- ईयतुः (*īyatuḥ*)—hold

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, you who have the qualities described before and are great-souled: by your greatness itself your two hands hold the thunderbolt weapon, which pervades everywhere and is made of gold.

##### English translation
You mighty one, whose hands in your might grasp the all-pervading golden thunderbolt.

#### Ṛk 4

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> वि॒श्वान॑रस्य व॒स्पति॒मना॑नतस्य॒ शव॑सः ।  
> एवै॑श्च चर्षणी॒नामू॒ती हु॑वे॒ रथा॑नाम् ॥४॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> विश्वानरस्य । वः । पतिम् । अनानतस्य । शवसः ।  
> एवैः । च । चर्षणीनाम् । ऊती । हुवे । रथानाम् ॥४

### Printed p. 6

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> पृष्ठ्याभिप्लवषडहयोर्द्वितीयेऽहनि ‘विश्वानरस्य' इति प्रतिपत्तृचः । सूत्रितं च- ‘ विश्वानरस्य वस्पतिमिंद्र इत्सोमपा एक इति मरुत्वतीयस्य प्रतिपदनुचरौ' (आश्व. श्रौ. ७. ६ ) इति ।।
> विश्वानरस्य विश्वान् शत्रून् प्रत्यृतस्य अनानतस्य शत्रूणामप्रह्वस्य शवसः बलस्य पतिं स्वामिनमिंद्रं वा । अत्रेंद्रसंबंधिनो मरुतोऽपि संकीर्त्यंते । हे मरुतः वः। युष्माकमित्यर्थः । यद्यपि मरुत्संशब्दनं नास्ति तथापि व इति सामर्थ्याल्लभ्यते । युष्माकं चर्षणीनां सैनिकानाम् एवैः गमनैः सह । यद्वा चर्षणीनाम् इंद्रस्य सेनारूपाणां वो युष्माकं गमनैरिति सामानाधिकरण्यम् । युष्माकं रथानां च ऊती ऊतिभिर्गमनैश्च सह हुवे आह्वयामि । गंतृभी रथैर्गंतृभिर्मरुद्भिश्च सहेंद्रं हुव इत्यर्थः । यद्वा । हे यजमानाः युष्मदीयाः सैनिकाः सरथा यदा प्रविशंति युद्धाय संग्रामं तदानीं तेषां साहाय्यायेंद्रं हुव इत्यर्थः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

On the second day of the Pṛṣṭhya and the Abhiplava ṣaḍahas, the ṛk beginning विश्वानरस्य (*viśvānarasya*) is the pratipad-tṛca. It is laid down in the sūtra (Āśv. Śrau. 7. 6) **NOTE** that विश्वानरस्य वस्पतिमिंद्र इत्सोमपा एक इति मरुत्वतीयस्य प्रतिपदनुचरौ (*viśvānarasya vaspatimiṃdra itsomapā eka iti marutvatīyasya pratipadanucarau*).

Explanation: विश्वानरस्य (*viśvānarasya*) is of the one directed against all enemies; अनानतस्य (*anānatasya*), of the one that does not bow to enemies; शवसः (*śavasaḥ*), of strength; पतिं (*patiṃ*), the lord, Indra. Here the Maruts, connected with Indra, are also mentioned. O Maruts, वः (*vaḥ*) means "of you"; although the word Marut does not occur, "of you" yields it by implication. Of you, चर्षणीनां (*carṣaṇīnāṃ*), of the soldiers, with the goings (एवैः (*evaiḥ*)). Or else चर्षणीनाम् (*carṣaṇīnām*) stands in apposition with "of you": of you who are Indra's army, with your goings. And of your chariots, with the protections (ऊती (*ūtī*)) and goings: I invoke (हुवे (*huve*)). The meaning is: I invoke Indra together with the moving chariots and the moving Maruts. Or else: O sacrificers, when your soldiers enter the battle with their chariots, I invoke Indra for their help.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- (O Maruts) वः (*vaḥ*)—your
- चर्षणीनां (*carṣaṇīnāṃ*)—of the form of an army
- एवैः (*evaiḥ*)—accompanied by the goings
- रथानां (*rathānāṃ*)—of your chariots
- ऊती च (*ūtī ca*)—and accompanied by the protections, and
- विश्वानरस्य (*viśvānarasya*)—of that which destroys all enemy folk
- अनानतस्य (*anānatasya*)—that does not bow to any enemy
- शवसः (*śavasaḥ*)—of the strength
- पतिं (*patiṃ*)—the lord
- हुवे (*huve*)—I call with prayer, namely Indra

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Maruts, accompanied by your goings in the form of an army, accompanied by the protections of your chariots, I call with prayer Indra, the lord of the strength that destroys all enemy folk and does not bow to any enemy.

##### English translation
I invoke (Indra) the lord of that might which subdues all enemies and bows to none,—(I invoke him) followed by your onsets as his soldiers and (surrounded) by the protection of your chariots (O Maruts).

#### Ṛk 5

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> अ॒भिष्ट॑ये स॒दावृ॑धं॒ स्व॑र्मीळ्हेषु॒ यं नरः॑ ।  
> नाना॒ हवं॑त ऊ॒तये॑ ॥५॥

#### Ṛk 6

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> प॒रोमा॑त्र॒मृची॑षम॒मिंद्र॑मु॒ग्रं सु॒राध॑सम् ।  
> ईशा॑नं चि॒द्वसू॑नाम् ॥६॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> परःऽमात्रम् । ऋचीषमम् । इंद्रम् । उग्रम् । सुऽराधसम् ।  
> ईशानम् । चित् । वसूनाम् ॥६

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> परोमात्रम् । परा मात्रा यस्य तादृशम् । अथवा मीयत इति मात्रं दूरदेशः । ततः परस्ताद्वर्तमानमपरिमितस्वरूपम् । तथापि ऋचीषमम् ऋचा स्तुत्या समम् । यद्यप्यपरिच्छिन्नः तथापि स्तुतिर्यावन्मात्रं विषयीकरोति तत्सम इत्यर्थः । तदेवाह। इंद्रं परमैश्वर्ययोगादिच्छानुकुलस्वरूपम् उग्रम् उद्गूर्णबलं सुराधसम् । राध इति धननाम । शोभनधनं शोभनान्नं वा ईशानं चित् ईश्वरं च । केषाम् । वसूनाम् अस्मभ्यं प्रदेयानां गवादिधनानाम् । एवंमहानुभावमिंद्रं हुव इति शेषः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Indra is परोमात्रम् (*paromātram*): one whose measure is beyond, or else मात्र (*mātra*) is that which is measured, a distant place, and he is what lies beyond it, of unlimited nature. Yet he is ऋचीषमम् (*ṛcīṣamam*), equal to the ṛk, the praise: though unlimited, he is equal to as much as the praise can take as its object. He is Indra, fierce, of uplifted strength, सुराधसम् (*surādhasam*), one of excellent wealth (राध (*rādha*) is a name for wealth) or of excellent food, and the lord of the treasures such as cattle that are to be given to us. Such a glorious Indra I call.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- ऋचीषमं (*ṛcīṣamaṃ*)—who is worthy of the best praise
- उग्रं (*ugraṃ*)—who has exceeding strength
- सुराधसं (*surādhasaṃ*)—who has excellent wealth
- ईशानं चित् (*īśānaṃ cit*)—who is the lord of the whole universe
- इंद्रं (*iṃdraṃ*)—Indra
- वसूनां (*vasūnāṃ*)—for the sake of wealth (let us call with prayer)

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

Let us call with prayer, for the sake of wealth, Indra who is beyond measure, worthy of the best praise, of exceeding strength, of excellent wealth, and lord of the whole universe.

### Printed p. 9

##### English translation

I direct praise to him that he may drink the Soma. What is the gain? It is for great wealth, for the obtaining of abundant riches. He is नृतुः (*nṛtuḥ*), the god who leads to the fruit, and he rules, that is, is able to hear, the praise offered in due order, the foremost, standing at the head of the sacrifice, belonging to men, the priests. - चोदामि (*codāmi*)—I urge

##### English translation

> इंद्रोते एतन्नामक आतिथिग्वेऽतिथिग्वनाम्नो राज्ञः पुत्रे । ' अतिथिग्वाय शंबरम्' (ऋ. सं. १. ५१. ६)  ‘ अतिथिग्वाय शंस्यम्' (ऋ. सं. ६. २६. ३) इत्यादिष्वतिथिग्वः प्रसिद्धः । तत्पुत्र इंद्रोते ऋज़ौ ऋजुगामिनावश्वौ आ ददे स्वीकृतवानस्मि । तथा ऋक्षस्य सूनवि ऋक्षनाम्नः पुत्रेऽन्यस्मिन् राजनि हरी हरितवर्णावश्वौ आ ददे। तथा आश्वमेधस्य अश्वमेधपुत्रे राजनि रोहिता रोहितवर्णावश्वौ आ ददे। नन्वनुक्रमण्यामुभयोरेव दानप्रशंसारूपत्वमुक्तम् अत्र कथं त्रयाणां दानकीर्तनमिति । नैष दोषः । ऋक्षाश्वमेधपुत्रयोरेव यागेऽस्यर्षेः प्रवृत्तेस्तयोरेव दानं प्रस्तुतम्। इंद्रोतस्तु स्वपित्रा सह तयोर्यज्ञदिदृक्षयागत्य तयोर्दानं दृष्ट्वा स्वपित्रा प्रेरितो दत्तवानश्वौ। अतस्तद्दानं प्रासंगिकमिति । एवमृक्षाश्वमेधयोर्दानस्तुतित्वं न व्याहन्यते । पितृपुत्रयोरभेदात्तयोः पक्षकर्तृत्वाच्च। इंद्रोतदानस्थ प्रासंगिकत्वं ‘षळश्वान्' इत्यत्र विस्पष्टयिष्यते ॥
> [ xxv. 2 ]

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Indrota is the son of the king named Atithigva; Atithigva is famed in passages such as अतिथिग्वाय शंबरम् (*atithigvāya śaṃbaram*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1. 51. 6) and अतिथिग्वाय शंस्यम् (*atithigvāya śaṃsyam*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6. 26. 3). From him, Indrota, I have received two straight-going horses. Likewise from the son of Ṛkṣa, another king, I have received two bay horses; and from the king, the son of Aśvamedha, two roan horses. An objection is raised: the Anukramaṇī says that the praise of gifts concerns only the two; how then is the gift of three recorded here? Answer: this is no fault. The seer's engagement at the sacrifice was with the sons of Ṛkṣa and Aśvamedha, so only their gift is the subject. But Indrota came with his own father, desiring to see their sacrifice, saw their gift, and urged by his father gave horses; so his gift is incidental. Thus the character of the ṛks as praise of the gifts of Ṛkṣa and Aśvamedha is not contradicted, since father and son are not different and they are the patrons. The incidental character of Indrota's gift will be made clear at षळश्वान् (*ṣaḷaśvān*).

## Sūkta 69 (Maṇḍala 8)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> ‘प्रप्र वः' इत्यष्टादशर्चं दशमं सूक्तं प्रियमेधस्यांगिरसस्यार्षम् । द्वितीया ‘नदं वः' इत्येषा चतुःसप्तकोष्णिक् चतुर्थ्याद्यास्तिस्रो गायत्र्य एकादशीषोडश्यौ पंक्ती शिष्टा दशानुष्टुभः ।‘अपादिंद्रः इत्यर्धर्चो वैश्वदेवो ‘वरुण इदिह' इत्याद्यास्त्रयोऽर्धर्चा वरुणदेवताकाः शिष्टा ऐंद्र्यः । तथा चानुक्रांतं—‘प्रप्र द्व्यूनानुष्टुभं द्विबृहत्यंतं द्वितीयोष्णिक् चतुर्थ्याद्यास्तिस्रो गायत्र्यः षोळश्येकादश्यौ पंक्ती अपाद्वैश्वदेवोऽर्धर्चस्त्रयो वारुणाः' इति । आद्यस्तृचः षोडशिशस्त्र आनुष्टुभः । सूत्रितं 'च- ‘ प्रप्र वस्त्रिष्टुभमिषमर्चत प्रार्चत ' (आश्व. श्रौ. ६. २) इति ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The tenth sūkta, of eighteen ṛks, beginning प्रप्र वः (*prapra vaḥ*), has as seer Priyamedha of the line of Aṅgiras. The second ṛk, नदं वः (*nadaṃ vaḥ*), is in the metre called catuḥsaptaka uṣṇik; the three ṛks beginning with the fourth are gāyatrī; the eleventh and the sixteenth are paṅkti; the remaining ten are anuṣṭubh. The half-ṛk अपादिंद्रः (*apādiṃdraḥ*) is addressed to the Viśvedevas; the three half-ṛks beginning वरुण इदिह (*varuṇa idiha*) have Varuṇa as deity; the rest belong to Indra. So it is recorded in the Anukramaṇī, in the passage quoted below. The first tṛca is an anuṣṭubh verse in the Ṣoḍaśin śastra, as the sūtra states: प्रप्र वस्त्रिष्टुभमिषमर्चत प्रार्चत (*prapra vastriṣṭubhamiṣamarcata prārcata*) (Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra 6. 2). **NOTE**

The Anuvāda: this sūkta beginning प्रप्र वः (*prapra vaḥ*) is the tenth sūkta in the seventh anuvāka. It has eighteen ṛks. The seer of this sūkta is Priyamedha, the son of the sage Aṅgiras. In this sūkta the second ṛk, नदं वः (*nadaṃ vaḥ*), is of the metre called चतुःसप्तकोष्णिक् (*catuḥsaptakoṣṇik*); its characteristic is that it has four pādas with seven syllables in each pāda. The three ṛks following the fourth ṛk (ṛks 4 to 6) are in the gāyatrī metre. The eleventh and the sixteenth ṛks are in the paṅkti metre. The remaining ṛks are in the anuṣṭubh metre. The first half of the eleventh ṛk, beginning अपादिंद्रः (*apādiṃdraḥ*), has the Viśvedevas as deity; the latter half of this ṛk, beginning वरुण इदिह (*varuṇa idiha*), and both halves of the next ṛk (the twelfth ṛk) have Varuṇa as deity.

### Printed p. 23

The remaining ṛks have Indra as deity. In the Anukramaṇī it is said: प्रप्र द्व्यृनाऽनुष्टुभं द्विबृहत्यंतं द्वितीयोष्णिक् चतुर्थाद्यास्तिस्रो गायत्र्यः षोळश्येकादश्यौ पंक्ती अपाद्वैश्वदेवोऽर्धर्चस्त्रयो वारुणाः (*prapra dvyṛnā 'nuṣṭubhaṃ dvibṛhatyaṃtaṃ dvitīyoṣṇik caturthādyāstisro gāyatryaḥ ṣoḷaśyekādaśyau paṃktī apādvaiśvadevo 'rdharcastrayo vāruṇāḥ*) **GLOSS** **NOTE**. The first three ṛks of this sūkta are used for the ṛks in the anuṣṭubh metre in the mantras of the Ṣoḍaśin śastra; this is explained by the sūtra of the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra, प्रप्र वस्त्रिष्टुभमिषमर्चत प्रार्चते (*prapra vastriṣṭubhamiṣamarcata prārcate*) (Ā. 6-2).

##### Particulars

Maṇḍala 8; Anuvāka 7; Sūkta 69. Aṣṭaka 6; Adhyāya 5; Vargas 5, 6, 7. Number of ṛks in the sūkta: 18. Seer: Priyamedha. Deity: ṛks 1 to 10 and 13 to 18, Indra **NOTE**; of ṛk 11 the first half, the Viśvedevas; of ṛk 11 the second half, and ṛk 12, Varuṇa. ##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> प्रप्र॑ वस्त्रि॒ष्टुभ॒मिषं॑ मं॒दद्वी॑रा॒येंद॑वे ।  
> धि॒या वो॑ मे॒धसा॑तये॒ पुरं॒ध्या वि॑वासति ॥१॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> हे अध्वर्य्वादयः वः यूयम् । प्रथमार्थे द्वितीया । त्रिष्टुभं स्तोभत्रयोपेतम् इषम् अन्नं प्रप्र । अपरः प्रशब्दः पूरणः । प्रभरतेति शेषः । उपसर्गश्रुतेर्योग्यक्रियाध्याहारः । कस्मै । मंदद्वीराय । यो वीरान हर्षयति स मंदद्वीरः । तस्मै इंदवे इंद्राय । इंदतेरैश्वर्यकर्मणः इदं रूपम् । अथवा फलैर्वृष्टिभिर्वोनत्तीतींदुरिंद्रः । तस्मै । स चेंद्रः वः युष्मान मेधसातये यज्ञसंभजनाय पुरंध्या बहुप्रज्ञया धिया कर्मणा आ विवासति । अभिमतफलयोजनेन सत्करोतीत्यर्थः ।।

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Adhvaryus and the rest, you; the accusative stands in the sense of the nominative. Bring forth food त्रिष्टुभं (*triṣṭubhaṃ*), accompanied by three stobhas; the second प्र (*pra*) is an expletive, and the verb प्रभरत (*prabharata*) is to be supplied, a suitable verb being understood because the preverb is heard. For whom? For मंदद्वीराय (*maṃdadvīrāya*): he who gladdens heroes is मंदद्वीरः (*maṃdadvīraḥ*); for him, इंदवे (*iṃdave*), Indra; this form comes from the root इंद (*iṃda*) in the sense of lordship. Or else Indu is Indra because he moistens you with showers of rain-fruits. That Indra honours you, for the joining of the sacrifice, with action पुरंध्या (*puraṃdhyā*), full of much wisdom: he honours you by bestowing the desired fruit.

#### Ṛk 2

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> न॒दं व॒ ओद॑तीनां न॒दं योयु॑वतीनाम् ।  
> पतिं॑ वो॒ अघ्न्या॑नां धेनू॒नामि॑षुध्यसि ॥२॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> नदम् । वः । ओदतीनाम् । नदम् । योयुवतीनाम् ।  
> पतिम् । वः । अघ्न्यानाम् । धेनूनाम् । इषुध्यसि ॥२

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> महाव्रते निष्केवल्ये ‘नदं वः' इत्येषा विहरणीया । तथैव पंचमारण्यके सूत्रितं च -- नदं व ओदतीनामित्येतयैतानि व्यतिषजति ' ( ऐ. आ. ५. १. ६ ) इति ।।
> ओदतीनाम् । ओदत्य उषसः  ‘ ओदती भास्वती' इति तन्नामसु पाठात् । तासां नदम् । उत्पादकमित्यर्थः । इंद्रेण ह्युषस उत्पद्यंत इंद्रस्यैव सूर्यत्वात् । ' विवस्वदिंद्र सुग्म्य' इति द्वादशादित्यमध्य इंद्रः पठितः । तादृशमिंद्रं हे यजमानाः वः युष्मदर्थम् । आह्वयतेत्यर्थः । तथा योयुवतीनां सर्वत्र मिश्रयंतीनां नदीनां नदं' शब्दयितारं वः युष्मदर्थमाह्वयामि । अघ्न्यानाम् अहंतव्यानां गवां पतिम् आह्वये । अथ प्रत्यक्षकृता । हे यजमान त्वं धेनूनां क्षीरादिना प्रीणयित्रीणां गवाम् इषुध्यसि अन्नमिच्छसि ।।

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

In the Mahāvrata, in the Niṣkevalya śastra, the ṛk नदं वः (*nadaṃ vaḥ*) is to be interspersed; so it is laid down in the fifth Āraṇyaka: नदं व ओदतीनामित्येतयैतानि व्यतिषजति (*nadaṃ va odatīnāmityetayaitāni vyatiṣajati*) (Ai. Ā. 5. 1. 6). ओदतीनाम् (*odatīnām*): the ओदत्य (*odatya*) are the dawns, since the word is read among their names. Their नदम् (*nadam*) is their producer; for the dawns are produced by Indra, since Indra is the sun, and Indra is read among the twelve Ādityas, in विवस्वदिंद्र सुग्म्य (*vivasvadiṃdra sugmya*) **GLOSS**. O sacrificers, call such an Indra for yourselves. Likewise, of the rivers that mingle everywhere, योयुवतीनाम् (*yoyuvatīnām*), the sounder, I call for you. I call the lord of the अघ्न्यानाम् (*aghnyānām*), the cows that are not to be killed. Then, addressing the sacrificer directly: O sacrificer, you desire food for the milch cows, धेनूनाम् (*dhenūnām*), that gladden with milk and the like.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- (O sacrificers) ओदतीनां (*odatīnāṃ*)—of all the dawns
- नदं (*nadaṃ*)—Indra, the producer; call him, praying, for your own purpose
- वः (*vaḥ*)—for your purpose
- योयुवतीनां (*yoyuvatīnāṃ*)—of the rivers that flow everywhere
- नदं (*nadaṃ*)—Indra, the maker of the roaring sound; call him
- वः (*vaḥ*)—for your purpose
- अघ्न्यानां (*aghnyānāṃ*)—of all the cows that are not fit to be killed
- पतिं (*patiṃ*)—Indra, the lord; I shall call him for you (O sacrificer)
- धेनूनां (*dhenūnāṃ*)—provided by the milch cows that satisfy with milk and the like
- इषुध्यसि (*iṣudhyasi*)—you desire milk, food and the like

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O sacrificers, pray and call, for your own purpose, Indra, the producer of all the dawns. Call, for your own purpose, Indra, the maker of the sound that roars like the rivers that flow everywhere. I shall call for you the lord of all the cows that are not fit to be killed, Indra. O sacrificer, you desire the milk and food provided by the milch cows that satisfy with milk and the like.

##### English translation
(Invoke) for yourselves the author of the dawns, (I invoke) for you the roarer of the rivers ; (I invoke) for you the lord of the inviolable ones ; (O sacrificer), you desire cows.

> जन्मन् । देवानाम् । विशः । त्रिषु । आ । रोचने । दिवः ॥३

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> अग्निहोत्रे पूर्वाहुतौ हुतायां ‘ता अस्य' इत्यनयोत्तरामाहुतिं कांक्षमाणस्तिष्ठेत् । तथा च सूत्रितं -- ता अस्य सूददोहस इति पूर्वामाहुतिमुपोत्थायोत्तरां कांक्षेत ' ( आश्व. श्रौ. २. ३ ) इति । महाव्रते निष्केवल्येऽप्येषा । तथैव पंचमारण्यके सूत्रितं- ता अस्य सूददोहस इत्येतदादिः सूददोहाः सूददोहाः ' ( ऐ. आ. ५. १. ६ ) इति ॥
> ताः प्रसिद्धाः सूददोहसः । सूद इति कूपनाम । तत्सदृशदोहनाः पृश्नयः पृश्निवर्णा गावः अस्य इंद्रस्य सोमं श्रीणंति मिश्रयंत्याशिरेण । कदा । त्रिषु त्रिष्वपि सवनेषु । गावो विशेष्यंते । देवानां जन्मन् जन्मस्थाने । दिवीत्यर्थः । दिवः आदित्यस्य आ रोचने आरोचमाने । अनेन द्यौर्विशेष्यते । तस्मिन् स्थाने विशः निविशंत्यः । यज्ञार्थोपयुक्तानां गवां द्युप्राप्तिः प्रसिद्धा ।।

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

In the Agnihotra, when the first oblation has been offered, the sacrificer is to stand waiting for the second oblation, reciting this ṛk; so it is laid down in the sūtra ता अस्य सूददोहस इति पूर्वामाहुतिमुपोत्थायोत्तरां कांक्षेत (*tā asya sūdadohasa iti pūrvāmāhutimupotthāyottarāṃ kāṃkṣeta*) (Āśv. Śrau. 2. 3) **NOTE**. At the Mahāvrata, in the Niṣkevalya śastra, this ṛk is also used; and so it is laid down in the fifth Āraṇyaka: ता अस्य सूददोहस इत्येतदादिः सूददोहाः सूददोहाः (*tā asya sūdadohasa ityetadādiḥ sūdadohāḥ sūdadohāḥ*) (Ai. Ā. 5. 1. 6). Those well-known cows are सूददोहसः (*sūdadohasaḥ*); सूद (*sūda*) is a name of a well, and so their milking is like (that of) a well. The पृश्नयः (*pṛśnayaḥ*) are cows of variegated colour. They mix the Soma of this Indra with the milk-mixture (आशिर (*āśira*)). When? In all three libations. The cows are further described: in the birthplace of the gods, that is, in heaven, in the shining place of the sun, by which heaven is specified; in that place the cows are dwelling. That cows employed for the sacrifice reach heaven is well known.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

In the second paryāya, in the Maitrāvaruṇa śastra, अभि प्र गोपतिम् (*abhi pra gopatim*) is the anurūpa of a tṛca (a group of three ṛks). It is laid down in the sūtra: अभि त्वा वृषभा सुतेऽभि प्र गोपतिं गिरा (*abhi tvā vṛṣabhā sute 'bhi pra gopatiṃ girā*) (Āśv. Śrau. 6. 4) **NOTE**. Worship, with praise, Indra, the lord of cows; प्र (*pra*) means worship excellently. यथा विदे (*yathā vide*): worship in such a way that he knows himself and the manner of the praise, or knows that he must go to the sacrifice. What sort of Indra? The son of truth, or of the sacrifice (सत्यस्य यज्ञस्य सत्यस्य वा (*satyasya yajñasya satyasya vā*)); he is figuratively called son because of his attachment to it. सत्पतिं (*satpatiṃ*): the protector of the good sacrificers.

> यत् यदा ब्रध्नस्य विष्टपं सूर्यस्य स्थानं गृहमिंद्रश्च अहं चोभौ उत् गन्वहि उद्गच्छावः तदानीं मध्वः मधुरं सोमरसं पीत्वा सचेवहि संसृष्टौ भवेव । कुत्र । सख्युः सर्वेषां सखिभूतस्यादित्यस्य । त्रिः सप्त इत्यनेन देवलोकानामुत्तममेकविंशं स्थानमुच्यते आदित्यस्यैकविंशत्वात् । तथा च ब्राह्मणं- द्वादश मासाः पंचर्तवस्त्रय इमे लोका असावादित्य एकविंशः ' (ऐ. ब्रा. १. ३०) इति । तादृश एकविंशस्थाने सचेवहीति ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

उद्यद्ब्रध्नस्य (*udyadbradhnasya*) is the last ṛk of the Ṣoḍaśin śastra. It is laid down in the sūtra: उद्यद्ब्रध्नस्य विष्टपमित्येषा परिधानीया (*udyadbradhnasya viṣṭapamityeṣā paridhānīyā*) (Āśv. Śrau. 6. 2) **NOTE**. When Indra and I, both of us, go up to the home, the place of the sun (ब्रध्नस्य विष्टपं सूर्यस्य स्थानं (*bradhnasya viṣṭapaṃ sūryasya sthānaṃ*)), then, having drunk the sweet Soma juice, we two shall be united. Where? In the place of the friend of all, Āditya. By त्रिः सप्त (*triḥ sapta*) is meant the twenty-first place, the highest of the worlds of the gods, since Āditya is the twenty-first. So the Brāhmaṇa says: द्वादश मासाः पंचर्तवस्त्रय इमे लोका असावादित्य एकविंशः (*dvādaśa māsāḥ paṃcartavastraya ime lokā asāvāditya ekaviṃśaḥ*) (Ai. Brā. 1. 30). In such a twenty-first place we two shall be united.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

To explain the meaning of the word काकुदं (*kākudaṃ*), Yāska has given an explanation (nirvacana) of this ṛk: सुदेवस्त्वं कल्याणदेवः कमनीयदेवो वा भवसि वरुण यस्य ते सप्त सिंधवः । सिंधुः प्रवणात् यस्य ते सप्त स्रोतांसि तानि ते काकुदमनुस्यक्ष-रंति सूर्मिः कल्याणोर्मिः स्रोतः सुषिरमनु यथा ॥ (*sudevastvaṃ kalyāṇadevaḥ kamanīyadevo vā bhavasi varuṇa yasya te sapta siṃdhavaḥ | siṃdhuḥ pravaṇāt yasya te sapta srotāṃsi tāni te kākudamanusyakṣa-raṃti sūrmiḥ kalyāṇormiḥ srotaḥ suṣiramanu yathā ‖*) (Ni. 5-27 **NOTE**).
O Varuṇa, you are सुदेव (*sudeva*), that is, a god who brings welfare, who does good to all, a bringer of auspiciousness; or a god desired (charming) by all. To the root of the tongue of such a you the seven famous rivers (Gaṅgā and the rest) flow and fall. How? As flowing water goes naturally to a hollow, that is, to a low region, and falls there, so the seven famous rivers go to the root of your tongue (that is, to the ocean) and fall. Here the word सिंधु (*siṃdhu*) means a flowing river; that is, because it has the nature of flowing, a river is called सिंधु (*siṃdhu*). सूर्मि (*sūrmi*) means having good waves, that is, a stream with plentiful water, as it rushes into a low region; thus an illustration is given for the word सुषिरां (*suṣirāṃ*).

> यः इंद्रः व्यतीन् विविधगमनान् ‘सुयुक्तान् सुष्ठु रथे संबद्धानश्वान् दाशुषे हविर्दात्रे यजमानाय गंतुं प्राप्तुम् उप अफाणयत् उपगमयति । फणतिर्गतिकर्मा । यदैवं करोति तदित् तदानीमेव तक्वः । तकतिर्गतिकर्मा । यज्ञगमनशीलः नेता उदकस्य फलस्य वा नायक इंद्रः वपुः उदकमुत्पादयतीति शेषः । यः इंद्रः उपमा उपमानभूतः अमुच्यत अन्यैर्वृष्टिनिवारकैरसुरादिभिर्मुक्तो भवति ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

In the Ṣoḍaśin śastra, यो व्यतीन् (*yo vyatīn*) is the third tṛca in Anuṣṭubh metre. It is laid down in the sūtra: यो व्यतीरफाणयदिति तृचा आनुष्टुभाः (*yo vyatīraphāṇayaditi tṛcā ānuṣṭubhāḥ*) (Āśv. Śrau. 6. 2) **NOTE**. The Indra who makes his व्यतीन् (*vyatīn*), horses of various movements, well yoked to the chariot, go (उप अफाणयत् (*upa aphāṇayat*)) to the sacrificer who gives the oblation, फणति (*phaṇati*) being a root of motion; just then that तक्वः (*takvaḥ*), तकति (*takati*) being a root of motion, that is, he who is given to going to the sacrifice, the leader (नेता (*netā*)) of the water or of the fruit, Indra, produces the वपुः (*vapuḥ*), the water; this is the word to be supplied. उपमा (*upamā*): the Indra who is the standard of comparison is अमुच्यत (*amucyata*), becomes freed from the other demons and the like who hold back the rain.

## Sūkta 70 (Maṇḍala 8)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> अष्टमेऽनुवाक एकादश सूक्तानि । तत्र  ‘ यो राजा ' इति पंचदशर्चं प्रथम सूक्तम् । अत्रानुक्रमणिका-- ' यो राजा पंचोना पुरुहन्मा बार्हतं त्रिप्रगाथाद्युष्णिगनुष्टुप्पुरउष्णिगंतम्' इति । पुरुहन्मा ऋषिः । स च क्वचित् कथंचिदविशेषितम्' (अनु. २, ३) इति परिभाषयांगिरसः । आद्यातृतीयापंचम्यो बृहत्यः। द्वितीयाचतुर्थीषष्ठ्यः सतोबृहत्यः । सप्तम्याद्याः षट् बृहत्यः । त्रयोदश्युष्णिक् चतुर्दश्यनुष्टुप् पंचदशी पुरउष्णिगाद्यद्वादशका द्व्यष्टका । ' आद्यश्चेत्पुरउष्णिक्' (अनु. ५.२ ) इति हि तल्लक्षणम् । इंद्रो देवता। महाव्रते निष्केवल्ये बार्हततृचाशीतावादित एकादशर्चः । तथा च पंचमारण्यके सूत्रितं -- यो राजा चर्षणीनामित्येकादश' (ऐ. आ. ५. २. ४) इति ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The eighth anuvāka has eleven sūktas. Of them the first is the one of fifteen ṛks beginning यो राजा (*yo rājā*). The Anukramaṇī speaks of it in the passage quoted in the Anuvāda below. The seer is Puruhanman; by the rule of the Anukramaṇī that an unspecified seer is of the line of Aṅgiras, he is an Āṅgirasa. The first, third and fifth ṛks are bṛhatī; the second, fourth and sixth are satobṛhatī; the six from the seventh on are bṛhatī; the thirteenth is uṣṇik, the fourteenth anuṣṭubh, and the fifteenth puraḥ-uṣṇik; the first twelve are of eight syllables each, in two groups of four, since the definition is that when the first pāda is of twelve syllables the metre is puraḥ-uṣṇik. The deity is Indra. In the Mahāvrata, in the Niṣkevalya śastra, the first eleven ṛks are used as the bṛhatī ṛks, and so the fifth Āraṇyaka of the Aitareya Āraṇyaka lays down: यो राजा चर्षणीनामित्येकादश (*yo rājā carṣaṇīnāmityekādaśa*) (Ai. Ā. 5-2-4). **NOTE**

The Anuvāda: the eighth anuvāka of the eighth maṇḍala has eleven sūktas. Of them this sūkta beginning यो राजा (*yo rājā*) is the first. It has fifteen ṛks. In the Anukramaṇī it is said: यो राजा पंचोना पुरुहन्मा जार्पतं त्रिप्रगाथाद्युष्णिगनुष्टुप्पुरउष्णिगंतं (*yo rājā paṃconā puruhanmā jārpataṃ tripragāthādyuṣṇiganuṣṭuppurauṣṇigaṃtaṃ*) **GLOSS** **NOTE**. The seer of this sūkta is Puruhanman; it is said in the Anukramaṇī (Anu. 2-3) **NOTE** that this seer belongs to the Āṅgirasa lineage. The first, third and fifth ṛks of this sūkta are in the bṛhatī metre. The second, fourth and sixth ṛks are in the satobṛhatī metre. The six ṛks from the seventh on are in the bṛhatī metre. The thirteenth is of the metre uṣṇik, the fourteenth anuṣṭubh, the fifteenth puraḥ-uṣṇik. Its definition is that if a ṛk has three pādas, and the first pāda has twelve syllables and the remaining pādas eight syllables each,

the metre is called puraḥ-uṣṇik. Indra is the deity. When the Niṣkevalya śastra mantras are recited in the Mahāvrata sacrifice, the first eleven ṛks of this sūkta are used for the ṛks of the bṛhatī metre; this is stated in the fifth Āraṇyaka of the Aitareya Āraṇyaka by the sentence यो राजा चर्षणीनामित्येकादश (*yo rājā carṣaṇīnāmityekādaśa*) (Ai.
