Sāyaṇa has said that the meaning of the lamentation sentence of the present Ṛk, न विजानामि (*na vijānāmi*), is that those who, without reflection and profound meditation, are content merely with hearing the śruti sentence remain in a state of doubt, without complete Brahman-knowledge. “I know only the recitation of the mantras of the various scriptures. Without knowledge of the principle of the Self there is no release from the sorrow of saṃsāra”: this lamenting was cited earlier in the Special Topics of the 5th Ṛk of this same sūkta (p. 230). This kind of state of doubt and this distress are expressed by निण्यः संनद्दो मनसा चरामि (*niṇyaḥ saṃnaddo manasā carāmi*) *[as supplied: the word is printed संनद्दो (*saṃnaddo*) here and संनद्धो (*saṃnaddho*) on p. 552]*. Explaining it, Sāyaṇa says अविद्याकामकर्मभिः सम्यग्बद्धो वेष्टितः । आत एव भावनासहिष्णुना बहिर्मुखेन विक्षिप्तेन चेतसा युक्तः संचरामि संसारे । इंद्रियपरवश एव सन् संसारे दुःखमनुभवामि (*avidyākāmakarmabhiḥ samyagbaddho veṣṭitaḥ | āta eva bhāvanāsahiṣṇunā bahirmukhena vikṣiptena cetasā yuktaḥ saṃcarāmi saṃsāre | iṃdriyaparavaśa eva san saṃsāre duḥkhamanubhavāmi*). The purport, he has stated, is: bound by ignorance and by the actions that arise from it and are accompanied by desire, attachment, aversion and the like, and without inner meditation, possessed of a mind given to outward tendencies, I experience the sorrow of saṃsāra. Here,
निण्यः (*niṇyaḥ*)—the word means “covered by ignorance”. Since निण्यं (*niṇyaṃ*), सस्वः (*sasvaḥ*), सनुतः (*sanutaḥ*) and so on are read among the six names of the concealed (Ni. 3-19), the use of the word निण्य (*niṇya*) may be seen in many places in the Ṛk-saṃhitā in contexts that give it the meaning “hidden”. For example—
कविर्न निण्यं विदथानि साधन्‌वृषा यत्सेकं विपिपानो अर्चात् । दिव इत्था जीजनत्सप्त कारूनह्ना चिच्च क्रुर्वयुना गृणंतः (*kavirna niṇyaṃ vidathāni sādhanvṛṣā yatsekaṃ vipipāno arcāt | diva itthā jījanatsapta kārūnahnā cicca krurvayunā gṛṇaṃtaḥ*)
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-16-3)
Here the meaning is that which is visible only to the vision of the poet and, not visible to ordinary intelligence, remains secret. In some other contexts the word is used, when the investigation of a principle is under way, to indicate the agitation of the mind when the principle is very hidden and the problem very complex and entangled.
