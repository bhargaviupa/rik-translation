**प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)**

वृषण् (*vṛṣaṇ*)—showerers of desired objects; वसू (*vasū*)—(of the people) givers of dwelling-places, or possessors of wealth; रुद्रा (*rudrā*)—those who drive away (destroy) sorrow and sin, or those who make a fearful noise (in battle); पुरुमंतू (*purumaṃtū*)—knowers of many things; वृधंता (*vṛdhaṃtā*)—those who increase (through praise and the like); अभिष्टौ (*abhiṣṭau*)—those who satisfy by giving desired objects, or those who are worshipped face to face; दस्रा (*dasrā*)—those who perform deeds of valour such as destroying (enemies): O Aśvin gods! यत् (*yat*)—for which reason; औचथ्यः (*aucathyaḥ*)—Dīrghatamas, son of Ucathya; वां (*vāṃ*)—from you; रेक्ष्णः (*rekṣṇaḥ*)—(with praise) asks wealth) or [(this sacrificer); यत् (*yat*)—for which reason; रेक्ष्णः (*rekṣṇaḥ*)—the food in the form of oblation; वां (*vāṃ*)—offers to you)] *[the brackets and parentheses are unbalanced in the supplied text and are kept as supplied]* (and for whatever reason you) अकवाभिः (*akavābhiḥ*)—by unobjectionable, that is, excellent; ऊती (*ūtī*)—protections; प्र सस्राथे (*pra sasrāthe*)—spread (your favour) extensively and liberally (for that reason); नः (*naḥ*)—to us; दशस्यतं (*daśasyataṃ*)—give all that is desired.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

O Aśvin gods, showerers of our desired objects, givers of dwelling-places, destroyers of sorrow and sin, knowers of many things, those who increase through praise and the like, those who satisfy by giving desired objects, and performers of deeds of valour such as destroying enemies! Because I, Dīrghatamas, son of Ucathya (printed "ಉಚಧ್ಯ", as supplied), praise you and desire wealth from you, and because you too spread your favour liberally with your excellent protections, graciously grant us everything that is desired. *[The Bhāvārtha is broken across the page boundary inside the word ಇಷ್ಟಾರ್ಥ|ಗಳನ್ನು; read together here.]*

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

तुष्टाव चैव सूक्ताभ्यां अबोधीत्यश्विनावृषिः । (*tuṣṭāva caiva sūktābhyāṃ abodhītyaśvināvṛṣiḥ |*) (Bṛ. Ve. 4-26) *[reference as supplied; the abbreviation "ಬೃ. ವೇ." is not expanded here]*

Dīrghatamas, helped by the gods, composed the sūkta beginning with the ṛk अबोध्यग्निर्ज (*abodhyagnirja*) *[as supplied]* (1-157) and the present sūkta (1-158), and praised the Aśvin gods: so runs the sentence of the Bṛhaddevatā. In the Bṛhaddevatā the hardship that first befell Dīrghatamas is described, and how the gods removed it; then it is told that the seer praised Agni and the other gods. The birth of Dīrghatamas from Mamatā, wife of Ucathya, the outrage that befell Mamatā during her pregnancy, and the trouble that thereby came to Dīrghatamas, were told earlier, in another context, incidentally and briefly. Even so, here, in the context in which the seer's own work and its distinctive character are directly set forth, since he himself says औचथ्यः (*aucathyaḥ*) ("son of Ucathya"), the story of his birth as Ucathya's son is told as stated in the Bṛhaddevatā *[the Kannada has "ಉಚಥ್ಯಭಾರೈಯಾದ ಮಮತೆಯಲ್ಲಿ", "Mamatā, wife (printed ಭಾರೈ) of Ucathya", as supplied]*:

द्वावुचथ्यबृहस्पती ऋषिपुत्रौ बभूवतुः । (*dvāvucathyabṛhaspatī ṛṣiputrau babhūvatuḥ |*)
आसीदुचथ्यभार्या तु ममता नाम भार्गवी ॥ (*āsīducathyabhāryā tu mamatā nāma bhārgavī ‖*)
तां कनीयान्बृहस्पतिर्मैथुनायोपचक्रमे । (*tāṃ kanīyānbṛhaspatirmaithunāyopacakrame |*)
शुक्रस्योत्सर्गकाले तु गर्भस्तं प्रत्यभाषत ॥ (*śukrasyotsargakāle tu garbhastaṃ pratyabhāṣata ‖*)
इहास्मि पूर्वसंभूतो न कार्यः शुक्रसंकरः । (*ihāsmi pūrvasaṃbhūto na kāryaḥ śukrasaṃkaraḥ |*)
तच्चुक्तप्रतिषेधं तु न ममर्ष बृहस्पतिः ॥ (*taccuktapratiṣedhaṃ tu na mamarṣa bṛhaspatiḥ ‖*)
स व्याजहार त गर्भं तमस्ते दीर्घतमस्त्विति । (*sa vyājahāra ta garbhaṃ tamaste dīrghatamastviti |*)
स च दीर्घतमा नाम बभूवर्षिरुचथ्यजः ॥ (*sa ca dīrghatamā nāma babhūvarṣirucathyajaḥ ‖*)

*Sense (from the Sanskrit; the supplied text gives no Kannada rendering of these lines):* Ucathya and Bṛhaspati were two sons of a seer. Ucathya's wife was Mamatā, of the Bhārgava line. The younger one, Bṛhaspati, approached her for union. At the time of the emission of seed the child in the womb spoke to him: "I am here, first come; the mixing of seed must not be done." Bṛhaspati did not tolerate that prohibition of the seed, and spoke to the child in the womb with words containing "tamas … dīrghatamas" (the line reads "ಸ ವ್ಯಾಜಹಾರ ತ ಗರ್ಭಂ", with ತ as supplied, apparently a misreading). And he became the seer named Dīrghatamas, born of Ucathya. *[Text ends at the bottom of printed p. 4 with this verse; the passage may continue on p. 5.]*

There were two brothers, Ucathya and Bṛhaspati. Ucathya had a wife named Mamatā. While she was pregnant, Bṛhaspati, the younger brother of Ucathya, had intercourse with her. At the time of the emission of seed, the child within prayed that the emission of Bṛhaspati's seed should be held back. Angered by this, Bṛhaspati cursed the child in the womb that it should be born blind from birth. Because the child was born with a long darkness, it was named Dīrghatamas. When the seer's son, filled with darkness, performed austerity and won over the gods, they showed favour and made him gain sight. At once he became the seer of some sūktas. Thus (the passage) tells of the composition of the sūktas addressed to Agni and the other deities, and then continues the story that took place in the time of his blindness.

(Bṛhaddevatā 4-21 to 25)

Because Dīrghatamas remained blind for a very long time, his attendants, wearied of attending on him, bound him, worn out as he was, and threw him into the water of a river. Among them, one named Traitana, going to strike him with his sword, split his own head and arms himself. Thus that servant, who underwent the atonement for his own sin, Dīrghatamas killed outright, and, though bewildered by the force of the water, he tried to stretch out his hands and feet. Then the river current pushed him to the border of the neighbouring country of Aṅga and threw him on the bank. *[The sentence runs over the page break: "…pushed him … to the bank".]* The king of Aṅga sent a woman named Uśik, who was with him, to Dīrghatamas, desiring a son.

Dīrghatamas, who had come out of the water, was pleased with her service and affection and caused sages such as Kakṣīvān to be born of her. Having told that story, (the Bṛhaddevatā) then tells that he composed sūktas addressed to the Aśvin deities and prayed to those gods in the present ṛks.

By the epithets वसू (*vasū*), रुद्रा (*rudrā*), पुरुमंतू (*purumaṃtū*), वृधंता (*vṛdhaṃtā*), वृषण् (*vṛṣaṇ*) and so on, the seer describes the nature of the Aśvin deities.

वसू (*vasū*)—वासयितार् प्रजानां । वसुना धनेन तद्वंतौ ना । (*vāsayitār prajānāṃ | vasunā dhanena tadvaṃtau nā |*) It is shown that this can be understood in two ways: as those who provide dwelling-places to the devotee people, or as those who possess wealth.

त्रिर्नो रयिं वहतमश्विना युवं त्रिर्देवताता त्रिरुतावतं धियः । (*trirno rayiṃ vahatamaśvinā yuvaṃ trirdevatātā trirutāvataṃ dhiyaḥ |*)
त्रिः सौभगत्वं त्रिरुत श्रवांसि नस्तिष्ठं वां सूरे दुहिता रुहद्रथं ॥ (*triḥ saubhagatvaṃ triruta śravāṃsi nastiṣṭhaṃ vāṃ sūre duhitā ruhadrathaṃ ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-34-5)

In such ṛks it is prayed that they should provide all wealth, food and the rest; and it is also suggested that they are the support of every kind of wealth. That they gave shelter to devotees such as Bhujyu, who were without refuge and had fallen into sorrow, and brought them to safe houses and the like, is described in many places (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-116-3 and others). Therefore whichever meaning is given to this epithet, it fits.

रुद्रा (*rudrā*)—रुत् दुःखं तद्भेतुभूतं पापं वा । तस्य द्रावयितारौ । संग्रामे (*rut duḥkhaṃ tadbhetubhūtaṃ pāpaṃ vā | tasya drāvayitārau | saṃgrāme*) भयंकरं शब्दयंत् वा । (*bhayaṃkaraṃ śabdayaṃt vā |*) *rut* means either sorrow or the sin that is its cause; (the word) is shown to be interpretable in one way as "those who destroy that sorrow or sin", or in another way as "those who make a fearful noise on the field of battle". And, quoting Yāska's sentence रुद्रो रौतीति सतः इति यास्कः । (*rudro rautīti sataḥ iti yāskaḥ |*) (*sic* ಸತಃ, as supplied; "ದಹರಿಸಿ" printed for "ಉದಹರಿಸಿ"), he has also given the derivation of this word. The author of the Nirukta, quoting the Kāṭhaka sentence and the Hāridravika sentence,

यदरुदत्तद्रस्य रुद्रत्वं (इति काठकं) । यवरोदीत्तद्रुद्रस्य रुद्रत्वं (*yadarudattadrasya rudratvaṃ (iti kāṭhakaṃ) | yavarodīttadrudrasya rudratvaṃ*)
(इति हारिद्रविकं) ॥ (नि. १०-७) (*(iti hāridravikaṃ) ‖ (ni. 10-7)*)

(Ni. 10-7)

has shown that the name Rudra became famous from making sound in a roaring noise. That both qualities, the destroying of sorrow and the making of sound, are present in the Aśvin deities has been shown earlier with examples. *[The printed "ಅಶ್ವಿನೀವೇವತೆಗಳಲ್ಲಿ" is kept as supplied.]* Or,

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 5-75-3)

उत नो रुद्रा चिन्मृळतामश्विना विश्वे देवासो रथस्पतिर्भगः । (*uta no rudrā cinmṛḷatāmaśvinā viśve devāso rathaspatirbhagaḥ |*)
ऋभुर्वाज ऋभुक्षणः परिज्मा विश्ववेदसः ॥ (*ṛbhurvāja ṛbhukṣaṇaḥ parijmā viśvavedasaḥ ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-93-7)

In such ṛks Sāyaṇa has interpreted the word रुद्रा (*rudrā*) as "the two sons of Rudra, the Aśvins" (रुद्रपुत्रौ अश्विन् (*rudraputrau aśvin*)). Along with it he has said that it may also mean स्तुत्या वा (*stutyā vā*), that is, "those worthy of praise". And since the word *rudra* is read among the names of praise (Ni. 3-19), he may have given the sense "worthy of praise" here. Therefore in the present ṛk there is room for all these meanings.

पुरुमंतू (*purumaṃtū*)—बहूनां ज्ञातारौ (*bahūnāṃ jñātārau*)—the meaning is: those with many kinds of knowledge. The wisdom of the Aśvin deities is highly praiseworthy. Therefore,

मेधां मे अश्विनावुभावाधत्तां । (*medhāṃ me aśvināvubhāvādhattāṃ |*)

(Trai. U. 4-16-5) *[abbreviation "ತ್ರೈ. ಉ." as supplied, not expanded]*

it is prayed: "May they bestow on me excellent wisdom." Also in the Ṛk-saṃhitā,

आ नूनं यातमश्विना रथेन सूरत्वचा । (*ā nūnaṃ yātamaśvinā rathena sūratvacā |*)
भुजी हिरण्यपेशसा कवी गंभीरचेतसा ॥ (*bhujī hiraṇyapeśasā kavī gaṃbhīracetasā ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 8 8-2) *[reference as supplied]*

in this ṛk the Aśvin deities are praised by the epithet गंभीरचेतसा (*gaṃbhīracetasā*). For this epithet Sāyaṇa has given the meaning प्रशस्तज्ञानौ (*praśastajñānau*), "having excellent knowledge".

रेक्षः (*rekṣaḥ*)—रेक्ष इति धननाम । रिच्यते प्रयतः । (*rekṣa iti dhananāma | ricyate prayataḥ |*) The authors of the Nirukta explain that wealth is called *rekṣaḥ* because, left behind by one who has died, it becomes a means of happiness to those who remain (Ni. 3-2). In the present ṛk Sāyaṇa says that this word may mean wealth or food in the form of oblation. If it is understood as wealth, स्तुतिव्याजेन प्रार्थयते (*stutivyājena prārthayate*), "he prays by means of praise"; and if it is understood as food in the form of oblation, हविर्लक्षणमन्नं (*havirlakṣaṇamannaṃ*) वां दास्यति ददातीति शेषः । (*vāṃ dāsyati dadātīti śeṣaḥ |*) — he explains that the purport is: "he will offer this food to you".

अकवाभिः (*akavābhiḥ*)—अकुत्सितैः (*akutsitaiḥ*) is explained as "not blameworthy, that is, not faulty", ऊतिभिः (*ūtibhiḥ*) "by protections"; the meaning is "worthy of praise". दस्रा (*dasrā*)—अस्मद्विरोध्युपक्षपयितार् (*asmadvirodhyupakṣapayitār*) "those who harm and destroy our enemies": thus it describes the nature of the Aśvin deities. It was shown earlier that this word can have two meanings: destroyers of enemies, or those of beautiful form.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

O Aśvin deities, who provide dwelling-places! With the intention that you must do good to us, you stand at the place of the venerable altar and hold a gracious mind that gives us abundantly wealth and the like in return for the small oblation we offer. Therefore, moving with a mind set on fulfilling all our desires, you give us cows that possess milk and other wealth and are the cause of the growth of the body. Which sacrificer indeed can offer you oblation so abundantly as to satisfy such an excellent, gracious mind of yours? You must be satisfied with our small offering itself.

**Printed English translation**

Granters of dwellings, who may make you offerings so propitiatory of that your good will, that (invoked) by our adoration, to the site of the altar, you may entertain (favourable intentions towards us), and grant us cows rich in milk, coming with minds made up to grant the desires (of the worshipper).

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

गोः पदे (*goḥ pade*)—भूम्याः सर्वैर्गंतव्यायाः स्तुत्याया वा वेदिरूपायाः (*bhūmyāḥ sarvairgaṃtavyāyāḥ stutyāyā vā vedirūpāyāḥ*) स्थाने । (*sthāne |*) This is explained as "on the earth in the form of the altar". One should first understand how the word गो (*go*) comes to have the name "earth", and then the identity (*tādātmya*) of the earth and the altar. The authors of the Nirukta have included the word गो (*go*) among the names of the earth (Ni. 2-5) and have then given its derivation.

Because it is formed from the root गम् (*gam*) or गा (*gā*) with the above sense, this name is well known also for the animal; and saying आदित्योऽपि गौरुच्यते, सर्वॆऽपि (*ādityo 'pi gaurucyate, sarvè 'pi*) रश्मयो गाव उच्यंते । (*raśmayo gāva ucyaṃte |*) they show that the word गो (*go*) is used also in the senses sun, ray and so on. In sentences like this the identity of the earth and the altar is stated. And when Śaṅkarācārya wrote the bhāṣya on the sentence होता वेदिषत् (*hotā vediṣat*) of the Karopaniṣad *[so printed]* (5-2), he wrote:

इयं वेदिः परोऽंतः पृथिव्याः । या यज्ञे प्रसिद्धा इयं वेदिः पृथिव्याः (*iyaṃ vediḥ paro 'ṃtaḥ pṛthivyāḥ | yā yajñe prasiddhā iyaṃ vediḥ pṛthivyāḥ*)

there is a śruti sentence itself. In the present ṛk Sāyaṇa has explained it as "on the earth in the form of the altar, easily approached by all and worthy of praise".

नमसा (*namasā*)—हविर्लक्षणेन अन्नेन । (*havirlakṣaṇena annena |*) "By food in the form of oblation." The word नमः (*namaḥ*) is read among the names of food (Ni. 3-9) and among the names of the thunderbolt (Ni. 3-11), and in the Ṛk-saṃhitā this word is used in both these senses, besides the established sense of salutation.

The word पुरंधि (*puraṃdhi*) means "one with abundant intelligence (*dhī*)". The question is: who is such a one? **भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

My servants threw me, whose body was decrepit with old age, face downwards into the river. Knowing that I was helpless, may the rivers, which are most beneficial to the world like a mother, not make me sink. **विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

The application (*viniyoga*) of this fifth ṛk, नमा गरन् (*namā garan*) *[so printed]*, is stated by the great sage Śaunaka in his work called Ṛgvidhāna as follows: if a murderous and violent man, a tiger or a wolf comes face to face during a journey, then, if this mantra न मा गरन् (*na mā garan*) is chanted, the fear that might arise from them is removed. "Three nights" means: fast for three days, begin to chant this mantra at night and continue chanting until sunrise, then bathe in a river, stand facing the sun and praise it; then thieves and sinful men do not come near such a man, and when he travels on the road such a man alone can protect even a hundred people who are with him: this is stated in Ṛgvidhāna 1-25. Here, for the word आततायि (*ātatāyi*),

अग्निदो गरदश्चैव शस्त्रोन्मत्तो धनापहः । (*agnido garadaścaiva śastronmatto dhanāpahaḥ |*)
क्षेत्रदारहरश्चैतान् षड्विद्यादाततायिन ॥ (*kṣetradāraharaścaitān ṣaḍvidyādātatāyina ‖*)

(Śukranīti, printed "ಶುಕ್ರಸೀತಿ" *[so printed]*)

there is the following explanation in the Śukranīti: one who sets fire to a house, one who kills by giving poison, one who holds a sharp weapon in his hand and causes fear to others, one who seizes the wealth of others, one who deceitfully seizes land such as paddy-fields, fields and gardens, and one who abducts another man's wife: these six are called *ātatāyins*. Śaunaka has said that if one sees such sinners and recites the ṛk न मा गरन् (*na mā garan*), that sin is removed.

सुसमुब्धं (*susamubdhaṃ*)—सुष्टु संकुचितसर्वांगं । (*suṣṭu saṃkucitasarvāṃgaṃ |*) This is explained as "having a body made contracted". Whether it is contracted because it is worn out by old age, or because the servants tied his hands and feet, is not explained. It is also difficult to say from which root this word is formed. The scholar Monier Williams, in his Sanskrit-English Dictionary, explains it as सम्+उभ् (उंभाति) (*sam+ubh (uṃbhāti)*) "to cover up, confined", saying that it is formed from the root उभ् (*ubh*). With this help, Sāyaṇa's word "contracted" (संकुचित (*saṃkucita*))

means "having a body made contracted because the servants bound it". But the scholar Wilson has translated this word as "decrepit"; then it must be understood that the body is worn out by old age and contracted. In the Ṛk-saṃhitā, apart from this ṛk, the word is used in two other places: in one place merely as उब्बं (*ubbaṃ*) and in another as समुब्धं (*samubdhaṃ*).

(ಮ್ಯ. ಸಂ. 5-2-1) *[the printed abbreviation "ಮ್ಯ. ಸಂ." is kept; probably Ṛ. Saṃ., not expanded]*

here too, for the word समुब्बं (*samubbaṃ*) *[so printed]* he gives first चक्रेण हतं (*cakreṇa hataṃ*) and the second time निगूढं (*nigūḍhaṃ*). So Sāyaṇa too has not given an exact meaning. In the present ṛk the contraction of the body is the purport; it may be due to old age or to binding.

नद्यः (*nadyaḥ*)—नद्यः कस्मान्नदना इमा भवंति शब्दवत्यः । (*nadyaḥ kasmānnadanā imā bhavaṃti śabdavatyaḥ |*) (Ni. 2-24) The word is formed from the root नद् (*nad*), which gives the sense "to make a sound". **प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)**

ऋतावृधा (*ṛtāvṛdhā*)—those who make the sacrifice increase; मही (*mahī*)—those of great form; विदथेषु (*vidatheṣu*)—in sacrifices; प्रचेतसा (*pracetasā*)—who give us consciousness, or who are to be worshipped by us with faith; द्यावापृथिवी (*dyāvāpṛthivī*)—the deities Heaven and Earth; यज्ञैः (*yajñaiḥ*)—with oblations and the like, which are the means of sacrifice, or for the sake of offering oblations; स्तुषे (*stuṣe*)—I praise. देवपुत्रे (*devaputre*)—having the sacrificers themselves as sons, or having the gods as sons. ये (*ye*)—which Heaven and Earth; देवेभिः (*devebhiḥ*)—performed by the sacrificers; सुदंससा (*sudaṃsasā*)—endowed with excellent works; इत्था (*itthā*)—certainly; धिया (*dhiyā*)—with a gracious mind; वार्याणि (*vāryāṇi*)—the desired oblations; प्रभूषतः (*prabhūṣataḥ*)—adorn, that is, accept. **भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

I praise and pray, for the sake of offerings of oblations and the like, to Heaven and Earth, who make the sacrifice increase, who have a great form and are praised in the sacrifices. Heaven and Earth, who have the gods themselves as sons, endowed with the excellent rites performed by the sacrificers, fulfil the desired objects of the sacrificers with their gracious mind.

**Printed English translation**

*[Printed heading "English Tranlation" [sic]; the Latin text breaks "augment-ers" over a line, joined here.]*

I glorify with sacred rites the mighty Heaven and Earth, the augmenters of sacrifice, who (are to be contemplated) with devotion at holy ceremonies; those two, who, cherishing their worshippers as children, are venerated by the devout, and therefore with favour bestow (upon us) desirable blessings.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

द्यावापृथिवी (*dyāvāpṛthivī*)—Except for one sūkta, beginning with the ṛk बळित्था पर्वतानां (*baḷitthā parvatānāṃ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 5-84), which has the Earth as its deity, there is no sūkta that has the Earth alone, or Heaven alone, as its deity. Heaven and Earth are referred to together in the other places. When they are so referred to together, in the matter of which of the two has the greater precedence, the authors of the Nirukta, anticipating that doubt, have explained as follows on the basis of the Ṛk-saṃhitā:

द्यावापृथिवीनामधेयान्युत्तराणि चतुर्विंशतिः । (*dyāvāpṛthivīnāmadheyānyuttarāṇi caturviṃśatiḥ |*) "स्वधे, पुरंधी, धिषणे (*svadhe, puraṃdhī, dhiṣaṇe*) रोदसी (*rodasī*) and so on are the twenty-four names of Heaven and Earth" (Ni. 3-21). तयोरेषा भवति । (*tayoreṣā bhavati |*)

कतरा पूर्वा कतरापरायोः कथा जाते कवयः को विवेद । (*katarā pūrvā katarāparāyoḥ kathā jāte kavayaḥ ko viveda |*)
विश्वं त्मना बिभृतो यद्ध नाम वि विर्तेते अहनी चक्रियेव ॥ (*viśvaṃ tmanā bibhṛto yaddha nāma vi virtete ahanī cakriyeva ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-185-1)

कतरा पूर्वा कतरापरा । एनयोः कथं जाते कवयः क एने विजानाति । (*katarā pūrvā katarāparā | enayoḥ kathaṃ jāte kavayaḥ ka ene vijānāti |*)
सर्वमात्मना बिभृतो यद्धैनयोः कर्म विवर्तेते चैनयोरहनी (*sarvamātmanā bibhṛto yaddhainayoḥ karma vivartete cainayorahanī*)
अहोरात्रे चक्रियेव चक्रयुक्ते इवेति द्यावापृथिव्योर्महिमानमाचष्ट (*ahorātre cakriyeva cakrayukte iveti dyāvāpṛthivyormahimānamācaṣṭa*)
आचष्टे । (*ācaṣṭe |*)

(Ni. 3-22)

"Which of these two deities is the earlier? What is the order of their origin? O seers, which of you knows this secret? They are the support of all the beings of the world; like the two wheels of a chariot, both are praised as running in succession": thus, quoting the Ṛk-saṃhitā, (the Nirukta authors) have explained. *[The sentence runs over the page break, "ರಥದ | ಎರಡು ಚಕ್ರಗಳಂತೆ".]*

"While innumerable days and dawns are passing, from what forest and from what trees were these Heaven and Earth, which stand imperishable, unbroken and firm, built?": thus the seer expresses his wonder, full of praise.

The inquiry expressed in the ṛk किं स्विध्वनं (*kiṃ svidhvanaṃ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-31-7) is indicated in many other places too; in some places an apt answer can also be seen. For example,

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-81-2)

it asks Viśvakarman, who created the earth, made the atmosphere spread and has all-seeing eyes, what the support was for their creation. As the answer to it,

किग्स् स्विद्वनं क उ स वृक्ष आसीत् । यतो द्यावा पृथिवी निष्कृतक्षुः । मनिषि- (*kigs svidvanaṃ ka u sa vṛkṣa āsīt | yato dyāvā pṛthivī niṣkṛtakṣuḥ | maniṣi-*)
णो मनसा पृच्छतेम तत् । यदध्यतिष्ठद्भवनानि धारयन् । ब्रह्म वनं (*ṇo manasā pṛcchatema tat | yadadhyatiṣṭhadbhavanāni dhārayan | brahma vanaṃ*)
ब्रह्म स वृक्ष आसीत् । यतो द्यावापृथिवी निष्कृतक्षुः । मनिषिणो मनसा (*brahma sa vṛkṣa āsīt | yato dyāvāpṛthivī niṣkṛtakṣuḥ | maniṣiṇo manasā*)
विब्रवीमि वः । ब्रह्माध्यतिष्ठद्भवनानि धारयन् ॥ (*vibravīmi vaḥ | brahmādhyatiṣṭhadbhavanāni dhārayan ‖*)

"For the creation of these Heaven and Earth Brahman (the imperishable thing called Brahman) itself is the forest and Brahman itself is the tree; from it they were made": thus it is said in the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa.

ऋतावृधा (*ṛtāvṛdhā*)—ऋतस्य यज्ञस्य सत्यस्य वा वर्धयित्र्यौ । (*ṛtasya yajñasya satyasya vā vardhayitryau |*) The word ऋत (*ṛta*) has the meanings "sacrifice" and "truth"; and since the sacrifice is performed and truth established only with the help of Heaven and Earth, the epithets ऋतावृधा (*ṛtāvṛdhā*) and ऋतावरी (*ṛtāvarī*) refer to Heaven and Earth *[printed "ವ್ಯಾವಾಪೃಥಿವಿಗಳ", for ದ್ಯಾವಾಪೃಥಿವಿಗಳ, as supplied]*.

मही (*mahī*)—महत्यौ । (*mahatyau |*) "Those of great form." प्रथनात्पृथिवीत्याहुः क एनामप्रथ (*prathanātpṛthivītyāhuḥ ka enāmapratha*)यिष्यत् किमाधारश्चेत्यथ वै दर्शनेन पृथुरप्रथिता चेदप्यन्यैः (*yiṣyat kimādhāraścetyatha vai darśanena pṛthuraprathitā cedapyanyaiḥ*) (Ni. 1-14): the name Pṛthivī is from being spread out widely. If it is asked who spread it out, it is hard to give the answer; even if no one had spread it, it is spread out of itself.

आपो वा इदमग्रे सलिलमासीत्तस्मिन्जापतिर्वायुर्भूत्वाचरत्स इमामप- (*āpo vā idamagre salilamāsīttasminjāpatirvāyurbhūtvācaratsa imāmapa-*)
श्यत्तां वराहो भूत्वाहरत्तां विश्वकर्माभूत्वा व्यमार्ट्सा प्रथत सा (*śyattāṃ varāho bhūtvāharattāṃ viśvakarmābhūtvā vyamārṭsā prathata sā*)
पृथिव्यभवत्तत्पृथिव्यै पृथिवित्वं । (*pṛthivyabhavattatpṛthivyai pṛthivitvaṃ |*)

(ಕೃ. ಸಂ. 7-1-5-1) *[abbreviation "ಕೃ. ಸಂ." as printed, not expanded; probably the Taittirīya Saṃhitā or Ṛk-saṃhitā]*

Here too the same quality is stated, and the greatness of the earth is shown.

विदथेषु (*vidatheṣu*)—यज्ञेषु । (*yajñeṣu |*) "In sacrifices", that is, at the places of sacrifice. By वेदयत्सु (*vedayatsu*) स्तोत्रेषु निमित्तभूतेषु (*stotreṣu nimittabhūteṣu*) the derivation of the word विदथ (*vidatha*) is shown. This word is read among the names of sacrifice, यज्ञः वेनः (*yajñaḥ venaḥ*) and so on (Ni. 3-16). And,

होता देवो अमर्त्यः पुरस्तादेति मायया । विदथानि प्रचोदयन् ॥ (*hotā devo amartyaḥ purastādeti māyayā | vidathāni pracodayan ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-27-7)

quoting this ṛk, the authors of the Nirukta show that the word विदथ (*vidatha*) is formed from the root विद् (*vid*), which gives the sense of knowledge. The opinions of the scholars who tried to give the exact meaning of this word and stated their own views were given in the earlier part (that is, Ṛgveda-saṃhitā, part 11, pages 321 to 325, "ಸೇಜು" printed for the page). Max Muller has explained the word as "sacrificial assembly", Oldenberg as "the sacrifice prescribed by the śāstra", and Bloomfield as "house"; this was shown earlier by quoting their own sentences. Since the different meanings "house", "sacrifice" and "assembly" are all possible for this word, all fit according to the context; and it was also shown earlier, with examples, that all these meanings occur in Sāyaṇa's bhāṣya itself. In the present ṛk the meaning विदथेषु यागेषु (*vidatheṣu yāgeṣu*), "in sacrifices", is given.

देवपुत्रे (*devaputre*)—देवाः सर्वे पुत्रस्थानीया ययोस्ते द्यावापृथिव्यौ । (*devāḥ sarve putrasthānīyā yayoste dyāvāpṛthivyau |*) "Heaven and Earth, who have all the deities as sons."

**Printed English translation**

Verily I propitiate, by my invocations, the mind of the benevolent father, and the great and spontaneous (affection) of the mother (of all beings): The parents, with kindness, have secured, by their excellent protections, the vast and manifold immortality of their progeny.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

In this ṛk Heaven and Earth are designated by the word पितरा (*pitarā*) *[printed "ನಿರ್ದೇಶಿಸದೆ", which would read "does not designate"; the sense requires "designates"; kept as supplied]*. But from this ṛk it cannot be known exactly which is the father and which the mother. Sāyaṇa has explained it in two ways: पितुः पितृस्थानीयायाः पृथिव्याः (*pituḥ pitṛsthānīyāyāḥ pṛthivyāḥ*), मातुः (*mātuḥ*)—मातांतरिक्षं निर्मियंते अस्मिन् भूतानि (*mātāṃtarikṣaṃ nirmiyaṃte asmin bhūtāni*) (Ni. 2-8). Formed from the root पा (*pā*), which means "to protect", the earth, which protects all beings, is called "father"; and since all beings are formed in the atmosphere, the atmosphere is called "mother": thus, quoting the statement of the Nirukta authors, he has given the first meaning.

This too is correct in one way from the point of view of derivation, but cannot accord with the traditional meaning. Ordinarily the sentences that designate the atmosphere as "father" and the earth as "mother" are the well-known ones.

तन्नो वातो मयोभु वातु भेषजं तन्माता पृथिवी तत् पिता द्यौः । (*tanno vāto mayobhu vātu bheṣajaṃ tanmātā pṛthivī tat pitā dyauḥ |*)
तद्गावाणः सोमसुतो मयोभुवस्तदश्विना शृणुतं धिष्ण्या युवं ॥ (*tadgāvāṇaḥ somasuto mayobhuvastadaśvinā śṛṇutaṃ dhiṣṇyā yuvaṃ ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-89-4)

Here the earth is designated "mother" and the atmosphere "father".

प्रैषः स्तोमः पृथिवीमंतरिक्षं वनस्पतीरोषधी राये अश्याः । (*praiṣaḥ stomaḥ pṛthivīmaṃtarikṣaṃ vanaspatīroṣadhī rāye aśyāḥ |*)
देवोदेवः सुहवो भूतु मह्यं मा नो माता पृथिवी मर्मत् धात् । (*devodevaḥ suhavo bhūtu mahyaṃ mā no mātā pṛthivī marmat dhāt |*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 5-42-16)

उप द्यां स्कंभथुः स्कंभनेनाप्रथतं पृथिवीं मातरं वि । (*upa dyāṃ skaṃbhathuḥ skaṃbhanenāprathataṃ pṛthivīṃ mātaraṃ vi |*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-72-2)

दौ ३ प्पितः पृथिवि मातरध्रुगन्ने भ्रातर्वसवो मृळता नः । (*dau 3 ppitaḥ pṛthivi mātaradhruganne bhrātarvasavo mṛḷatā naḥ |*)
विश्व आदित्या अदिते सजोषा अस्मभ्यं शर्म बहुलं वि यंत ॥ (*viśva ādityā adite sajoṣā asmabhyaṃ śarma bahulaṃ vi yaṃta ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-51-5)

In many such ṛks (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-90-7, 1-185-11, 6-20-6 and others) the atmosphere is addressed as "father" and the earth as "mother". Thus, not only in the sentences of the Saṃhitā but also in

द्याः पिता॥ पृथिवी माता (*dyāḥ pitā‖ pṛthivī mātā*) (Tai. Brā. 3-7-5-4-5)

and in Brāhmaṇa sentences such as

अथ यत्राहोपहूते द्यावापृथिवी इति । तदग्नीध आ दधाति तदग्नीत्पाश्ना- (*atha yatrāhopahūte dyāvāpṛthivī iti | tadagnīdha ā dadhāti tadagnītpāśnā-*)
त्युपहूता पृथिवी मातोप मां पृथिवी माता ह्वायतामग्निराग्निध्रात्सा- (*tyupahūtā pṛthivī mātopa māṃ pṛthivī mātā hvāyatāmagnirāgnidhrātsā-*)
होपहूता द्यौष्टितोप मां द्यौष्टिता । (*hopahūtā dyauṣṭitopa māṃ dyauṣṭitā |*)

and in Upaniṣad sentences such as मधु द्यौरस्तु नः पिता । (*madhu dyaurastu naḥ pitā |*) (Bṛ. U. 6-3-6), the epithet "father" is established for the presiding deity of the heaven-world; therefore in the present ṛk the meaning that Sāyaṇa gave the second time, with यद्वा (*yadvā*), is the proper one.

सुरेतसा (*suretasā*)—For this word he has given the meaning शोभनसामर्थ्येन (*śobhanasāmarthyena*). Although the meaning "capacity for procreation" is ordinarily well known for this word, the word is used in other senses too.

जनिता दिपो जनिता पृथिव्याः पिबा सोमं मदाय कं शतक्रतो । (*janitā dipo janitā pṛthivyāḥ pibā somaṃ madāya kaṃ śatakrato |*)
यं ते भागमधारयन्विश्वाः सेहानः पृतना उरु ज्रयः समप्सुजिन्मरुत्वा (*yaṃ te bhāgamadhārayanviśvāḥ sehānaḥ pṛtanā uru jrayaḥ samapsujinmarutvā*)
इंद्र सत्पते ॥ (*iṃdra satpate ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-36-4)

in such ṛks Indra is said to be the creator of Heaven and Earth. But

यश्चापश्चंद्रा बृहतीर्जजान कस्मै देवाय हविषा विधेम । (*yaścāpaścaṃdrā bṛhatīrjajāna kasmai devāya haviṣā vidhema |*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-121-9)

here it is Hiraṇyagarbha, and

नाभ्या आसीदंतरिक्षं शीर्ष्णो द्यौः समवर्तत । (*nābhyā āsīdaṃtarikṣaṃ śīrṣṇo dyauḥ samavartata |*)
पद्भ्यां भूमिर्दिशः श्रोत्रात्तथा लोका अकल्पयन् । (*padbhyāṃ bhūmirdiśaḥ śrotrāttathā lokā akalpayan |*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-90-14)

in this ṛk the Puruṣa in the form of Virāṭ is said to have created Heaven and Earth. Thus, whenever the creation of the two is described, it is clearly stated that both have one origin; that is what this ṛk says with the epithet सयोनी (*sayonī*).

मायिनः (*māyinaḥ*)—माया प्रज्ञा । (*māyā prajñā |*) स्वविषयप्रज्ञावंतः । (*svaviṣayaprajñāvaṃtaḥ |*) It is explained as "having wisdom in the matter of their duty". मायेति प्रज्ञानाम । (*māyeti prajñānāma |*) The authors of the Nirukta have included माया (*māyā*), वयनं (*vayanaṃ*) and others among the names of wisdom (Ni. 3-13). Moreover,

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-88-6)

The world-famous Heaven and Earth give happiness to all the worlds, keep the rule of *ṛta*, have the capacity to hold water, are skilled in their duty and are shining. Those two, the divine Heaven and Earth. are the diffusers of happiness on all, encouragers of truth, able to sustain the water (of the rains), auspicious of birth, and energetic (in action) : in the interval between whom proceeds the pure and divine Sun for (the discharge of his) duties.

परिक्षिता पितरा पूर्वजावरी ऋतस्य योना क्षयतः समोकसा । (*parikṣitā pitarā pūrvajāvarī ṛtasya yonā kṣayataḥ samokasā |*)
द्यावापृथिवी वरुणाय सव्रते धृतवत्पयो महिषाय पिन्वतः ॥ (*dyāvāpṛthivī varuṇāya savrate dhṛtavatpayo mahiṣāya pinvataḥ ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-65-8)

in such ṛks it is also said that the womb of *ṛta* is the common dwelling-place of Heaven and Earth. All the points of these descriptions are indicated in the present ṛk too.

धिषणे (*dhiṣaṇe*)—It is read among the names of Heaven and Earth, स्वधे, पुरंधी (*svadhe, puraṃdhī*) and so on (Ni. 3-21). For this word, धिषणा वाक् धिषेर्दधात्यर्थे । (*dhiṣaṇā vāk dhiṣerdadhātyarthe |*) धीसादिनीति वा । (*dhīsādinīti vā |*) (printed "ನಿ ೮-೩"): it is said that it may be formed from the root धिष् (*dhiṣ*), which gives the sense "to hold"; धीसानिनीति वा (*dhīsāninīti vā*) *[so printed]*: "it extends the intelligence", that is, it explains the meaning clearly: such is the derivation of the authors of the Nirukta.

द्यावापृथिवी गच्छ स्वाहेति । प्रजापतिवै प्रजाः सृष्टा ता द्यावापृथिवीभ्यां (*dyāvāpṛthivī gaccha svāheti | prajāpativai prajāḥ sṛṣṭā tā dyāvāpṛthivībhyāṃ*)
परगृह्णात्ता इमा द्यानापृथिवीभ्यां परिगृहीताः । (*paragṛhṇāttā imā dyānāpṛthivībhyāṃ parigṛhītāḥ |*)

(ಶ. ಪ್ರಾ. 3-8-4-17) *[the abbreviation "ಶ. ಪ್ರಾ." is printed so, for Śa. Brā.; not expanded]*

Here it is said that Heaven and Earth seized all the beings created by Prajāpati and kept them under their own rule; so the name may be well known as formed from the root धिष् (*dhiṣ*), "to hold", indicating this sense.

रजसः (*rajasaḥ*)—For the word रजस् (*rajas*) the meaning "water" is given here. रजः । (*rajaḥ |*) रजो रजतेर्ज्योती (*rajo rajaterjyotī*) रज उच्यते उदकं रज उच्यते । लोका रजांस्युच्यंते । (*raja ucyate udakaṃ raja ucyate | lokā rajāṃsyucyaṃte |*) (Ni. 4-19): formed from the root रंज् (*raṃj*), which gives the sense "to shine", the word रजस् (*rajas*) means "light", "water" and "worlds". Moreover there is a statement of the Nirukta that it is also a synonym for blood and for the days: असृगहनी रजसी उच्येते (*asṛgahanī rajasī ucyete*). The authors of the Nirukta quote the ṛk

अज्जमुक्थैरहिं गृणीषे बुद्धे नदीनां रजःसु पीदन् । (*ajjamukthairahiṃ gṛṇīṣe buddhe nadīnāṃ rajaḥsu pīdan |*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-34-16)

and, as there रजस्सु (*rajassu*) is explained as "in the waters", the meaning "water" is given in the present ṛk too. The other meanings of the word रजस् (*rajas*) are given in their respective contexts.
