॥ Prati-padārthaḥ ॥ (p. 3)

जन्मने (janmane) — for [the deities] called the Ṛbhus, who were born [as gods] | देवाय (devāya) — for the deity (for the company of deities) | रत्नधातमः (ratna-dhātamaḥ) — giving lovely jewels and the like (riches) | अयं स्तोमः (ayaṃ stomaḥ) — this hymn | विप्रेभिः (viprebhiḥ) — by the intelligent, the learned priests and others | आसया (āsayā) — with [their] mouth | अकारि (akāri) — has been made.

॥ Bhāvārthaḥ ॥

The deities called the Ṛbhus, having been men before, attained divinity. Addressing that company of deities who thus took a divine birth, the learned priests and other men have made this hymn with their own mouths. This excellent hymn gives jewels and other precious things to those who praise, through the grace of the Ṛbhu deities.

॥ English Translation ॥ (as printed in the source)

This hymn, the bestower of riches has been addressed by the sages with their own mouths to the race (of Ribhus) having divine birth.

॥ Viśeṣa-viṣayagaḷu (Special Topics) ॥ (p. 4)

It is said in many places in the Ṛgveda that the Ṛbhus, having been men formerly, attained divinity by their excellent conduct and by the performance of good works. Formerly, to the ṛṣi Aṅgiras there was a son named Nītimañjarī [as read]. In his line there were three sons born to a ṛṣi named Sudhanvan: Ṛbhu, Vibhu and Vāja. By good works — helping the gods and the like — they were born as gods or became gods; so it is believed. Of these three sons of Sudhanvan, Ṛbhu and the rest, the custom is to call them "Ṛbhus"; the three sons are called by the name of the eldest. Because they were pupils of Tvaṣṭṛ, they are also called "divine craftsmen". It is said in various places that they made for Indra and the other gods needed things — that is, they made Indra's chariot, Agni's camasa vessel, and so on. In this sūkta some of the deeds they did are described. It is said everywhere that they have wondrous power and are capable of doing any work; since they have powers and capabilities like those of the gods, the ṛṣis, thinking them to belong to the company of gods, have variously described them and the deeds they did. It is believed that they dwell in the orb of the sun and carry out their works through the sun's rays. More details about them will be given later.

आसया (āsayā) — the singular instrumental word; āsya — a variant form of the word mukha, "mouth"; the sense: "by the mouth", "by the lips".

रत्नधातमः (ratnadhātamaḥ) — this word occurs in the first rik of the Ṛgveda, agnim īḷe purohitam (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-1-1). Ratnāni dadhāti (dadāti) iti ratnadhāḥ atiśayena dhanapradaḥ: one who gives wealth, such as gems, in the highest degree.

ततक्षुः मनसा (tatakṣuḥ manasā) — sampāditavantaḥ, kṛtavantaḥ: "created" [English added by the print]. Being skilled in the craft of building, and being of true resolve, the Ṛbhus created with the power of their intellect [Indra's chariot and horses] — items that serve the sacrifice: weapons and the like, the chariot and other things — and are able to make anything. Those deities' powers are above those of modern craftsmen: they need no tools or materials; by mere mental resolve they are able to make whatever they wish. This is what the word manasā conveys.

हरी (harī) — haraṇāt vahate iti harī: because they carry (vahate) the chariot, Indra's horses are called Hari. This dual word is, in the Ṛgveda, generally used when speaking of Indra's two horses.

शमीभिः (śamībhiḥ) — śamayanti pāpānīti śamyaḥ karmāṇi: among the thirty-six words for 'action' beginning with apas, aḥnaḥ, the word śamī or śimī is read (Ni. [2-1]); hence śamībhiḥ means "by sacrificial acts". The sentence in which it occurs, śamībhir yajñam āśata, can be understood in several ways. [The Ṛbhu deities] pervade [our] sacrifice with their own works — that is, with works of craft; or with works that give the sacrificer what he wishes; i.e. they come to the sacrificial ground. Or: the sacrificer, who is made to perform havis-giving to the Ṛbhu deities, is pleased by these works and accepts the oblations; and so on. The main idea is that these Ṛbhus, with their cunning craftsmanship, make the equipment needed in the sacrifice so that the sacrifice is completed with all its parts, and, pleased with the oblations the sacrificer offers, give him the fruit of the sacrifice.

॥ Bhāvārthaḥ ॥

The Ṛbhus go always on the right path; therefore their mantras are never in vain, and are fruitful. By the power of such mantras they made their aged father and mother young again.

॥ English Translation ॥ (as printed in the source)

The honest Ribhus repeating true prayers made (their) parents young again by their skilful acts encountering no opposition through their infallible mantras.

॥ Viśeṣa-viṣayagaḷu (Special Topics) ॥ (pp. 15–17)

When the mantras of the Ṛbhu deities are recited at the time of reciting the vaiśvadeva-śastra mantras, this mantra is said as the dvitīya-chandomā mantra. This matter is stated in the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra, in the section of the second [day], agniṃ vo devam, in the sūtra mahī dyauḥ pṛthivī ca no yuvānā pitarā punar iti tṛcau (Āś. 8-10).

युवाना (yuvānā) — yuvānau: youthful; those who are in the state of youth, not in old age.

पितरा (pitarā) — svakīyau mātāpitarau — their aged parents.

पुनः (punaḥ) — again; as they were formerly in the state of youth.

सत्यमन्त्राः (satyamantrāḥ) — avitathamantrasāmarthyopetāḥ. By performing the puraścaraṇa and similar rites, those who have obtained mantra-siddhi: if one performs the recitation of the mantra for the sake of a given fruit, the fruit is certainly obtained.

ऋजूयवः (ṛjūyavaḥ) — ṛjukāmāḥ; ṛjutām ātmana icchanti: those who rely on justice or the path of truth; or those who wish to be establishers of the path of truth.

ऋभवः (ṛbhavaḥ) — Yāska has given three derivations of the word ṛbhu: (1) ṛbhava uru bhāntīti vā — "because they shine (bhānti) greatly (uru)"; hence they are called ṛbhavaḥ; some hold that the word ṛbhu or ṛbhu comes of joining the final r of the word uru [read as ṛu] and the first letter of bhānti — as ahaḥ + rātriḥ = hora [i.e. horā]. (2) ṛtena bhāntīti vā — those who shine by ṛta — truth or sacrifice. (3) ṛtena bhavanti iti vā — "those who are [what they are] by ṛta, i.e. by capacity"; some say that the initial ṛ of ṛtena and the bha of bhavanti joined together and became ṛbhu.

विष्टी (viṣṭī) — viṣṭī, viṣḷ vyāptau: among the twenty-six words for karman beginning with apas, aḥnaḥ, the word viṣṭi is read (Ni. 2-1); it therefore means "action". Viṣṭī — vyāptiyuktāḥ: those who have pervasion. By "pervasion" is meant: sarveṣu kāryeṣv etadīyasya mantrasāmarthyasyāpratighāto 'tra vyāptir ucyate — in all works their power of mantra is not hindered.

The Ṛbhus — the Kannada here says — were formerly men; the manner in which they afterwards attained divinity this rik explains in detail:

विष्टी शमी तरणित्वेन वाघतो मर्तासः सन्तो अमृतत्वमानशुः । सौधन्वना ऋभवः सूरचक्षसः संवत्सरे समपृच्यन्त धीतिभिः ॥ (ऋ. सं. [१-११०-४])

viṣṭī śamī taraṇitvena vāghato martāsaḥ santo amṛtatvam ānaśuḥ | saudhanvanā ṛbhavaḥ sūracakṣasaḥ saṃvatsare samapṛcyanta dhītibhiḥ || (ṛ. saṃ. [1-110-4])

(Read from the Kannada print; the reference numerals read as "೧-೧೧೦-೪" and are given with a query. The Kannada gives no English gloss of the rik beyond the commentary, which follows; the gloss is mine and tentative:) "Being mortals, by action (viṣṭī), by works (śamī), by swiftness [or: by the speed of the sun], the Sudhanvan-born Ṛbhus, the sacrificer-offering [vāghataḥ] and sun-eyed, attained immortality; in the course of a year they were united with the acts [of their works]." Yāska explains it:

कृत्वा कर्माणि श्रिप्रत्वेन वोढारो मेधाविनो वा मर्तासः सन्तोऽमृतत्वमानशिरे ऋभवः सूरख्याना वा सूरप्रज्ञा वा संवत्सरे समपृच्यन्त धीतिभिः कर्मभिः ॥ kṛtvā karmāṇi śriprat-vena voḍhāro medhāvino vā martāsaḥ santo 'mṛtatvam ānaśire ṛbhavaḥ sūrakhyānā vā sūraprajñā vā saṃvatsare samapṛcyanta dhītibhiḥ karmabhiḥ ||

(The first words of Yāska's text are partly garbled in the print; I give them as read and without translating.) The chief sense: the sons of Sudhanvan, these Ṛbhus, formerly were men. By means of the sun-like brilliance of their intelligence, or their power of reason, speedily — that is, in a single year — they completed all sacrifices and so obtained amṛtatva, divinity (freedom from death). Yāska gives another sense of the word ṛbhu:

आदित्यरश्मयोऽप्यृभव उच्यन्ते ॥ ādityaraśmayo 'py ṛbhava ucyante ||

॥ Viśeṣa-viṣayagaḷu (Special Topics) ॥ (pp. 21–22)

In the evening, at the third pressing (tṛtīya-savana), when the soma-juice is offered to the Ṛbhus, it is offered by addressing — together with the Ṛbhus — Indra accompanied by the Ādityas, and the deities Ṛbhu, Vibhu, Vāja, Bṛhaspati and the Viśvedevas. This matter is stated in the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra: indram ādityavantam ṛbhumantaṃ vibhumantaṃ vājavantaṃ bṛhaspatimantaṃ viśvedevyāvantam āveti (Āś. 5-3 [?]).

सं अग्मत (saṃ agmata) — Skandasvāmin explains these words as saṃgacchadhvam, "unite" — [taking agmata as] "may [the soma] come together for the sake of drinking soma".

मदासः (madāsaḥ) — mādyanty ebhir iti madāḥ somāḥ: since the soma-drinking gives intoxication (mada), the soma-juice is called madāḥ.

मरुत्वता (marutvatā) — maruto 'sya santīti marutvān: with Indra, who is accompanied by the Marut deities.

आदित्येभिः (ādityebhiḥ) — Yāska has given these derivations of the word āditya (Ni. [2-13]): (1) ādityaḥ kasmād ādatte rasān ādatte bhāsaṃ jyotiṣām ādīpto bhāseti vādites putra iti vā — "Why is [the sun] Āditya? Because he takes (ādatte) the juices; or takes the light of the luminaries; or because he is lit up [from all sides] and shines; or because he is the son of Aditi." That is, ādatte means he who takes or draws: he draws up the water (rasān) by his rays; the water thus drawn up becomes cloud and produces rain; hence, because he draws up water by his rays from the sea and other water-bodies, he is called Āditya. (2) Or: ādatte bhāsaṃ jyotiṣām — because at his rising the sun's light dims the light of all other things (the moon and so on) and takes it up; hence the name. (3) Or: ādīpto bhāseti vā — because he is lit up on all sides, shining everywhere around. (4) Or: aditeḥ putra iti vā — he is the son of the goddess Aditi, mother of the gods; hence Āditya.

॥ Viśeṣa-viṣayagaḷu (Special Topics) ॥ (pp. 32–33)

At the time of reciting the mantras of the vaiśvadeva-śastra, this rik and the next are recited for the third chandomā mantra. This matter is stated in the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra, in the section of the third [day] beginning mahi [agniṃ mahi], in the sūtra indra iṣe dadātu naḥ (Ṛ. Saṃ. [8-93-?]) te no ratnāni dhattaneti ekā dve ca (Āś. 8-11). In this rik the power of the Ṛbhu deities is not described; rather it is a prayer that the Ṛbhus should favour the sacrificer with jewels and the other forms of wealth, and with the fruits of sacrificial rites such as yāga.

त्रिरा साप्तानि (trirā sāptāni) — "three times seven", that is, the repetition of seven, or twenty-one. These are chiefly twenty-one kinds of sacrifices in the sacrificial order. They are divided into three groups.

  1. agnyādheya-darśapūrṇamāsādīnāṃ saptānāṃ haviryajñānām eko vargaḥ — "one group of seven haviryajñas, agnyādheya, darśapūrṇamāsa and the rest." The name haviryajña is given to one group of seven sacrifices beginning with the agnyādheya and darśapūrṇamāsa. Since the thing offered to the deities is mainly havis, these are called haviryajñas. Agnyādheya, agnihotra, darśapūrṇamāsa, āgrayaṇa, cāturmāsya, dākṣāyaṇa-yajña and sautrāmaṇī — these seven belong to this group (Ai. Brā. [3-2]).

  2. aupāsanahomo vaiśvadevam ity-ādīnāṃ saptānāṃ pākayajñānāṃ vargo dvitīyaḥ — "the second group is that of seven pākayajñas, aupāsana-homa, vaiśvadeva and the rest." The name pākayajña is given to the group of seven sacrifices beginning with aupāsana and vaiśvadeva; these are also called gṛhyayajñas. Of them the thing offered chiefly to the deities is cooked food. Aupāsana, vaiśvadeva, pārvaṇa, aṣṭakā, māsiśrāddha, sarpabali and īśānabali belong to this group (according to the explanation of Dhūrtasvāmin on the Āpastamba-paribhāṣā-sūtra).

  3. agniṣṭomo 'tyagniṣṭoma ity-ādīnāṃ saptānāṃ somasaṃsthānāṃ vargas tṛtīyaḥ — "the third group is the seven soma-saṃsthās, agniṣṭoma, atyagniṣṭoma and the rest." The seven soma-sacrifices — agniṣṭoma, atyagniṣṭoma, ukthya, ṣoḍaśin, vājapeya, atirātra and aptoryāma — form the third group, called somayajñas or soma-saṃsthās; in these, what is offered to the deities is the soma-juice.

We have given the account of these three kinds of groups in Part [1] of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā, at pp. [?]–[?] and [?]–[?] [the page numerals in the print are partly unreadable].

एकमेकम् (ekam ekam) — krameṇa pratyekam: of the things we wish for — wealth, grain, cattle and the like — either one or all, each one by one they should give; that is the sense.

सुशस्तिभिः (suśastibhiḥ) — śasyate ābhir iti śastayaḥ ṛcaḥ: because the gods are praised by riks, these are called śastayaḥ; suśastayaḥ means excellent riks or hymns of praise.
