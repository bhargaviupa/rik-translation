## Preface (Pīṭhike)

In this part of the Ṛgveda, the ninth Maṇḍala is completed in full, and forty sūktas of the tenth Maṇḍala are finished. Since all the sūktas of the ninth Maṇḍala have Soma as their deity, and since we have already explained the whole subject of the deity Soma at length in the earlier part, we do not wish to say much about it here. But the sūktas of the tenth Maṇḍala have a special distinction from many points of view. Besides praises of the common deities such as Agni and Indra, as in the other Maṇḍalas, the tenth Maṇḍala contains other deities found nowhere else in the Ṛgveda; persons who are well known as presiding deities of inanimate things (or as inanimate things themselves); strange and charming dialogues; ancient histories; lofty Vedāntic doctrines; and various sūktas useful in the Śrauta and Smārta rites. The view of some Western scholars that the sūktas of this Maṇḍala are not as ancient as those of the other Maṇḍalas cannot be accepted entirely. The fact that the sūktas of the Ṛgveda have been arranged in a systematic manner, and the fact that some sūktas of this Maṇḍala contain not merely hymns of praise but also references to other matters, may have given occasion to this opinion of the Western scholars.

Among the subjects dealt with in the present part, that of the deities called Pitṛs is the chief. The seers have discussed what the Pitṛs are, what the state of men after death is, whether heaven and hell exist or not, and similar matters. In this connection the dialogue between Yama and the seer named Naciketas is interesting. We have explained it in summary, as it is stated in the Kaṭhopaniṣad. Besides this, these sūktas give an opportunity to learn about various social conditions: what the conduct and speech of ordinary people were in that very ancient time, how the funeral rites were performed, whether women practised sahagamana (accompanying the husband in death), and so on.

Although hymns addressed to deities have the chief place in the Ṛgveda, references to other subjects are found here and there in good measure. The dialogue between Yama and his sister Yamī, held for the purpose of sexual union, is one of these. Earlier we explained the dialogue held for the same purpose between the seer Agastya and his wife Lopāmudrā. Descriptions of many such matters relating to the art of love are met with here and there.

Kavaṣa, the son of Ilūṣa, was of the Śūdra caste and a gambler. Once, when he came and sat among the seers who were performing a sacrifice, the seers drove him away, saying that he was not fit for their company, and

### Printed p. ii

took him to a distant desert region without water or shade and left him there to die. Enraged at this, Kavaṣa prayed to the river Sarasvatī and to the other deities; Sarasvatī flowed into the waterless sandy wilderness where he was and made water stand all around him. Seeing this, the seers repented of their wrongful act, went to him, begged him to forgive their offence, brought him back, and made him a participant in their sacrifices and the like. He is the seer of 5 sūktas (30–34) in the tenth Maṇḍala of the Ṛgveda. Therefore it can be supported by many examples that the seers of the Vedic age gave importance to merit and not to birth. Among the seers of the Veda there are some who are Kṣatriyas or kings, Vaiśyas and Śūdras. These too hold an honoured place among the seers, and the mantras seen by them are used especially in sacrifices and rites.

This seer Kavaṣa was addicted to the game of dice. Therefore his parents, his elder and younger brothers and the rest of his kinsfolk had abandoned him. The joy that arises in winning at dice, the sorrow that arises in losing, and the anxiety of the players while the game is going on, that is, while the dice are rolling, whether they will win or lose: all this this seer has described in a manner that touches the heart. Descriptions of many such matters are to be found in the Ṛgveda in abundance.

To publish such a Ṛgveda Saṃhitā, extremely ancient, extremely profound, extremely vast, and the foundation of all the śāstras, in simple Kannada together with a critical translation is not an easy task. Even so, with the noble aim that through such a great undertaking all people, from the learned to the unlearned, may gain growth of knowledge and become worthy of the grace of God, His Highness Śrī Jayacāmarājendra Oḍeyar Bahadur, G.C.B., G.C.S.I., the sovereign of the throne of Karnataka, a lover of Vedic learning, himself a scholar, beloved of his subjects and devoted to God, and his Government, are encouraging with their generous patronage the publication of this work, which requires great expense; this must be called the good fortune of the multitude of the people of Karnataka. May the Lord of all, the Veda-Puruṣa, bestow on our generous-minded sovereign, on his family, on the princesses, on the prince bearing the auspicious name Śrīkaṇṭhadatta Nṛsiṃharāja Oḍeyar, and on the whole multitude of subjects, in still greater measure all worldly blessings such as long life, health and prosperity, and the spiritual knowledge that leads to the highest good: this I pray.

## Sūkta 101 (Maṇḍala 9)

The hundred-and-first Sūkta

Introduction.

Since we have already explained, in the earlier part, the matter of the Soma deity and the other topics of this Maṇḍala, there is no need to explain them again here. Special points will be noted here and there, in special contexts and in explaining the sense of difficult words.

#### Ṛk 1

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> पु॒रोजि॑ती वो॒ अंध॑सः सु॒ताय॑ मादयि॒त्नवे॑ ।  
> अप॒ श्वानं॑ शृथिष्टन॒ सखा॑यो दीर्घजि॒ह्वं ॥ १ ॥॑

### Printed p. 3

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> पुरःजिती । वः । अंधसः । सुताय । मादयित्नवे !  
> अप । श्वानं । शृधिष्ट्वन । सखायः । दीर्घजिह्वं । १ ॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> हे सखायः सखिभूताः समानख्याना वा हे स्तोतारः वो यूयं पुरोजिती ॥ षष्ट्याः पूर्वसवर्णदीर्घः । पुरस्थितजयस्यांधसोऽदनीयस्य सोमस्य
> स्वभूताय सुतायाभिषुताय मादयत्नवेऽत्यंतं मदकराय रसाय दीर्घजिह्वं दीर्घा जिह्वा यस्य सः ॥ दीर्घजिह्वी च छंदसि। पा. ४-१-५९ । इति जीषंतत्वेन
> निपातितः ॥ तादृशं श्वानमप श्वथिष्टन । अपश्नथयत । अपबाधध्वं! यथा श्वा राक्षसा
> ना सुतं सोमं न लिहंति तथा कुरुतेत्र्थः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

"O friends", that is, you who are companions, or praisers of like fame: you drive off the dog. The Soma is called अंधसः (*aṃdhasaḥ*), "to be eaten", and has victory standing before it (पुरोजिती (*purojitī*)). The dog is to be driven off for the sake of the pressed (सुताय (*sutāya*)), your own, intoxicating (मादयित्नवे (*mādayitnave*)) juice, that greatly gladdens. दीर्घजिह्वं (*dīrghajihvaṃ*) means one whose tongue is long. श्वथिष्टन (*śvathiṣṭana*) means: strike away, drive off. The sense is: act so that dogs, that is, rākṣasas, do not lick the pressed Soma.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- सखायः (*sakhāyaḥ*)—O friends, praisers
- वः (*vaḥ*)—you
- पुरोजिती (*purojitī*)—who has established victory before you
- अंधसः (*aṃdhasaḥ*)—fit for food (fit to be eaten)
- सुताय (*sutāya*)—pressed
- मादयित्नवे (*mādayitnave*)—possessing a gladdening juice; for the enjoyment of such a Soma
- दीर्घजिह्वं (*dīrghajihvaṃ*)—having a long tongue
- श्वानं (*śvānaṃ*)—the dog
- अपश्चथिष्टन (*apaścathiṣṭana*)—strike and drive away

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O friends, O praisers: for the enjoyment of the Soma juice, which has made victory ready for you, is fit to be eaten, has been pressed, and gladdens, strike and drive far away this dog with the long tongue (so that it may not lick the juice).

##### English translation
For (the drinking of) the effused exhilarating (juice) of the Soma who has placed victory before you, you friends, destroy the long-tongued dog.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

In the Maṇḍalas of the Ṛgveda other than the ninth, the Sūktas are divided in the order of the seers, so ordinarily one Sūkta has one seer; only in some cases are there two or three seers. In the ninth Maṇḍala, however, all the Sūktas have one and the same deity, Pavamāna Soma, so it is natural and unavoidable that the Sūktas of this Maṇḍala have various seers.

### Printed p. 4

Although ordinarily one Sūkta has one seer, in some cases ṛks seen by many seers have been gathered together and regarded as a single Sūkta. Among such Sūktas of this Maṇḍala, the 97th, 101st, 107th and 108th are the chief. In the 97th Sūkta there are Vasiṣṭha and some seers of Vasiṣṭha's gotra; in the 101st Sūkta there are the seers Prajāpati, Manu, and, of Manu's line, Nahuṣa, Yayāti and Andhīgu; in the 107th Sūkta there are the Seven Sages. Who these Seven Sages are we have already explained earlier. In the 108th Sūkta there are seven seers of Vasiṣṭha's line, beginning with Gauravīti. Some Sūktas seen by many seers are found also in the tenth Maṇḍala.

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> यो धार॑या पाव॒कया॑ परिप्र॒स्यंद॑ते सु॒तः ।  
> इंदु॒रश्वो॒ न कृत्त्वः॑ ॥ २ ॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> यः । धारया । पावकया । परिप्रस्यंदते । सुतः ।  
> इंदुः । अश्वः । न । कृत्त्वः ॥ २ ॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> पवित्रेष्टां द्वितीयस्याज्यभागस्य यो धारयेत्येषानुवाक्या । सूत्रितं
> च। पावकवंतावाज्यभागावग्नी रक्षांसि सेधति यो धारया पावकया । आ. २.१२ । इति॥
> सुतोऽभिषुतः कृत्त्वः । कृत्वति कर्मनाम । कर्मणि साधुर्यः इंदुः सोमः
> पावकया पापानां शोधकया धारया परिप्रस्यंदते परितः प्रक्षरति । कथमिव ।
> अश्वो नाश्वो यथा वेगेन प्रगच्छति तद्वत् ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

In the Pavitreṣṭi, this ṛk यो धारया (*yo dhārayā*) is the anuvākyā for the second Ājyabhāga. It is also given in the Sūtra: पावकवंतावाज्यभागावग्नी रक्षांसि सेधति यो धारया पावकया (*pāvakavaṃtāvājyabhāgāvagnī rakṣāṃsi sedhati yo dhārayā pāvakayā*) (Ā. Sū. 2.12). सुतः (*sutaḥ*) means pressed. कृत्त्वः (*kṛttvaḥ*): *kṛtvan* is a name for action; he who is good in action. Indu, that is Soma, flows forth all around (परिप्रस्यंदते (*pariprasyaṃdate*)) in a stream (धारया (*dhārayā*)) that cleanses sins (पावकया (*pāvakayā*)). How? Like a horse (अश्वो न (*aśvo na*)), that is, as a horse goes forth with speed, so he.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- यः (*yaḥ*)—who
- सुतः (*sutaḥ*)—pressed
- कृत्वर्यः (*kṛtvaryaḥ*)—fit for rites; such a one
- इंदुः (*iṃduḥ*)—the god Soma
- पावकया (*pāvakayā*)—purifying
- धारया (*dhārayā*)—in the form of a stream
- अश्वो न (*aśvo na*)—like a swift-going horse
- परिप्रस्यंदते (*pariprasyaṃdate*)—flows

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

The god Soma, who has been pressed and is fit for rites, flows in a purifying stream, like a swift-going horse.

### Printed p. 5

##### English translation
The Indu good for sacred rites which when effused flows forth in a purifying stream, (swift) as a horse :

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

In the Pavitreṣṭi, when the second Ājyabhāga is offered in the fire, this ṛk यो धारया (*yo dhārayā*) is to be recited as the anuvākyā mantra. This is explained by the Sūtra of the Āśvalāyana Śrautasūtra, पावकवंतावाज्यभागावग्नी रक्षांसि सेधति यो धारया पावकया (*pāvakavaṃtāvājyabhāgāvagnī rakṣāṃsi sedhati yo dhārayā pāvakayā*) (Ā. 2-12).

#### Ṛk 3

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> तं दु॒रोष॑म॒भी नरः॒ सोमं॑ वि॒श्वाच्या॑ धि॒या ।  
> य॒ज्ञं हि॑न्वं॒त्यद्रि॑भिः ॥ ३ ॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> तं । दुरोषं । अभि । नरः । सोमं । विश्वाच्या । धिया ।  
> यज्ञं । हिन्वंति । अद्रिभिः ॥ ३ ॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> नरः कर्मनेतार ऋत्विजो दुरोषं । रोषतेर्हिंसार्थस्य रेफलोपे
> दीर्घाभाव ओषतेर्डाहार्थस्य वा खलि रूपमिति संदेहादनवग्रहः ॥ तं दुर्दहं
> दुर्वधं ना यज्ञं यष्टव्यं तं सोमं विश्वाच्या सर्वान्मानांचित्रा धिया
> बुद्ध्याद्रिभिर्गावभिरभि हिन्वंति । अभिप्रेरयंति । अभिषुण्वंतीति यावत् ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

नरः (*naraḥ*) are the priests, the leaders of the rite. दुरोषं (*duroṣaṃ*): Sāyaṇa remarks that it may be from the root रोष (*roṣa*) in the sense of violence, with loss of the r and no lengthening, or from the root ओष (*oṣa*) in the sense of burning; because of this doubt there is no avagraha. Thus the Soma is hard to burn, hard to kill, and is to be sacrificed to. The priests urge it on (अभि हिन्वंति (*abhi hinvaṃti*)), that is, press it, with the stones (अद्रिभिः (*adribhiḥ*)), with a mind (धिया (*dhiyā*)) that is विश्वाच्या (*viśvācyā*). **NOTE**

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- नरः (*naraḥ*)—the priests, leaders of the rite
- दुरोषं (*duroṣaṃ*)—unharmed
- यज्ञं (*yajñaṃ*)—worthy of worship
- तं सोमं (*taṃ somaṃ*)—that same Soma
- विश्वाच्या (*viśvācyā*)—spreading everywhere
- धिया (*dhiyā*)—together with intelligence

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

The priests, leaders of the rite, with an intelligence that spreads everywhere, crush and press with the pressing stones the Soma, who is unharmed and worthy of worship.

> प॒वित्र॑वंतो अक्षरन्दे॒वान्ग॑च्छंतु वो॒ मदाः॑ ॥ ४ ॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> पृष्ठस्य षष्ठेऽहनि ब्राह्मणाच्छंसिशस्त्रे सुतास इति तिस्रः । सूत्रितं च । दधिक्राव्योऽकारिषमित्यनुष्टुप् सुतासो मधुमत्तमा इति च तिस्रः । आ. ८-३ । इति ॥
> मधुमत्तमा अतिशयेन माधुर्योपेताः अत एव मंदिनो मदकराः
> सुतासोऽभिषुताः सोमाः पवित्रवंतः पवित्रॆ वर्तमानाः संत इंद्रायेंद्रार्थमक्षरन् । पात्रेषु क्षरंति । अथ प्रत्यक्षकृतः । वो युष्माकं मदा मदहेतवो
> रसा देवानिंद्रादीन्गच्छंतु ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

On the sixth day of the Pṛṣṭha (Ṣaḍaha), in the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin's śastra, the three ṛks beginning सुतास (*sutāsa*) are used. It is also given in the Sūtra: दधिक्राव्योऽकारिषमित्यनुष्टुप् सुतासो मधुमत्तमा इति च तिस्रः । (*dadhikrāvyo 'kāriṣamityanuṣṭup sutāso madhumattamā iti ca tisraḥ |*) (Ā. Sū. 8-3). The Somas are मधुमत्तमाः (*madhumattamāḥ*), supremely sweet, and therefore मंदिनः (*maṃdinaḥ*), intoxicating; pressed (सुतासः (*sutāsaḥ*)), they stand in the strainer (पवित्रवंतः (*pavitravaṃtaḥ*)) and flow into the vessels for Indra's sake. Then, turning to the Somas directly: "your" intoxicating juices (मदाः (*madāḥ*)), the causes of intoxication, are to go to the gods, Indra and the others.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- मधुमत्तमाः (*madhumattamāḥ*)—endowed with the utmost sweetness
- मुंदिनः (*muṃdinaḥ*)—gladdening
- सुतासः (*sutāsaḥ*)—pressed
- सोमाः (*somāḥ*)—the Soma juices
- पवित्रवंतः (*pavitravaṃtaḥ*)—being in the ten-fringed strainer
- इंद्राय (*iṃdrāya*)—for Indra's drinking
- अक्षरन् (*akṣaran*)—flow (O Soma juices)
- वः (*vaḥ*)—your
- मदाः (*madāḥ*)—gladdening juices
- देवान् (*devān*)—to the gods
- गच्चंतु (*gaccaṃtu*)—let them flow and go

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

The Soma juices, endowed with the utmost sweetness, gladdening and pressed, stand in the ten-fringed strainer and flow for Indra's drinking. O gladdening Soma juices, flow facing the gods.
