## Preface (Pīṭhike)

In this part of the Ṛgveda, the ninth Maṇḍala is completed in full, and forty sūktas of the tenth Maṇḍala are finished. Since all the sūktas of the ninth Maṇḍala have Soma as their deity, and since we have already explained the whole subject of the deity Soma at length in the earlier part, we do not wish to say much about it here. But the sūktas of the tenth Maṇḍala have a special distinction from many points of view. Besides praises of the common deities such as Agni and Indra, as in the other Maṇḍalas, the tenth Maṇḍala contains other deities found nowhere else in the Ṛgveda; persons who are well known as presiding deities of inanimate things (or as inanimate things themselves); strange and charming dialogues; ancient histories; lofty Vedāntic doctrines; and various sūktas useful in the Śrauta and Smārta rites. The view of some Western scholars that the sūktas of this Maṇḍala are not as ancient as those of the other Maṇḍalas cannot be accepted entirely. The fact that the sūktas of the Ṛgveda have been arranged in a systematic manner, and the fact that some sūktas of this Maṇḍala contain not merely hymns of praise but also references to other matters, may have given occasion to this opinion of the Western scholars.

Among the subjects dealt with in the present part, that of the deities called Pitṛs is the chief. The seers have discussed what the Pitṛs are, what the state of men after death is, whether heaven and hell exist or not, and similar matters. In this connection the dialogue between Yama and the seer named Naciketas is interesting. We have explained it in summary, as it is stated in the Kaṭhopaniṣad. Besides this, these sūktas give an opportunity to learn about various social conditions: what the conduct and speech of ordinary people were in that very ancient time, how the funeral rites were performed, whether women practised sahagamana (accompanying the husband in death), and so on.

Although hymns addressed to deities have the chief place in the Ṛgveda, references to other subjects are found here and there in good measure. The dialogue between Yama and his sister Yamī, held for the purpose of sexual union, is one of these. Earlier we explained the dialogue held for the same purpose between the seer Agastya and his wife Lopāmudrā. Descriptions of many such matters relating to the art of love are met with here and there.

Kavaṣa, the son of Ilūṣa, was of the Śūdra caste and a gambler. Once, when he came and sat among the seers who were performing a sacrifice, the seers drove him away, saying that he was not fit for their company, and

### Printed p. ii

took him to a distant desert region without water or shade and left him there to die. Enraged at this, Kavaṣa prayed to the river Sarasvatī and to the other deities; Sarasvatī flowed into the waterless sandy wilderness where he was and made water stand all around him. Seeing this, the seers repented of their wrongful act, went to him, begged him to forgive their offence, brought him back, and made him a participant in their sacrifices and the like. He is the seer of 5 sūktas (30–34) in the tenth Maṇḍala of the Ṛgveda. Therefore it can be supported by many examples that the seers of the Vedic age gave importance to merit and not to birth. Among the seers of the Veda there are some who are Kṣatriyas or kings, Vaiśyas and Śūdras. These too hold an honoured place among the seers, and the mantras seen by them are used especially in sacrifices and rites.

This seer Kavaṣa was addicted to the game of dice. Therefore his parents, his elder and younger brothers and the rest of his kinsfolk had abandoned him. The joy that arises in winning at dice, the sorrow that arises in losing, and the anxiety of the players while the game is going on, that is, while the dice are rolling, whether they will win or lose: all this this seer has described in a manner that touches the heart. Descriptions of many such matters are to be found in the Ṛgveda in abundance.

To publish such a Ṛgveda Saṃhitā, extremely ancient, extremely profound, extremely vast, and the foundation of all the śāstras, in simple Kannada together with a critical translation is not an easy task. Even so, with the noble aim that through such a great undertaking all people, from the learned to the unlearned, may gain growth of knowledge and become worthy of the grace of God, His Highness Śrī Jayacāmarājendra Oḍeyar Bahadur, G.C.B., G.C.S.I., the sovereign of the throne of Karnataka, a lover of Vedic learning, himself a scholar, beloved of his subjects and devoted to God, and his Government, are encouraging with their generous patronage the publication of this work, which requires great expense; this must be called the good fortune of the multitude of the people of Karnataka. May the Lord of all, the Veda-Puruṣa, bestow on our generous-minded sovereign, on his family, on the princesses, on the prince bearing the auspicious name Śrīkaṇṭhadatta Nṛsiṃharāja Oḍeyar, and on the whole multitude of subjects, in still greater measure all worldly blessings such as long life, health and prosperity, and the spiritual knowledge that leads to the highest good: this I pray.

## Sūkta 101 (Maṇḍala 9)

The hundred-and-first Sūkta

Introduction.

Since we have already explained, in the earlier part, the matter of the Soma deity and the other topics of this Maṇḍala, there is no need to explain them again here. Special points will be noted here and there, in special contexts and in explaining the sense of difficult words.

#### Ṛk 1

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> पु॒रोजि॑ती वो॒ अंध॑सः सु॒ताय॑ मादयि॒त्नवे॑ ।  
> अप॒ श्वानं॑ शृथिष्टन॒ सखा॑यो दीर्घजि॒ह्वं ॥ १ ॥॑

### Printed p. 3

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> पुरःजिती । वः । अंधसः । सुताय । मादयित्नवे !  
> अप । श्वानं । शृधिष्ट्वन । सखायः । दीर्घजिह्वं । १ ॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> हे सखायः सखिभूताः समानख्याना वा हे स्तोतारः वो यूयं पुरोजिती ॥ षष्ट्याः पूर्वसवर्णदीर्घः । पुरस्थितजयस्यांधसोऽदनीयस्य सोमस्य
> स्वभूताय सुतायाभिषुताय मादयत्नवेऽत्यंतं मदकराय रसाय दीर्घजिह्वं दीर्घा जिह्वा यस्य सः ॥ दीर्घजिह्वी च छंदसि। पा. ४-१-५९ । इति जीषंतत्वेन
> निपातितः ॥ तादृशं श्वानमप श्वथिष्टन । अपश्नथयत । अपबाधध्वं! यथा श्वा राक्षसा
> ना सुतं सोमं न लिहंति तथा कुरुतेत्र्थः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

"O friends", that is, you who are companions, or praisers of like fame: you drive off the dog. The Soma is called अंधसः (*aṃdhasaḥ*), "to be eaten", and has victory standing before it (पुरोजिती (*purojitī*)). The dog is to be driven off for the sake of the pressed (सुताय (*sutāya*)), your own, intoxicating (मादयित्नवे (*mādayitnave*)) juice, that greatly gladdens. दीर्घजिह्वं (*dīrghajihvaṃ*) means one whose tongue is long. श्वथिष्टन (*śvathiṣṭana*) means: strike away, drive off. The sense is: act so that dogs, that is, rākṣasas, do not lick the pressed Soma.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- सखायः (*sakhāyaḥ*)—O friends, praisers
- वः (*vaḥ*)—you
- पुरोजिती (*purojitī*)—who has established victory before you
- अंधसः (*aṃdhasaḥ*)—fit for food (fit to be eaten)
- सुताय (*sutāya*)—pressed
- मादयित्नवे (*mādayitnave*)—possessing a gladdening juice; for the enjoyment of such a Soma
- दीर्घजिह्वं (*dīrghajihvaṃ*)—having a long tongue
- श्वानं (*śvānaṃ*)—the dog
- अपश्चथिष्टन (*apaścathiṣṭana*)—strike and drive away

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O friends, O praisers: for the enjoyment of the Soma juice, which has made victory ready for you, is fit to be eaten, has been pressed, and gladdens, strike and drive far away this dog with the long tongue (so that it may not lick the juice).

##### English translation
For (the drinking of) the effused exhilarating (juice) of the Soma who has placed victory before you, you friends, destroy the long-tongued dog.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

In the Maṇḍalas of the Ṛgveda other than the ninth, the Sūktas are divided in the order of the seers, so ordinarily one Sūkta has one seer; only in some cases are there two or three seers. In the ninth Maṇḍala, however, all the Sūktas have one and the same deity, Pavamāna Soma, so it is natural and unavoidable that the Sūktas of this Maṇḍala have various seers.

### Printed p. 4

Although ordinarily one Sūkta has one seer, in some cases ṛks seen by many seers have been gathered together and regarded as a single Sūkta. Among such Sūktas of this Maṇḍala, the 97th, 101st, 107th and 108th are the chief. In the 97th Sūkta there are Vasiṣṭha and some seers of Vasiṣṭha's gotra; in the 101st Sūkta there are the seers Prajāpati, Manu, and, of Manu's line, Nahuṣa, Yayāti and Andhīgu; in the 107th Sūkta there are the Seven Sages. Who these Seven Sages are we have already explained earlier. In the 108th Sūkta there are seven seers of Vasiṣṭha's line, beginning with Gauravīti. Some Sūktas seen by many seers are found also in the tenth Maṇḍala.

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> यो धार॑या पाव॒कया॑ परिप्र॒स्यंद॑ते सु॒तः ।  
> इंदु॒रश्वो॒ न कृत्त्वः॑ ॥ २ ॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> यः । धारया । पावकया । परिप्रस्यंदते । सुतः ।  
> इंदुः । अश्वः । न । कृत्त्वः ॥ २ ॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> पवित्रेष्टां द्वितीयस्याज्यभागस्य यो धारयेत्येषानुवाक्या । सूत्रितं
> च। पावकवंतावाज्यभागावग्नी रक्षांसि सेधति यो धारया पावकया । आ. २.१२ । इति॥
> सुतोऽभिषुतः कृत्त्वः । कृत्वति कर्मनाम । कर्मणि साधुर्यः इंदुः सोमः
> पावकया पापानां शोधकया धारया परिप्रस्यंदते परितः प्रक्षरति । कथमिव ।
> अश्वो नाश्वो यथा वेगेन प्रगच्छति तद्वत् ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

In the Pavitreṣṭi, this ṛk यो धारया (*yo dhārayā*) is the anuvākyā for the second Ājyabhāga. It is also given in the Sūtra: पावकवंतावाज्यभागावग्नी रक्षांसि सेधति यो धारया पावकया (*pāvakavaṃtāvājyabhāgāvagnī rakṣāṃsi sedhati yo dhārayā pāvakayā*) (Ā. Sū. 2.12). सुतः (*sutaḥ*) means pressed. कृत्त्वः (*kṛttvaḥ*): *kṛtvan* is a name for action; he who is good in action. Indu, that is Soma, flows forth all around (परिप्रस्यंदते (*pariprasyaṃdate*)) in a stream (धारया (*dhārayā*)) that cleanses sins (पावकया (*pāvakayā*)). How? Like a horse (अश्वो न (*aśvo na*)), that is, as a horse goes forth with speed, so he.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- यः (*yaḥ*)—who
- सुतः (*sutaḥ*)—pressed
- कृत्वर्यः (*kṛtvaryaḥ*)—fit for rites; such a one
- इंदुः (*iṃduḥ*)—the god Soma
- पावकया (*pāvakayā*)—purifying
- धारया (*dhārayā*)—in the form of a stream
- अश्वो न (*aśvo na*)—like a swift-going horse
- परिप्रस्यंदते (*pariprasyaṃdate*)—flows

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

The god Soma, who has been pressed and is fit for rites, flows in a purifying stream, like a swift-going horse.

### Printed p. 5

##### English translation
The Indu good for sacred rites which when effused flows forth in a purifying stream, (swift) as a horse :

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

In the Pavitreṣṭi, when the second Ājyabhāga is offered in the fire, this ṛk यो धारया (*yo dhārayā*) is to be recited as the anuvākyā mantra. This is explained by the Sūtra of the Āśvalāyana Śrautasūtra, पावकवंतावाज्यभागावग्नी रक्षांसि सेधति यो धारया पावकया (*pāvakavaṃtāvājyabhāgāvagnī rakṣāṃsi sedhati yo dhārayā pāvakayā*) (Ā. 2-12).

#### Ṛk 3

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> तं दु॒रोष॑म॒भी नरः॒ सोमं॑ वि॒श्वाच्या॑ धि॒या ।  
> य॒ज्ञं हि॑न्वं॒त्यद्रि॑भिः ॥ ३ ॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> तं । दुरोषं । अभि । नरः । सोमं । विश्वाच्या । धिया ।  
> यज्ञं । हिन्वंति । अद्रिभिः ॥ ३ ॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> नरः कर्मनेतार ऋत्विजो दुरोषं । रोषतेर्हिंसार्थस्य रेफलोपे
> दीर्घाभाव ओषतेर्डाहार्थस्य वा खलि रूपमिति संदेहादनवग्रहः ॥ तं दुर्दहं
> दुर्वधं ना यज्ञं यष्टव्यं तं सोमं विश्वाच्या सर्वान्मानांचित्रा धिया
> बुद्ध्याद्रिभिर्गावभिरभि हिन्वंति । अभिप्रेरयंति । अभिषुण्वंतीति यावत् ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

नरः (*naraḥ*) are the priests, the leaders of the rite. दुरोषं (*duroṣaṃ*): Sāyaṇa remarks that it may be from the root रोष (*roṣa*) in the sense of violence, with loss of the r and no lengthening, or from the root ओष (*oṣa*) in the sense of burning; because of this doubt there is no avagraha. Thus the Soma is hard to burn, hard to kill, and is to be sacrificed to. The priests urge it on (अभि हिन्वंति (*abhi hinvaṃti*)), that is, press it, with the stones (अद्रिभिः (*adribhiḥ*)), with a mind (धिया (*dhiyā*)) that is विश्वाच्या (*viśvācyā*). **NOTE**

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- नरः (*naraḥ*)—the priests, leaders of the rite
- दुरोषं (*duroṣaṃ*)—unharmed
- यज्ञं (*yajñaṃ*)—worthy of worship
- तं सोमं (*taṃ somaṃ*)—that same Soma
- विश्वाच्या (*viśvācyā*)—spreading everywhere
- धिया (*dhiyā*)—together with intelligence

#### Ṛk 4

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> सु॒तासो॒ मधु॑मत्तमाः॒ सोमा॒ इंद्रा॑य म॒ुंदिनः॑।  
> प॒वित्र॑वंतो अक्षरन्दे॒वान्ग॑च्छंतु वो॒ मदाः॑ ॥ ४ ॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> सुतासः । मधुमत्त्वा तमाः । सोमाः । इंद्राय । मंदिनः ।  
> पवित्रवंतः । अक्षरन् । देवान् । गच्छंतु । वः । मदाः ॥ ४ ॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> पृष्ठस्य षष्ठेऽहनि ब्राह्मणाच्छंसिशस्त्रे सुतास इति तिस्रः । सूत्रितं च । दधिक्राव्योऽकारिषमित्यनुष्टुप् सुतासो मधुमत्तमा इति च तिस्रः । आ. ८-३ । इति ॥
> मधुमत्तमा अतिशयेन माधुर्योपेताः अत एव मंदिनो मदकराः
> सुतासोऽभिषुताः सोमाः पवित्रवंतः पवित्रॆ वर्तमानाः संत इंद्रायेंद्रार्थमक्षरन् । पात्रेषु क्षरंति । अथ प्रत्यक्षकृतः । वो युष्माकं मदा मदहेतवो
> रसा देवानिंद्रादीन्गच्छंतु ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

On the sixth day of the Pṛṣṭha (Ṣaḍaha), in the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin's śastra, the three ṛks beginning सुतास (*sutāsa*) are used. It is also given in the Sūtra: दधिक्राव्योऽकारिषमित्यनुष्टुप् सुतासो मधुमत्तमा इति च तिस्रः । (*dadhikrāvyo 'kāriṣamityanuṣṭup sutāso madhumattamā iti ca tisraḥ |*) (Ā. Sū. 8-3). The Somas are मधुमत्तमाः (*madhumattamāḥ*), supremely sweet, and therefore मंदिनः (*maṃdinaḥ*), intoxicating; pressed (सुतासः (*sutāsaḥ*)), they stand in the strainer (पवित्रवंतः (*pavitravaṃtaḥ*)) and flow into the vessels for Indra's sake. Then, turning to the Somas directly: "your" intoxicating juices (मदाः (*madāḥ*)), the causes of intoxication, are to go to the gods, Indra and the others.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- मधुमत्तमाः (*madhumattamāḥ*)—endowed with the utmost sweetness
- मुंदिनः (*muṃdinaḥ*)—gladdening
- सुतासः (*sutāsaḥ*)—pressed
- सोमाः (*somāḥ*)—the Soma juices
- पवित्रवंतः (*pavitravaṃtaḥ*)—being in the ten-fringed strainer
- इंद्राय (*iṃdrāya*)—for Indra's drinking
- अक्षरन् (*akṣaran*)—flow (O Soma juices)
- वः (*vaḥ*)—your
- मदाः (*madāḥ*)—gladdening juices
- देवान् (*devān*)—to the gods
- गच्चंतु (*gaccaṃtu*)—let them flow and go

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

The Soma juices, endowed with the utmost sweetness, gladdening and pressed, stand in the ten-fringed strainer and flow for Indra's drinking. O gladdening Soma juices, flow facing the gods.

### Printed p. 7

##### English translation
The sweet-flavoured exhilarating Soma juices effused (and) filtered flow for Indra ; you exhilarating (juices), proceed to the gods.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

For the Śastra mantras recited on the sixth day of the sacrifice called Pṛṣṭha by the priest called Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin, three Ṛks of this sūkta (4–6), beginning with सुतासः (*sutāsaḥ*), are to be recited together. This is set out by the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra (Ā. 8-3) in the sūtra दधिक्रावोऽकारिसमित्यनुष्टुप् सुतासो मधुमत्तमा इति च तिस्त्रः (*dadhikrāvo 'kārisamityanuṣṭup sutāso madhumattamā iti ca tistraḥ*).

#### Ṛk 5

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> इंदु॒रिंद्रा॑य पवत॒ इति॑ दे॒वासो॑ अब्रुवन् ।  
> वा॒चस्पति॑र्मखस्यते॒ विश्व॒स्येशा॑न॒ ओज॑सा ॥ ५ ॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> इंदुः । इंद्राय । पवते । इति । देवासः । अब्रुवन् ।  
> वाचः । पतिः । मखस्यते । विश्वस्य । ईशानः । ओजसा ॥ ५ ॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> इंदुः सोम इंद्रायेंद्रार्थं पवते कलशे क्षरतीति देवासः स्तुतिकारिणः
> स्तोतारोऽब्रुवन् । वदंति । यदा स्तोतार एनं वदंति तदानीं वाचः स्तुतेः
> पतिः पालयिता ! यद्वा । शब्दस्य स्वाम्यत्यंतं शब्दायमान इत्यर्थः । तादृशः
> सोमो मखस्यते । स्तुतिभिः पूजामिच्छति ॥ लालसायां सुगागमः ॥ कीदृशः ।
> ओजसा बलेन विश्वस्य सर्वस्येशानः प्रभुः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Indu, that is Soma, flows for Indra, that is, streams into the vessel for Indra's sake. The देवासः (*devāsaḥ*), the praisers, the singers, "say" this. When the singers speak of him thus, he is the lord, the protector, of वाचः (*vācaḥ*), praise. Or else: he is the master of sound, that is, he makes much sound. Such a Soma मखस्यते (*makhasyate*): he desires worship through praises. Of what sort is he? Through his might (ओजसा (*ojasā*)) he is the ruler, the lord, of all (विश्वस्य (*viśvasya*)).

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- इंदुः (*iṃduḥ*)—Soma, the god
- इंद्राय (*iṃdrāya*)—for Indra's drinking
- पवते (*pavate*)—flows
- इति (*iti*)—thus
- देवासः (*devāsaḥ*)—the praisers
- अब्रुवन् (*abruvan*)—extol
- ओजसा (*ojasā*)—by his own power
- विश्वस्य (*viśvasya*)—of the whole world
- ईशानः (*īśānaḥ*)—the lord
- वाचः (*vācaḥ*)—of praise
- पतिः (*patiḥ*)—the protector; Soma the god
- मखस्यते (*makhasyate*)—desires our worship

### Printed p. 8

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

The praisers extol: "Soma the god flows for Indra's drinking." Soma, who by his own power is the lord of the whole world and the protector of all praises, desires our worship.

##### English translation

#### Ṛk 6

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> स॒हस्र॑धारः पवते समु॒द्रो वा॑चमींख॒यः ।  
> सोमः॒ पती॑ रयी॒णां सखेंद्र॑स्य दि॒वेदि॑वे ॥ ६ ॥

- दिवेदिने (*divedine*)—every day
- पवते (*pavate*)—flows

#### Ṛk 7

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> आ॒यं पू॒षा र॒यिर्भगः॒ सोमः॑ पुना॒नो अ॑र्षति ।  
> पति॒र्विश्व॑स्य॒ भूम॑नो॒ व्य॑ख्य॒द्रोद॑सी उ॒भे ॥ ७ ॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> अयं । पूषा । रयिः । भगः । सोमः । पुनानः । अर्षति ।  
> पतिः । विश्वस्य भूमनः । वि । अख्यत् । रोदसी इति । उभे इति ॥ ७ ॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> पूषा पोषकः सर्वेषां भगो भजनीयो रयिर्धनहेतुरयं सोमः

> व्यख्यत् । स्वतेजसा प्रकाशयति । अनेन लोकद्वयवर्तित्वं सूचितं ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

This Soma is पूषा (*pūṣā*), the nourisher of all; भगः (*bhagaḥ*), to be worshipped; रयिः (*rayiḥ*), the cause of wealth. Being purified by the filter, he अर्षति (*arṣati*), goes to the vessel. Likewise Soma, the lord, the protector, of all beings (भूमनः (*bhūmanaḥ*)), has made both रोदसी (*rodasī*), heaven and earth, shine by his own splendour. By this his presence in both worlds is indicated.

- पुनानः (*punānaḥ*)—purified in the ten-fold filter
- अर्षति (*arṣati*)—flows toward the vessel
- विश्वस्य (*viśvasya*)—of all
- भूमनः (*bhūmanaḥ*)—beings
- पतिः (*patiḥ*)—the protector, Soma
- उभे रोदसी (*ubhe rodasī*)—both heaven and earth
- व्यख्यत् (*vyakhyat*)—has caused to shine

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

Soma the god, nourisher of all, worshipful, giver of wealth, is purified in the ten-fold filter and flows toward the vessel. Soma, the protector of all beings, has made heaven and earth both shine.

#### Ṛk 8

## Sūkta 102 (Maṇḍala 9)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> क्राणा शिशुरित्यष्टर्चं षष्ठं सूक्तं । आप्त्यस्य त्रितस्यार्षं । इदमुत्तराणि
> चत्वारि सूक्तान्षौष्टिहानि । पवमानः सोमो देवता । तथा चानुक्रम्यते ।
> क्राणाष्टौ त्रित औष्टिहं वा इति ॥ गतो विनियोगः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The sūkta क्राणा शिशुः (*krāṇā śiśuḥ*) has eight Ṛks and is the sixth sūkta. The seer is Trita Āptya. This sūkta and the four following sūktas are in the Auṣṭiha metre. The deity is Pavamāna Soma. So the Anukramaṇī says: क्राणाष्टौ त्रित औष्टिहं वा इति॥ (*krāṇāṣṭau trita auṣṭihaṃ vā iti‖*) The application (viniyoga) has been stated before.

##### Anuvāda

This sūkta beginning क्राणा शिशुः (*krāṇā śiśuḥ*) is the sixth sūkta in the sixth anuvāka. It has eight Ṛks. The seer of this sūkta is one called Trita Āstya. The four sūktas following this one are in the Uṣṇik metre. The deity is Pavamāna Soma. In the Anukramaṇī it is said: क्राणाष्टौ त्रित औष्ठिहु वै (*krāṇāṣṭau trita auṣṭhihu vai*). The application of the sūkta has been stated before.

- विश्वा (*viśvā*)—all
- प्रिया (*priyā*)—pleasing oblations
- परि भुवत् (*pari bhuvat*)—pervades
- अध (*adha*)—and
- द्विता (*dvitā*)—exists divided in both heaven and earth

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

- यत् (*yat*)—when

- अध (*adha*)—immediately then
- यज्ञस्य (*yajñasya*)—of the sacrifice
- सप्त धामभिः (*sapta dhāmabhiḥ*)—with the seven metres beginning with Gāyatrī, which are the supports
- प्रियं (*priyaṃ*)—the gladdening Soma (the priests praise)

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

- अप ऋणोः (*apa ṛṇoḥ*)—drive far away

## Sūkta 103 (Maṇḍala 9)
Sūkta 103. Maṇḍala 9; Anuvāka 6; Sūkta 103. Aṣṭaka 7; Adhyāya 5; Varga 6. Number of Ṛks in the sūkta: 6. Ṛṣi: Dvita Āptya. Deity: Pavamāna Soma. Metre: Uṣṇik.

Sāyaṇa's introduction: the sūkta beginning प्र पुनानाय (*pra punānāya*) has six Ṛks and is the seventh sūkta. Its seer is Dvita Āptya; metre and deity are as before. The Anukramaṇī says: प्र पुनानाय षड्द्वित आप्त्य इति (*pra punānāya ṣaḍdvita āptya iti*). The viniyoga has been given already.

> वाघद्भिः । वाघत इत्यृत्विज्ञाम । हविषां प्रेरकैर्ऋत्विग्भिर्वाघदूह्यमानोऽमर्त्यो मनुष्यधर्मरहितः । यद्वा । वाघद्भिर्य्तिग्भिः पुनानः पूयमानो
> वाघत् स्तोत्काणां यष्ट्णाणां धनादीनि प्रापयन् परिगच्छ ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Soma, mounting the same chariot as Indra, go about to the divine powers (स्वधा (*svadhā*), strengths) that belong to the gods, that is, to the divine hosts. Soma is being purified by the वाघद्भिः (*vāghadbhiḥ*) (वाघत (*vāghata*) means the priests), who are the bearers of the oblations; he is immortal, free of human nature. Or else: being purified by the priests, go about bringing wealth and the like to the praisers and sacrificers.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- पुनानः (*punānaḥ*)—one who has attained purification
- वाघद्भिः (*vāghadbhiḥ*)—by the priests
- वाघत् (*vāghat*)—one who is praised
- अमर्त्यः (*amartyaḥ*)—free from the nature of death; you who are so
- इंद्रेण (*iṃdreṇa*)—with Indra
- सरथं (*sarathaṃ*)—mounting the one common chariot
- दैवीः (*daivīḥ*)—for the gods
- स्वधाः अनु (*svadhāḥ anu*)—towards the host that is of the nature of strength
- परि याहि (*pari yāhi*)—go and join

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Soma, you who have attained purification, are praised by the priests and are free from mortality: mount the same chariot as Indra, go towards the host of gods endowed with strength, and join them.

### Printed p. 32

##### English translation
Proceed in the same chariot with Indra to the celestial hosts, a priest purified by priests, immortal.

Soma, who like a horse desires battle, is shining, is pressed out for the gods, pervades the vessels and has attained purification, flows encompassing everything on all sides.

## Sūkta 104 (Maṇḍala 9)
**Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya (introduction)**
Not in a numbered block here; the Sanskrit introduction runs as follows in Gist form.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

In the seventh anuvāka there are eleven sūktas; of them the first is the six-verse सखाय (*sakhāya*) sūkta. Its seers are Parvata and Nārada of the Kāṇva line, or else two Apsarases, daughters of Kaśyapa, named Śikhaṇḍinī. Metre and deity are as before. The Anukramaṇī says: सखायः पर्वतनारदौ काश्यप्यै द्वे शिखंडिन्यौ वाप्सरसाविति (*sakhāyaḥ parvatanāradau kāśyapyai dve śikhaṃḍinyau vāpsarasāviti*). The use (viniyoga) of the sūkta has been stated.

##### Anuvāda

In the seventh anuvāka of the ninth maṇḍala there are eleven sūktas. Of them, this sūkta beginning सखायः (*sakhāyaḥ*) is the first, with six Ṛks. Its seers are the two sages Parvata and Nārada, born in the Kaṇva line; or else the seers are two Apsaras women, daughters of the sage Kaśyapa, named Śikhaṇḍinī. As in the preceding sūkta, the deity and metre are the same. In the Anukramaṇī it is said: सखायः पर्वतनारदौ काश्यपौ द्वे शिखंडिन्या वाप्सरसौ (*sakhāyaḥ parvatanāradau kāśyapau dve śikhaṃḍinyā vāpsarasau*). The use of the sūkta was stated earlier.

Sūkta 104. Maṇḍala 9; Anuvāka 7; Sūkta 104. Aṣṭaka 2; Adhyāya 5; Varga 7. Number of Ṛks in the sūkta: 6.
Seer: Parvata and Nārada, or the two Śikhaṇḍinīs, Kāśyapī Apsarases.
Deity: Pavamāna Soma.
Metre: Uṣṇih.

#### Ṛk 1

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> सखा॑य॒ आ निषी॑दत पुना॒नाय॒ प्र गा॑यत ।  
> शिशुं॒ न य॒ज्ञैः परि॑ भूषत श्रि॒ये ॥ १ ॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> सखायः । आ । नि । सीदत । प्रसानाय । प्र । गायत ।  
> शिशुं । स । यज्ञैः । परि । भूषत । त्रिये ॥ १ ॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

### Printed p. 34

> हे सखायः सखिभूताः स्तोतार ऋत्विजः आ नि षीदत । स्तोतुमुपविशत ।
> अथ पुनानाय पूयमानाय सोमाय प्र गायत । प्रकर्षेण गायत । तमभिष्टुत । ततोऽभिषुतं सोमं यज्ञैर्यजनीयैर्हविर्भिमिश्रणैश्च श्रिये शोभनार्थं परि भूषत । परितोऽलंकुरुत । तत्र दृष्टांतः । शिशुं न यथा शिशुं बालं
> पुत्रं पितर आभरणैरलंकुर्वंति तद्वत् ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O friends, praisers, priests, sit down to praise. Then sing forth to Soma who is being purified, sing with excellence, praise him. Then adorn the pressed Soma all round, for beauty, with the sacrificial offerings and mixtures of oblations. The illustration: as parents adorn a child, a son, with ornaments, so.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- सखायः (*sakhāyaḥ*)—O priests, who are friends
- आ नि षीदत (*ā ni ṣīdata*)—sit down
- पुनानाय (*punānāya*)—praise Soma, the purifier
- प्र गायत (*pra gāyata*)—sing well
- शिशुं न (*śiśuṃ na*)—as mothers adorn their sons
- यज्ञैः (*yajñaiḥ*)—with oblations, the means of worship
- श्रिये (*śriye*)—for the increase of prosperity
- परि भूषत (*pari bhūṣata*)—adorn Soma

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O priests, friends, sit down. Recite the song that praises Soma the purifier. As mothers adorn their sons, adorn Soma with oblations, the means of worship, for the increase of prosperity.

##### English translation
Sit down, friends, sing to the filtered Soma, decorate him with offerings to beautify him as (parents decorate) a child.

#### Ṛk 2

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> समी॑ व॒त्सं न मा॒तृभिः॑ सृ॒जता॑ गय॒साध॑नं ।  
> दे॒वा॒व्यं१॒॑मद॑म॒भि द्विश॑वसं ॥ २ ॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> सं । ईमिति । वत्सं । न । मातृभिः । सृजत । गयसाधनं ।  
> देव अव्यं । मदं । अभि । द्विशवसं ॥ २ ॥

> प्रवर्ज्येऽभिष्टवे समि वत्समित्येका । सूत्रितं च । समि वत्सं न मातृभिः
> सं वत्स इव मातृभिः । आ. ४.७ । इति ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

In the Pravargya, in the Abhiṣṭava, the one Ṛk समि वत्सं (*sami vatsaṃ*) is used. It is stated in the sūtra: समि वत्सं न मातृभिः सं वत्स इव मातृभिः (*sami vatsaṃ na mātṛbhiḥ saṃ vatsa iva mātṛbhiḥ*) (Ā. 4.7).

### Printed p. 35

> हे ऋत्विजः गयसाधनं गृहस्य साधनमीमेनं सोमं मातृभिर्मातृभूताभिर्वसतीवरीभिः सं सृजत । संमिश्रयत । कथमिव । वत्सं न यथा वत्सं
> मातृभिर्गोभिः संयोजयंति तद्वत् । कीदृशं । देवाव्यं देवानां रक्षकं मदं
> मदनहेतुं द्विशवसं द्विगुणवेगमतिशयितबलं वा । यद्वा । द्वयोर्लोकयोस्तत
> स्थिता देवमनुष्या इत्यर्थः । तेषां हविर्धनप्रदानेन वर्धयितारं तं सोममभ्रि
> सं सृजत ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O priests, unite this Soma, the support of the house, with the maternal waters (वसतीवरी (*vasatīvarī*)), as they join a calf with its mother cows. He is protector of the gods, cause of exhilaration, of twofold speed or surpassing strength. Or else: the two worlds, and the gods and men stationed there; he increases them by granting oblations and wealth. Mix that Soma.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- (O priests) वत्सं (*vatsaṃ*)—a calf
- मातृभिः न (*mātṛbhiḥ na*)—as one joins it with its mother cow
- देवाव्यं (*devāvyaṃ*)—protector of the gods
- मदं (*madaṃ*)—cause of delight
- द्विशवसं (*dviśavasaṃ*)—possessing two kinds of strength
- गयसाधनं (*gayasādhanaṃ*)—the accomplisher for the house
- ईं (*īṃ*)—this Soma
- मातृभिः (*mātṛbhiḥ*)—with the waters such as the Vasatīvarī
- अभि सं सृजत (*abhi saṃ sṛjata*)—mix

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O priests, as one joins a young calf with its mother, mix this Soma—protector of the gods, cause of delight, possessing two kinds of strength, accomplisher for the house—with the Vasatīvarī and other waters.

##### English translation
Associate him the support of the mansion with the maternal (waters) as the calf (with the mother)—the protector of the gods, the exhilarator, endowed with twofold strength.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

The use of this Ṛk समि वत्सं (*sami vatsaṃ*) in the Abhiṣṭava of the Pravargya rite is explained by the sūtra of the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra: समि वत्सं न मातृभिः सं वत्स इव मातृभिः (*sami vatsaṃ na mātṛbhiḥ saṃ vatsa iva mātṛbhiḥ*) (Ā. 4-7).

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Purify Soma, the accomplisher of strength and of the increase of wealth and the like, with the filter. This Soma, so that he may be for vigour and for the gods' drinking, and so that he may bring the greatest happiness to Mitra and to Varuṇa—purify him thus.

- यथा (*yathā*)—in what manner

- वरुणाय (*varuṇāya*)—to Varuṇa
- शंतमः (*śaṃtamaḥ*)—he becomes the giver of happiness; in that manner
- दक्षसाधनं (*dakṣasādhanaṃ*)—Soma, the accomplisher of strength
- पुनात (*punāta*)—purify

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

In the manner in which this Soma becomes fit for strength and for the drinking of the gods, and becomes the giver of happiness to Mitra and Varuṇa, in that manner purify this Soma, the accomplisher of strength.

##### English translation
Purify the bestower of strength so that he may make for invigoration
for the banquet (of the gods) and may grant much happiness to Mitra and
to Varuna.
