**Prefatory note: In praise of Agni**

In this prefatory note to the first Adhyāya of the fourth Aṣṭaka, the hymns up to the 28th Sūkta of the 5th Maṇḍala are hymns in praise of Agni. These portions were composed by seers born in the Atri lineage. These seers praised the great qualities of Agni and described Agni, who is of the nature of truth, as the supporter of the whole world. In this section some of Agni's chief special qualities are praised in many ways. Chiefly:

### Printed p. 2

(1) Agni is the conductor of the sacrifice, the bearer of the oblation, and through the sacrifice the supporter of the whole world, gods and men and the rest. Therefore all sacrifices rest upon him alone: this is the matter.

(2) This Agni is renowned as Jātavedas and as Aṅgiras. Since Aṅgiras is the original cause of the kindling of the sacrificial fire, on many occasions identity of Agni and Aṅgiras is shown, and Agni too is addressed as Aṅgiras.

(3) Since this Agni abides in three seats, he is also renowned as Trita, and the fact that he thereby declares his all-pervasiveness is also a principal point.

(4) Since the sacrifice is the support of the life of the gods, and since Agni is the conductor of the sacrifice, all the gods rely upon Agni alone for their existence. Agni, the cause of the existence of gods, men and the rest, is the Supreme Reality, and is praised as स हि सत्यो भवति (*sa hi satyo bhavati*).

Thus the description of Agni's principal qualities is the subject of praise of these seers. How these points are described in the present section is briefly examined here. First of all:

**Agni as the bearer of oblations**

Before everything else, in this section Agni's character as bearer of the oblation is praised. The conduct of the sacrifice, which is the basis of all the affairs of the world, is indispensable for the continuance and course of the world. If this sacrifice is to be performed completely, Agni himself must be the accomplisher of this work. Therefore he alone must be the chief Hotṛ and the carrier of the oblation. मन्ये त्वा जातवेदसं स हव्यावक्षानुषक् (*manye tvā jātavedasaṃ sa havyāvakṣānuṣak*): the seer first expresses his faith, saying that it is our firm belief that Agni, who is of the form of Jātavedas, is the unceasing bearer of the oblation. (5. 9. 1.)

The gods, who believe the sacrificial oblation to be their very food, become agitated even if Agni, the distributor of the oblation, vanishes for a little while. On such occasions they humbly entreat Agni and pray that he perform the work of carrying the oblation. Some such occasions are described in the Ṛk-saṃhitā itself. For example, the account of the 51st Sūkta of Ṛ. Saṃ. 10 may be examined here. In this Sūkta, which is being cited, the gods entreat Agni for the work of carrying the oblation, and Agni points out a condition dear to him and gives the assurance that if the gods will keep that condition he will perform the work of carrying the oblation. The account runs as follows.

The gods: O Agni, while you were hiding in the waters and out of sight, the covering that concealed you was exceedingly thick. Yet, O Jātavedas, one among us alone, knowing your manifold form, was ready to choose you. Please be a benefactor to us.

Agni: O gods, who among you knows my endless forms? To which of you, and how, have my manifold forms become visible? O Mitra and Varuṇa, where is my dwelling, which carries the gods and is radiant?

The gods: O Agni, of the form of Jātavedas, we searched for you a long time, hidden within the waters and the plants. Then, from your ten places of deposit, and because your flame spreads, Yama recognised you by way of those directions.

Agni: O gods, I hid in order to escape the work of the Hotṛ. Fearing the burden of that work, lest you should again appoint me to it, I came here and hid myself. For that very reason my various forms too are hidden within these waters. I, who am of the form of Agni, do not wish to take up that burden again.

The gods: O Agni, the devout Manu wishes to perform the sacrifice and offer the oblation. Therefore come to help him, and through him us. To be hidden here in the cave is an ornament to you as well. Make the paths along which the gods travel straight, and with a generous mind carry our oblations to us and be a benefactor to us.

Agni: As a charioteer who drives the chariot takes the road and performs his task, so my elder brothers too were engaged in this very work of carrying the oblation. But, unable to bear that burden, they died. O gods, frightened by that experience, I came far away and hid myself here. As a deer is startled by the twang of the hunter's bow, I too trembled in fear.

The gods: O Agni, however heavy a task may fall to your share, we shall grant that it does not become the cause of your weakening or death. O Agni, who are of the form of Jātavedas, carry the oblation of the gods, who grant you freedom from death, and distribute it to them, and be of help.

Agni: O gods, if I am to bear this burden of the rite, the oblation that is full of essence and consists of the Prayāja and Anūyāja must be set apart for me alone. Offer me the ghee, which is the essence of the waters, and the Puruṣa, which is the essence of the plants, and grant me long life.

The gods: O Agni, let the oblations consisting of the essential Prayājas and Anūyājas be yours. Let the ghee, the essence of the waters, and all the vigorous oblations be yours. Let the chief portion of the sacrifice be yours, and let the four quarters and the intermediate regions bow to you in humility and show you reverence. Thus this occasion describes the greatness of Agni.

This same account in narrative form is given in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā in the following way.

अग्नेस्त्रयो ज्यायाग्‌ंसो भ्रातर आसन्ते देवेभ्यो हव्यं वहंतः प्रामायंत सोऽग्निरबिभेदित्थं वावस्य आर्तिमारिष्यतीति स निलायत सोऽपः प्राविशत्तं देवताः प्रॆषमैच्छन्तं मत्स्यः प्राब्रवीत्तमशपद्धियाधिया त्वा वध्यासुर्यो मा प्रावोच इति तस्मान्मत्स्यं धियाधिया घ्नंति शप्पो हि तमन्वदिंदन्तमब्रुवन्नुप न आ वर्तस्व हव्यं नो वहेति सोऽब्रवीद्वरं वृणै यदेव गृहीतस्याहुतस्य बहिः परिधि स्कंदात्तन्ने भ्रातृणां भागधेयमसदिति । (*agnestrayo jyāyāgṃso bhrātara āsante devebhyo havyaṃ vahaṃtaḥ prāmāyaṃta so 'gnirabibheditthaṃ vāvasya ārtimāriṣyatīti sa nilāyata so 'paḥ prāviśattaṃ devatāḥ prèṣamaicchantaṃ matsyaḥ prābravīttamaśapaddhiyādhiyā tvā vadhyāsuryo mā prāvoca iti tasmānmatsyaṃ dhiyādhiyā ghnaṃti śappo hi tamanvadiṃdantamabruvannupa na ā vartasva havyaṃ no vaheti so 'bravīdvaraṃ vṛṇai yadeva gṛhītasyāhutasya bahiḥ paridhi skaṃdāttanne bhrātṛṇāṃ bhāgadheyamasaditi |*)
(Tai. Saṃ. 2-6-6-1)

Agni had three elder brothers. All of them, engaged in the work of carrying the oblation to the gods, had no strength to bear the burden of that work and died of that very exertion. Agni, realising that if he came into the sight of the gods that burden would cling to him, and that the fate of his brothers was ready for him too, escaped from there, hid in the water and disappeared. The gods tried to find him. Then a fish told them that Agni was there. Then Agni cursed it: "Since you have brought me to the notice of the gods, may men kill you the moment they have formed the intention." As a result of this curse men kill shoals of fish as they please. Then the gods approached Agni and prayed: "O Agni, come, carry the oblation for us, and be a benefactor to us." Then Agni said: "I will agree if I am granted a boon." It was this: "Whatever portion of the sacrificial oblation falls outside the paridhi on the sacrificial altar, all of it should go to my brothers, who died for your sake. Then I shall agree to this work." Then the gods agreed and kept that condition, so it is said.

In this account described in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā there are two new points. That the fish told the gods of Agni's whereabouts while he was hidden, and were cursed by him, and that Agni prayed that his brothers should receive a share of the oblation, are not in the Ṛk-saṃhitā. And that there the gods gave the assurance of bringing about for Agni freedom from death is not in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā.

In the same manner, in another passage of the Ṛk-saṃhitā also, there is a conversation between Indra and Agni, which shows that Indra and the other gods are benefited by Agni himself, and the supreme excellence of Agni. The account of that occasion is as follows.

इमं नो अग्न उपयज्ञमेहि पंचयामं त्रिवृतं सप्ततंतुं । असो हव्यवाळुतनः पुरोगा ज्योगेव दीर्घं तम आशयिष्ठाः। (*imaṃ no agna upayajñamehi paṃcayāmaṃ trivṛtaṃ saptataṃtuṃ | aso havyavāḷutanaḥ purogā jyogeva dīrghaṃ tama āśayiṣṭhāḥ|*)
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-124-1)

### Printed p. 5

O Agni, come to this sacrifice of ours, which has five kinds of oblation, is threefold in nature and has seven strands, and take part in it. Being the chief among us, become the bearer of our oblations. You have been hidden in this darkness for a long time; this is not right.

अदेवाद्वेदः प्रचता गुहा यन् प्रपश्यमानो अमृतत्वमेमि । शिवंयत्संतमशिवो जहामि स्वात्सख्यादरणीं नाभिमेमि ॥ (*adevādvedaḥ pracatā guhā yan prapaśyamāno amṛtatvamemi | śivaṃyatsaṃtamaśivo jahāmi svātsakhyādaraṇīṃ nābhimemi ‖*)
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-124-2)

Agni: O gods, as you pray, from the non-divine place and from non-divine things I impel the divine form. Emerging from this darkness I shall manifest immortality. In the pure time of sacrifice, the moment I see impurity I go far away. Remembering our former bond, I accept your invitation. Thus the dialogue of Indra and Agni, just like the preceding occasion, fully shows that in the work of carrying the oblation the gods entreat Agni and that no one except Agni has the ability for it. That same point is stated in the present context as स हव्या वक्ष्यानुषक् (*sa havyā vakṣyānuṣak*) (5-9-1).

**The relation of Agni and Aṅgiras**

As, in the account explained above, it was Yama among the gods who recognised Agni hidden in the waters, so in some other places it is clear that the Aṅgirases found this same Agni, hidden in a cave, and worshipped him. This is त्वामग्ने अंगिरसो गुहा हितमन्वविंदन् (*tvāmagne aṃgiraso guhā hitamanvaviṃdan*): the Aṅgirases searched for and found Agni, hidden within all the trees and the waters. (5-11-6) For this reason on many occasions Agni himself is addressed as Aṅgiras. (5-11-7) By sentences such as त्वं नो आग्नॆ अंगिरः स्तुतः (*tvaṃ no āgnè aṃgiraḥ stutaḥ*) (5-10-7), these two points, identity of Agni and the Aṅgirases in one place and close relationship in another, both show the eminence of the Aṅgirases.

In the Ṛk-saṃhitā about ninety Sūktas have Aṅgiras and the seers of his line as their authors. Seers such as Vasiṣṭha, Bhāradvāja, Atri, Vāmadeva, Viśvāmitra and Gṛtsamada praise Aṅgiras as their ancient forefather. Virūpa, Navagva and Daśagva all belong to the line of this Aṅgiras. The sentence of the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa, "In the beginning there were only two groups, the Ādityas and the Aṅgirases," shows the eminence of this line. It is said that this seer Aṅgiras and his descendants, leaving on this earth the power of their good works, went to heaven and there attained the state of Pitṛs. By the power of these works they are seers, Pitṛs and gods. The harmony and eminence in all their deeds show their greatness.

### Printed p. 6

In the Ṛk-saṃhitā the relation of identity of Aṅgiras with many gods is stated. Among them, his relation with Agni and Indra is the closest. Since the first work of producing the sacrificial fire belonged to the seer Aṅgiras, Agni is specified as Aṅgiras. In many sentences such as सहसः सुनो अंगिरः (*sahasaḥ suno aṃgiraḥ*) Agni is called Aṅgiras. (8-60-2; 8-74-11; 8-77-5; 8-84-4; 8-102-17) अंगिरोनामकमुनिः कारणत्वात् अंगाररूपस्य अग्नेरंगिरस्त्व (*aṃgironāmakamuniḥ kāraṇatvāt aṃgārarūpasya agneraṃgirastva*): Sāyaṇa has stated that because the Aṅgirases were the cause of Agni's manifestation, Agni too is designated by the name Aṅgiras. (Sā. Bhā. 1-1-6) Because he was worshipped by these same Aṅgirases, Agni is regarded as the best among the Aṅgirases. The oblation, hallowed by mantra, carries the sacrificer who has offered it to Agni, who is the best of all the Aṅgirases. (1-31-2; 1-45-3; 1-127-2; 8-28-10; 8-43-18) Because they had this kind of power they attained godhood and the state of Pitṛs: thus in one or two places the matter of the Aṅgirases is briefly stated in respect of their greatness. [Ṛgveda Saṃhitā, Part 1, pp. 641-642; Part 8, pp. 188-189] In the Brāhmaṇas, Upaniṣads and the rest also, the direct relation of Agni and the Aṅgirases is stated in places in the form of praise. Those points are also briefly stated here. The very point that is indicated in the present Adhyāya by the sentence अंगिरसो गुहा हितमन्विंदन् (*aṃgiraso guhā hitamanviṃdan*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 5-11-6) is also praised by the Brāhmaṇas and Upaniṣads.

As stated in the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa, in explaining the Agnimāruta śastra earlier, it is said that when Prajāpati became enamoured of his own daughter, his seed, by the grace of Agni Vaiśvānara, became the cause of the origin of Bhṛgu, the Ādityas and the Aṅgirases. येऽंगारा आसंस्तेऽंगिरसोऽभवन् (*ye 'ṃgārā āsaṃste 'ṃgiraso 'bhavan*) (Ai. Brā. 3-34) At Indra's consecration these same Aṅgirases are entreated to send the royal throne together with the Maruts. (Ai. Brā. 8-12) There they are regarded as gods. Moreover, in the narrative that states the relation between the Ādityas and the Aṅgirases, it is explained that the earth, burning in nature, which was given to the Aṅgirases as a fee, they threw away without checking its burning, and so they became the cause of its present uneven (unsettled) state. (Ai. Brā. 6-34, 35) The dealings between the Aṅgirases and the Ādityas for achieving the state of heaven are described by Śrutis such as the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa (2-2-3-5) and the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa (3-2-1-10). This description will be given at a suitable place later. Since the subject of this section is to state the relation of Agni and the Aṅgirases in the present context, which is one of the

**The power of expansion of Agni**

In this same Adhyāya the epithets Trita and Adhrigu make known special qualities of Agni. The word Trita has various meanings. It may be a proper name denoting a person called Trita. (The history concerning the person Trita was given earlier in Part 8, pp. 443-447.) Or this word may be an epithet of Indra and other gods and make known their special qualities. In what order this epithet indicates the special qualities of which gods, and what the propriety of this use of the word is, will be stated when the occasion arises. In the present context (5-9-4) Sāyaṇa's explanation is that it is Agni pervading the three places. Nirukta sentences such as मूर्धा दिवः (*mūrdhā divaḥ*) describe this nature of Trita.
