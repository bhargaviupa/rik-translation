Part 28

The eighth adhyāya in the seventh aṣṭaka

The forty-first sūkta in the tenth maṇḍala

**Introduction (Jīḷike)**

In the twenty-seventh part of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā the first forty sūktas of the tenth maṇḍala were completed. This twenty-eighth part begins with the forty-first sūkta; the first seven adhyāyas of the seventh aṣṭaka and twenty vargas of the eighth adhyāya are completed in it. The seer of the sūkta now beginning, the forty-first, which opens with समानमु त्यं (*samānamu tyaṃ*), is Suhastya, the son of the woman Ghoṣā. It is therefore necessary to describe briefly here the background of Ghoṣā, the mother of this seer, and of the seer Suhastya.

### Printed p. 2

It was explained earlier, with quotations from the Nirukta and other texts, what differing opinions exist about the principle the Aśvin deities represent. Many occasions that tell of the greatness of these deities were also set out. Many seers have praised them. Among them, the 39th and 40th sūktas of the tenth maṇḍala, composed by the woman seer Ghoṣā, and the 41st sūkta, composed by her son Suhastya, are well known. In these sūktas the seers praise the extraordinary great qualities of the Aśvins.

Although the sūktas composed by the seer named Ghoṣā are only two, the great help the Aśvins did her and her greatness were praised earlier in many mantras. Because she was an excellent devotee, these deities showed her great favour. Giving her personal background, the Bṛhaddevatā speaks of her thus:

आसीत्काक्षीवती घोषा पापरोगेण दुर्भगा । उवास षष्टिं वर्षाणि पितुरेव गृहे पुरु ॥ (*āsītkākṣīvatī ghoṣā pāparogeṇa durbhagā | uvāsa ṣaṣṭiṃ varṣāṇi pitureva gṛhe puru ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 7, 42)

A disfiguring and dreadful disease befell the woman Ghoṣā, daughter of Kakṣīvān. Her beauty was destroyed. For that very reason, without marriage, she had to live sixty years in her father's house.

आतस्थे महतीं चिंतां न पुत्रो न पतिर्मम । जरां प्राप्ता मुधा तस्मात् प्रपद्येऽहं शुभस्पती ॥ (*ātasthe mahatīṃ ciṃtāṃ na putro na patirmama | jarāṃ prāptā mudhā tasmāt prapadye 'haṃ śubhaspatī ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 7. 45)

"Unmarried, without husband and without son, I have grown old. My life is going to waste." Thus constant worry beset her. So she resolved: "I shall take refuge in the Aśvin deities, famed as Śubhaspatis (lords of radiance), praise them and obtain their grace"; and she prayed and cried out to the Aśvins.

यथैत् मामकस्तात आराध्यावाप यौवनं । आयुरारोग्यमैश्वर्यं सर्वभूतहने विषं ॥ (*yathait māmakastāta ārādhyāvāpa yauvanaṃ | āyurārogyamaiśvaryaṃ sarvabhūtahane viṣaṃ ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 7. 44)

"My father worshipped these same Aśvin deities and by their grace obtained youth, long life, health, strength, and a poison able to kill all beings. So I too shall worship them and obtain my wish."

### Printed p. 3

रूपवत्तां च सौभाग्यमहं तस्य सुता यदि । ममापि मंत्राः प्रादुः स्युरैस्तोष्टे मयाश्विन् ॥ (*rūpavattāṃ ca saubhāgyamahaṃ tasya sutā yadi | mamāpi maṃtrāḥ prāduḥ syuraistoṣṭe mayāśvin ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 7. 45)

"If I too come to know the way of praising and worshipping the Aśvin deities according to rule, I shall worship them and by their grace obtain beauty and well-being. As my father obtained a boon from them, I too shall obtain my wishes." Thus she reflected on the manner of praising the Aśvins.

चिंतयंतीति सूक्ते द्वे यो वां परि ददर्श सा। स्तुषौ तावश्विनौ देवौ प्रीत् तस्या भगांतरं ॥ (*ciṃtayaṃtīti sūkte dve yo vāṃ pari dadarśa sā| stuṣau tāvaśvinau devau prīt tasyā bhagāṃtaraṃ ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 7. 46)

While she was thus absorbed in meditation she received the vision of two sūktas, beginning with the mantras यो वां परि (*yo vāṃ pari*) and रथं यांतं (*rathaṃ yāṃtaṃ*) (10-39; 10-40), and praised the Aśvins with them. The Aśvin deities, pleased with this, entered her body.

प्रविश्य विजरारोगां सुभगां चक्रतुश्च ता । भर्तारं ददतुस्तस्यॆ सुहस्त्यं च सुतं मुनिं ॥ (*praviśya vijarārogāṃ subhagāṃ cakratuśca tā | bhartāraṃ dadatustasyè suhastyaṃ ca sutaṃ muniṃ ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 7. 47)

Then they removed her old age, freed her from disease and gave her a beautiful form. They also bestowed an excellent husband and an excellent son named Suhastya. Suhastya too is a renowned seer, like her.

ददतुस्तत्सुवर्णाभ्यां यन्नासत्येति कीर्त्यते । काक्षीवत्कै च घोषायै न तस्यामाजुरोऽनया ॥ (*dadatustatsuvarṇābhyāṃ yannāsatyeti kīrtyate | kākṣīvatkai ca ghoṣāyai na tasyāmājuro 'nayā ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 6. 48)

The manner in which the Aśvin deities came with their excellent suparṇas (horses having wings) and favoured Ghoṣā is described in laudatory sentences such as अमाजुरश्चिद्भवथः (*amājuraścidbhavathaḥ*) and न तस्य विद्म (*na tasya vidma*) (10-39-2; 10. 40. 11).

Ghoṣā is praised as belonging to the company of Brahmavādinīs, who have the power of supersensory vision and excellent skill in ritual action. These Brahmavādinīs are divided into three groups. Each of the three groups possesses a different kind of power and the ability to see the deities; the power of the woman seers of these groups is described thus:

### Printed p. 4

नवकः प्रथमस्ता सां वर्गस्तुष्टाव देवताः । यषिभिर्देवताभिश्च समूदे मध्यमो गणः ॥ (*navakaḥ prathamastā sāṃ vargastuṣṭāva devatāḥ | yaṣibhirdevatābhiśca samūde madhyamo gaṇaḥ ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 2, 85)

Of these, the first group has nine members. They praised the deities but did not speak directly with them. Those of the second group alone spoke directly both with the seers and with the deities.

आत्मनो भाववृत्तानि जगौ वर्गस्तथोत्तमः । उत्तमस्य तु वर्गस्य य ऋषिः सैव देवता ॥ (*ātmano bhāvavṛttāni jagau vargastathottamaḥ | uttamasya tu vargasya ya ṛṣiḥ saiva devatā ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 2. 86)

The seers who composed a sūkta praising their own bhāvavṛtta, that is, the special states of change of the self, are of the highest group. The seers of this group who composed the sūkta are themselves the deities named in that sūkta. Thus the Brahmavādinīs are of three kinds. Stating that Ghoṣā belongs to the first group, the text says:

घोषा, गोधा, विश्ववारा, अपालोपनिषन्निषत् । ब्रह्मजाया जुहूर्नाम अगस्त्यस्य स्वसादितिः ॥ (*ghoṣā, godhā, viśvavārā, apālopaniṣanniṣat | brahmajāyā juhūrnāma agastyasya svasāditiḥ ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 2, 82)

Ghoṣā (10-39, 40); Godhā; Viśvavārā (5. 28); Apālā (7. 91); Upaniṣat and Niṣat (composers of the khila sūkta beginning प्र धारयंत (*pra dhārayaṃta*)); Juhū Brahmajāyā (10. 109); the sister of Agastya (10. 60. 6); Aditi (some mantras in 4. 18). Thus it is indicated that Ghoṣā is one of the Brahmavādinīs of the first group. Therefore it becomes clear that Ghoṣā was a human woman with supersensory power able to realise divinity. Though a human woman, it is clear that by her divine power she worshipped the deities, saw them directly and obtained their grace.

It is also stated that although she had such divine power, because of her physical defect she could not obtain a proper husband, and that a life without husband and son grieved her mind. From such examples we learn how physical health and beauty serve as means to happiness in life. From this and many similar circumstances the nature of social life in the Vedic age is indicated: women lacking these two remained unmarried in their fathers' houses even when very old.

Even when life is sorrowful, Ghoṣā's life shows that by leading a pure life and by acts of worship of the deities this sorrow can be removed. Her excellent

### Printed p. 5

praise and worship pleased the Aśvins, and it is praised in many places that they brought her the highest happiness. This was explained earlier (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1. 112. 7 etc.).

It is also stated that a son named Suhastya was born to Ghoṣā by the grace of the Aśvins. This Suhastya too is an excellent composer of praises. He is known as the composer of the 41st sūkta of Ṛ. Saṃ. 10. The Ṛks of the present sūkta show his skill in composition. In them he praises the Aśvin deities. Ghoṣā and Ghauṣeya, both in their works, praised the divine powers of the Aśvins. These divine powers, and the differing opinions about the Aśvins, were stated earlier. Here only some points described in the present sūktas will be examined.

Sentences such as व्युष्टा उषसो हवामहे (*vyuṣṭā uṣaso havāmahe*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10. 41. 1) and प्रातर्युजं नासत्याधितिष्ठथः (*prātaryujaṃ nāsatyādhitiṣṭhathaḥ*) (10, 41. 2) show the natural relation between the Aśvin deities and Uṣas. It was also stated earlier that, according to one view cited by the Nirukta authors, the Aśvins are of the form of day and night (Ni. 12, 1). Many have approved this view. They are invoked at both day and night. This is clear in the very Ṛks composed by Ghoṣā. For example:

युवां मृगेव वारणा मृगण्यवो दोषा वस्तोर्हविषा नि ह्वयामहे। युवं होत्रामृतुथा जुह्वते नरेषं जनाय वहथः शुबस्पती ॥ (*yuvāṃ mṛgeva vāraṇā mṛgaṇyavo doṣā vastorhaviṣā ni hvayāmahe| yuvaṃ hotrāmṛtuthā juhvate nareṣaṃ janāya vahathaḥ śubaspatī ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10, 40, 4; the Kannada prints य (*ya*). सं (*saṃ*).)

"O Aśvin deities, at day and night we call and invite you with oblations." Thus their appearance at both these times is stated.

ताविद्दोषा ता उषसि शुभस्पती ता यामन्नुद्रवर्तनी (*tāviddoṣā tā uṣasi śubhaspatī tā yāmannudravartanī*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8. 22. 14)

Mantras composed by other seers, such as this, also make the matter clear. But here there is another refinement. According to some, "day and night" should be understood as the twilight at the time of the appearance of day and the twilight at the time of the appearance of night. These twilights consist of both light and dimness. But this refinement is not so fitting. Another view is that there is no simultaneous companionship in them. The answer to this objection is that, just as unity and companionship are spoken of in Mitra and Varuṇa, who represent day and night, so they must be spoken of here too. The view that the Aśvins appear at the time of Uṣas' appearance is stated in many places. But the time of Uṣas' appearance is not only the morning; the beginning of night too is designated Uṣas. For example:

### Printed p. 6

सुरुक्मे हि सुपेशसाधि श्रिया विराजतः । उषासावेह सीदशां ॥ (*surukme hi supeśasādhi śriyā virājataḥ | uṣāsāveha sīdaśāṃ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1. 18. 6)

Here the word Uṣas is used in the dual, उषासा (*uṣāsā*). For this word the meaning "day and night" is given. The Bhāṣya explains: उषसा एतत् रात्रेरप्युपलक्षणं उषःशब्दो दिवसस्कोपलक्षकः रात्रिरुषाश्च (*uṣasā etat rātrerapyupalakṣaṇaṃ uṣaḥśabdo divasaskopalakṣakaḥ rātriruṣāśca*). The Aśvins are described by नाना जात् (*nānā jāt*) (5-73.4); the purport of this sentence is that they are born in both day and night.

Along with this close relation of Uṣas and the Aśvins, a close relation of the goddess Sūryā and the Aśvins is also stated. Whether this allows an identity of Uṣas and Sūryā to be spoken of must be stated later, when Sūryā is explained (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10, 45). That is, since Sūryā is said to have mounted the three-wheeled chariot of the Aśvins, it is necessary here to examine the nature of the three wheels. In रथं त्रिचक्रं सवना गनिग्यतं (*rathaṃ tricakraṃ savanā ganigyataṃ*) (10. 41. 1), and likewise in other places (10. 85, 15), the three-wheeled chariot described can on the whole be said to be the three-wheeled chariot of Āditya.

This chariot is described in some places as one-wheeled, in others as two-wheeled, in still others as three-wheeled. Wherever one wheel is mentioned it is the wheel in the form of Āditya; where two wheels are mentioned they are the two wheels of Āditya's chariot touching heaven and earth. The same two wheels belong to the chariot of the Aśvins also. Stating this:

न्यंघ्नस्य मूर्धनि चक्रं रथस्य येमथुः । परि द्यामन्यदीयते ॥ (*nyaṃghnasya mūrdhani cakraṃ rathasya yemathuḥ | pari dyāmanyadīyate ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1. 20. 19; the Kannada prints य (*ya*). सं (*saṃ*).)

"O Aśvins, of the two wheels of your chariot you have set one on a firm mountain-top; the other you have made go around the whole heaven." These two wheels are visible to the world. The third is hidden.

द्वे ते चक्रे सूर्ये ब्रह्माण यतुथा विदुः । अथैकं चक्रं यद्दु हा तदद्धातय इद्दिदुः ॥ (*dve te cakre sūrye brahmāṇa yatuthā viduḥ | athaikaṃ cakraṃ yaddu hā tadaddhātaya iddiduḥ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10. 85. 16)

"O Sūryā, the skilled Brāhmaṇas can see two wheels of your chariot; but the other wheel alone is hidden." In explaining this, Sāyaṇa explains द्वे एव चंद्र सूर्यात्मके (*dve eva caṃdra sūryātmake*) as the two wheels in the form of moon and sun. And since the third wheel is hidden, he says that its nature is not accessible to ordinary awareness.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

In the verse ज्योतिष्मंतं केतुमंतं त्रिचक्रं सुखं रथं सुषदं भूरिवारं । चित्रामघा यस्य योगेऽधिजज्ञे तं वां हुवे अति रिक्तं पिबध्यै ॥ (*jyotiṣmaṃtaṃ ketumaṃtaṃ tricakraṃ sukhaṃ rathaṃ suṣadaṃ bhūrivāraṃ | citrāmaghā yasya yoge 'dhijajñe taṃ vāṃ huve ati riktaṃ pibadhyai ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8. 58. 3), this chariot, which belongs to all the gods, is described. The word त्रिचक्रं (*tricakraṃ*) shows the special power of this chariot: it means that it is by nature one that travels through the earth-world, the atmosphere-world and the heaven-world. Here the hidden third wheel pervades all the worlds, yet keeps its secret nature concealed. Describing the same thing in another way, the verse इदं त एकं पर ऊत एकं तृतीयेन ज्योतिषा संविकस्व । (*idaṃ ta ekaṃ para ūta ekaṃ tṛtīyena jyotiṣā saṃvikasva |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10. 56. 1) and others point out three kinds of light. In sum, if the Aśvin deities are of the form of day and night, then the two wheels of their chariot are likewise of the form of the sun and the moon. The third is more secret. Ghoṣā and the son of Ghoṣā have praised most highly the Aśvin deities, who possess a chariot with wheels that are all-pervading and of the nature of light.

## Sūkta 41 (Maṇḍala 10)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> समानमिति तृचं द्वादशं सूक्तं घोषायाः पुत्रस्य सुहस्त्यस्यार्षं
> जागतमाश्विनं । अनुक्रांतं च । समानं तृचं सुहस्तो फौषेय इति ॥ प्रातरनुवाकाश्विनशस्त्रयोरुक्तो विनियोगः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The twelfth sūkta, beginning समानं (*samānaṃ*), a group of three verses, has as its seer Suhastya, son of Ghoṣā; its metre is Jagatī and its deity is the Aśvins. The Anukramaṇī says: समानं तृचं सुहस्तो फौषेय इति (*samānaṃ tṛcaṃ suhasto phauṣeya iti*). Its use (viniyoga) is in the Prātaranuvāka and the Aśvina-śastra.

##### Anuvāda

This sūkta, beginning *samānam*, is the twelfth sūkta in the third anuvāka of the tenth maṇḍala. It has three Ṛks. The seer of this sūkta is Suhastya, son of the woman Ghoṣā. The deities are the Aśvins; the metre is Jagatī. The Anukramaṇī says समानं तृचं सुहस्तो घौषेयः (*samānaṃ tṛcaṃ suhasto ghauṣeyaḥ*). This sūkta is used for the Prātaranuvāka and the Aśvina-śastra mantras.

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> हे अश्विनौ युवयोः समानमु साधारणमेव त्यं तं पुरुहूतं बहुभिराहूतमुक्थं प्रशस्यं त्रिचक्रं सवना सवनानि यज्ञान्गनिग्मतं प्रतिगच्छंतं
> परिज्ञानं परितो गंतारं विदथ्यं यज्ञहितं रथमुषसो व्युष्टौ सुवृक्तिभिः

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> अस्तेवेत्येकादशर्चं त्रयोदशं सूक्तमांगिरसस्य कृष्णाख्यस्यार्षं

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The thirteenth sūkta, beginning अस्तेव (*asteva*), of eleven verses, has as seer Kṛṣṇa, the Āṅgirasa; its metre is Triṣṭubh and its deity Indra. It is prescribed by the sūtra यज्ञे दिव इति सूक्ते अस्तेव सु प्रतरं । अ-७-९ । इति (*yajñe diva iti sūkte asteva su prataraṃ | a-7-9 | iti*).

##### Anuvāda

This sūkta, beginning *asteva*, is the thirteenth sūkta in the third anuvāka. It has eleven Ṛks. The seer of this sūkta is Kṛṣṇa, son of Aṅgiras; the deity is Indra; the metre is Triṣṭubh. The Anukramaṇī says अस्तेवैकादश कृष्णः (*astevaikādaśa kṛṣṇaḥ*). The use of this sūkta is for the sūkta which the priest called Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin must recite as an āvāpa for the increase of soma in the ukthya sacrifices connected with the Abhiplava, as set out in the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra by the sūtra यज्ञे दिव इति सूक्ते अस्तेव सु प्रतरं (*yajñe diva iti sūkte asteva su prataraṃ*) (Ā. 7-9).

> किमं॒ग त्वा॑ मघवन्नो॒जमा॑हुः शिती॒हि मा॑ त्रिश॒यं त्वा॑ शृ  
> होमि ।

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> अंगेति सामान्याह्वानं । अंग हे मघवन्थनवञ्चक्रेंद्र त्वा त्वां किं किमर्थं
> भोजं स्तोतृणां भोजकमभीष्टमाहुः सर्वे विद्वांसः । यदि न ददासीत्यध्याहारः । तस्माद्ददास्केव स्तोतृभ्यो धनानीत्यर्थः । तथा सति मा स्तोतारं शिशीहि ।
> धनदानेन तीक्ष्णीकुरु । त्वा त्वां शिशयं स्तोतृणां संस्कर्तारं शृणोमि । किंच
> मम धीर्बुद्धिरप्पस्वती कर्मवत्यस्तु । भवतु । किंच नोऽस्माकं वसुविदं धनस
> लंबकं भगं भाग्यमा भर । आहर ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

अंग (*aṃga*) is a general form of address. "O Maghavan, O wealthy Indra, why do all the learned call you भोजं (*bhojaṃ*), the one who feeds the singers, the giver of what they desire?" One must supply: "if you do not give". Therefore you should give wealth to the singers. That being so, "sharpen me, the singer": make me keen by the gift of wealth. "I hear you as शिशयं (*śiśayaṃ*), the one who refines (संस्कर्तारं (*saṃskartāraṃ*)) the singers." And "let my understanding be अप्पस्वती (*appasvatī*), endowed with works." And "bring us the fortune (भगं (*bhagaṃ*)) that finds wealth."

**Pratipadartha**
- अंग (*aṃga*)—O revered one
- मघवन् (*maghavan*)—O wealthy one
- शक्र (*śakra*)—O Indra
- त्वा (*tvā*)—you
- किं (*kiṃ*)—for what reason
- भोजं (*bhojaṃ*)—as the giver of enjoyment to the worshippers
- आहुः (*āhuḥ*)—they have said
- (therefore) मा (*mā*)—me
- शिशीहि (*śiśīhi*)—invigorate by the gift of wealth
- त्वा (*tvā*)—you
- शिशयं (*śiśayaṃ*)—as the one who refines
- कृणोमि (*kṛṇomi*)—I have heard
- मम (*mama*)—my
- धीः (*dhīḥ*)—understanding
- अप्पस्वती (*appasvatī*)—devoted to rites
- अस्तु (*astu*)—let it be
- नः (*naḥ*)—to us
- वसुविदं (*vasuvidaṃ*)—that brings wealth
- भगं (*bhagaṃ*)—fortune
- आ भर (*ā bhara*)—provide

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O revered and wealthy Indra, for what reason have they called you the giver of enjoyment to the worshippers? Therefore invigorate me by the gift of wealth. I have heard of you as such a refiner. Make my understanding devoted to rites. Provide us the fortune that brings wealth.

##### English translation
Why, Maghavat, do (the wise) call you the nourisher(of worshippers)? Quicken me, (Indra), with wealth, (for) I hear that you are the quickener (of the worshippers); may my understanding, Shakra, be productive of works; grant us, Indra, a destiny that brings wealth.

#### Ṛk 4

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> हे इंद्र त्वां जना ममसत्येषु संग्रामेषु वि द्वयंते ! विविधमात्रयंति
> साहाय्यार्थं । एतदेव दर्शयति । समीके युद्धे संतस्थाः सह शिष्यतेन जना

> सखित्वं न वष्टि । न कामयते ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

"O Indra, people call upon you in battles (ममसत्येषु (*mamasatyeṣu*), i.e. conflicts) in manifold ways, for help." This is what he shows: standing together in the fight (समीके (*samīke*)) with joint effort, people call upon you. At this call the hero Indra makes his ally (युजं (*yujaṃ*)) the one who is a bearer of oblations (हविष्मान् (*haviṣmān*)). With a man who does not press Soma (असुन्वता (*asunvatā*)) he does not desire (न वष्टि (*na vaṣṭi*)) friendship.

**Pratipadartha**
- इंद्र (*iṃdra*)—O Indra
- त्वां (*tvāṃ*)—you
- the people (printed जनाति (*janāti*))
- ममसत्येषु (*mamasatyeṣu*)—in the battles
- विह्वयंते (*vihvayaṃte*)—call in many ways
- समीके (*samīke*)—in battle
- युजं (*yujaṃ*)—as a friend
- कृणुते (*kṛṇute*)—they make
- अत्र (*atra*)—at these times
- यः (*yaḥ*)—whichever battle **NOTE**
- हविष्मात् (*haviṣmāt*)—the one who has the oblation
- शूरः (*śūraḥ*)—the hero Indra
- युजं (*yujaṃ*)—as a friend
- कृणुते (*kṛṇute*)—I make for myself **NOTE**
- असुन्वता (*asunvatā*)—with one who does not press the leaking things **NOTE**
- सख्यं (*sakhyaṃ*)—friendship
- न वष्टि (*na vaṣṭi*)—does not desire

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, people call you in battles in manifold ways. In battle they make you their friend. At these times, whichever sacrificer provides the oblation, the hero Indra makes him his friend. Indra does not desire friendship without drying up the Soma. **NOTE**

##### English translation
The people earnestly call upon you in battles, Indra, (they call upon you) when standing together in the conflict : at this(call) Indra makes him his friend who offers oblations; the hero desires not friendship with him who presents no libation.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

- शत्रून् (*śatrūn*)—enemies
- नि युवति (*ni yuvati*)—drives far away
- वृत्रं (*vṛtraṃ*)—their violence
- हंति (*haṃti*)—destroys

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

As one gives abundant, moving wealth in the form of cattle and the like to a poor man, the sacrificer endowed with oblation who presses and offers intoxicating Soma juices to Indra: for that sacrificer, in the forenoon itself, Indra drives far away the enemies who have good helpers and good weapons. He destroys their violence.

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> अंतॆर्जठरे यमिंद्रं वृषसवासो वृषसवा अग्नौ सोमस्य वर्षित्प्रभर्धर्युभिरभिषुतास्तीव्रास्तीव्ररसा बहुलांतासो बहुलमन्नादिकमंते येभ्यत्ते
> बहुलांताः सोमाः प्राग्मन् प्रगच्छंति स मघवा धनवानिंद्रो दामानं दाकारं
> यजमानं नाह नैव नि यंसत् । नियच्छति । किंतु सुन्वते सोमाभिषवं कुर्वते
> यजमानाय भूर्यधिकं वामं वननीयं धनं नि वहति । निकरां प्रासयति ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The Indra into whose belly (अंतः (*aṃtaḥ*)) the Soma-juices have entered—pressed in the fire by the Adhvaryus, the showerers of Soma, sharp (strong in juice), and having abundant food at their end—that wealthy Indra does not restrain (नि यंसत् (*ni yaṃsat*)) the giver, the sacrificer. On the contrary, to the sacrificer who presses Soma he brings abundant, desirable wealth.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Indra also, having driven away (*atidīvya*) the assailant (प्रहां (*prahāṃ*), the striker), conquers. अति (*ati*) is used in the sense of honoured; the root दीव् (*dīv*) is in the sense of going; having made the honoured one go, he conquers. Further, "as": here the affix of manner is to be seen as dropped. As a gambler (श्वघ्नी (*śvaghnī*)) selects from among the players the opponent with whom the कृत (*kṛta*) throw is agreed, examining and taking him, so Indra at the time of battle examines the strong enemy and takes him up for slaying. Further: the man who longs to worship or praise the gods does not obstruct (रुणद्धि (*ruṇaddhi*): does not cover) his riches for that purpose. On the contrary, powerful (स्वधावान् (*svadhāvān*)) Indra unites that god-loving man with wealth.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

## Sūkta 43 (Maṇḍala 10)

##### Anuvāda (introduction)

The fourth Anuvāka of the tenth Maṇḍala of the Ṛgveda-saṃhitā contains eighteen sūktas. Of these, the sūkta beginning अच्छा मे (*acchā me*) is the first. It has eleven ṛks. The seer and deity of this sūkta are the same as in the preceding sūkta. The tenth and eleventh ṛks are in the Triṣṭubh metre; the remaining ṛks are in the Jagatī metre. The Anukramaṇikā says: अच्चा मे द्वित्रिष्टुबंतमिति (*accā me dvitriṣṭubaṃtamiti*). In the Ukthya sacrifice this sūkta is employed for the mantras to be recited by the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin priest at the third pressing. This is explained by the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra, in the sūtra अच्चा म इंद्रं बृहस्पते युवमिंद्रश्च वस्व इति याज्ये (*accā ma iṃdraṃ bṛhaspate yuvamiṃdraśca vasva iti yājye*) (Ā. 6-1). It is also to be recited on the sixth day of the sacrifice; this is explained by the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra, in the sūtra अच्चा म इंद्रमिति नित्यमैकाहिकं (*accā ma iṃdramiti nityamaikāhikaṃ*) (Ā. 8-3).

##### Particulars

Maṇḍala 10; Anuvāka 4; Sūkta 43; Aṣṭaka 7; Adhyāya 8; Varga 24, 25. Number of ṛks in the sūkta: 11. Ṛṣi: Kṛṣṇa. Devatā: Indra. Metre: 1–9 Jagatī; 10, 11 Triṣṭubh.

> आंगिरसस्य कृष्णस्य मे मम स्वर्विदः सर्वस्य लंभयित्र्यः सध्रीजीः संगता

> अलिंगंति तद्वदिंद्रं मे स्तुतयः परि ष्टजंते ॥

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

In passages that show valour or cruelty, the comparison with the श्वघ्नि (*śvaghni*), one who has valour or one who cheats, is shown in many contexts. Sāyaṇa gives this word different meanings in different contexts. It is made of two words, श्वान् (*śvān*) and घ्न (*ghna*), and घ्न (*ghna*) is formed from the root हन् (*han*). The word can be explained either as श्वानं हंति (*śvānaṃ haṃti*), "he kills a dog", or as शुना (सह) हंति (*śunā (saha) haṃti*), "he kills together with a dog". In the first three places of its use Sāyaṇa gives the word the meaning "hunter" or "one who pursues game".
पुनःपुनर्जायमाना पुराणी समानं वर्णमभि शुंभमाना । कृत्स्नव कृत्सुर्विज आमिनाना मर्तस्य देवी जरयंत्यायुः ॥ (*punaḥpunarjāyamānā purāṇī samānaṃ varṇamabhi śuṃbhamānā | kṛtsnava kṛtsurvija āminānā martasya devī jarayaṃtyāyuḥ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-92-10)

### Printed p. 32
This verse describes Ushas. Just as the श्वघ्नि (*śvaghni*) is cruel, so Ushas too, pitiless, brings all creatures under the sway of old age and harms them; this is the sense of the sentence. Sāyaṇa explains: शुना मृगान् हतवान् (*śunā mṛgān hatavān*), he who has killed game with a dog is श्वहा (*śvahā*), and his wife is श्वघ्नी (*śvaghnī*), the wife of a hunter who kills game by means of dogs. Likewise in शृघ्नद यो जिगिनास् (*śṛghnada yo jiginās*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-12-4), where the subject is Indra defeating and harming his enemies, Indra is compared to a cruel hunter. There too this same meaning is given. In other contexts, however, the same word is given another meaning. For example:
एवारे वृषभा सुशेऽसिन्वन्भूरादयः । श्श्विनेद विवता चरस् ॥ (*evāre vṛṣabhā suśe 'sinvanbhūrādayaḥ | śśvineda vivatā caras ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-43-44 **NOTE** 45-46 **NOTE**) **NOTE**
Here too Indra is praised. In this context Sāyaṇa, quoting श्वघ्नी कितवो भवति स्वं हंति (*śvaghnī kitavo bhavati svaṃ haṃti*) (Ni. 5.22), gives the word the meaning of one who cheats: one who seizes the property of others. Many English scholars too have expressed the opinion that the meaning of this word has been wrongly taken. Since a gambler is commonly one who loses his own property and takes the property of others, the word श्वघ्नी (*śvaghnī*) may be said to mean a gambler. But the original sense of the words श्वन् (*śvan*) (dog) and हन् (*han*) (kill) does not here give the general sense; this derivation is possible only if the meaning "hunter" is taken. Yet the meaning "hunter" is not possible in the places where this word occurs ... **NOTE**. The question naturally arises whether a gambler can have the epithet श्वघ्न (*śvaghna*). It is possible that Sāyaṇa gives this meaning on the strength of a Gṛhya-sūtra. There is a section called "श्वग्रहप्रायश्चित्त (*śvagrahaprāyaścitta*)" for destroying diseases such as epilepsy. As the name itself shows, young children suffer epilepsy and similar diseases through the affliction of an evil seizer (श्व ग्रह (*śva graha*)). In the Hiraṇyakeśi and Pāraskara Gṛhya-sūtras the expiation of this fault is prescribed as follows.
The father of the afflicted child performs the rite according to rule, takes the child to the gambling-place of the gamblers and performs certain prescribed acts there. Then he lays the child upon the dice-board and recites mantras meaning "O dog, go far away". In this rite the gambler is an assistant. Since the gambler helps in it, he is as it were the destroyer of the dog-seizer; hence he too became कृष्ण (*kṛṣṇa*) **NOTE**. On this basis it may be said that Sāyaṇa (also showing the authority of Yāska's sentence) explains the word त्वघ्निप् (*tvaghnip*) **NOTE** as gambler. (15-2-37)

> दिवस्परीति द्वादशर्चं तृतीयं सूक्तं भालंदनस्य वत्सप्रेरार्षं त्रैष्टुधमाग्नेयं । तथा चानुक्रांतं । दिवस्परि द्वादश वत्सप्रिराग्नेयं त्विति ॥ प्रातरनुपाकाश्चिनशस्त्रयोरिदमादिके द्वे सूक्ते अस्य सूक्तस्यांत्यां वर्जयित्वा । सूत्रितं चदिवस्परीति सूक्तयोः पूर्वस्कोत्तमामुद्धरेत् । आ-४-१३। इति ॥ क्षामवत्यामक्रंद
> दग्निरित्येषा याज्या । सूत्रितं च । अग्ने त्वमस्मद्युयोध्यमीवा अक्रंददग्निः
> स्तनयॆन्निवद्यौः । आ-३-१३ । इति ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The sūkta beginning दिवस्परि (*divaspari*) has twelve Ṛks and is the third sūkta. Its seer is Vatsapri, son of Bhalandana (the printed Sanskrit reads वत्सप्रेरार्षं (*vatsaprerārṣaṃ*)) **NOTE**; its deity is Agni; its metre is Triṣṭubh. The Anukramaṇī is quoted accordingly. In the Prātaranuvāka and the Āśvina-śastra, the two sūktas beginning with this one, the first and the following, are used, leaving out the last Ṛk of this sūkta. The sūtra is cited: दिवस्परीति सूक्तयोः पूर्वस्कोत्तमामुद्धरेत् (*divasparīti sūktayoḥ pūrvaskottamāmuddharet*) (Ā. 4-13). The fourth Ṛk, beginning अक्रंददग्निः (*akraṃdadagniḥ*), is the yājyā in the oblation to Agni Kṣāmavat. The sūtra is cited: अग्ने त्वमस्मद्युयोध्यमीवा (*agne tvamasmadyuyodhyamīvā*) and the rest (Ā. 3-13). **NOTE**

##### Anuvāda

This sūkta, beginning दिवस्परि (*divaspari*), is the third sūkta in the fourth anuvāka. It has twelve Ṛks. The seer of this sūkta is Vatsapri Bhālandana; the deity is Agni; the metre is Triṣṭubh. The Anukramaṇī says दिवस्परि द्वादश वत्सप्रिराग्नियं तु (*divaspari dvādaśa vatsaprirāgniyaṃ tu*). At the time of reciting the Prātaranuvāka and the Āśvinaśastra mantras, this sūkta, except its last Ṛk, and the following sūkta are used, as explained by the Āśvalāyana-Śrautasūtra sūtra दिवस्परीति सूक्तयोः पूर्वस्कोत्तमामुद्धरेत् (*divasparīti sūktayoḥ pūrvaskottamāmuddharet*) (Ā. 4-13). In the homa addressed to Agni Kṣāmavat, the fourth Ṛk of this sūkta, अक्रंददग्निः (*akraṃdadagniḥ*), is to be recited as the yājyā mantra, as explained by the Āśvalāyana-Śrautasūtra sūtra अग्ने त्वमस्मद्युयोध्यमीवा (*agne tvamasmadyuyodhyamīvā*) and the rest (Ā. 4-15).

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Agni was born first, above (परि (*pari*)) the heavenly world, in the form of the Sun. Jātavedas, meaning either "one whose wisdom is born" or "one whose wealth is born", was born second, above us, in the form of the earthly fire. Nṛmaṇāḥ, "one whose mind is attached to men by way of favour towards them", was born third in the waters, that is, in the atmosphere, in the form of lightning. The Svādhīḥ, the one of good wisdom, praises this Agni, kindling him without cease.
Or else the meaning of this mantra should be seen according to the manner stated by the Vājasaneyins. Sāyaṇa quotes the Brāhmaṇa (Śata. 6-7-4.3): breath is heaven, and from breath he was first born; "second" means that when the man-shaped one begot him, and "third in the waters" means that when the waters begot him the third time; Prajāpati is Nṛmaṇāḥ; "whoever kindles him begets him"; and so on to Svādhīḥ. **NOTE**

##### English translation

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

The description of the greatness of Agni, who pervades the universe and sustains it, is in Sūkta 10-45 extremely poetic. The manifestations of Agni, who is without birth and immortal, in his different forms are here denoted as the varieties of his birth. The Saṃhitā and other texts declare that all the other deities are only varieties of this Agni, who is without beginning, without a second and all-pervading. Agni, the sovereign of the whole universe, shines in the three worlds in three kinds of forms and is famed by three names. Āditya in the heavenly world and Indra of the middle world are only other forms of this Agni and nothing different. There are also many subordinate varieties relating to terrestrial Agni. Thus the aim of the present sūkta is to praise the greatness of Agni, who displays his own nature in many forms and sustains and nourishes the universe.
1) The manifestation of Agni in three forms in the three worlds.
2) The three forms visible to the perception of the world, and the divine nature that lies hidden within.
3) Agni's leadership of the sacrifice and, through it, his sustaining of the universe, and similar matters, are described in the present sūkta. The first Ṛk states the threefold order of Agni's birth. Although this matter was touched upon briefly earlier, when the purport of the Ṛk beginning श्रीणि जाना (*śrīṇi jānā*) was explained, here its detail is set out at length; many passages that bear on the birth of Agni are cited and the special points of the several contexts are given. **NOTE**
The purport of the first Ṛk is: "Agni was first born in the heavenly world. Afterwards, in the form of Jātavedas, he was born on the earth. That same Agni, a friend to men, was born the third time in the atmosphere."
द्युलोकस्य उपरि आदित्यात्मना जातः द्वितीयं पार्थिवात्मना जच्छे तृतीयं अंतरिक्षे वैद्युतात्मना जच्छे (*dyulokasya upari ādityātmanā jātaḥ dvitīyaṃ pārthivātmanā jacche tṛtīyaṃ aṃtarikṣe vaidyutātmanā jacche*) — Sāyaṇa's explanation is that the Agni who is of the nature of Āditya in the heavenly world and of the nature of lightning in the atmosphere was born also on the earth, in the form of terrestrial Agni. Such transformations of Agni will be set out in the special topics of the next Ṛk. What are the kinds of his birth? What are the sources of the birth? What are the forms of the birth? How these points are described is examined here.

The passages that explain the birth of Agni are many, and to one who looks at them superficially they seem to contradict one another. But on close examination no contradiction remains. It is said that the first birth of Agni is in the heavenly world. How is that? The order of the birth is explained at length.
**The birth of Agni in the heavenly world**
स जायमानः परमे व्योमन्याविरग्नि रभवन्मातरिश्वने । अस्य क्रत्वा समिधानस्य मज्मना प्र द्यावा शोचिः पृथिवी आरोचयत् ॥ (*sa jāyamānaḥ parame vyomanyāviragni rabhavanmātariśvane | asya kratvā samidhānasya majmanā pra dyāvā śociḥ pṛthivī ārocayat ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-143-2)
When the purport of this mantra was explained earlier, the account of how Agni became manifest before Mātariśvan was given. In this mantra the manifestation of Agni in the three worlds, in three different ways, is also indicated.
First: मातरि अंतरिक्षे श्वसिति प्राणिति सूर्यरूपेण चेष्टत इति सूर (*mātari aṃtarikṣe śvasiti prāṇiti sūryarūpeṇa ceṣṭata iti sūra*) — the one who moves in the atmosphere in the form of the Sun (Sūra).
Second: the Agni who is of the nature of wind.
Third: the sacrificer on earth who carries out the sacrifice. This is the purport given.
Since it is said that the Agni who is visible in these three forms made all three worlds shine, the identity of Agni and Mātariśvan is shown.
उदु ष्टुतः समिधा यहो अद्यौद्वर्मन्बिवो अधि नाभा पृथिव्याः । मित्रो आग्निरीड्यो मातरिश्वा दूतो वक्षद्यजथाय देवान् ॥ (*udu ṣṭutaḥ samidhā yaho adyaudvarmanbivo adhi nābhā pṛthivyāḥ | mitro āgnirīḍyo mātariśvā dūto vakṣadyajathāya devān ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-5-9)
In passages such as this the word Mātariśvan is explained as a word that tells a special quality of Agni. If it is asked what the original form of Agni was, before he took manifestation in these forms, the answer is as follows.
असच्चॆ सच्च परमे व्योमन्नहस्य जन्मन्नदिशेरुपस्थे । अग्निर्ह नः प्रथमजा यतस्य पूर्व आयुनि वृशभश्च धेनुः ॥ (*asaccè sacca parame vyomannahasya janmannadiśerupasthe | agnirha naḥ prathamajā yatasya pūrva āyuni vṛśabhaśca dhenuḥ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-5-7)

In the supreme divine world, the seat of Dakṣa, Agni was first, in an unmanifest form and in a manifest form, the overseer of all the affairs of the world. Afterwards he was born before all the creatures of the world; thus both the supramundane and the mundane forms of Agni are described. His first birth in the heavenly world is in the form of Āditya, and his eternal radiance is there itself.
स जायमानः परमे व्योमनि व्रतान्यग्निर्वतपा अरक्षत । व्यंतरिक्षममिमीत सुक्रतुर्वैश्वानरो महिना नाकमस्पृशत् ॥ (*sa jāyamānaḥ parame vyomani vratānyagnirvatapā arakṣata | vyaṃtarikṣamamimīta sukraturvaiśvānaro mahinā nākamaspṛśat ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-8-2)
Agni, called Vaiśvānara, born in the highest sky in the form of the Sun, protects the sacred rites. This same Agni has built the three worlds. Established in the heavenly world by his greatness, he shines. Such descriptions are praises of Agni in the form of Āditya.
शुचिं न यामन्निषिरं स्वर्द्भशं केतुं दिवो रोचनस्थामुषर्बुधं । अग्निं मूर्धानं दिवो अप्रतिष्टुतं तमीमहे नमसा वाजिनं बृहत् ॥ (*śuciṃ na yāmanniṣiraṃ svardbhaśaṃ ketuṃ divo rocanasthāmuṣarbudhaṃ | agniṃ mūrdhānaṃ divo apratiṣṭutaṃ tamīmahe namasā vājinaṃ bṛhat ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-2-14)
This Agni is the chief of the heavenly world. He is one who shines in the heavenly world, a great one, the one who makes the heavenly world known. We worship this Agni, who is worshipful in the sacrifice. Such praises denote Agni in the form of Āditya. The greatness of this Agni in the form of Āditya is as follows:
यो विश्वाभि विपश्यति भुवना सं च पश्यति । स नः वर्षदति द्विषः ॥ (*yo viśvābhi vipaśyati bhuvanā saṃ ca paśyati | sa naḥ varṣadati dviṣaḥ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-187-4)
यो अस्य पारे रजसः शुक्रो अग्निरजायत । स नः वर्षदति द्विषः ॥ (*yo asya pāre rajasaḥ śukro agnirajāyata | sa naḥ varṣadati dviṣaḥ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-187.5)
The Agni in the form of the Sun, who by his radiance makes all the worlds shine, who can see all the worlds, who shines with his light in a high region beyond the region of the atmosphere, that same Agni can protect us all. This Agni in the form of Āditya is born in the heavenly world.
असौ ना आदित्य एष रुक्मस्त्वितीव वा असावादित्योऽथो, . . . दुर्मरं वा एतस्यायुः श्रियो एष रोचतेग्निरम्यतोऽभवद्वयोभिरिति सर्वैदा एष वयोभिरमृतोऽभवद्यदेनं द्यौरजनयदिति द्यौर्वा एतमजनयत्सुरेशा इति सुरेता एषा यस्या एषरेतः ॥ (*asau nā āditya eṣa rukmastvitīva vā asāvādityo 'tho, . . . durmaraṃ vā etasyāyuḥ śriyo eṣa rocategniramyato 'bhavadvayobhiriti sarvaidā eṣa vayobhiramṛto 'bhavadyadenaṃ dyaurajanayaditi dyaurvā etamajanayatsureśā iti suretā eṣā yasyā eṣaretaḥ ‖*) (Ka. Brā. 6-7.22) **NOTE**

The golden disc of Āditya shines in the heavenly world with its immortal radiance. This is the power of Agni, who pervades all the worlds. The heavenly world bears this Agni in the form of Āditya. Because the heavenly world bore this Agni in its womb, it was as though it bore an excellent child. In this way and the like, the praise of the Agni born in the heavenly world occurs.
**Praise of the lightning Agni born in the atmosphere**
Agni who dwells in the atmosphere, shining in the form of lightning, is described as अपां नपात् (*apāṃ napāt*), अपां गर्बः (*apāṃ garbaḥ*) and so on.
प्र केतुना बृहता यात्यग्निरा रोदसी वृषभो रोरवीति । दिवश्चिदंता उपमा उदानळपामुपस्थे महिषो ववर्ध ॥ (*pra ketunā bṛhatā yātyagnirā rodasī vṛṣabho roravīti | divaścidaṃtā upamā udānaḷapāmupasthe mahiṣo vavardha ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-8-1)
The lightning Agni, born of the water that dwells in the atmosphere, who lies and plays on the lap of the waters, pervades heaven and earth entire. He bellows like a bull. In this way the praise of the Agni born in the atmosphere occurs. This same Agni is also designated Āpta. Although Agni is born also of the water that is on earth, the word Āpta is, in general, a qualifier only of the lightning Agni born of the water dwelling in the atmosphere. In the contexts where there is a description such as धनोरधि प्रवतः (*dhanoradhi pravataḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-144-5), this point is clear.
स श्शितानस्तन्यतू रोचनस्था अजरेभिर्नानदद्भर्यविष्ठः । यः पादकः पुरुतमः पुरूणि पृथून्यग्निरनुयाति भर्वन् ॥ (*sa śśitānastanyatū rocanasthā ajarebhirnānadadbharyaviṣṭhaḥ | yaḥ pādakaḥ purutamaḥ purūṇi pṛthūnyagniranuyāti bharvan ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6.6-2)
In passages such as this the description of the lightning Agni is clear. This Agni in the form of lightning shines exceedingly. Shining in the atmosphere he produces a great sound. He spreads and pervades with rays that are undiminishing and sound-making. Thus he is praised. The description that Agni is born of the water of the atmosphere is very frequent in the Ṛksaṃhitā.
वनेषु व्यंन्तरिक्षं तशान वाजमर्वत्सु पय उस्रियासु । हृत्तु क्रतुं वरुणो अप्पंग्निं दिवि सूरमदधात्सोमदौ ॥ (*vaneṣu vyaṃntarikṣaṃ taśāna vājamarvatsu paya usriyāsu | hṛttu kratuṃ varuṇo appaṃgniṃ divi sūramadadhātsomadau ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 5-85-2)
Where it is said that the god Varuṇa placed Agni in the water just as he placed Āditya and the others in the heavenly world and the rest, the purport is that the word अप् (*ap*) means the water of the atmosphere, and that the Agni placed in it is the lightning Agni. The statement "तृतीयमप्पु (*tṛtīyamappu*)" in the present Ṛk is likewise a praise of this lightning Agni.

**The glory of terrestrial Agni**
**The many kinds of birth of Agni on the earth**
This matter has been touched upon briefly in some places earlier; yet, since it is the main subject here, only some points not stated before are indicated. Agni, produced for the sacrifice by the act of churning in the aranis (fire-sticks), and so on: thus the praise of terrestrial Agni occurs. In many contexts this description is in excellent poetic form. For example:
उत स्म यं शिशुं यथा नवं जनिष्टारणे । धर्तारं मानुषीणां विशामग्निं स्वध्वरं ॥ (*uta sma yaṃ śiśuṃ yathā navaṃ janiṣṭāraṇe | dhartāraṃ mānuṣīṇāṃ viśāmagniṃ svadhvaraṃ ‖*) (Ka. Ka. Ka. Ka.) **NOTE**
The aranis have produced Agni, the sustainer of all men and the one with an excellent sacrifice, as one gives birth to a new child; thus the relation of mother and son between the aranis and Agni is shown. **NOTE**
Just as the water that dwells in the atmosphere is the source of the birth of Agni, so too the water of the earth is a source of his birth. The plants and the trees bear him like a womb.
अपां गर्भं दर्शतमोषधीनां वना जजान सुभगा विरूपं । देवासश्चिन्मनसा सं हि जग्मुः पनिष्कं जातं तवसं दुवस्मन् ॥ (*apāṃ garbhaṃ darśatamoṣadhīnāṃ vanā jajāna subhagā virūpaṃ | devāsaścinmanasā saṃ hi jagmuḥ paniṣkaṃ jātaṃ tavasaṃ duvasman ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-1-15)
The attractive aranis produced Agni, who is the womb-child of the waters and the plants. The gods too approached this worshipful Agni and praised him. In this way the order of the birth of the sacrificial Agni is told. Since this point has been explained in many contexts up to here, it need not be set out here.
Besides the threefold birth described thus in the three worlds, many other kinds of birth are also told. For example:
एता उ त्याः प्रत्यदृश्रन्नुरस्ताज्योतिर्यच्छंतीरुषसो विभातीः । अजेजननद्स्सर्यं यज्ञमग्निमपाचीनं तमो अगाढभुत् ॥ (*etā u tyāḥ pratyadṛśrannurastājyotiryacchaṃtīruṣaso vibhātīḥ | ajejananadssaryaṃ yajñamagnimapācīnaṃ tamo agāḍhabhut ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 7.28.1)
The shining dawns have spread their radiance. The same dawns have produced the sun and the sacrificial Agni. This Agni is the son of the three worlds. How is that?
यं त्वा द्यावापृथिवी यं त्वापश्वस्टा यं त्वा सुजनिमा जहानः । पंथामनु प्रविद्वान्तित्रयाणं द्युमदग्ने समिधानो वि भाहि ॥ (*yaṃ tvā dyāvāpṛthivī yaṃ tvāpaśvasṭā yaṃ tvā sujanimā jahānaḥ | paṃthāmanu pravidvāntitrayāṇaṃ dyumadagne samidhāno vi bhāhi ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-26)

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

O Agni, you whom heaven and earth begot, you whom the waters of the atmosphere begot, you whom the god Tvaṣṭṛ begot: having such a divine birth, and knowing the path of the fathers, you shine forth exceedingly.

"The plants bore this very sacrificial Agni in their womb. The waters too begot this same Agni. The trees also bore him in their womb. The plants and the rest beget Agni, who is of the nature of an embryo."

In the same way the priests too begot him." (6. 7. 1-2) Though he is praised in this manner and in other ways, he is also described as begotten by whomever begotten; and he is praised as the father-like protector of all, gods, humans and the rest.

Brā. 6-7-405) **NOTE**

Agni was first born in the heavenly world. Breath (prāṇa) is what is called the heavenly world; that is, Agni's first manifestation is from breath. Agni, in the form of Jātavedas, was born a second time from us; the third time he was born from the waters. This same Agni is beneficial to men. This deathless Agni is Prajāpati, the primal cause of the whole world. Making this immortal Agni blaze, the sacrificer worships him in the form of meditation.

The one beneficial to men begot you in the atmosphere. Prajāpati himself is this benefactor of people. O Agni, this same Prajāpati, benefactor of people, begot you in the udder-region of the heavenly world. The buffaloes made you, stationed in the high region of the third world, blaze up. The breaths are the buffaloes; these breaths made you blaze in the high region of the heavenly world. Thus it is explained.

In places such as अभि द्विजन्मा त्रिवृदन्नमृज्यते (*abhi dvijanmā trivṛdannamṛjyate*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-140-2) Agni's birth is said to be twofold. What these two are, is explained again in the mantra अभि द्विजन्मा त्री रोचनानि (*abhi dvijanmā trī rocanāni*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-149-4):

द्वाभ्यां अरणीभ्यां जायमानः । यद्वा । मथनेन आधानसंस्कारेण च उत्पन्नत्वात् द्विजन्मत्वं अथवा द्वयोर्द्यावापृथिव्योर्जायमानः अरण्योर्वा जायमानः ॥ (*dvābhyāṃ araṇībhyāṃ jāyamānaḥ | yadvā | mathanena ādhānasaṃskāreṇa ca utpannatvāt dvijanmatvaṃ athavā dvayordyāvāpṛthivyorjāyamānaḥ araṇyorvā jāyamānaḥ ‖*) (Sā. Bhā.)

Sāyaṇa explains that Agni is praised by the epithet "twice-born" either because he is born from two pieces of wood; or because he is born twice, once by churning and once by the ritual of installation (ādhāna); or because he is born from heaven and earth.

There are contexts in which Agni is also praised as having been born of himself. This same meaning is stated in some places for the epithet "तनूनपात् (*tanūnapāt*)". For example, the Bṛhaddevatā states this very point:

तनूनपादयं त्वेव नाम्ना यच्छत्यसौ तनुं । नपादिति प्रजामाहुरमुतोऽस्य च संभवात् ॥ (*tanūnapādayaṃ tveva nāmnā yacchatyasau tanuṃ | napāditi prajāmāhuramuto 'sya ca saṃbhavāt ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 3-1)

This Agni is also famous by the name Tanūnapāt. The divine Agni causes his own form to expand by his own power. The meaning of "नपात् (*napāt*)" as a son born of one's own body is well known. Because the birth of this divine Agni is from his own body, the name "तनूनपात् (*tanūnapāt*)" is well known. The purport of the epithet "अपां नपात् (*apāṃ napāt*)" has been explained in many places before.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Agni, we know your three forms, called Agni, Vāyu and Āditya, which are stationed threefold in the three places, earth, atmosphere and heaven. Further, we know your many abodes or stations, variously maintained, such as the Gārhapatya, Āhavanīya, Anvāhāryapacana and the like. Further, we who know the Veda know that supreme, excellent name of yours, hidden and unknown to those who do not know the Veda, which is famed in the Veda. And we know that source (utsa) from which you have come, that is, were born: the one who causes to well up, the one who produces, the causal self.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

In the special topics of the previous Ṛk, Agni's threefold origin was explained. How, according to the threefold origin in the three worlds, he becomes manifest in three different forms in the three worlds, is explained in the present Ṛk and in the following ones.

For the sentence विद्वा ते अग्ने त्रेधा त्रयाणि (*vidvā te agne tredhā trayāṇi*) **NOTE** the Sanskrit comment is given in the Kannada in a garbled form (तद त्रिषु स्थानेम (*tada triṣu sthānema*) …, द्विती (*dvitī*)), and the sentence is not translated. The sense that remains: he becomes visible on earth in the form of Agni, in the atmosphere in the form of Vāyu (or Indra), and in the heaven in the form of Āditya, as *Sāṇi* explains **NOTE**. The meaning is that these three forms, Agni, Vāyu (or Indra) and Āditya, are three forms of one power. Besides these, many intermediate forms are also described. Mainly these three are the supporters and protectors of the three worlds. In addition there are the variant forms अग्निपदमान (*agnipadamāna*), अग्निवनस्पति (*agnivanaspati*) and अग्निहुणॆ (*agnihuṇè*) **NOTE**, the distinctions of form जातवेदस् (*jātavedas*), वैश्वानर (*vaiśvānara*) and द्रविणोदाः (*draviṇodāḥ*), the variants of terrestrial Agni, दक्षिणाग्नि (*dakṣiṇāgni*), गार्हपत्य (*gārhapatya*) and आहवनीय (*āhavanīya*), and many other intermediate varieties. Among these, the topics of such distinctions as वैश्वानर (*vaiśvānara*) and प्रविणोदाः (*praviṇodāḥ*) **NOTE** have been given before (in the 8th part). Some principal topics not explained before are stated here directly.

These distinctions have no difference in principle. The fundamental principle of all of them is one. This is the source of all things moving and unmoving. This one principle shines in three forms in the three worlds. How?

भवद्भूतस्य भव्यस्य जंगमस्थावरस्य च । अस्यके सूर‍्यमेवैकं प्रभवं प्रलयं विदुः ॥ (*bhavadbhūtasya bhavyasya jaṃgamasthāvarasya ca | asyake sūryamevaikaṃ prabhavaṃ pralayaṃ viduḥ ‖*) (Pu. De. 1.11) **NOTE**

Whatever is now current, whatever was before, whatever will be hereafter, whatever is moving, whatever has a fixed state: for all these the one Āditya is the primal source, and he alone is the cause of the dissolution of all these.

### Printed p. 65
असतश्च सतश्चैव योनिरेषा प्रजापतिः । यदक्षरं च वाच्यं च यथैतद्ब्रह्म शाश्वतं ॥ (*asataśca sataścaiva yonireṣā prajāpatiḥ | yadakṣaraṃ ca vācyaṃ ca yathaitadbrahma śāśvataṃ ‖*)

Whatever exists in the form of being, whatever exists in the form of non-being: for all of them this Sūrya, called Prajāpati, is the source. This Prajāpati-principle is imperishable and beyond the praise of speech; yet the intermediate varieties that are visible in the forms of Sūrya and the rest are fit for description.

कृत्कृष हि त्रिधात्मानं एष लोकेषु शिष्कति । देवान्यथायथं सर्वान् निवेश्य स्टेषु रश्मिषु ॥ (*kṛtkṛṣa hi tridhātmānaṃ eṣa lokeṣu śiṣkati | devānyathāyathaṃ sarvān niveśya sṭeṣu raśmiṣu ‖*)

This Sūrya, the primal substance of the world, has spread his own nature threefold and pervaded the three worlds. He has established all the other gods each in his own place. For all of them the rays of this Sūrya are the supports (adhikaraṇa-sthāna).

एतद्भूतेषु लोकेषु अग्निभूतं स्थितं त्रिधा । यषयो गीर्भिरर्चंति व्यंजितं नामभिस्त्रिभिः ॥ (*etadbhūteṣu lokeṣu agnibhūtaṃ sthitaṃ tridhā | yaṣayo gīrbhirarcaṃti vyaṃjitaṃ nāmabhistribhiḥ ‖*)

After creation, in the three worlds that have come into being, this primal substance, abiding in the form of Agni, exists in three forms. The seers praise this Agni, who has threefold designations for the three kinds, indicating him by three names.

तिष्ठत्येव हि भूतानां जठरे जठरे ज्वलन् । त्रिस्थानं चैनमर्चंति होत्रायां वृक्तबर्हिषः । (*tiṣṭhatyeva hi bhūtānāṃ jaṭhare jaṭhare jvalan | tristhānaṃ cainamarcaṃti hotrāyāṃ vṛktabarhiṣaḥ |*)

This Agni pervades the belly of every being in the world. The sacrificers, at the time of their invocation, praise and worship this Agni as having three stations. What are the three forms of this powerful Agni?

इहैष पवमानोऽग्निर्मध्यमोऽग्निर्वनस्पतिः । अमुष्मिन्नेव विप्रैस्तु लोकेऽग्निशुचिरुच्यते ॥ (*ihaiṣa pavamāno 'gnirmadhyamo 'gnirvanaspatiḥ | amuṣminneva vipraistu loke 'gniśucirucyate ‖*)

The part on this earth the priests praise as Agni Pavamāna; the part in the atmosphere as Agni Vanaspati; the part in the heavenly world as Agni Śuci. And besides:

इहाग्निभूतस्वृषिभिर्लोके स्तुतिभिरीळितः । जातवेदास्तुतो मध्ये स्तुतो वैश्वानरो दिवि ॥ (*ihāgnibhūtasvṛṣibhirloke stutibhirīḷitaḥ | jātavedāstuto madhye stuto vaiśvānaro divi ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 1-67)

### Printed p. 66
The praisers extol the Agni manifested on earth simply as Agni. The Agni in the atmosphere is known as Jātavedas, and the Agni in the heavenly world as Vaiśvānara. But in this matter there is no exact agreement; there are differences of opinion. For example, the earlier seers hold that Vaiśvānara Agni is Āditya, whereas Śākapūṇi holds that terrestrial Agni is the one called Vaiśvānara Agni; the author of the Nirukta states these differences of opinion (Ni. 7-23). Whatever these differences, it is agreed by all that the three forms of Agni have spread through the three worlds and become famous under different names.

अग्निरस्मिन्नथेंद्रस्तु मध्यतो वायुरेव च । सूरो दिवीति विज्ञेयास्ति प्र एनेह देवताः ॥ (*agnirasminnatheṃdrastu madhyato vāyureva ca | sūro divīti vijñeyāsti pra eneha devatāḥ ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 1-69)

Agni in this world, Indra and Vāyu in the middle world, and Sūrya in the heavenly world are the varieties of Agni. For all these varieties and for all things in the world Agni is the primal form. The author of the Nirukta too, explaining this, says तिस्र एव देवताः (*tisra eva devatāḥ*) (the number of the gods is only three; these three are three parts of one principle). But what is the greatness of these three varieties?

वितासामेव माहात्मान्नामान्यत्वं विधियते । तत्तत्स्थानविभागेन तत्र तत्रेह दृश्यते ॥ (*vitāsāmeva māhātmānnāmānyatvaṃ vidhiyate | tattatsthānavibhāgena tatra tatreha dṛśyate ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 1-70)

By their very greatness they are designated by different names. These different names too are famous in accordance with their respective stations, since each by its own part pervades a different world. Thus the topics of the varieties of Agni are praised.

When explaining the purport of the sentence इदं विष्णुर्वि चक्रमे त्रेधा नि धदे पदं (*idaṃ viṣṇurvi cakrame tredhā ni dhade padaṃ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-22-17), Durgācārya has stated as follows:

पृथिव्यामंतरिक्षे दिवीति शाकपूणिः । पार्थिवोऽग्निर्भूत्वा पृथिव्यां यत्किंचिदस्ति तद्विक्रमते तदधितिष्ठति । अंतरिक्षे विद्युदात्मना दिवि सूर्यात्मना । यदुक्तं । तमू अकृण्वन् त्रेधा भुवे कं (*pṛthivyāmaṃtarikṣe divīti śākapūṇiḥ | pārthivo 'gnirbhūtvā pṛthivyāṃ yatkiṃcidasti tadvikramate tadadhitiṣṭhati | aṃtarikṣe vidyudātmanā divi sūryātmanā | yaduktaṃ | tamū akṛṇvan tredhā bhuve kaṃ*) (Ni. 12-19)

In the manner stated before, the striding of Viṣṇu is also explained. Of the three strides of Viṣṇu, one is taken on earth in the form of terrestrial Agni, another in the atmosphere in the form of lightning, and the third in the heavenly world in the form of Sūrya. A Vedic sentence also supports this.

The same matter that the Bṛhaddevatā passages quoted above have praised and explained, namely that Agni, who is of the nature of Prajāpati, shines forth in these forms (Agni, Vāyu and Āditya), is set out still more fully in the Śatapatha-brāhmaṇa.

सोऽकामयत प्रजापतिः । भूय एव स्यात्पजायेतेति सोऽग्निना पृथिवीं मिथुनग्ं समभवत्तत आंडग्ं समवर्तत तदभ्यमृशत्पुष्यत्विति पुष्यतु भूयोऽस्त्वितेव तदब्रवीत् ॥ (*so 'kāmayata prajāpatiḥ | bhūya eva syātpajāyeteti so 'gninā pṛthivīṃ mithunagṃ samabhavattata āṃḍagṃ samavartata tadabhyamṛśatpuṣyatviti puṣyatu bhūyo 'stviteva tadabravīt ‖*)
(Ga. Bā. 12-1)**NOTE** (Śa. Brā. 6-1-201)

Prajāpati wished that this subtle principle, the root of the world, should grow. Only then did he unite with the earth in the form of Agni and come into contact with her; and an egg came into being. He wished again that it should grow.

स यो गर्भोऽंतरासीत् । स वायुरसृज्यताथ यदश्रु संक्षरितमासीत्तानि वयाग् स्यभवन्नथ यः कपाले रसो लिप्त आसीत्ता मरीचयोऽभवन्नथ यत्कपालमासीत्तदंतरिक्षमभवत् । (*sa yo garbho 'ṃtarāsīt | sa vāyurasṛjyatātha yadaśru saṃkṣaritamāsīttāni vayāg syabhavannatha yaḥ kapāle raso lipta āsīttā marīcayo 'bhavannatha yatkapālamāsīttadaṃtarikṣamabhavat |*)

Then that embryo, which lay within, came forth in the form of Vāyu. The tears that were produced at that time were transformed into birds. The juices that were smeared on the skull became the rays of the sun. The skull itself was transformed into the atmosphere.

सोऽकामयत । भूयॆ एव स्यात्प्रजायेतेति स वायुनांतरिक्षं मिथुनग्ं समभवत्तत आंडग्ं समवर्तत तदभ्यमृतद्यशो विभृहीति ततोऽसावादित्यः । (*so 'kāmayata | bhūyè eva syātprajāyeteti sa vāyunāṃtarikṣaṃ mithunagṃ samabhavattata āṃḍagṃ samavartata tadabhyamṛtadyaśo vibhṛhīti tato 'sāvādityaḥ |*)

Again he wished that this too should grow and increase as offspring. Then, in the form of Vāyu, he came into contact with the atmosphere, and again an egg appeared. Blessing it with "Be glorious", he touched it. From that, Āditya came into being.

सोऽकामयत। भूय एव स्यात्प्रजायेतेति स आदित्येन दिवं मिथुनग्ं समभवत्तत आंडग्ं समुवर्तत . . , . ॥ (*so 'kāmayata| bhūya eva syātprajāyeteti sa ādityena divaṃ mithunagṃ samabhavattata āṃḍagṃ samuvartata . . , . ‖*)
(reference printed garbled)**NOTE**

He wished to grow still further. Then, in the form of Āditya, he came into contact with the heaven-world, and from that too an egg arose.

Thus the account describes how he pervades the three worlds in the forms of Agni, Vāyu and Āditya. Explaining the very Ṛk in question, विद्या तेs अग्ने श्रेधा प्रयाणीति (*vidyā tes agne średhā prayāṇīti*),

the text says अग्निर्वायुरादित्य एतानि हास्य तानि त्रेधा त्रयाणि (*agnirvāyurāditya etāni hāsya tāni tredhā trayāṇi*), stating the matters mentioned above (Śa. Brā. 6-7-4-4). Then, explaining the next Ṛk, "समुद्रे त्वा (*samudre tvā*)", it says:

समुद्रे त्वा नृमणा अप्सुंतरिति प्रजापतिर्वै नृमणा अप्सु त्वा प्रजापतिरित्येतन्नृचक्षा ईधे दिवो अग्न ऊधन्निति प्रजापतिर्वै स्वच्छक्षा आपो दिव ऊधस्तृतीये त्वा रजसि तस्थिवाग्‌ंसमिति द्यौर्वृ तृतीयग्ं रजोऽपामुपस्थे महिषा अवर्धन्निति प्राणा वै महिषादिवि त्वा प्राणा अवर्धन्नित्येतत् । (*samudre tvā nṛmaṇā apsuṃtariti prajāpatirvai nṛmaṇā apsu tvā prajāpatirityetannṛcakṣā īdhe divo agna ūdhanniti prajāpatirvai svacchakṣā āpo diva ūdhastṛtīye tvā rajasi tasthivāgṃsamiti dyaurvṛ tṛtīyagṃ rajo 'pāmupasthe mahiṣā avardhanniti prāṇā vai mahiṣādivi tvā prāṇā avardhannityetat |*) (Śa. Brā. 6-7-4-5)

O Agni, he who is beneficial to men made you blaze in the waters (in water). Prajāpati is the one who is thus beneficial to men. O Agni, he who can see all the doings of men fully made you blaze in the atmosphere also. Prajāpati is the one who has such divine sight. Then, by the direction of that same Prajāpati, the mahiṣas made you, who stand in the third, the heaven-world, grow. The vital breaths are thus called mahiṣas. That is, the purport stated is that the vital breaths made you thrive and shine in the heaven-world.

That the Āditya who shines in the heaven-world is none other than Agni is well known from Vedic statements such as असौ वा आदित्य एषोऽग्निः स एष ज्योतिष्मान् (*asau vā āditya eṣo 'gniḥ sa eṣa jyotiṣmān*) (Śa. Brā. 6-4-1-11). If it is asked why this Agni is called Indra, the answer is:

रसान् रश्मिभिरादाय वायुनायं गतः सह । वर्षत्येव च यल्लोके तेनेंद्र इति स स्मृतः ॥ (*rasān raśmibhirādāya vāyunāyaṃ gataḥ saha | varṣatyeva ca yalloke teneṃdra iti sa smṛtaḥ ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 1-8)

This same Agni draws up the sap with his rays and, together with Vāyu, rains again upon the earth. Therefore he became famous as Indra, say the Bṛhaddevatā passages. Thus the varieties of Agni's forms described in other Śruti texts are explained in this way in the Ṛk-saṃhitā passages themselves. This Agni is the original principle, without beginning or end.

अग्निरस्मि जन्मना जातवेदा धृतं मे चिक्षुरमृतं म आसन् । अर्कस्त्रिधातू रजसो विमानोऽजस्रो घर्मो हविरस्मि नाम ॥ (*agnirasmi janmanā jātavedā dhṛtaṃ me cikṣuramṛtaṃ ma āsan | arkastridhātū rajaso vimāno 'jasro gharmo havirasmi nāma ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-5-4)

त्रिभिः पवित्रैरपुसोद्ध्यंकं हृदा मतिं ज्योतिरनुप्रजानन् । वर्षिष्ठं रत्नमकृत स्वधाभिरादिद्द्यावापृथिवी पर्यपश्यत् ॥ (*tribhiḥ pavitrairapusoddhyaṃkaṃ hṛdā matiṃ jyotiranuprajānan | varṣiṣṭhaṃ ratnamakṛta svadhābhirādiddyāvāpṛthivī paryapaśyat ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-1-8)

By the very fitness of my nature I am one who knows all the affairs of the universe (I am Jātavedas). My radiance is self-luminous and illuminates the world. As the life-breath that gives vitality to the whole world, I have spread my nature in three divisions. How? —
1. रजसः विमानः (*rajasaḥ vimānaḥ*)—the form of Vāyu (or Indra), presiding over the atmosphere.
2. अजस्रो घर्मः (*ajasro gharmaḥ*)—the form of Āditya (in the heaven-world), of imperishable, luminous nature.
3. हविरस्मि नाम (*havirasmi nāma*)—the form of Agni, the recipient of oblations on the earth.

The Bṛhadāraṇyaka too, praising this very matter thus explained, says:
स त्रेधात्मानं व्यभजत आदित्यं तृतीयं वायुं तृतीयं (*sa tredhātmānaṃ vyabhajata ādityaṃ tṛtīyaṃ vāyuṃ tṛtīyaṃ*) (Bṛ. U. 1-2-3).
Thus the same primal principle which the Upaniṣads and other texts describe as the one without a second, the universal principle, the Supreme Brahman, the Supreme Light, is praised in the Ṛk-saṃhitā in the passages quoted above (2-26-8).

All-embracing Agni, through the function of his inner organ, knows the venerable, self-luminous radiance called Supreme Brahman (Sā. Bhā.)**NOTE**; he shines both combined with the world-purifying forms of Agni, Vāyu and Sūrya and distinct from them. He also extends himself in these same forms of Agni, Vāyu and Sūrya, and by this effort he surveys the whole world, so it is praised.

This primal principle is the cause of the creation, maintenance and dissolution of the world. What these three forms are:

अस्य वामस्य पलितस्य होतुस्तस्य भ्राता मध्यमो अस्त्यश्च । तृतीयो भ्राता घृतपृष्टो अस्यात्रापश्यं विश्वतिं सप्तपुत्रं ॥ (*asya vāmasya palitasya hotustasya bhrātā madhyamo astyaśca | tṛtīyo bhrātā ghṛtapṛṣṭo asyātrāpaśyaṃ viśvatiṃ saptaputraṃ ‖*) (Pu. Saṃ. 1-164-1)**NOTE**

For Āditya, the protector of the world, Vāyu of the middle world and Agni who dwells on the earth are brothers. He is the soul of the universe, the lord of the universe; he made the universe shine in the form of the cosmic egg. He himself protects the world he has created, and again by the act of dissolution he absorbs it into himself. He is like the thread of the world. By differences of function and of place many names of him are well known: Tanūnapāt, Narāśaṃsa, Mātariśvā, Vaiśvānara and so on are all varieties of his form. Because he pervades the three worlds he is also called Trita; the meaning of this word is given as त्रिषु स्थानेषु वर्तमानः त्रितः (*triṣu sthāneṣu vartamānaḥ tritaḥ*). (The matter of the seer suggested by the word Trita was explained earlier in Part 8 of the Ṛgveda-saṃhitā.)

Agni, in the form of Trita, has three bonds. All the deities are his varieties.

असि यमो अस्यादित्यो अर्वन्नसि त्रितो गुह्येन व्रतेन । असि सोमेन समया विपृक्त आहुस्ते त्रीणि दिवि बंधनानि ॥ (*asi yamo asyādityo arvannasi trito guhyena vratena | asi somena samayā vipṛkta āhuste trīṇi divi baṃdhanāni ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-163-3)

Agni (in the form of lightning), in the form of Varuṇa's horse, is of the nature of Vāyu. He is also of the form of Āditya. He has three kinds of bonds in the heaven-world. What the kinds of this bond are was explained earlier, in Part 11, pp. 242-43. There is also the statement that there are three kinds of bonds in each of the three worlds. As in the heaven-world there are three kinds of bonds, so on the earth there are three kinds of bonds, food, place and seed; and in the atmosphere three kinds, cloud, lightning and thunder, which cause the production of rain; so the bhāṣya says. Since he is thus the cause of the whole universe, Agni is the navel of all. This matter will be explained later in the special topics of Sūktas 10-51 and 52. It is proper here to know about the rites performed with designations of forms such as Agni Śuci, Agni Pavamāna and Agni Pāvaka, and the nature of their importance. The Agni who shines in these three forms in the three worlds was called above Agni Pavamāna, Agni Śuci and Agni Vanaspati. The Taittirīya-saṃhitā calls the same Agni Pavamāna, Agni Śuci and Agni Pāvaka. Only if oblations are offered in three ways, designating him by these three forms, is there complete satisfaction for Agni of complete form; the spiritual and divine importance of these rites is stated in the Taittirīya-saṃhitā and in the Śatapatha-brāhmaṇa.

अग्नये पवमानाय पुरोडाशमष्टाकपालं निर्वपेदग्नये पावकायाग्नये शुचये ज्योगामयावी यदग्नये पवमानाय . . . ॥ (*agnaye pavamānāya puroḍāśamaṣṭākapālaṃ nirvapedagnaye pāvakāyāgnaye śucaye jyogāmayāvī yadagnaye pavamānāya . . . ‖*) (Tai. Saṃ. 2-2-4-2)

In such passages the oblations to be offered to Agni of the three forms, and the importance of this sacrifice, are praised. The divisions Pavamāna, Pāvaka and Śuci may also be divisions of the terrestrial sacrificial fire. How? On the question what the real secret of these three forms of Agni, who is of the form of Prajāpati, is, the Taittirīya and Śatapatha-brāhmaṇa passages give one or two explanations.

प्रजापतिर्वाचस्सत्यमपश्यत् । तेनाग्निमाधत्त । तेन वै स आर्ध्नोत् । भूर्भुवन्नुवरित्याह । एतद्वै वाचस्सत्यं । य एतेनाग्निमाधत्ते । युद्धोत्येव । अथो सत्यप्राशूरेव भवति । अथो य एवं विद्वानभिचरति । स्त्वणुत एनैनं । भूरित्याह । प्रजा ना एव तद्यजमानस्सृजते । भुव इत्याह । आस्मिन्नेव लोके प्रति तिष्ठति । सुवरित्याह । सुवर्ग एव लोके (*prajāpatirvācassatyamapaśyat | tenāgnimādhatta | tena vai sa ārdhnot | bhūrbhuvannuvarityāha | etadvai vācassatyaṃ | ya etenāgnimādhatte | yuddhotyeva | atho satyaprāśūreva bhavati | atho ya evaṃ vidvānabhicarati | stvaṇuta enainaṃ | bhūrityāha | prajā nā eva tadyajamānassṛjate | bhuva ityāha | āsminneva loke prati tiṣṭhati | suvarityāha | suvarga eva loke*)

प्रतितिष्ठति । त्रिभिरक्षरैर्गाहपत्यमादधाति । त्रय इमे लोकाः । एष्टेवैनं लोकेषु प्रतिष्ठितमाधत्ते । (*pratitiṣṭhati | tribhirakṣarairgāhapatyamādadhāti | traya ime lokāḥ | eṣṭevainaṃ lokeṣu pratiṣṭhitamādhatte |*)
(Śai. Brā. 1-1-5-1, 2)**NOTE**

Prajāpati saw the truth that belongs to speech. Then, by that truth, the means of attaining the desired, he arranged the laying of the fire and created the auspicious principles Bhūḥ, Bhuvaḥ, Svaḥ. By this he became valiant in truth (or: by this truth he himself thrived). Those who obstruct and harm the spread of this truth will certainly be harmed themselves; they certainly have no welfare.

The Gārhapatya and the other fires are principles that reflect these worlds, Bhūḥ and the others. All of them are truth in the form of speech. All were created by Prajāpati and are parts of Prajāpati.

प्रजापतिः प्रजा अस्यजत । . . . सा विराडभवत् । तं देवसुरा व्यगृह्णत । सोऽब्रवीत्प्रजापतिः । मम वा एषा । (*prajāpatiḥ prajā asyajata | . . . sā virāḍabhavat | taṃ devasurā vyagṛhṇata | so 'bravītprajāpatiḥ | mama vā eṣā |*)
(Kai. Brā. 1-1-10-1)**NOTE**

Prajāpati created creatures. Then, when his virility had somewhat diminished, he performed austerity. Then that virility became the forms of Agni. Then gods and asuras all began to seize it in rivalry. Then Prajāpati said: "This Agni called Virāṭ and the original forms of Agni belong to me; the forms of Agni changed from this original form belong to you." Therefore the original forms of Agni became a part of Prajāpati. These are of three kinds. What their nature is, or the reason why they take three forms, is told in the form of a legend as follows.

देवासुरास्संयुक्ता आसन् । ते देवा विजयमुपयंतः । अग्नौ वामं वसु संस्यदधत । इदमु नो भविष्यति । यदि नो जेष्यंतीति । तदग्निर्नोथ्यहमशक्कोत् । तत्रेधा विन्यदधात् । पशुषु तृतीयं । अपुनी तृतीयं । आदित्ये तृतीयं । (*devāsurāssaṃyuktā āsan | te devā vijayamupayaṃtaḥ | agnau vāmaṃ vasu saṃsyadadhata | idamu no bhaviṣyati | yadi no jeṣyaṃtīti | tadagnirnothyahamaśakkot | tatredhā vinyadadhāt | paśuṣu tṛtīyaṃ | apunī tṛtīyaṃ | āditye tṛtīyaṃ |*)
(Kai. Brā. 1-1-6.1)**NOTE**

Gods and asuras began to fight. Then the gods won. They deposited in Agni the wealth won from the asuras. Then Agni could not carry all of it. Then he divided it in three and placed it in three places: one part in cattle and the like, another part in the waters, and the remaining third part in Āditya.

तद्देवा विजित्य । पुनरवारुरुथ्यंत । तेग्नये पवमानाय पुरोडाशमष्टाकपालं निरवपन् । पशवो वा अग्निः पवमानः । यदेव पशुष्वा (*taddevā vijitya | punaravāruruthyaṃta | tegnaye pavamānāya puroḍāśamaṣṭākapālaṃ niravapan | paśavo vā agniḥ pavamānaḥ | yadeva paśuṣvā*)

तत्तेनानारुंधत । तेऽग्नये पावकाय । आपो ना अग्निः पावकः । यदेवाप्स्वासीत् । तत्तेनानारुंधत । तेऽग्नये शुचये । आसौ वा आदित्योऽग्निश्शुचिः । यदेवादित्य आसीत् । तत्तेनानारुंधत । (*tattenānāruṃdhata | te 'gnaye pāvakāya | āpo nā agniḥ pāvakaḥ | yadevāpsvāsīt | tattenānāruṃdhata | te 'gnaye śucaye | āsau vā ādityo 'gniśśuciḥ | yadevāditya āsīt | tattenānāruṃdhata |*)
(Tai. Brā. 1-1-6-2, 3)

The gods wished to regain the wealth thus divided. Then they offered an eight-potsherd purodāśa to Agni Pavamāna. The very cattle in which Agni had placed a third part of the wealth were of the nature of Agni Pavamāna. Therefore, offering the oblation to Agni Pavamāna, they obtained the wealth that was there. In the same way they offered to Agni Pāvaka also. Water removes defects and brings purity; this water is called Agni Pāvaka. Therefore they obtained the wealth placed in Agni Pāvaka. In the same way they offered to Agni Śuci also. Āditya is of the nature of Agni Śuci. Therefore they obtained the third part deposited in Āditya. For this reason the offering of oblations is of three kinds. This threefold offering corresponds to the three original forms of Agni. How?

त्रीणि हवीग्ंषि निर्वपति । विराज एव विक्रांतं यजमानोऽनु विक्रमते । अग्नये पवमानाय । अग्नये शुचये । यदग्नये पवमानाय निर्वपति । पुनात्येवैनं । यदग्नये पावकाय । पूतविवास्मिन्नन्नाद्यं दधाति । यदग्नये शुचये । ब्रह्मवर्चसमेवास्मिन्नु परिष्पाद्दधाति । (*trīṇi havīgṃṣi nirvapati | virāja eva vikrāṃtaṃ yajamāno 'nu vikramate | agnaye pavamānāya | agnaye śucaye | yadagnaye pavamānāya nirvapati | punātyevainaṃ | yadagnaye pāvakāya | pūtavivāsminnannādyaṃ dadhāti | yadagnaye śucaye | brahmavarcasamevāsminnu pariṣpāddadhāti |*) (Tai. Brā. 1-1-5-10)

If three kinds of oblations are offered to Agni of the form of Virāṭ, divided into the three forms Agni Pavamāna, Agni Pāvaka and Agni Śuci, the sacrificer attains all-pervading power. As the fruit of offering to Agni Pavamāna, purity comes to the sacrificer. As the fruit of offering to Agni Pāvaka, the purified sacrificer attains the capacity of eating food. As the fruit of offering to Agni Śuci, he attains brahmavarcas. These are the three kinds of prowess that come to the sacrificer: 1) purity, 2) food (the capacity of acquiring offspring, cattle and the like), and 3) brahmavarcas.

In Tai. Brā. 1-1-10 Sāyaṇa has given the purport of Pavamāna as self-pure, of Pāvaka as one who purifies others, and of Śuci as shining.

The order of offering oblations to Agni of these three forms, the requirement that this offering correspond to the nature of the respective original forms, and the fruit of each offering are stated in the Śatapatha-brāhmaṇa in this way. Here, a description of the prescribed order of offering the oblation is not relevant.

### Printed p. 73
The chief purpose is to examine what account this injunction gives of the fundamental forms of Agni.

स ना अग्नये पवमानाय निर्वपति । प्राणो वै पवमानः प्राणमेवास्मिन्नेतद्दधाति । तद्वेतयैवास्मिंस्तद्दधाति अन्नग्ं हि प्राणोऽन्नमेषाहुतिः ॥ (*sa nā agnaye pavamānāya nirvapati | prāṇo vai pavamānaḥ prāṇamevāsminnetaddadhāti | tadvetayaivāsmiṃstaddadhāti annagṃ hi prāṇo 'nnameṣāhutiḥ ‖*) (Śa. Brā. 2-2-16)

Agni as Pavamāna is of the nature of the vital breath. By offering the oblation to Agni Pavamāna, the breath is, as it were, established in the sacrificer. Food is the support of the breath and is of the nature of the breath. Therefore by offering this oblation he establishes the breath.

अथाग्नये पावकाय निर्वपति । अन्नं वै पावकमन्नमेवास्मिन्नेतद्दधाति तद्वेतय्यैवास्मिंस्तद्दधात्येषाह्येव प्रत्यक्षमन्नमाहुतिः ॥ (*athāgnaye pāvakāya nirvapati | annaṃ vai pāvakamannamevāsminnetaddadhāti tadvetayyaivāsmiṃstaddadhātyeṣāhyeva pratyakṣamannamāhutiḥ ‖*) (Śa. Brā. 2-2-1-7)

Next he offers the oblation to Agni Pāvaka, the purifier. Food is what purifies. By offering this oblation, food is, as it were, established in the sacrificer. This purifying food he establishes through this oblation.

अथाग्नये शुचये निर्वपति । वीर्यं वै शुचि यद्वा अस्यैतदुज्वलत्येतदस्य वीर्यग्ं शुचि वीर्यमेवास्मिन्नेतद्दधाति . . . . . अथास्येतद्वीर्यग्ं शुच्युज्वलति ॥ (*athāgnaye śucaye nirvapati | vīryaṃ vai śuci yadvā asyaitadujvalatyetadasya vīryagṃ śuci vīryamevāsminnetaddadhāti . . . . . athāsyetadvīryagṃ śucyujvalati ‖*) (Śa. Brā. 2-2-1-8)

Next he offers the oblation to Agni Śuci. Since the shining Agni Śuci is of the nature of vigour, as a result of offering this oblation vigour is, as it were, established in the sacrificer.

Such injunctions make known the three forms of Agni, namely Pavamāna-hood, Pāvaka-hood and Śuci-hood, and the fruits that accrue to the sacrificer by worshipping these forms.

अग्निर्ह यत्र देवेभ्यो मनुष्यानभ्युपाववर्त तद्धीक्षांचक्रे मृष सर्वे एवात्मना मनुष्यानभ्युपावृतमिति । (*agnirha yatra devebhyo manuṣyānabhyupāvavarta taddhīkṣāṃcakre mṛṣa sarve evātmanā manuṣyānabhyupāvṛtamiti |*) (Śa. Brā. 2-2-1-13)

When Agni, from the company of the gods, came to join men, he thought: "I cannot go with my complete form."

स एतास्ति स्रस्तनूरेषु लोकेषु विन्यधत्त । यदस्य पवमानग्ं रूपमासीत्तदस्यां पृथिव्यां न्यधत्ताथ यत्पावकं तदंतरिक्षेऽथ यच्चुचि तद्दिवि तद्वा ऋषयः प्रतिबुबुधिरे य उ तह्यृषय आसुरसर्वेण वै न आत्मनाग्निरभ्युपावृतदिति तस्मा एतानि हवीगंषि निरवपन् ॥ (*sa etāsti srastanūreṣu lokeṣu vinyadhatta | yadasya pavamānagṃ rūpamāsīttadasyāṃ pṛthivyāṃ nyadhattātha yatpāvakaṃ tadaṃtarikṣe 'tha yaccuci taddivi tadvā ṛṣayaḥ pratibubudhire ya u tahyṛṣaya āsurasarveṇa vai na ātmanāgnirabhyupāvṛtaditi tasmā etāni havīgaṃṣi niravapan ‖*) (Śa. Brā. 2-2-1-14)

Then Agni established his three forms in the three worlds: the Pavamāna form in the earth, the purifying Pāvaka form in the atmosphere, and the shining Śuci form in the heavenly world. The sages of that time, understanding this division of Agni and thinking, "Agni has not come to us in his complete form," realised the necessity of offering this full oblation.

स यदग्नये पवमानाय निर्वपति । यदेवास्यास्यां पृथिव्याग्ं रूपं तदेवास्यैतेनाप्नोत्यथ यदग्नये पावकाय निर्वपति यदेवास्यांतरिक्षे रूपं तदेवास्यैतेनाप्नोत्यथ यदग्नये शुचये निर्वपति यदेवास्य दिवि रूपं तदेवास्यैतेनाप्नोत्येवमु कृत्स्नमेवाग्निमनपनिहितमादत्ते तस्मादु निर्वपेदेवोत्तराणि हवीग्ंषि ॥ ( (*sa yadagnaye pavamānāya nirvapati | yadevāsyāsyāṃ pṛthivyāgṃ rūpaṃ tadevāsyaitenāpnotyatha yadagnaye pāvakāya nirvapati yadevāsyāṃtarikṣe rūpaṃ tadevāsyaitenāpnotyatha yadagnaye śucaye nirvapati yadevāsya divi rūpaṃ tadevāsyaitenāpnotyevamu kṛtsnamevāgnimanapanihitamādatte tasmādu nirvapedevottarāṇi havīgṃṣi ‖ (*) (Śa. Brā. 2-2-1-15)

In the same way, when they offered the oblation, they learned its fruit. If one offers the oblation to Agni Pavamāna, he obtains the form of Agni that is established in the earth. If one offers the oblation to Agni Pāvaka, the sacrificer obtains the form of Agni that pervades the atmosphere. If one offers the oblation to Agni Śuci, he obtains the form of Agni that is in the heavenly world. In this way he obtains the form of Agni in his complete, unimpaired form. Therefore this full oblation is to be offered; thus the greatness of the three forms of Agni is praised.

Thus the lords of these worlds who shine in all three worlds are described as Sūrya, Indra (Vāyu) and Agni in one context; as Agni Pavamāna, Agni Pāvaka and Agni Śuci in another; and as Vaiśvānara, Jātavedas and the earthly Agni in yet another: in each case three different forms of a single principle.

अयं प्रयोगस्त्वतेषां ज्योतिषां त्रिषु वर्ततां । लोकेषु मंत्रविद्विद्यान् प्रयोगे नावसीदति ॥ (*ayaṃ prayogastvateṣāṃ jyotiṣāṃ triṣu vartatāṃ | lokeṣu maṃtravidvidyān prayoge nāvasīdati ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 1-90)

Knowing how the three luminous forms rest in the three worlds, one should offer oblations and the like in accordance with the respective forms. The wise man, knowing this, does not act contrary to it.

नियतेऽयं नृभिर्यस्मान्न यत्यस्मादसौ च तं । तेनेमौ चक्रतुः कर्म सनामानौ पृथक् पृथक् ॥ (*niyate 'yaṃ nṛbhiryasmānna yatyasmādasau ca taṃ | tenemau cakratuḥ karma sanāmānau pṛthak pṛthak ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 1-91)

This earthly Agni is impelled by men. The Agni of the heavenly world leads him away from the earth. Thus, although both have the same name, the work of each is different.

यद्विद्यते हि जातः सञ्जातैर्यद्वात्र विद्यते । तेनेमौ तुल्यनामानौ उभौ लोकौ समाप्नुतः ॥ (*yadvidyate hi jātaḥ sañjātairyadvātra vidyate | tenemau tulyanāmānau ubhau lokau samāpnutaḥ ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 1-92)

Because he knows everything at the very moment of birth, and because he is known by the creatures that are born, these two forms, though they have the same name, pervade the two worlds. (The nature of Agni in the form of Jātavedas is explained in the 8th part of the Ṛ. Saṃ.)

विसृजन्नयमेतेषां भ्राजते व्योम्मि मध्यमः । निपातमात्रे कथ्यंते तथाग्नेयानि कानिचित् ॥ (*visṛjannayameteṣāṃ bhrājate vyommi madhyamaḥ | nipātamātre kathyaṃte tathāgneyāni kānicit ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 1-93)

Of these three forms, the Agni who pervades the middle world, the atmosphere, shines in the form of lightning while pouring down rain. Thus there are varieties of names describing the respective qualities in accordance with the respective functions. But these varieties of names are incidental. They do not show any real difference in Agni. For example:

असौ तु रश्मिभिः केशी तेनैनानाह केशिनः । एतेषां तु पृथक्त्वेन त्रयाणां केशिनामिह ॥ (*asau tu raśmibhiḥ keśī tenainānāha keśinaḥ | eteṣāṃ tu pṛthaktvena trayāṇāṃ keśināmiha ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 1-94)

Because the earthly Agni has hair in the form of flames, the Agni of the middle world has hair in the form of lightning, and the Agni of the heavenly world, in the form of the sun, has hair in the form of rays, the sages have designated these three as त्रयः केशिनः (*trayaḥ keśinaḥ*).

एतेषां तु पृथक्त्वेन त्रयाणां केशिनामिह । संलक्ष्यंते प्रक्रियासु त्रयः केशिन इत्यृचि ॥ (*eteṣāṃ tu pṛthaktvena trayāṇāṃ keśināmiha | saṃlakṣyaṃte prakriyāsu trayaḥ keśina ityṛci ‖*)

### Printed p. 76
Since the nature of these varieties of Agni, with three kinds of hair, is different, they are designated "त्रयः केशिनः (*trayaḥ keśinaḥ*)" according to the respective differences of procedure.

न चैवेषां प्रसूतिर्वा विभूतिस्थान जन्म वा । निर्वक्तुं शक्त्यमेतैर्हि कृत्स्नं व्याप्तमिदं जगत् ॥ (*na caiveṣāṃ prasūtirvā vibhūtisthāna janma vā | nirvaktuṃ śaktyametairhi kṛtsnaṃ vyāptamidaṃ jagat ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 1-96)

But how is the individual birth of each? What are the individual special powers? Which are the separate places? It is not possible to say such things. Every variety pervades the whole world.

वैश्वानरं श्रितो ह्यग्निरग्निं वैश्वानरः श्रितः । अनयोर्जातवेदास्तु तथैते जातवेदसी ॥ (*vaiśvānaraṃ śrito hyagniragniṃ vaiśvānaraḥ śritaḥ | anayorjātavedāstu tathaite jātavedasī ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 1-97)

Agni is merged in Vaiśvānara. (The greatness of Vaiśvānara is explained in the 8th part of the Ṛ. Saṃ.) Vaiśvānara is merged in Agni. Jātavedas is hidden in both these forms. Therefore the first two varieties are the two forms of Jātavedas. Not these alone: all the gods are hidden and merged in this very Agni.

जातवेदाः श्रितो ह्यग्निमग्निं वैश्वानरः श्रितः । द्रविणोदास्तथेध्मश्च श्रितश्चाग्निं तनूनपात् ॥ नराशंसः श्रितश्चैनं एनमेवाश्रितस्त्वीळः । बर्हिर्द्वारश्च देव्योऽग्निमेनमेव तु संश्रिताः ॥ नक्तोषासा च देव्यौ च होतारावेतदाश्रयौ । देव्यस्ति स्रः श्रिताश्चैनं त्वष्टा चैवैतदाश्रयः ॥ श्रितो वनस्पतिश्चैनं स्वाहाकृतय एव च । (*jātavedāḥ śrito hyagnimagniṃ vaiśvānaraḥ śritaḥ | draviṇodāstathedhmaśca śritaścāgniṃ tanūnapāt ‖ narāśaṃsaḥ śritaścainaṃ enamevāśritastvīḷaḥ | barhirdvāraśca devyo 'gnimenameva tu saṃśritāḥ ‖ naktoṣāsā ca devyau ca hotārāvetadāśrayau | devyasti sraḥ śritāścainaṃ tvaṣṭā caivaitadāśrayaḥ ‖ śrito vanaspatiścainaṃ svāhākṛtaya eva ca |*) (Bṛ. De. 1.106-110)

Jātavedas, Vaiśvānara, Draviṇodāḥ, Idhma, Tanūnapāt, Narāśaṃsa, Iḷā (Barhis), the door-deities, the deities Nakta and Uṣas, the Hotṛs, Vanaspati and all the other deities are merged in this Agni. This Agni is the all-pervading great principle. This great principle is described as "मूर्धा भुवो भवति (*mūrdhā bhuvo bhavati*)", that is, in the night he is the lord of the earth, and so on. (This explanation was given earlier.)

यदेदेनमदधुर्यज्ञॆयासो दिवि देवाः सूर्यमादितेयं । यदा चरिष्णू मिथुनावभूतामादित्पापश्यन्भुवनानि विश्वा ॥ (*yadedenamadadhuryajñèyāso divi devāḥ sūryamāditeyaṃ | yadā cariṣṇū mithunāvabhūtāmāditpāpaśyanbhuvanāni viśvā ‖*) (Pu. Saṃ. 10-88-11) **NOTE**

The revered gods established this Agni in the heavenly world in the form of the sun. When this Sūra and the Vaiśvānaras became visible to the world, the gods became able to survey the whole world with the help of these, the sun and the others, who are parts of Agni. ##### Gist of the bhāṣya

"O Agni, you who are born in the sea, the great ocean, and who stand in the midst of the waters in the form of the submarine fire: Varuṇa, whose mind is attached to men as their benefactor, kindled you." Also, the Āditya, the beholder of men, who sees them directly, kindled you in the sun's orb in the heavenly world, which stands in the place of the udder (ऊधन् (*ūdhan*), said to be *ūdhasthānīya*) of the heavenly world. Also, the great gods of the middle region, the Maruts and others, the praisers, magnified you with praises as you stood in the third region, the atmosphere, in the form of lightning; that region fills the three, and is the place (the lap) of the waters, that is, the rain-waters.

Alternatively, the meaning is to be seen in the manner stated by the Vājasaneyins. It is this: समुद्रे त्वा नृमणा अप्स्यंतरिति (*samudre tvā nṛmaṇā apsyaṃtariti*): Prajāpati is nṛmaṇāḥ; "you in the waters", that is, Prajāpati. नृचक्षा ईधे दिवो अग्न ऊढन् (*nṛcakṣā īdhe divo agna ūḍhan*): Prajāpati is nṛcakṣāḥ; the waters are the udder of heaven. तृतीये त्वा रजसि तस्थिवांसं (*tṛtīye tvā rajasi tasthivāṃsaṃ*): heaven is the third region. *apāmupasthe mahiṣā avardhan*: the vital breaths are the mahiṣāḥ; "in heaven the breaths magnified you." The reference is given as Śata 7-7-4.5. **NOTE**

What is he doing? He licks the earth (क्षाम (*kṣāma*) is the earth) and is anointing, that is, scorching, the plants. Further, the moment he is born he, kindled, sees in various ways whatever has been burnt by him; हि (*hi*) merely fills out the verse-quarter. Further, he shines with his own radiance between heaven and earth (रोदसी (*rodasī*)). Or else the meaning stated by the Vājasaneyins is to be seen. It is this: अक्रंददग्निः स्तनयन्निव द्यौरिति क्रंदतीव हि पर्जन्यः स्तनयन्मा रेरिहद्वीरुधः समंजन्निति क्षामां वै रेरिह्यमाणो वीरुधः समनक्ति सद्यो जज्ञानो वि हीमिद्दो अख्यदिति सद्यो ना एष जज्ञान इदं सर्वं विख्यापयत्या रोदसी भानुना भात्यंतरितीमे वै द्यावापृथिवी रोदसी ते एष भानुना भात्यंतः । (*akraṃdadagniḥ stanayanniva dyauriti kraṃdatīva hi parjanyaḥ stanayanmā rerihadvīrudhaḥ samaṃjanniti kṣāmāṃ vai rerihyamāṇo vīrudhaḥ samanakti sadyo jajñāno vi hīmiddo akhyaditi sadyo nā eṣa jajñāna idaṃ sarvaṃ vikhyāpayatyā rodasī bhānunā bhātyaṃtaritīme vai dyāvāpṛthivī rodasī te eṣa bhānunā bhātyaṃtaḥ |*) (Śa. Brā. 6-7-3-2). Thus it is a praise of the lightning-fire; or else it is a praise of the forest-fire.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

The three preceding Ṛks have told of Agni's three kinds of birth, the varieties of Agni's forms corresponding to these births, the meditation appropriate to each form, and so on. In the present 4th and 5th Ṛks other great qualities of Agni are described. Of these the main ones are:
1. the likeness between Agni and Parjanya;
2. the radiance of Agni pervading both heaven and earth;
3. Agni's being addressed as guardian of Soma; and
4. the relation between Agni and Uṣas.

First, explaining this very Ṛk (4), the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa states the relation between Agni and Parjanya thus:
अक्रंददग्निः स्तनयन्निव द्यौरिति । क्रंदतीव हि पर्जन्यः स्तनयन् क्षामा रेरिहद्वीरुधः समंजन्निति क्षामा वै रेरिह्यमाणो वीरुधः समनक्ति सद्यो जज्ञानो वि हीमिद्दो अख्यडिति सद्यो ना एष जज्ञान इदं सर्वं विख्यापयत्या रोदसी भानुना भात्यंतरितीमे वै द्वावापृथिवी रोदसी ते एष भानुना भात्यंतः ॥ (*akraṃdadagniḥ stanayanniva dyauriti | kraṃdatīva hi parjanyaḥ stanayan kṣāmā rerihadvīrudhaḥ samaṃjanniti kṣāmā vai rerihyamāṇo vīrudhaḥ samanakti sadyo jajñāno vi hīmiddo akhyaḍiti sadyo nā eṣa jajñāna idaṃ sarvaṃ vikhyāpayatyā rodasī bhānunā bhātyaṃtaritīme vai dvāvāpṛthivī rodasī te eṣa bhānunā bhātyaṃtaḥ ‖*) (Śa. Brā. 6-7-3-2)
"Agni cried aloud like the thundering firmament." Just as Parjanya roars, Agni too makes a roaring sound. As Parjanya, touching the earth again and again, spreads out establishing his power in plants and the like, so Agni too spreads, as if scenting the earth again and again, and entering plants and the like and spreading. He shines between heaven and earth with his radiance, and makes these two worlds shine with his radiance. This is the explanation of the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa, which Sāyaṇa has put as वैद्युतस्य स्तुतिः (*vaidyutasya stutiḥ*), "this is a praise of Agni in the form of lightning". Sāyaṇa's opinion is that this is a praise of the forest-fire. Since the explanation आ रोदसी भानुना भात्यंतः (*ā rodasī bhānunā bhātyaṃtaḥ*) is there, it would be proper to say that it is the lightning-fire. It is also said that Parjanya is not merely similar to Agni but, having Agni's particular function, is a transformation of him:
तद्यदस्य तन्नामाकरोदाधित्यस्तद्रूपमभवदादित्यो वा ईशान आदित्यो ह्यस्य सर्वस्वेष्टे सोऽब्रवीदेतावान्वा अस्ति . . . . ॥ (*tadyadasya tannāmākarodādhityastadrūpamabhavadādityo vā īśāna ādityo hyasya sarvasveṣṭe so 'bravīdetāvānvā asti . . . . ‖*) (Śa. Brā. 6-1-3-15)

### Printed p. 81
Agni himself is the one called Bhava. Since everything comes into being from the fall of rain, this Bhava is of the form of Parjanya; thus Parjanya is said to be a special manifestation of Agni. Therefore it would not be wrong to say that in the present Ṛk the action is that of Parjanya or of the lightning-fire.

This lightning-fire is praised in the 5th Ṛk as सोमगोपाः (*somagopāḥ*). Sāyaṇa has explained it as सोमस्य पालकः (*somasya pālakaḥ*). Soma does not mean merely the Soma juice. All the characteristics that the lightning-fire has are also characteristics of Soma. Soma, having divinity, is of self-luminous brilliant form. Descriptions such as स सूर्यस्य रश्मिभिः परिव्यत (*sa sūryasya raśmibhiḥ parivyata*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-86-32) tell of Soma's brilliant divine nature. This Soma, of the form of brilliance, like Agni has three places. They are:
धत्ते सोम दिवि ज्योतिरत्यृथिव्यां यदुरावंतरिक्षे (*dhatte soma divi jyotiratyṛthivyāṃ yadurāvaṃtarikṣe*) (Śa. Brā. 3-9-4-12)
"O Soma-god, with the brilliance of yours that has spread in the heavenly world, the brilliance that shines in the atmospheric world, and the brilliance that is terrestrial, with all of these be nourishing to the sacrificer", and so on. The nature of Agni that the epithet त्रिषधस्थ (*triṣadhastha*) conveys, the same is conveyed by this description of Soma. In त्री षधस्था पुनानः कृणुते हरिः (*trī ṣadhasthā punānaḥ kṛṇute hariḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-103-2), although according to the context the meaning is the three vessels beginning with the Droṇakalaśa, yet on the basis of the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa passage above it would not be wrong to give here too the meaning "one who is in the three worlds".
नाभा पृथिव्या धरुणो महो दिवोऽपामूर्मौ सिंधुष्वंतरुक्षितः । इंद्रस्य वज्रो वृषभः विभूवसुः सोमो हृदे पवते चारु मत्सरः ॥ (*nābhā pṛthivyā dharuṇo maho divo 'pāmūrmau siṃdhuṣvaṃtarukṣitaḥ | iṃdrasya vajro vṛṣabhaḥ vibhūvasuḥ somo hṛde pavate cāru matsaraḥ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-72-2)
This Soma is the supporter of the heavenly world; the navel-like centre of the broad earth; the one in the middle of the atmosphere; a thunderbolt-weapon for Indra. Such a Soma is gratifying to Indra, it says.
पर्जन्यः पिता महिषस्य पर्णिनः (*parjanyaḥ pitā mahiṣasya parṇinaḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 9-82-3): Parjanya himself is the father of this Soma. He is also called "Parjanya-increased" (9-113-3).

The falcon that is described in the narrative as having brought Soma from the atmosphere is itself of the form of Agni.
नवं नु स्तोममग्नये दिवः श्येनाय जीजनं । (*navaṃ nu stomamagnaye divaḥ śyenāya jījanaṃ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-15-4)

### Printed p. 82
The meaning "Agni in the form of the falcon of the heavenly world is the guardian of the Agni of the form of lightning" is proper in the present context. (Since there is the task of guarding the terrestrial Soma juice and distributing it among the gods, there is no room in the present context for the meaning that this epithet is given to Agni on that account.)

That Agni shines endowed with radiance in both heaven and earth, and the constant relation between Agni and Uṣas, have been stated in many places before.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Agni is the केतुः (*ketuḥ*), the making-known, of all; the embryo of the भुवनस्य (*bhuvanasya*), that is, of water. Just as he is born, appearing, he fills (आपृणात् (*āpṛṇāt*)) heaven and earth. Going forth he splits even the firm mountain, the cloud. When? When the five peoples, men, sacrifice to Agni.

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

Agni, the one who makes known to the whole world, the embryo of the water, as soon as he is born pervades heaven and earth. At the time of moving about he splits even the firm cloud (he sends down rain). Therefore the people, of the five classes, also worship this Agni.

##### English translation
The manifester of all, the germ of the world, Agni, as soon as born fills heaven and earth (with light) : he fractures as he advances the solid cloud, for which the five classes of men praise him.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Agni desires oblations उशिघ्नवींषि (*uśighnavīṃṣi*) **NOTE**; he is the purifier, cleanser of the whole world; अरतिः (*aratiḥ*), a goer, or one who impels beings; सुमेधाः (*sumedhāḥ*), of good wisdom; immortal, free from the nature of death. Agni has been placed among mortals, that is, men. This Agni sends up smoke. Further, bearing a shining, radiant form, with white brilliance he reaches the heaven; "goes" is the supplied remainder.
