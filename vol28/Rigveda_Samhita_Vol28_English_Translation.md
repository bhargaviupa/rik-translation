Ṛgveda Saṃhitā (with explanation in Kannada translation)

Part 28

The eighth adhyāya in the seventh aṣṭaka

The forty-first sūkta in the tenth maṇḍala

**Introduction (Jīḷike)**

In the twenty-seventh part of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā the first forty sūktas of the tenth maṇḍala were completed. This twenty-eighth part begins with the forty-first sūkta; the first seven adhyāyas of the seventh aṣṭaka and twenty vargas of the eighth adhyāya are completed in it. The seer of the sūkta now beginning, the forty-first, which opens with समानमु त्यं (*samānamu tyaṃ*), is Suhastya, the son of the woman Ghoṣā. It is therefore necessary to describe briefly here the background of Ghoṣā, the mother of this seer, and of the seer Suhastya.

### Printed p. 2

It was explained earlier, with quotations from the Nirukta and other texts, what differing opinions exist about the principle the Aśvin deities represent. Many occasions that tell of the greatness of these deities were also set out. Many seers have praised them. Among them, the 39th and 40th sūktas of the tenth maṇḍala, composed by the woman seer Ghoṣā, and the 41st sūkta, composed by her son Suhastya, are well known. In these sūktas the seers praise the extraordinary great qualities of the Aśvins.

Although the sūktas composed by the seer named Ghoṣā are only two, the great help the Aśvins did her and her greatness were praised earlier in many mantras. Because she was an excellent devotee, these deities showed her great favour. Giving her personal background, the Bṛhaddevatā speaks of her thus:

आसीत्काक्षीवती घोषा पापरोगेण दुर्भगा । उवास षष्टिं वर्षाणि पितुरेव गृहे पुरु ॥ (*āsītkākṣīvatī ghoṣā pāparogeṇa durbhagā | uvāsa ṣaṣṭiṃ varṣāṇi pitureva gṛhe puru ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 7, 42)

A disfiguring and dreadful disease befell the woman Ghoṣā, daughter of Kakṣīvān. Her beauty was destroyed. For that very reason, without marriage, she had to live sixty years in her father's house.

आतस्थे महतीं चिंतां न पुत्रो न पतिर्मम । जरां प्राप्ता मुधा तस्मात् प्रपद्येऽहं शुभस्पती ॥ (*ātasthe mahatīṃ ciṃtāṃ na putro na patirmama | jarāṃ prāptā mudhā tasmāt prapadye 'haṃ śubhaspatī ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 7. 45)

"Unmarried, without husband and without son, I have grown old. My life is going to waste." Thus constant worry beset her. So she resolved: "I shall take refuge in the Aśvin deities, famed as Śubhaspatis (lords of radiance), praise them and obtain their grace"; and she prayed and cried out to the Aśvins.

यथैत् मामकस्तात आराध्यावाप यौवनं । आयुरारोग्यमैश्वर्यं सर्वभूतहने विषं ॥ (*yathait māmakastāta ārādhyāvāpa yauvanaṃ | āyurārogyamaiśvaryaṃ sarvabhūtahane viṣaṃ ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 7. 44)

"My father worshipped these same Aśvin deities and by their grace obtained youth, long life, health, strength, and a poison able to kill all beings. So I too shall worship them and obtain my wish."

### Printed p. 3

रूपवत्तां च सौभाग्यमहं तस्य सुता यदि । ममापि मंत्राः प्रादुः स्युरैस्तोष्टे मयाश्विन् ॥ (*rūpavattāṃ ca saubhāgyamahaṃ tasya sutā yadi | mamāpi maṃtrāḥ prāduḥ syuraistoṣṭe mayāśvin ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 7. 45)

"If I too come to know the way of praising and worshipping the Aśvin deities according to rule, I shall worship them and by their grace obtain beauty and well-being. As my father obtained a boon from them, I too shall obtain my wishes." Thus she reflected on the manner of praising the Aśvins.

चिंतयंतीति सूक्ते द्वे यो वां परि ददर्श सा। स्तुषौ तावश्विनौ देवौ प्रीत् तस्या भगांतरं ॥ (*ciṃtayaṃtīti sūkte dve yo vāṃ pari dadarśa sā| stuṣau tāvaśvinau devau prīt tasyā bhagāṃtaraṃ ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 7. 46)

While she was thus absorbed in meditation she received the vision of two sūktas, beginning with the mantras यो वां परि (*yo vāṃ pari*) and रथं यांतं (*rathaṃ yāṃtaṃ*) (10-39; 10-40), and praised the Aśvins with them. The Aśvin deities, pleased with this, entered her body.

प्रविश्य विजरारोगां सुभगां चक्रतुश्च ता । भर्तारं ददतुस्तस्यॆ सुहस्त्यं च सुतं मुनिं ॥ (*praviśya vijarārogāṃ subhagāṃ cakratuśca tā | bhartāraṃ dadatustasyè suhastyaṃ ca sutaṃ muniṃ ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 7. 47)

Then they removed her old age, freed her from disease and gave her a beautiful form. They also bestowed an excellent husband and an excellent son named Suhastya. Suhastya too is a renowned seer, like her.

ददतुस्तत्सुवर्णाभ्यां यन्नासत्येति कीर्त्यते । काक्षीवत्कै च घोषायै न तस्यामाजुरोऽनया ॥ (*dadatustatsuvarṇābhyāṃ yannāsatyeti kīrtyate | kākṣīvatkai ca ghoṣāyai na tasyāmājuro 'nayā ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 6. 48)

The manner in which the Aśvin deities came with their excellent suparṇas (horses having wings) and favoured Ghoṣā is described in laudatory sentences such as अमाजुरश्चिद्भवथः (*amājuraścidbhavathaḥ*) and न तस्य विद्म (*na tasya vidma*) (10-39-2; 10. 40. 11).

Ghoṣā is praised as belonging to the company of Brahmavādinīs, who have the power of supersensory vision and excellent skill in ritual action. These Brahmavādinīs are divided into three groups. Each of the three groups possesses a different kind of power and the ability to see the deities; the power of the woman seers of these groups is described thus:

### Printed p. 4

नवकः प्रथमस्ता सां वर्गस्तुष्टाव देवताः । यषिभिर्देवताभिश्च समूदे मध्यमो गणः ॥ (*navakaḥ prathamastā sāṃ vargastuṣṭāva devatāḥ | yaṣibhirdevatābhiśca samūde madhyamo gaṇaḥ ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 2, 85)

Of these, the first group has nine members. They praised the deities but did not speak directly with them. Those of the second group alone spoke directly both with the seers and with the deities.

आत्मनो भाववृत्तानि जगौ वर्गस्तथोत्तमः । उत्तमस्य तु वर्गस्य य ऋषिः सैव देवता ॥ (*ātmano bhāvavṛttāni jagau vargastathottamaḥ | uttamasya tu vargasya ya ṛṣiḥ saiva devatā ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 2. 86)

The seers who composed a sūkta praising their own bhāvavṛtta, that is, the special states of change of the self, are of the highest group. The seers of this group who composed the sūkta are themselves the deities named in that sūkta. Thus the Brahmavādinīs are of three kinds. Stating that Ghoṣā belongs to the first group, the text says:

घोषा, गोधा, विश्ववारा, अपालोपनिषन्निषत् । ब्रह्मजाया जुहूर्नाम अगस्त्यस्य स्वसादितिः ॥ (*ghoṣā, godhā, viśvavārā, apālopaniṣanniṣat | brahmajāyā juhūrnāma agastyasya svasāditiḥ ‖*) (Bṛ. De. 2, 82)

Ghoṣā (10-39, 40); Godhā; Viśvavārā (5. 28); Apālā (7. 91); Upaniṣat and Niṣat (composers of the khila sūkta beginning प्र धारयंत (*pra dhārayaṃta*)); Juhū Brahmajāyā (10. 109); the sister of Agastya (10. 60. 6); Aditi (some mantras in 4. 18). Thus it is indicated that Ghoṣā is one of the Brahmavādinīs of the first group. Therefore it becomes clear that Ghoṣā was a human woman with supersensory power able to realise divinity. Though a human woman, it is clear that by her divine power she worshipped the deities, saw them directly and obtained their grace.

It is also stated that although she had such divine power, because of her physical defect she could not obtain a proper husband, and that a life without husband and son grieved her mind. From such examples we learn how physical health and beauty serve as means to happiness in life. From this and many similar circumstances the nature of social life in the Vedic age is indicated: women lacking these two remained unmarried in their fathers' houses even when very old.

Even when life is sorrowful, Ghoṣā's life shows that by leading a pure life and by acts of worship of the deities this sorrow can be removed. Her excellent

### Printed p. 5

praise and worship pleased the Aśvins, and it is praised in many places that they brought her the highest happiness. This was explained earlier (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1. 112. 7 etc.).

It is also stated that a son named Suhastya was born to Ghoṣā by the grace of the Aśvins. This Suhastya too is an excellent composer of praises. He is known as the composer of the 41st sūkta of Ṛ. Saṃ. 10. The Ṛks of the present sūkta show his skill in composition. In them he praises the Aśvin deities. Ghoṣā and Ghauṣeya, both in their works, praised the divine powers of the Aśvins. These divine powers, and the differing opinions about the Aśvins, were stated earlier. Here only some points described in the present sūktas will be examined.

Sentences such as व्युष्टा उषसो हवामहे (*vyuṣṭā uṣaso havāmahe*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10. 41. 1) and प्रातर्युजं नासत्याधितिष्ठथः (*prātaryujaṃ nāsatyādhitiṣṭhathaḥ*) (10, 41. 2) show the natural relation between the Aśvin deities and Uṣas. It was also stated earlier that, according to one view cited by the Nirukta authors, the Aśvins are of the form of day and night (Ni. 12, 1). Many have approved this view. They are invoked at both day and night. This is clear in the very Ṛks composed by Ghoṣā. For example:

युवां मृगेव वारणा मृगण्यवो दोषा वस्तोर्हविषा नि ह्वयामहे। युवं होत्रामृतुथा जुह्वते नरेषं जनाय वहथः शुबस्पती ॥ (*yuvāṃ mṛgeva vāraṇā mṛgaṇyavo doṣā vastorhaviṣā ni hvayāmahe| yuvaṃ hotrāmṛtuthā juhvate nareṣaṃ janāya vahathaḥ śubaspatī ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10, 40, 4; the Kannada prints य (*ya*). सं (*saṃ*).)

"O Aśvin deities, at day and night we call and invite you with oblations." Thus their appearance at both these times is stated.

ताविद्दोषा ता उषसि शुभस्पती ता यामन्नुद्रवर्तनी (*tāviddoṣā tā uṣasi śubhaspatī tā yāmannudravartanī*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8. 22. 14)

Mantras composed by other seers, such as this, also make the matter clear. But here there is another refinement. According to some, "day and night" should be understood as the twilight at the time of the appearance of day and the twilight at the time of the appearance of night. These twilights consist of both light and dimness. But this refinement is not so fitting. Another view is that there is no simultaneous companionship in them. The answer to this objection is that, just as unity and companionship are spoken of in Mitra and Varuṇa, who represent day and night, so they must be spoken of here too. The view that the Aśvins appear at the time of Uṣas' appearance is stated in many places. But the time of Uṣas' appearance is not only the morning; the beginning of night too is designated Uṣas. For example:

### Printed p. 6

सुरुक्मे हि सुपेशसाधि श्रिया विराजतः । उषासावेह सीदशां ॥ (*surukme hi supeśasādhi śriyā virājataḥ | uṣāsāveha sīdaśāṃ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1. 18. 6)

Here the word Uṣas is used in the dual, उषासा (*uṣāsā*). For this word the meaning "day and night" is given. The Bhāṣya explains: उषसा एतत् रात्रेरप्युपलक्षणं उषःशब्दो दिवसस्कोपलक्षकः रात्रिरुषाश्च (*uṣasā etat rātrerapyupalakṣaṇaṃ uṣaḥśabdo divasaskopalakṣakaḥ rātriruṣāśca*). The Aśvins are described by नाना जात् (*nānā jāt*) (5-73.4); the purport of this sentence is that they are born in both day and night.

Along with this close relation of Uṣas and the Aśvins, a close relation of the goddess Sūryā and the Aśvins is also stated. Whether this allows an identity of Uṣas and Sūryā to be spoken of must be stated later, when Sūryā is explained (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10, 45). That is, since Sūryā is said to have mounted the three-wheeled chariot of the Aśvins, it is necessary here to examine the nature of the three wheels. In रथं त्रिचक्रं सवना गनिग्यतं (*rathaṃ tricakraṃ savanā ganigyataṃ*) (10. 41. 1), and likewise in other places (10. 85, 15), the three-wheeled chariot described can on the whole be said to be the three-wheeled chariot of Āditya.

This chariot is described in some places as one-wheeled, in others as two-wheeled, in still others as three-wheeled. Wherever one wheel is mentioned it is the wheel in the form of Āditya; where two wheels are mentioned they are the two wheels of Āditya's chariot touching heaven and earth. The same two wheels belong to the chariot of the Aśvins also. Stating this:

न्यंघ्नस्य मूर्धनि चक्रं रथस्य येमथुः । परि द्यामन्यदीयते ॥ (*nyaṃghnasya mūrdhani cakraṃ rathasya yemathuḥ | pari dyāmanyadīyate ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1. 20. 19; the Kannada prints य (*ya*). सं (*saṃ*).)

"O Aśvins, of the two wheels of your chariot you have set one on a firm mountain-top; the other you have made go around the whole heaven." These two wheels are visible to the world. The third is hidden.

द्वे ते चक्रे सूर्ये ब्रह्माण यतुथा विदुः । अथैकं चक्रं यद्दु हा तदद्धातय इद्दिदुः ॥ (*dve te cakre sūrye brahmāṇa yatuthā viduḥ | athaikaṃ cakraṃ yaddu hā tadaddhātaya iddiduḥ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10. 85. 16)

"O Sūryā, the skilled Brāhmaṇas can see two wheels of your chariot; but the other wheel alone is hidden." In explaining this, Sāyaṇa explains द्वे एव चंद्र सूर्यात्मके (*dve eva caṃdra sūryātmake*) as the two wheels in the form of moon and sun. And since the third wheel is hidden, he says that its nature is not accessible to ordinary awareness.
