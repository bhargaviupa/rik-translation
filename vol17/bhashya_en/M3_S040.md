## Introduction

The hymn beginning ⟦इंद्र त्वेति⟧, of nine Ṛks, is the second hymn; it is Viśvāmitra's, in the Gāyatrī metre, and addressed to Indra. And so it is stated in the Anukramaṇī: ⟦इंद्र त्वा गायत्रं हीति⟧. In the Agniṣṭoma, at the morning pressing, this hymn belongs to the śastra of the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin. And this is laid down in the sūtra: ⟦इंद्र त्वा वृषभमुद्देवभीति तिस्रः⟧ (⟦आ. ५-१०⟧), that is, the hymn with this opening and the three Ṛks with the opening that follows it**NOTE**{Introduction: the second opening quoted from the sūtra, after the opening of this hymn, is misread in the supplied text; is it 'ud ghed abhi'?}.

The first Ṛk is, at the morning pressing, the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin's yājyā for the Soma that has been set forth. And this is laid down in the sūtra: ⟦इंद्र त्वा वृषभं⟧, ⟦मरुतो यस्य⟧ (⟦आ. ५-५⟧).

## Ṛk 1

He delights, he sports, in the indu, the flowing Soma-juice: therefore he is Indra. Or else: he shows all this world directly: therefore he is Indra. The word is the vocative of that. O ⟦इंद्र⟧, we call you, ⟦वृषभं⟧, the showerer of desires, ⟦सुते⟧, when the Soma has been pressed out with the stones; we summon you for the drinking of the Soma. You, being such, ⟦पाहि⟧, drink, the Soma that is ⟦मध्वो⟧, intoxicating, and ⟦अंधसः⟧, of the nature of food.

Yāska has explained the word Indra in many ways: he is Indra because he ⟦इरां दृणातीति⟧, splits irā; or because he ⟦ददातीति⟧, gives irā; or because he ⟦दधातीति⟧, places irā; or because he ⟦दारयतीति⟧, causes irā to be split; or because he ⟦धारयतीति⟧, holds irā; or because he runs for the indu; or because he delights in the indu; or because he kindles beings**NOTE**{Ṛk 1: in the quotation from Yāska the last derivation and the verb of the next sentence are damaged in the supplied text; they are translated as 'indhe' (kindles) and 'samaindhan' (kindled) — to be confirmed against the Nirukta.}. "In that they kindled him with the breaths, that is the Indra-hood of Indra." Wherever a given sense is suitable, there that sense is to be adopted. ⟦इदंदर्शनादित्योपमन्यवः⟧: "from the seeing of this", says Aupamanyava. He sees this, the brahman, directly: therefore he is Indra. And so it is heard in the Āraṇyaka: ⟦स एतमेव पुरुषं ब्रह्मततमपश्यदिदमदर्शमिती ३⟧, ⟦तस्मादिंद्रो नामेदंड्रो ह वै नाम तमिदंद्रं संतमिंद्र इत्याचक्षते परोक्षेण परोक्षप्रिया इव हि देवाः⟧ (⟦ऐ. आ. २-४-३⟧): "He saw this very Person as the brahman, spread out: 'I have seen this.' Therefore he is Idandra by name; Idandra indeed is his name. Him, who is Idandra, they call Indra in an indirect way; for the gods are, as it were, fond of the indirect."

⟦इंद्र⟧: the root is ⟦इदि परमैश्वर्य⟧, idi in the sense of supreme lordship. Supreme lordship is the having of the form of the world through his own māyā. With this intention it is heard: ⟦इंद्रो मायाभिः पुरुरूप ईयत इति⟧, "Indra, through his māyās, goes about in many forms." When the word indu is the accompanying word, the suffix ḍa is added to the root ram by the rule ⟦रमतेरन्येभ्योऽपि⟧ ⟦दृश्यत इति डः⟧, that is, by "it is seen after other roots also"; the vowel u of the first member is elided. From the root ⟦इदि परमैश्वर्य⟧ the word is laid down ready-made, as ending in the suffix ran, by the rule beginning ⟦दृजेंद्राग्रेत्यादिना⟧. In this way these derivations are to be understood. As a vocative it has the udātta on its first syllable. ⟦हवामहे⟧: it is the form in laṭ of the root hvayati, with samprasāraṇa by the rule ⟦बहुलं छंदसीति⟧. ⟦मध्वः⟧: the word madhu ends in the suffix un by the rule beginning ⟦फलिपाट⟧. Because the teaching of augments is not invariable, the augment num is absent. Because a rule that is preceded by a technical name is not invariable, guṇa is absent. There is the substitution of the semivowel. ⟦अंधसः⟧: from the root ad the suffix asun is added by the rule ⟦अदेर्नुम्⟧ ⟦धश्चेत्यसुन्⟧. The accent is that of a suffix marked with n.

## Ṛk 2

At the morning pressing the Ṛk ⟦इंद्र क्रतुविदमिति⟧ is the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin's yājyā of the śastra. And this is laid down in the sūtra: ⟦इंद्र क्रतुविदं सुतमिति याज्या⟧ (⟦आ. ५-१०⟧), "the Ṛk with this opening is the yājyā."

O ⟦इंद्र⟧, ⟦हर्य⟧, desire to drink, this Soma that is ⟦क्रतुविदं⟧, the procurer of kratu, of understanding, and ⟦सुतं⟧, pressed out. O ⟦पुरुष्टुत⟧, Indra praised by many, ⟦पिब⟧, drink, this Soma that is ⟦तातृपिं⟧, satisfying, causing fullness. ⟦वृषस्व⟧: pour this Soma, when drunk, into your belly. The sense is: hold it in such a way that it does not fall below the navel.

⟦हर्य⟧: it is the form in loṭ of the root ⟦हर्य गतिकांत्योरित्यस्य⟧, harya in the senses of going and desiring. It is without accent. ⟦पुरुष्टुत⟧: the ṣ in the Saṃhitā text is by the rule ⟦स्तुतस्तोमयोश्चंदसीति⟧. ⟦वृषस्व⟧: it is the form in loṭ of the root ⟦वृष सेचन⟧, vṛṣ in the sense of sprinkling. Because it follows the particle āṅ, it is without accent. ⟦तातृपिं⟧: from the root ⟦तृप⟧ ⟦प्रीणन⟧, tṛp in the sense of gratifying, the suffix kin is added by the rule ⟦सदादिभ्यो दर्शनादिति किन्⟧. Because that suffix is treated like liṭ, there is reduplication and the rest. In the Saṃhitā text the reduplicating syllable is lengthened by the rule ⟦संहितायामन्येषामपीत्यभ्यासस्य⟧. Because the suffix is marked with n, the word has the udātta on its first syllable.

## Ṛk 3

O ⟦स्तवान⟧, you who are being praised by the praisers; O ⟦विश्वते⟧, lord of the clans, the Maruts; O ⟦इंद्र⟧: accompanied by ⟦विश्वेभिर्देवेभिः⟧, all the gods who are worthy of sacrifice, that ⟦धितावानं⟧ — what is shared, partaken of, is ⟦वानं⟧, the oblation; that which has its oblation placed, deposited, is so called; the meaning is "which has its oblations made ready" — this sacrifice of ours, ⟦प्र तिरः⟧, increase it exceedingly. The sense is: make it complete by accepting the oblation.

⟦णः⟧: the ṇ in the Saṃhitā text is by the rule ⟦उपसर्गाद्बहुलमिति⟧. ⟦धितावानं⟧: from the root ḍudhāñ in the senses of holding and nourishing comes the niṣṭhā suffix. In a bahuvrīhi compound the accent is that of the first member. ⟦तिरः⟧: from the root tarati the suffix śa is added by transposition. The accent is that of the suffix. ⟦स्तवान⟧: the root is ṣṭuñ in the sense of praising. By the rule ⟦सम्यानच् स्तुव⟧, even in the absence of the accompanying word, the suffix ānac is added in the sense of the object. Because it is a vocative, it is without accent.

## Ṛk 4

O ⟦सत्पते⟧, Indra: ⟦इमे⟧, these, ⟦सोमाः⟧, the Somas given by us, which are ⟦चंद्रासः⟧, gladdening, ⟦इंदवः⟧, bright, and ⟦सुताः⟧, pressed out, ⟦प्र यंति⟧, go forth exceedingly, to the ⟦क्षयं⟧ — food and the like dwell, abide, in it, therefore kṣaya is the belly. The sense is: hold them.

⟦क्षयं⟧: from the root ⟦क्षि⟧, kṣi in the senses of dwelling and going, the suffix gha is added in the sense of the locus. While the udātta on the final syllable would result from the accent of the suffix, the udātta on the first syllable is by the rule ⟦क्षयो निवास⟧. ⟦चंद्रासः⟧: the root is ⟦चदि आह्लादने⟧, cadi in the senses of gladdening and shining. From this the suffix rak is added by the rule beginning ⟦यितंचीत्यादिना⟧. The augment asuk is by the rule ⟦आज्ञसेरसुगित्यसुक्⟧. The accent is that of the suffix. ⟦इंदवः⟧: the suffix u is added by the rule ⟦उंदेरिच्चा देर⟧. Because "marked with n" continues from the preceding rule, the word has the udātta on its first syllable.

## Ṛk 5

O ⟦इंद्र⟧, ⟦दधिष्व⟧, hold, in your ⟦जठरे⟧, belly, the Soma that is ⟦वरेण्यं⟧, to be chosen by all, and ⟦सुतं⟧, pressed out. ⟦इंदवः⟧, these bright Somas, are yours, ⟦द्युक्षासः⟧: they dwell, abide, in heaven, therefore they are called so. The sense is: therefore do you hold them.

⟦दधिष्व⟧: it is the form in loṭ of the root dadhāti. Because the augment iṭ is anudātta by the rule ⟦अनुदात्ता⟧, "augments are anudātta", the accent is that of the suffix. ⟦वरेण्यं⟧: the root is vṛñ in the sense of sharing. The suffix eṇya is added by the rule ⟦वृज एण्य⟧. Because the word belongs to the group beginning with vṛṣa, it has the udātta on its first syllable. ⟦द्युक्षासः⟧: from the root kṣiyati the suffix ḍa is added by the rule ⟦दृश्यत इति⟧, "it is seen after other roots also". The accent is that of a final member formed with a kṛt suffix.

## Ṛk 6

⟦गिर्वणो⟧: O you who are to be adored with songs. And so Yāska says: ⟦गिर्वणा देवो भवति गीर्भिरेनं⟧ ⟦वनयंति⟧ (⟦नि. ६-१४⟧), "Girvaṇas is a god; they adore him with songs." O Indra who are such, ⟦पाहि⟧, drink, ⟦नः⟧, our, this Soma that is ⟦सुतं⟧, pressed out; since you are ⟦अज्यसे⟧, sprinkled, ⟦धाराभिः⟧, with the streams, ⟦मधोः⟧, of the intoxicating Soma. O ⟦इंद्र⟧, only ⟦यशः⟧, food, that is ⟦त्वादातं⟧, purified, made clear, by you, comes to be among us.

⟦गिर्वणः⟧: from the root ⟦वन⟧, van in the sense of sharing, comes the suffix asun. The absence of lengthening of the penultimate of gir is a Vedic peculiarity. The udātta on the first syllable is the one taught in the sixth book by the rule on the vocative. ⟦पाहि⟧: it is the form in loṭ of the root ⟦पा⟧, pā in the sense of drinking. Because the vocative is treated as not being there, the word stands at the beginning of the pāda and so does not lose its accent. The accent is that of the suffix. ⟦त्वादातं⟧: the root is ⟦दैप् शोधने⟧, daip in the sense of purifying. Although the letter p is there, "the not ending in a diphthong is not something brought about by an indicatory letter"; so this root does end in a diphthong. Then the substitution of ā is by the rule ⟦अदेच⟧. From this root the suffix kta is added in the sense of the object. Because, through the prohibition "not dāp" in the rule ⟦दाधा घ्नदाबित्यत्रादाबिति⟧, the technical name ghu does not apply, the substitute dad taught by the rule ⟦दद्योरिति⟧ does not come about. The first member is the instrumental of the word yuṣmad**NOTE**{Ṛk 6: the form quoted before 'iti yuṣmacchabdasya tṛtīyā' is misread in the supplied text; which form of yuṣmad does Sāyaṇa name here?}; the compound is by the rule ⟦कृता बहुलमिति समासः⟧. By the rule ⟦तृतीया कर्मणीति⟧ the first member keeps its original accent. ⟦यशः⟧: the root is ⟦अशू व्याप्तौ⟧, aśū in the sense of pervading. The suffix asun is added by the rule ⟦अशेयुट्टेत्यसुन्⟧; in conjunction with it the root takes the augment yuṭ. Because the suffix is marked with n, the word has the udātta on its first syllable.

## Ṛk 7

⟦वनिनः⟧: vana is worship directed to the deities; he who has it is a vanin. Of him, the sacrificer who is worshipping the gods, the oblations, Soma and the others, which are ⟦द्युम्नानि⟧, shining, and ⟦अक्षिता⟧, free from wasting, complete, ⟦सचंते⟧, come together, ⟦अभि⟧, on all sides, to ⟦इंद्रं⟧, Indra. Among them, ⟦सोमस्य पीत्वी⟧, having drunk the Soma, ⟦वावृधे⟧: that Indra grows.

⟦वनिनः⟧: the suffix ini is added by the rule ⟦अत इनिठनानि⟧. The accent is that of the suffix. ⟦सचंते⟧: it is the form in laṭ of the root ⟦षच समवाय⟧, ṣac in the sense of coming together. ⟦अक्षिता⟧: it is the niṣṭhā form of the root ⟦क्षि क्षय⟧, kṣi in the sense of wasting. Here, because of the words "in the sense of ṇyat", there is no lengthening. For that very reason the substitution of n for the niṣṭhā suffix, taught by the rule ⟦क्षियो दीर्घादिति⟧, is absent. In the compound with the negative particle nañ, the accent is that of the particle. ⟦पीत्वी⟧: from the root ⟦पा⟧, pā in the sense of drinking, the suffix ktvā has the substitute ⟦त्वी⟧ by being laid down ready-made in the rule ⟦स्नात्वादयश्चेति⟧. The accent is that of the suffix. The vowel ī is by the rule beginning ⟦घुमास्थागापेत्यादिना⟧.

## Ṛk 8

O ⟦वृत्रहन्⟧, Indra: ⟦अर्वावतः⟧, from the hither, the near place, or ⟦परावतः⟧, from the very distant place, ⟦आ गहि⟧, come, towards ⟦नः⟧, us. And having come, ⟦जुषस्व⟧, attend to, ⟦इमाः⟧, these, ⟦गिरः⟧, words, ⟦नः⟧, of ours, which have the nature of praise.

⟦परावतः⟧: the suffix vati is added by the rule ⟦उपसर्गाच्छंदसि धात्वर्थ इति वतिः⟧. The accent is that of the suffix.

## Ṛk 9

O ⟦इंद्र⟧: ⟦यत्⟧, in which place, ⟦अंतरा⟧, between ⟦परावतं⟧, the very remote place, and ⟦अर्वावतं⟧, the near place, that is, in the region in the middle of the two, you are ⟦हूयसे⟧, duly worshipped with sacrifice, ⟦ततः⟧, from that place, ⟦आ गहि⟧, come, ⟦इह⟧, here to this sacrifice; come for the drinking of the Soma.

⟦परावतं⟧, ⟦अर्वावतं⟧: the accusative is by the rule ⟦अंतरांतरेण युक्ते⟧ (⟦पा. २-३-४⟧). ⟦ततः⟧: the suffix tasil stands for the ablative. The accent is that of a suffix marked with l.
