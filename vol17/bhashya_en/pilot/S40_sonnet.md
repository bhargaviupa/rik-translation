## Introduction

The second sūkta, beginning ⟦इंद्र त्वा⟧, of nine Ṛks, is Vaiśvāmitra (its seer is Viśvāmitra), its metre is Gāyatrī, and its deity is Indra. So the Anukramaṇī says: ⟦इंद्र त्वा गायत्रं हीति⟧. In the Agniṣṭoma, at the morning pressing, this sūkta is the śastra of the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin. It is also laid down in the sūtra: ⟦इंद्र त्वा वृषभमुद्देवभीति तिस्रः⟧ (Ā. 5-10). The first Ṛk is the prasthita-yājyā of the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin at the morning pressing. This too is laid down in the sūtra: ⟦इंद्र त्वा वृषभं मरुतो यस्य⟧ (Ā. 5-5).

## Ṛk 1

He who delights and sports (ramate, krīḍati) in the Soma juice as it flows (sravati) in the drop (indu) is Indra. Or else: he who shows this whole world directly (sākṣāt) is Indra. The word ⟦इंद्र⟧ is here in the vocative. O Indra, we call you, ⟦वृषभं⟧, the showerer of desires, at the Soma ⟦सुते⟧, that is, pressed with the pressing stones; we invite you in order that you may drink the Soma. You, then, drink (⟦पाहि⟧, that is, pib) the Soma, which is characterized as food (⟦अंधसः⟧) and is ⟦मध्वः⟧, intoxicating.

Yāska explained the word Indra in many ways: ⟦इंद्र इरां दृणातीति⟧, he splits the earth (irā); ⟦वेरां ददातीति⟧, or he gives the earth; ⟦वेरां दधातीति⟧, or he places it; ⟦वेरां दारयतीति⟧, or he tears it open; ⟦वेरां धारयतीति⟧, or he supports it; ⟦वेंदवे द्रवतीति⟧, or he runs to the drop; ⟦वेंदौ रमत इति⟧, or he delights in the drop; ⟦वेंद्दे भूतानीति वा⟧ **NOTE**{The text reads this clause with the letters as supplied and I cannot construe it; is it meant to say that beings kindle him?}. Then: ⟦तद्यदेनं प्राणैः सम्यंधंस्तदिंद्रस्येंद्रत्वमिति⟧, that is, because beings kindled him with their breaths, that is Indra's Indra-hood **NOTE**{The verb ⟦सम्यंधंस्⟧ is not clear to me as supplied; I have translated it as "kindled".}. In whatever place a given sense fits, that sense is to be accepted there. The followers of Upamanyu say: ⟦इदंदर्शनादित्योपमन्यवः⟧, that is, from seeing this: Indra sees this Brahman directly. And so it is heard in the Āraṇyaka: ⟦स एतमेव पुरुषं ब्रह्मततमपश्यदिदमदर्शमिती ३⟧, that is, he saw this very Person, Brahman stretched out everywhere, and said: "I have seen this." Then: ⟦तस्मादिंद्रो नामेदंड्रो ह वै नाम तमिदंद्रं संतमिंद्र इत्याचक्षते परोक्षेण परोक्षप्रिया इव हि देवाः⟧, that is, therefore his name is Indra; his name is indeed Idaṇdra; him, being Idaṃdra, they call Indra, in an indirect way, for the gods are fond of what is indirect (Ai. Ā. 2-4-3).

Another derivation: Indra, from the root ⟦इदि परमैश्वर्य⟧, that is, the root idi in the sense of supreme lordship. Supreme lordship is the taking on of the form of the world by his own māyā. With this idea it is heard: ⟦इंद्रो मायाभिः पुरुरूप ईयत⟧, Indra goes about in many forms by his māyās. Thus these etymologies are to be understood.

Grammar. When the word indu stands as the preceding member, the suffix ḍa is added to the root ram by the rule ending ⟦दृश्यत इति डः⟧; the final o of the first member is elided (⟦पूर्वपदस्योकारलोपः⟧). Or else, from idi in the sense of supreme lordship, the word is irregularly formed as ending in the suffix ran by the rule beginning with ṛjendrāgra. As a vocative, it has the acute accent on its first syllable (⟦आमंत्रिताद्युदात्तत्वं⟧).

⟦हवामहे⟧: it is the present-tense (laṭ) form of the verb that the text writes as haiyati **NOTE**{The text reads this verb as haiyate; I cannot tell whether hvayati is meant.}, with samprasāraṇa by the rule ⟦बहुलं छंदसीति⟧.

⟦मध्वः⟧: the word madhu ends in the suffix un by the rule beginning with phalipāṭi (⟦फलिपाटीत्यादिनोन्प्रत्ययांतः⟧). Because the teaching about augments is not universal (⟦आगमानुशासनस्यानित्यत्वान्नु मभावः⟧), the augment num does not occur. Because a rule that is preceded by a technical term is not universal (⟦संज्ञापूर्वकस्य विधेरनित्यत्वाद्दुणाभावः⟧), guṇa does not occur. Then comes the substitution of a semivowel (yaṇ).

⟦अंधसः⟧: from the root ad, by the rule ⟦अदेर्नुम्⟧ the augment num is added and, by the rule ⟦धश्चेत्यसुन्⟧, dh is substituted and the suffix asun is added. The word ends in the sound represented by ⟦नित्सर्वः⟧ **NOTE**{The words nit-sarvaḥ at the end of this block seem to refer to the accent from the n-mark; is sarvaḥ the intended word?}.

## Ṛk 2

For the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin at the morning pressing, the verse beginning ⟦इंद्र क्रतुविदमिति⟧ is the śastra-yājyā. It is also laid down in the sūtra: ⟦इंद्र क्रतुविदं सुतमिति याज्या⟧ (Ā. 5-10).

O Indra, ⟦क्रतुविदं⟧, the obtainer of kratu, that is, of insight; ⟦सुतं⟧, pressed: desire (⟦हर्य⟧) to drink this Soma. O ⟦पुरुष्टुत⟧, Indra, praised (stuta) by the many (puru), drink this Soma, ⟦तातृपिं⟧, that is, satisfying **NOTE**{The text next has a word read as āsyāyanakāriṇi; I take it to mean "causing refreshment" but the letters are not clear to me.}. Having drunk this Soma, ⟦वृषस्व⟧, pour it, that is, sprinkle it into the belly. The sense is: hold it so that it does not fall below the navel.

Grammar. ⟦हर्य⟧: the form is the imperative (loṭ) of the root harya, in the senses of going and desiring (⟦हर्य गतिकांत्योरित्यस्य⟧). It loses its accent (nighāta). ⟦पुरुष्टुत⟧: by the rule ⟦स्तुतस्तोमयोश्चंदसीति⟧ the change of s to ṣ takes place in the saṃhitā. ⟦वृषस्व⟧: the form is the imperative of the root vṛṣ in the sense of sprinkling (⟦वृष सेचन⟧). Because it follows ā, it loses its accent. ⟦तातृपिं⟧: from the root tṛp in the sense of pleasing, the suffix kin is added in the Veda by the rule ⟦सदादिभ्यो दर्शनादिति⟧. Because it is treated like the perfect (liṭ), there is reduplication and so on; by the rule ⟦संहितायामन्येषामपीत्यभ्यासस्य⟧ the reduplicating syllable is lengthened. Because the suffix is n-marked, the first syllable has the acute accent.

## Ṛk 3

O ⟦स्तवान⟧, praised by the singers; O ⟦विश्वते⟧, lord of the viś, that is, of the Maruts; O Indra, together with ⟦विश्वेभिर्देवेभिः⟧, that is, all the gods worthy of the sacrifice: you, ⟦धितावानं⟧. Here vāna is the oblation, because it is shared (⟦वन्यते संभज्यत⟧); dhita means placed; so the sacrifice is one in which the oblation has been placed, that is, in which the oblation has been gathered. This sacrifice of ours, ⟦प्र तिरः⟧, make it grow vigorously. The sense is: make it complete by accepting the oblation.

Grammar. ⟦णः⟧: by the rule ⟦उपसर्गाद्बहुलमिति⟧ the change of n to ṇ takes place in the saṃhitā. ⟦धितावानं⟧: from the root ḍudhāṇ in the senses of holding and nourishing (⟦डुधाण् धारणपोषणयोरित्यस्मान्निष्ठा⟧) the niṣṭhā suffix is added. In a bahuvrīhi compound the accent is that of the first member. ⟦तिरः⟧: from the root tṛ the suffix śa is added by vyatyaya, an irregular exchange. The accent is that of the suffix. ⟦स्तवान⟧: from the root ṣṭuñ in the sense of praising; the suffix ānac is added in the passive sense even in the absence of a governing word, by the rule that the text writes as ⟦सम्यानच् स्तुव⟧ **NOTE**{The wording of this rule and of the root ⟦स्तुत्⟧ is unclear as supplied.}. Because it is a vocative, it loses its accent.

## Ṛk 4

O ⟦सत्पते⟧ Indra, lord of the good: these Soma juices, ⟦चंद्रासः⟧, gladdening, and ⟦इंदवः⟧, bright, ⟦सुता⟧, pressed and given by us, ⟦प्र यंति⟧, go forth vigorously, to ⟦क्षयं⟧, which is the belly, because food and the like dwell (kṣiyanti) in it. The sense is: hold them.

Grammar. ⟦क्षयं⟧: from the root ki, in the senses of dwelling and going (⟦क्षि निवासगत्योरित्यस्मादधिकरणे⟧), the suffix gha is added in the sense of the locus. Although the suffix accent would make the word end in the acute accent, it has the acute accent on its first syllable by the rule ⟦क्षयो निवास⟧. ⟦चंद्रासः⟧: the root is cadi, in the senses of gladdening and shining (⟦चदि आह्लादने दीप्ता च⟧). From it, by the rule beginning ⟦सा यितंचीत्यादिना⟧ the suffix is added, which the text writes as rakṣ **NOTE**{The suffix named after this rule is written as rakṣ in the text; I cannot tell what is meant.}. The augment asuk is added by the rule ⟦आज्ञसेरसुगित्यसुक्⟧. The accent is that of the suffix. ⟦इंदवः⟧: from the root unda the suffix u is added by the rule ⟦उंदेरिच्चा देरित्युप्रत्ययः⟧. Because the notion of nit continues from the previous rule, the first syllable has the acute accent.

## Ṛk 5

O Indra, hold, ⟦दधिष्क⟧, that is, keep in your belly, the ⟦वरेण्यं⟧, that is, the Soma to be chosen by all, the pressed Soma. These bright Soma juices are ⟦द्युक्षासः⟧, yours: they dwell (kṣiyanti, nivasanti) in heaven (div); this is why they are dyukṣāsaḥ. Therefore you should hold them: this is the sense.

Grammar. ⟦दधिष्क⟧: the form is the imperative of the root dhā. Because the augment iṭ is unaccented by the rule ⟦अनुदात्ता इतीटो⟧, the accent is that of the suffix. ⟦वरेण्यं⟧: from the root vṛj in the sense of sharing, the suffix eṇya is added by the rule beginning with vṛja. Because it belongs to the group beginning with vṛṣa, the first syllable has the acute accent. ⟦द्युक्षासः⟧: from the root kṣi, the suffix ḍa is added by the rule ending ⟦दृश्यत इति⟧. The accent is the one for the last member of a compound ending in a kṛt suffix.

## Ṛk 6

⟦गिर्वणो⟧ means one to be served with songs (gīrbhir vananīya). So Yāska says: ⟦गिर्वणा देवो भवति गीर्भिरेनं वनयंति⟧, that is, a god is girvaṇas because people serve him with songs (Ni. 6-14). O Indra, such as you are, drink this pressed Soma of ours (⟦पाहि⟧, that is, drink), since you are anointed, ⟦अज्यसे⟧, that is, sprinkled, ⟦धाराभिः⟧ with the streams of ⟦मधोः⟧, the intoxicating Soma. O Indra, ⟦त्वादातमित्⟧: the ⟦यशः⟧, that is, the food, which is among us comes to be purified and made clear by you alone.

Grammar. ⟦गिर्वणः⟧: from the root van in the sense of sharing, the suffix asun is added; the non-lengthening of the penultimate vowel of gir is Vedic. By the rule that the text writes as ⟦अमंत्रितस्यॆति षाष्टिकमाद्युदात्तत्वं⟧ **NOTE**{The first word of this rule is not clear as supplied.} the first syllable has the acute accent according to the sixtieth-chapter rule. ⟦पाहि⟧: the form is the imperative of the root pā in the sense of drinking. Because a vocative is treated as non-existent and because the word stands at the beginning of a pāda, there is no loss of accent (⟦पादादित्वादनिघातः⟧). The accent is that of the suffix. ⟦त्वादातं⟧: the root is ḍaip in the sense of purifying (⟦दैप् शोधने⟧). Even though it has the letter p, the property of not ending in ec is not produced by an indicatory letter (⟦सत्यपि पकारे नानुबंधकृतमनेजंतत्वमित्येजंत एवायं⟧), so this root ends in ec. Then the ā-substitution takes place by the rule ⟦अदेच इत्यात्वं⟧. From this root the suffix kta is added in the passive sense. Because in ⟦दाधा घ्नदाबित्यत्रादाबिति प्रतिषेधेन⟧ there is a prohibition, the designation ghu does not apply, and therefore the substitution of dad by the rule ⟦दो दद्योरिति⟧ does not take place. Then the instrumental of the word yuṣmad **NOTE**{The text here has a letter group that I cannot read as a word, ⟦तैति⟧, before the instrumental of yuṣmad.}; the compound is formed by the rule ⟦कर्तकरणे कृता बहुलमिति⟧; by the rule ⟦तृतीया कर्मणीति⟧ the accent is the natural accent of the first member. ⟦यशः⟧: from the root aś in the sense of pervading (⟦अशू व्याप्तौ⟧), the suffix asun is added by the rule ⟦अशेयुट्टेत्यसुन्⟧. In connection with it, the augment yuṭ is added to the root. Because the suffix is n-marked, the first syllable has the acute accent.

## Ṛk 7

⟦वनिनः⟧: one who has vana, that is, devotion directed to the deity, is vanī. Of that sacrificer who devotes himself to the gods, the shining (⟦द्युम्नानि⟧), undiminished (⟦अक्षिता⟧), that is, free from loss, full oblations, Soma and the rest, ⟦सचंते⟧, that is, gather round (⟦अभि⟧, abhitaḥ) Indra; they unite with him. Among them, ⟦पीत्वी⟧, that is, having drunk Soma, ⟦वावृधे⟧: that Indra grows.

Grammar. ⟦वनिनः⟧: the suffix ini is added by the rule ⟦अत इनिठनानितीनिप्रत्ययः⟧. Its accent follows from that. ⟦सचंते⟧: the form is the present (laṭ) of the root ṣac in the sense of uniting (⟦षच समवाय⟧). ⟦अक्षिता⟧: the niṣṭhā of the root ki in the sense of loss. Here, by the rule ⟦अत्र ण्यदर्थ इति⟧, there is no lengthening; and for the same reason, by the rule ⟦क्षियो दीर्घादिति⟧, there is no change of the niṣṭhā to na. In the compound with the negative particle, the accent is that of the compound. ⟦पीत्वी⟧: from the root pā in the sense of drinking, the suffix ktvā has the substitution tvī by the irregular formation given by ⟦स्नात्वादयश्चेति⟧. The accent is that of the suffix. The change to ī takes place by the rule beginning ⟦घुमास्थागापेत्यादिना⟧.

## Ṛk 8

O Indra, slayer of Vṛtra, ⟦आ गहि⟧, come to us, that is, towards us, from the near place (⟦अर्वावतः⟧), that is, from the side of this place, or from the very distant place (⟦परावतः⟧). And having come, ⟦जुषस्व⟧, that is, serve, accept, these ⟦गिरः⟧, that is, these words of praise of ours.

Grammar. ⟦परावतः⟧: the suffix vati is added by the rule ⟦उपसर्गाच्छंदसि धात्वर्थ इति⟧. The accent is that of the suffix.

## Ṛk 9

O Indra, in the place where you are ⟦हूयसे⟧, that is, properly worshipped, between ⟦परावतं⟧, the region that is very far away **NOTE**{The text reads this word as ⟦नरावतम⟧ in the bhāṣya, with the first letter different from the Ṛk; I have translated it as the far region.} and ⟦अर्वावतं⟧, the region that is near, that is, in the region in the middle (⟦अंतरा⟧) between them: from that place, come ⟦इह⟧, to this sacrifice. Come to drink the Soma **NOTE**{The text has a word ending ⟦वागच्छ⟧ here; I took it as "come" and cannot read the letters before it.}.

Grammar. ⟦परावतं अर्नावतं⟧ **NOTE**{The second word is written differently from the Ṛk's arvāvatam; I could not decide what is meant.}: ⟦अंतरांतरेण युक्ते⟧ (Pā. 2-3-4): so the accusative case. ⟦ततः⟧: the suffix tasil is added in place of the fifth case. The accent is the one that results from the l-marking.
