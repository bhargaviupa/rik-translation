Chapters 3, 4, 5 of the Third Aṣṭaka. (Sūktas 40–62 of the Third Maṇḍala and Sūktas 1–18 of the Fourth Maṇḍala.)

Third chapter of the Third Aṣṭaka.

All the sūktas of this chapter belong to the Third Maṇḍala. Since a detailed account of Viśvāmitra, the seer of the Third Maṇḍala, was written at the beginning of the previous, that is the sixteenth, part, it is unnecessary to explain it again here; nevertheless we shall say a word or two briefly.

This Viśvāmitra is one of the famous Seven Seers. In the Ṛgveda the Ṛks seen by this seer number 501 in all. About the seer Viśvāmitra, Yāska has told an ancient story in his Nirukta—

### Printed p. 2
विश्वामित्र ऋषिः सुदासः वैजवनस्य पुरोहितो बभूव । विश्वामित्रः सर्वमित्रः । सर्वं संस्कृतं । सुदाः कल्याणदानः । पैजवनः पिजवनस्य पुत्रः । पिजवनः पुनः स्पर्धनीयजवो वामिश्रीभावगतिर्वा । स वित्तं गृहीत्वा विपाट्टुतुद्रोभिः संभेदमाययौ । अनुययुरितरे । स विश्वामित्रो नदीस्तुष्टाव । गाधा भवत इति । अपि द्विवदपि बहुवत् तद्यद्विदुपरिष्कात्तद्व्याख्यास्यामः ॥ (*viśvāmitra ṛṣiḥ sudāsaḥ vaijavanasya purohito babhūva | viśvāmitraḥ sarvamitraḥ | sarvaṃ saṃskṛtaṃ | sudāḥ kalyāṇadānaḥ | paijavanaḥ pijavanasya putraḥ | pijavanaḥ punaḥ spardhanīyajavo vāmiśrībhāvagatirvā | sa vittaṃ gṛhītvā vipāṭṭutudrobhiḥ saṃbhedamāyayau | anuyayuritare | sa viśvāmitro nadīstuṣṭāva | gādhā bhavata iti | api dvivadapi bahuvat tadyadvidupariṣkāttadvyākhyāsyāmaḥ ‖*) (Ni. 7-24)

Viśvāmitra means that this seer is a friend to all, or that all are his friends; this is the literal sense of the word Viśvāmitra. This seer was the family priest of the king named Sudās, son of Pijavana. Once, taking the wealth given by that king, he set out to go to his hermitage and came to the confluence of the rivers Vipāṭ and Śutudri (the present Beas and Sutlej in the Punjab). Some thieves, wishing to seize the wealth he carried, were following him. Learning this, Viśvāmitra tried by some means to cross these rivers lying across his way, but they were very deep with the water of the flood and he could not. Then the seer praised these rivers, asking them to lessen their water and make it easy for him to pass; this is said in Ṛ. Saṃ. sūkta 3-33 and in Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-53-9 to 11. In Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-33-5 this seer Viśvāmitra declares himself the son of Kuśika.

The Rāmāyaṇa, the Bhārata and other Purāṇas say that there was enmity and jealousy between Viśvāmitra and the seer Vasiṣṭha. Support for this is found in some places in the Ṛgveda too; it will be set out on those occasions. In the story of Śunaśśepha the part of the seer Viśvāmitra is very important. This story, told at length in the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa, we have explained fully in the third part of the Ṛ. Saṃ. Besides, descriptions concerning Viśvāmitra occur in Vedic works such as the Aitareya Āraṇyaka, Aitareya Brāhmaṇa, Taittirīya Saṃhitā, Kāṭhaka Saṃhitā, Maitrāyaṇīya Saṃhitā, Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇa, Pañcaviṃśa Brāhmaṇa and Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa.

## Sūkta 40 (Maṇḍala 3)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> इंद्र त्वेति नवर्चं द्वितीयं सूक्तं वैश्वामित्रं गायत्रमैंद्रं । तथा
> चानुक्रांतं । इंद्र त्वा गायत्रं हीति ॥ अग्निष्टोमे प्रातःसवने ब्राह्मणाच्छंसिशस्त्र इदं सूक्तं । सूत्रितं च । इंद्र त्वा वृषभमुद्देवभीति तिस्रः । आ. ५-१० । इति ॥
> आद्या प्रातःसवने ब्राह्मणाच्छंसिनः प्रस्थितयाज्या । सूत्रितं च । इंद्र त्वा वृषभं
> मरुतो यस्य । आ. ५-५ । इति ।

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The second sūkta, beginning इंद्र त्वा (*iṃdra tvā*), nine Ṛks, is Vaiśvāmitra (seer Viśvāmitra), its metre Gāyatrī, its deity Indra. The Anukramaṇī agrees: इंद्र त्वा गायत्रं हीति (*iṃdra tvā gāyatraṃ hīti*). In the Agniṣṭoma, at the morning pressing, the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin recites this sūkta as his śastra. The sūtra says: इंद्र त्वा वृषभमुद्देवभीति तिस्रः (*iṃdra tvā vṛṣabhamuddevabhīti tisraḥ*) (Ā. Sū. 5-10). The first Ṛk is the prasthita-yājyā of the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin at the morning pressing; the sūtra says: इंद्र त्वा वृषभं मरुतो यस्य (*iṃdra tvā vṛṣabhaṃ maruto yasya*) (Ā. Sū. 5-5).**NOTE**

Anuvāda: The sūkta इंद्र त्वा (*iṃdra tvā*) is the second sūkta in the fourth anuvāka of the third maṇḍala. It has nine Ṛks. Its seer is Viśvāmitra, its deity Indra, its metre Gāyatrī. The Anukramaṇī begins इंद्र त्वा गायत्रं हि (*iṃdra tvā gāyatraṃ hi*). This sūkta is applied in the Agniṣṭoma sacrifice, in the morning-pressing rite of pressing Soma juice, to the recitation of the śastra-mantra by the priest called Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin, as explained by the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra इंद्र त्वा वृषभमुद्देदभीति तिस्रः (*iṃdra tvā vṛṣabhamuddedabhīti tisraḥ*) (Ā. 5-10); and the first Ṛk of this sūkta is applied to the prasthita-yājyā mantra to be recited by the priest Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin at the time of the morning pressing, as explained by the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra इंद्र त्वा वृषभं मरुतो यस्य (*iṃdra tvā vṛṣabhaṃ maruto yasya*) (Ā. 5-5).

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

"Indra" is so called because he delights and sports in the Soma juice flowing in the drop (indu); or else because he shows this whole world directly. The word is a vocative: O Indra, showerer of desires, we call you at the Soma pressed with the stones, to drink Soma; do you drink the intoxicating Soma, which is of the nature of food (andhas). Yāska explained the word Indra in many ways: he gives, splits, bears, or has the earth bear food (irā); or he runs to the drop; or he delights in the drop; or beings kindle him with their breaths, and that is Indra's Indra-hood. Whichever sense suits a place is to be accepted there. Upamanyu's school says इदंदर्शनादित्योपमन्यवः (*idaṃdarśanādityopamanyavaḥ*): he sees this Brahman directly. So it is heard in the Āraṇyaka: स एतमेव पुरुषं ब्रह्मततमपश्यदिदमदर्शमिती ३ । तस्मादिंद्रो नामेदंड्रो ह वै नाम तमिदंद्रं संतमिंद्र इत्याचक्षते परोक्षेण परोक्षप्रिया इव हि देवाः । (*sa etameva puruṣaṃ brahmatatamapaśyadidamadarśamitī 3 | tasmādiṃdro nāmedaṃḍro ha vai nāma tamidaṃdraṃ saṃtamiṃdra ityācakṣate parokṣeṇa parokṣapriyā iva hi devāḥ |*) (Ai. Ā. 2-4-3); that is, he saw this Person, the all-pervading Brahman, and said "I have seen this"; therefore the name is Indra, and the gods love the hidden. The root इदि (*idi*) has the sense of supreme lordship; supreme lordship is taking the form of the world by his own māyā. With this idea it is heard: इंद्रो मायाभिः पुरुरूप ईयते (*iṃdro māyābhiḥ pururūpa īyate*). Thus these etymologies are to be understood. The remainder is grammar.

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, since we have pressed the Soma juice and made it ready, we pray and call you, the showerer of desires. Do you, of great glory, drink this Soma juice, sweet and called food.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

Indra—The commentator explains the etymology of the word Indra thus: इंदौ स्रवति सोमरसे रमते क्रीडतीतींद्रः (*iṃdau sravati somarase ramate krīḍatītīṃdraḥ*). When the Soma plants are crushed and the juice pressed out, Indra, seeing the drops of Soma juice oozing and dripping, dances with joy; hence the name Indra. Or: यद्वा इदं सर्वं जगत्साक्षाद्दर्शयतीतींद्रः (*yadvā idaṃ sarvaṃ jagatsākṣāddarśayatītīṃdraḥ*). Because he shows this whole world directly, the name Indra is fitting for him.

Besides, Yāska has given the etymology of the word Indra in many ways—
इंद्र इरां दृणातीति वेरां ददातीति वेरां दधातीति वेरां दारयतीति वेरां धारयतीति नेंदने द्रवतीति वेंदौ रमत इति वेंधे भूतानीति वा तद्यदेनं प्राणैः सम्यंधंस्तदिंद्रस्येंद्रत्वमिति विज्ञायत इदंकरणादित्याग्रायण इदंदर्शनादित्योपमन्यव इंदतेर्वैश्वर्यकर्मण इञ्छत्रोणां दारयिता वा द्रावयिता वादरयिता च यज्वनां । (*iṃdra irāṃ dṛṇātīti verāṃ dadātīti verāṃ dadhātīti verāṃ dārayatīti verāṃ dhārayatīti neṃdane dravatīti veṃdau ramata iti veṃdhe bhūtānīti vā tadyadenaṃ prāṇaiḥ samyaṃdhaṃstadiṃdrasyeṃdratvamiti vijñāyata idaṃkaraṇādityāgrāyaṇa idaṃdarśanādityopamanyava iṃdatervaiśvaryakarmaṇa iñchatroṇāṃ dārayitā vā drāvayitā vādarayitā ca yajvanāṃ |*) (Ni. 10-8). He himself has given the sense of this.

(1) दृ विदारण इति धातुः । इरामन्नमुद्धिश्य तन्निष्टादकजलसिध्यर्थं दृणाति मेघं विदीर्णं करोतीतींद्रः (*dṛ vidāraṇa iti dhātuḥ | irāmannamuddhiśya tanniṣṭādakajalasidhyarthaṃ dṛṇāti meghaṃ vidīrṇaṃ karotītīṃdraḥ*)—The root dṛ means to split, to cut. Irā means food. Food comes to us from herbs and trees, that is, from the plant world. For that plant world to grow, water is necessary. For that water, that is, for rain to fall, he cuts the cloud (with lightning, the thunderbolt); therefore the name Indra.

(2) डुदाञ् दान इति धातुः । इरामन्नं वृष्टनिष्पादनेन ददातीतींद्रः ॥ (*ḍudāñ dāna iti dhātuḥ | irāmannaṃ vṛṣṭaniṣpādanena dadātītīṃdraḥ ‖*)—The root dā means to give. He gives irā, food, by means of vṛṣṭi (rain); therefore the name Indra.

### Printed p. 6
(3) धाञ् पोषणार्थः । इरामन्नं तृप्ति कारणं सस्यं दधाति जलप्रदानेन पुष्पातीतींद्रः ॥ (*dhāñ poṣaṇārthaḥ | irāmannaṃ tṛpti kāraṇaṃ sasyaṃ dadhāti jalapradānena puṣpātītīṃdraḥ ‖*)—The root dhā means to nourish. He nourishes with the gift of water (rain) the plants and the like that give food; therefore the name Indra.

(4) इरामुत्पादयितुं कर्षकमुखेन भूमिं विदारयतीतींद्रः । (*irāmutpādayituṃ karṣakamukhena bhūmiṃ vidārayatītīṃdraḥ |*)—To produce food the earth must be ploughed. When the earth is ploughed, he makes it split with the plough and so makes it favourable to the crop; therefore the name Indra.

(5) पूर्वोक्त पोषणमुखेनेरां धारयति विनाशराहित्येन स्थापयतीतींद्रः । (*pūrvokta poṣaṇamukhenerāṃ dhārayati vināśarāhityena sthāpayatītīṃdraḥ |*)—As said before, he nourishes the plants and the like and protects them so that they do not perish; therefore the name Indra.

(6) इंदुः सोमो वल्लीरसः । तदर्थं यागभूमौ द्रवति धावतीतींद्रः (*iṃduḥ somo vallīrasaḥ | tadarthaṃ yāgabhūmau dravati dhāvatītīṃdraḥ*)—Indu means the Soma juice prepared from the Soma plant. He runs (hastens) to the place of sacrifice for the drinking of such Soma; therefore the name Indra.

(7) इंदौ यथोक्ते सोमे रमते क्रीडतीतींद्रः । (*iṃdau yathokte some ramate krīḍatītīṃdraḥ |*)—Satisfied with the Soma juice mentioned before, he moves about at ease; therefore the name Indra.

(8) इांइंधी दीप्ताविति धातुः । भूतानि प्राणिदेहानिंदे जीवचैतन्यरूपेणांतःप्रविश्य दीपयतीतींद्रः ॥ (*iāṃiṃdhī dīptāviti dhātuḥ | bhūtāni prāṇidehāniṃde jīvacaitanyarūpeṇāṃtaḥpraviśya dīpayatītīṃdraḥ ‖*)**NOTE** The root indh means to shine, to illumine. Entering the bodies of living beings in the form of the life-force, and shining while impelling them to do their respective acts; therefore the name Indra.

(9) यद्यस्मात्कारणादेनं परमात्मरूपमिंद्रं देवं प्राणैर्वाक्चक्षुरादींद्रियैः प्राणापानादिवायुभिश्च सहितं सम्मैंधन्नु पासका ध्यानेन सम्यक्रकाशितवंतस्तत्तस्मात्कारणादिंद्रनाम संपन्नं । (*yadyasmātkāraṇādenaṃ paramātmarūpamiṃdraṃ devaṃ prāṇairvākcakṣurādīṃdriyaiḥ prāṇāpānādivāyubhiśca sahitaṃ sammaiṃdhannu pāsakā dhyānena samyakrakāśitavaṃtastattasmātkāraṇādiṃdranāma saṃpannaṃ |*)**NOTE** Because worshippers, joining this god of the form of the Supreme Self with the sense organs such as speech and sight and with the vital airs such as prāṇa and apāna, conceive him in meditation as shining brightly; hence the name Indra.

(10) आग्रायणनामको मुनिरिदंकरणादिंद्र इति निर्वचनं मन्यते । इंद्रो हि परमात्मरूपेणेदं जगत्करोति (*āgrāyaṇanāmako muniridaṃkaraṇādiṃdra iti nirvacanaṃ manyate | iṃdro hi paramātmarūpeṇedaṃ jagatkaroti*)—Idaṅkaraṇa means that Indra, in the form of the Supreme Self, makes (creates) this world; hence the name Indra came to this god, says the sage named Āgrāyaṇa.

(11) औपम्यनवनामको मुनिरिदंदर्शनादिंद्र इति निर्वचनवाह । इदमित्यापरोक्ष्यमुच्यते । (*aupamyanavanāmako muniridaṃdarśanādiṃdra iti nirvacanavāha | idamityāparokṣyamucyate |*)**NOTE**—The sage Aupamanyava says that because he sees this, that is, everything, the name Indra arose. Here "this" means the imperceptible, what does not appear visibly to the eye. For that very reason the Śruti says परोक्षप्रिया इव हि देवाः (*parokṣapriyā iva hi devāḥ*).

(12) इवि परमैश्वर्य इति धातुः । स्वमायॆया जगद्रूपत्वं परमैश्वर्यं । तद्योगादिंद्रः । (*ivi paramaiśvarya iti dhātuḥ | svamāyèyā jagadrūpatvaṃ paramaiśvaryaṃ | tadyogādiṃdraḥ |*)—The root idi has the sense of supreme lordship. This god has the supreme lordship of taking the form of the world by his own māyā; hence the name Indra. This idea is supported by the Vedic sentence इंद्रो मायाभिः पुरुरूप ईयते (*iṃdro māyābhiḥ pururūpa īyate*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-47-18).

(13) इनशब्दस्येश्वरवाचकस्याकारलोपे सति नकारांतमिन्निति पदं भवति । द्य भय इति धातुः । स च परमेश्वरः शत्रूणां दारयिता भीषयितेतींद्रः ॥ (*inaśabdasyeśvaravācakasyākāralope sati nakārāṃtaminniti padaṃ bhavati | dya bhaya iti dhātuḥ | sa ca parameśvaraḥ śatrūṇāṃ dārayitā bhīṣayitetīṃdraḥ ‖*)—The word ina denotes the Lord. If the vowel a over the n of this word ina is dropped, the word in remains. The root dṛ**NOTE** means fear. That god, the Supreme Lord, causes fear to enemies; hence the name Indra.

(14) द्रु गताविति धातुः । शत्रूणां द्रावयिता पलायनं प्रापयितेतींद्रः ॥ (*dru gatāviti dhātuḥ | śatrūṇāṃ drāvayitā palāyanaṃ prāpayitetīṃdraḥ ‖*)—The root dru has the sense of going, that is, go, run away. This god makes enemies run away; hence the name Indra.

(15) यज्ञनां यागानुष्ठायिनामादरयिता भयस्य परिहर्ता (*yajñanāṃ yāgānuṣṭhāyināmādarayitā bhayasya parihartā*)—He honours those who worship him with sacrifices and the like, and removes their fear; hence the name Indra for this god. In the same way many etymologies can be devised for the word Indra.
