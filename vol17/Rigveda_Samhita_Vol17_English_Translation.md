Chapters 3, 4, 5 of the Third Aṣṭaka. (Sūktas 40–62 of the Third Maṇḍala and Sūktas 1–18 of the Fourth Maṇḍala.)

Third chapter of the Third Aṣṭaka.

All the sūktas of this chapter belong to the Third Maṇḍala. Since a detailed account of Viśvāmitra, the seer of the Third Maṇḍala, was written at the beginning of the previous, that is the sixteenth, part, it is unnecessary to explain it again here; nevertheless we shall say a word or two briefly.

This Viśvāmitra is one of the famous Seven Seers. In the Ṛgveda the Ṛks seen by this seer number 501 in all. About the seer Viśvāmitra, Yāska has told an ancient story in his Nirukta—

### Printed p. 2
विश्वामित्र ऋषिः सुदासः वैजवनस्य पुरोहितो बभूव । विश्वामित्रः सर्वमित्रः । सर्वं संस्कृतं । सुदाः कल्याणदानः । पैजवनः पिजवनस्य पुत्रः । पिजवनः पुनः स्पर्धनीयजवो वामिश्रीभावगतिर्वा । स वित्तं गृहीत्वा विपाट्टुतुद्रोभिः संभेदमाययौ । अनुययुरितरे । स विश्वामित्रो नदीस्तुष्टाव । गाधा भवत इति । अपि द्विवदपि बहुवत् तद्यद्विदुपरिष्कात्तद्व्याख्यास्यामः ॥ (*viśvāmitra ṛṣiḥ sudāsaḥ vaijavanasya purohito babhūva | viśvāmitraḥ sarvamitraḥ | sarvaṃ saṃskṛtaṃ | sudāḥ kalyāṇadānaḥ | paijavanaḥ pijavanasya putraḥ | pijavanaḥ punaḥ spardhanīyajavo vāmiśrībhāvagatirvā | sa vittaṃ gṛhītvā vipāṭṭutudrobhiḥ saṃbhedamāyayau | anuyayuritare | sa viśvāmitro nadīstuṣṭāva | gādhā bhavata iti | api dvivadapi bahuvat tadyadvidupariṣkāttadvyākhyāsyāmaḥ ‖*) (Ni. 7-24)

Viśvāmitra means that this seer is a friend to all, or that all are his friends; this is the literal sense of the word Viśvāmitra. This seer was the family priest of the king named Sudās, son of Pijavana. Once, taking the wealth given by that king, he set out to go to his hermitage and came to the confluence of the rivers Vipāṭ and Śutudri (the present Beas and Sutlej in the Punjab). Some thieves, wishing to seize the wealth he carried, were following him. Learning this, Viśvāmitra tried by some means to cross these rivers lying across his way, but they were very deep with the water of the flood and he could not. Then the seer praised these rivers, asking them to lessen their water and make it easy for him to pass; this is said in Ṛ. Saṃ. sūkta 3-33 and in Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-53-9 to 11. In Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-33-5 this seer Viśvāmitra declares himself the son of Kuśika.

The Rāmāyaṇa, the Bhārata and other Purāṇas say that there was enmity and jealousy between Viśvāmitra and the seer Vasiṣṭha. Support for this is found in some places in the Ṛgveda too; it will be set out on those occasions. In the story of Śunaśśepha the part of the seer Viśvāmitra is very important. This story, told at length in the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa, we have explained fully in the third part of the Ṛ. Saṃ. Besides, descriptions concerning Viśvāmitra occur in Vedic works such as the Aitareya Āraṇyaka, Aitareya Brāhmaṇa, Taittirīya Saṃhitā, Kāṭhaka Saṃhitā, Maitrāyaṇīya Saṃhitā, Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇa, Pañcaviṃśa Brāhmaṇa and Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa.

## Sūkta 40 (Maṇḍala 3)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> इंद्र त्वेति नवर्चं द्वितीयं सूक्तं वैश्वामित्रं गायत्रमैंद्रं । तथा
> चानुक्रांतं । इंद्र त्वा गायत्रं हीति ॥ अग्निष्टोमे प्रातःसवने ब्राह्मणाच्छंसिशस्त्र इदं सूक्तं । सूत्रितं च । इंद्र त्वा वृषभमुद्देवभीति तिस्रः । आ. ५-१० । इति ॥
> आद्या प्रातःसवने ब्राह्मणाच्छंसिनः प्रस्थितयाज्या । सूत्रितं च । इंद्र त्वा वृषभं
> मरुतो यस्य । आ. ५-५ । इति ।

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The second sūkta, beginning इंद्र त्वा (*iṃdra tvā*), nine Ṛks, is Vaiśvāmitra (seer Viśvāmitra), its metre Gāyatrī, its deity Indra. The Anukramaṇī agrees: इंद्र त्वा गायत्रं हीति (*iṃdra tvā gāyatraṃ hīti*). In the Agniṣṭoma, at the morning pressing, the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin recites this sūkta as his śastra. The sūtra says: इंद्र त्वा वृषभमुद्देवभीति तिस्रः (*iṃdra tvā vṛṣabhamuddevabhīti tisraḥ*) (Ā. Sū. 5-10). The first Ṛk is the prasthita-yājyā of the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin at the morning pressing; the sūtra says: इंद्र त्वा वृषभं मरुतो यस्य (*iṃdra tvā vṛṣabhaṃ maruto yasya*) (Ā. Sū. 5-5).**NOTE**

Anuvāda: The sūkta इंद्र त्वा (*iṃdra tvā*) is the second sūkta in the fourth anuvāka of the third maṇḍala. It has nine Ṛks. Its seer is Viśvāmitra, its deity Indra, its metre Gāyatrī. The Anukramaṇī begins इंद्र त्वा गायत्रं हि (*iṃdra tvā gāyatraṃ hi*). This sūkta is applied in the Agniṣṭoma sacrifice, in the morning-pressing rite of pressing Soma juice, to the recitation of the śastra-mantra by the priest called Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin, as explained by the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra इंद्र त्वा वृषभमुद्देदभीति तिस्रः (*iṃdra tvā vṛṣabhamuddedabhīti tisraḥ*) (Ā. 5-10); and the first Ṛk of this sūkta is applied to the prasthita-yājyā mantra to be recited by the priest Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin at the time of the morning pressing, as explained by the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra इंद्र त्वा वृषभं मरुतो यस्य (*iṃdra tvā vṛṣabhaṃ maruto yasya*) (Ā. 5-5).

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

"Indra" is so called because he delights and sports in the Soma juice flowing in the drop (indu); or else because he shows this whole world directly. The word is a vocative: O Indra, showerer of desires, we call you at the Soma pressed with the stones, to drink Soma; do you drink the intoxicating Soma, which is of the nature of food (andhas). Yāska explained the word Indra in many ways: he gives, splits, bears, or has the earth bear food (irā); or he runs to the drop; or he delights in the drop; or beings kindle him with their breaths, and that is Indra's Indra-hood. Whichever sense suits a place is to be accepted there. Upamanyu's school says इदंदर्शनादित्योपमन्यवः (*idaṃdarśanādityopamanyavaḥ*): he sees this Brahman directly. So it is heard in the Āraṇyaka: स एतमेव पुरुषं ब्रह्मततमपश्यदिदमदर्शमिती ३ । तस्मादिंद्रो नामेदंड्रो ह वै नाम तमिदंद्रं संतमिंद्र इत्याचक्षते परोक्षेण परोक्षप्रिया इव हि देवाः । (*sa etameva puruṣaṃ brahmatatamapaśyadidamadarśamitī 3 | tasmādiṃdro nāmedaṃḍro ha vai nāma tamidaṃdraṃ saṃtamiṃdra ityācakṣate parokṣeṇa parokṣapriyā iva hi devāḥ |*) (Ai. Ā. 2-4-3); that is, he saw this Person, the all-pervading Brahman, and said "I have seen this"; therefore the name is Indra, and the gods love the hidden. The root इदि (*idi*) has the sense of supreme lordship; supreme lordship is taking the form of the world by his own māyā. With this idea it is heard: इंद्रो मायाभिः पुरुरूप ईयते (*iṃdro māyābhiḥ pururūpa īyate*). Thus these etymologies are to be understood. The remainder is grammar.

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, since we have pressed the Soma juice and made it ready, we pray and call you, the showerer of desires. Do you, of great glory, drink this Soma juice, sweet and called food.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

Indra—The commentator explains the etymology of the word Indra thus: इंदौ स्रवति सोमरसे रमते क्रीडतीतींद्रः (*iṃdau sravati somarase ramate krīḍatītīṃdraḥ*). When the Soma plants are crushed and the juice pressed out, Indra, seeing the drops of Soma juice oozing and dripping, dances with joy; hence the name Indra. Or: यद्वा इदं सर्वं जगत्साक्षाद्दर्शयतीतींद्रः (*yadvā idaṃ sarvaṃ jagatsākṣāddarśayatītīṃdraḥ*). Because he shows this whole world directly, the name Indra is fitting for him.

Besides, Yāska has given the etymology of the word Indra in many ways—
इंद्र इरां दृणातीति वेरां ददातीति वेरां दधातीति वेरां दारयतीति वेरां धारयतीति नेंदने द्रवतीति वेंदौ रमत इति वेंधे भूतानीति वा तद्यदेनं प्राणैः सम्यंधंस्तदिंद्रस्येंद्रत्वमिति विज्ञायत इदंकरणादित्याग्रायण इदंदर्शनादित्योपमन्यव इंदतेर्वैश्वर्यकर्मण इञ्छत्रोणां दारयिता वा द्रावयिता वादरयिता च यज्वनां । (*iṃdra irāṃ dṛṇātīti verāṃ dadātīti verāṃ dadhātīti verāṃ dārayatīti verāṃ dhārayatīti neṃdane dravatīti veṃdau ramata iti veṃdhe bhūtānīti vā tadyadenaṃ prāṇaiḥ samyaṃdhaṃstadiṃdrasyeṃdratvamiti vijñāyata idaṃkaraṇādityāgrāyaṇa idaṃdarśanādityopamanyava iṃdatervaiśvaryakarmaṇa iñchatroṇāṃ dārayitā vā drāvayitā vādarayitā ca yajvanāṃ |*) (Ni. 10-8). He himself has given the sense of this.

(1) दृ विदारण इति धातुः । इरामन्नमुद्धिश्य तन्निष्टादकजलसिध्यर्थं दृणाति मेघं विदीर्णं करोतीतींद्रः (*dṛ vidāraṇa iti dhātuḥ | irāmannamuddhiśya tanniṣṭādakajalasidhyarthaṃ dṛṇāti meghaṃ vidīrṇaṃ karotītīṃdraḥ*)—The root dṛ means to split, to cut. Irā means food. Food comes to us from herbs and trees, that is, from the plant world. For that plant world to grow, water is necessary. For that water, that is, for rain to fall, he cuts the cloud (with lightning, the thunderbolt); therefore the name Indra.

(2) डुदाञ् दान इति धातुः । इरामन्नं वृष्टनिष्पादनेन ददातीतींद्रः ॥ (*ḍudāñ dāna iti dhātuḥ | irāmannaṃ vṛṣṭaniṣpādanena dadātītīṃdraḥ ‖*)—The root dā means to give. He gives irā, food, by means of vṛṣṭi (rain); therefore the name Indra.

### Printed p. 6
(3) धाञ् पोषणार्थः । इरामन्नं तृप्ति कारणं सस्यं दधाति जलप्रदानेन पुष्पातीतींद्रः ॥ (*dhāñ poṣaṇārthaḥ | irāmannaṃ tṛpti kāraṇaṃ sasyaṃ dadhāti jalapradānena puṣpātītīṃdraḥ ‖*)—The root dhā means to nourish. He nourishes with the gift of water (rain) the plants and the like that give food; therefore the name Indra.

(4) इरामुत्पादयितुं कर्षकमुखेन भूमिं विदारयतीतींद्रः । (*irāmutpādayituṃ karṣakamukhena bhūmiṃ vidārayatītīṃdraḥ |*)—To produce food the earth must be ploughed. When the earth is ploughed, he makes it split with the plough and so makes it favourable to the crop; therefore the name Indra.

(5) पूर्वोक्त पोषणमुखेनेरां धारयति विनाशराहित्येन स्थापयतीतींद्रः । (*pūrvokta poṣaṇamukhenerāṃ dhārayati vināśarāhityena sthāpayatītīṃdraḥ |*)—As said before, he nourishes the plants and the like and protects them so that they do not perish; therefore the name Indra.

(6) इंदुः सोमो वल्लीरसः । तदर्थं यागभूमौ द्रवति धावतीतींद्रः (*iṃduḥ somo vallīrasaḥ | tadarthaṃ yāgabhūmau dravati dhāvatītīṃdraḥ*)—Indu means the Soma juice prepared from the Soma plant. He runs (hastens) to the place of sacrifice for the drinking of such Soma; therefore the name Indra.

(7) इंदौ यथोक्ते सोमे रमते क्रीडतीतींद्रः । (*iṃdau yathokte some ramate krīḍatītīṃdraḥ |*)—Satisfied with the Soma juice mentioned before, he moves about at ease; therefore the name Indra.

(8) इांइंधी दीप्ताविति धातुः । भूतानि प्राणिदेहानिंदे जीवचैतन्यरूपेणांतःप्रविश्य दीपयतीतींद्रः ॥ (*iāṃiṃdhī dīptāviti dhātuḥ | bhūtāni prāṇidehāniṃde jīvacaitanyarūpeṇāṃtaḥpraviśya dīpayatītīṃdraḥ ‖*)**NOTE** The root indh means to shine, to illumine. Entering the bodies of living beings in the form of the life-force, and shining while impelling them to do their respective acts; therefore the name Indra.

(9) यद्यस्मात्कारणादेनं परमात्मरूपमिंद्रं देवं प्राणैर्वाक्चक्षुरादींद्रियैः प्राणापानादिवायुभिश्च सहितं सम्मैंधन्नु पासका ध्यानेन सम्यक्रकाशितवंतस्तत्तस्मात्कारणादिंद्रनाम संपन्नं । (*yadyasmātkāraṇādenaṃ paramātmarūpamiṃdraṃ devaṃ prāṇairvākcakṣurādīṃdriyaiḥ prāṇāpānādivāyubhiśca sahitaṃ sammaiṃdhannu pāsakā dhyānena samyakrakāśitavaṃtastattasmātkāraṇādiṃdranāma saṃpannaṃ |*)**NOTE** Because worshippers, joining this god of the form of the Supreme Self with the sense organs such as speech and sight and with the vital airs such as prāṇa and apāna, conceive him in meditation as shining brightly; hence the name Indra.

### Printed p. 7
(10) आग्रायणनामको मुनिरिदंकरणादिंद्र इति निर्वचनं मन्यते । इंद्रो हि परमात्मरूपेणेदं जगत्करोति (*āgrāyaṇanāmako muniridaṃkaraṇādiṃdra iti nirvacanaṃ manyate | iṃdro hi paramātmarūpeṇedaṃ jagatkaroti*)—Idaṅkaraṇa means that Indra, in the form of the Supreme Self, makes (creates) this world; hence the name Indra came to this god, says the sage named Āgrāyaṇa.

(11) औपम्यनवनामको मुनिरिदंदर्शनादिंद्र इति निर्वचनवाह । इदमित्यापरोक्ष्यमुच्यते । (*aupamyanavanāmako muniridaṃdarśanādiṃdra iti nirvacanavāha | idamityāparokṣyamucyate |*)**NOTE**—The sage Aupamanyava says that because he sees this, that is, everything, the name Indra arose. Here "this" means the imperceptible, what does not appear visibly to the eye. For that very reason the Śruti says परोक्षप्रिया इव हि देवाः (*parokṣapriyā iva hi devāḥ*).

(12) इवि परमैश्वर्य इति धातुः । स्वमायॆया जगद्रूपत्वं परमैश्वर्यं । तद्योगादिंद्रः । (*ivi paramaiśvarya iti dhātuḥ | svamāyèyā jagadrūpatvaṃ paramaiśvaryaṃ | tadyogādiṃdraḥ |*)—The root idi has the sense of supreme lordship. This god has the supreme lordship of taking the form of the world by his own māyā; hence the name Indra. This idea is supported by the Vedic sentence इंद्रो मायाभिः पुरुरूप ईयते (*iṃdro māyābhiḥ pururūpa īyate*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-47-18).

(13) इनशब्दस्येश्वरवाचकस्याकारलोपे सति नकारांतमिन्निति पदं भवति । द्य भय इति धातुः । स च परमेश्वरः शत्रूणां दारयिता भीषयितेतींद्रः ॥ (*inaśabdasyeśvaravācakasyākāralope sati nakārāṃtaminniti padaṃ bhavati | dya bhaya iti dhātuḥ | sa ca parameśvaraḥ śatrūṇāṃ dārayitā bhīṣayitetīṃdraḥ ‖*)—The word ina denotes the Lord. If the vowel a over the n of this word ina is dropped, the word in remains. The root dṛ**NOTE** means fear. That god, the Supreme Lord, causes fear to enemies; hence the name Indra.

(14) द्रु गताविति धातुः । शत्रूणां द्रावयिता पलायनं प्रापयितेतींद्रः ॥ (*dru gatāviti dhātuḥ | śatrūṇāṃ drāvayitā palāyanaṃ prāpayitetīṃdraḥ ‖*)—The root dru has the sense of going, that is, go, run away. This god makes enemies run away; hence the name Indra.

(15) यज्ञनां यागानुष्ठायिनामादरयिता भयस्य परिहर्ता (*yajñanāṃ yāgānuṣṭhāyināmādarayitā bhayasya parihartā*)—He honours those who worship him with sacrifices and the like, and removes their fear; hence the name Indra for this god. In the same way many etymologies can be devised for the word Indra.

Besides these meanings, the commentator says that in each context whichever meaning suits Indra should be accepted there. For example: इदं दर्शनादित्यौवमन्यवः । इदं ब्रह्म साक्षात्पश्यतीतींद्रः । (*idaṃ darśanādityauvamanyavaḥ | idaṃ brahma sākṣātpaśyatītīṃdraḥ |*) And in the Āraṇyaka it is heard: स एतमेव पुरुषं ब्रह्मततममपश्यदिदमदर्शमिती ३ । तस्मादिंद्रो नामेदंद्रो ह वै नाम तमिदंद्रं संतमिंद्र इत्याचक्षते परोक्षेण परोक्षप्रिया इव हि देवाः (*sa etameva puruṣaṃ brahmatatamamapaśyadidamadarśamitī 3 | tasmādiṃdro nāmedaṃdro ha vai nāma tamidaṃdraṃ saṃtamiṃdra ityācakṣate parokṣeṇa parokṣapriyā iva hi devāḥ*) (Ai. Ā. 2-4-3).

Here the word इदं (*idaṃ*) denotes Brahman. Since Indra sees that Brahman directly, that is, is able to see it, he is named Indra. After explaining the meaning in this way, the commentator quoted the Aitareya Āraṇyaka passage above, from स एतमेव (*sa etameva*) to इव हि देवाः (*iva hi devāḥ*), in support of it.

वृषभं (*vṛṣabhaṃ*)—This word is formed from the root वृषु वर्षणे (*vṛṣu varṣaṇe*). It means one who pours down the desired objects of those who serve him, as rain pours down. The word is generally used of Indra.

सोमे सुते (*some sute*)—ग्रावभिरभिषुते सोमे (*grāvabhirabhiṣute some*): in the Soma pressed with the stones. The meaning is: after the Soma plants have been crushed with stones, the juice squeezed out, and the juice strained and made ready, fit for drinking. I have explained the method of preparing the Soma juice at length many times before; see Ṛ. Saṃ. Part 10, pages 631, 637 and 697. For Indra's drinking of Soma, see Part 14, page 606.

मध्वः अंधसः (*madhvaḥ aṃdhasaḥ*)—मदकरमंधसोऽन्नलक्षणं सोमं (*madakaramaṃdhaso 'nnalakṣaṇaṃ somaṃ*). The word मध्वः (*madhvaḥ*) is an adjective of the word अंधसः (*aṃdhasaḥ*). It may be given the meanings sweet, tasty, delightful, causing intoxication or joy. The word अंध (*aṃdha*) is read among the names of food (Ni. 3-9). Besides, Yāska says of the word अंधस् (*aṃdhas*): अंध इत्यन्ननामाध्यानीयं भवति । तमोऽप्यंध उच्यते नास्मिन् ध्यानं भवति । न दर्शनमंधंतम इत्यभिभाषंते । अयमपीतरोऽन्न एतस्मादेव । पश्यदक्षण्वान्न विचेतदंध इत्यपि निगमो भवति ॥ (*aṃdha ityannanāmādhyānīyaṃ bhavati | tamo 'pyaṃdha ucyate nāsmin dhyānaṃ bhavati | na darśanamaṃdhaṃtama ityabhibhāṣaṃte | ayamapītaro 'nna etasmādeva | paśyadakṣaṇvānna vicetadaṃdha ityapi nigamo bhavati ‖*) (Ni. 5-1).

That is, the word अंधः (*aṃdhaḥ*) is a name for food. Why? Because food is needed by everyone, it is prayed for (desired) by everyone; therefore अंधः (*aṃdhaḥ*) means food. Also in connection with Soma

juice, the use of the word अंधः (*aṃdhaḥ*) in the sense of the Soma juice, which is of the nature of food, is especially frequent in the Ṛgveda. अंधः (*aṃdhaḥ*) is also a name for darkness (tamas). For in darkness nothing is seen, so nothing can be done. Even in worldly usage, people say अंधंतम (*aṃdhaṃtama*) when darkness has fallen so that nothing can be seen by the eye. He says that there is also an example of this in the Vedic text पश्यदक्षण्वान्न उचेतदंधः (*paśyadakṣaṇvānna ucetadaṃdhaḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-164-16).

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

For the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin priest at the morning pressing, this verse इंद्र क्रतुविदं (*iṃdra kratuvidaṃ*) is the śastra-yājyā; and it is prescribed in the sūtra इंद्र क्रतुविदं सुतमिति याज्या (*iṃdra kratuvidaṃ sutamiti yājyā*) (Ā. 5-10). "O Indra, desire to drink this pressed Soma, the obtainer of kratu, that is, of wisdom (prajñā). O Indra, praised by many, drink this Soma, which satisfies and refreshes. Having drunk it, pour it into the belly; the sense is: hold it so that it does not fall below the navel."

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

At the morning pressing the priest called Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin is to use this verse इंद्र क्रतुविदं (*iṃdra kratuvidaṃ*) as a śastra-yājyā mantra; this is stated by the sūtra of the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra इंद्र क्रतुविदं सुतमिति याज्या (*iṃdra kratuvidaṃ sutamiti yājyā*).

क्रतुविदं (*kratuvidaṃ*)—For the word क्रतु (*kratu*) Yāska gave the explanation क्रतुं कर्म वा प्रज्ञां ना (*kratuṃ karma vā prajñāṃ nā*) (Ni. 2-28). Moreover the word क्रतु (*kratu*) is read among the twenty-six names of action beginning with अपः (*apaḥ*) and अष्टः (*aṣṭaḥ*) (Ni. 3-1), and among the eleven names of wisdom beginning with केतः (*ketaḥ*) and केतुः (*ketuḥ*) (Ni. 3-13). Therefore क्रतुविदं (*kratuvidaṃ*) means प्रज्ञया लंभकं (*prajñayā laṃbhakaṃ*): that which can be obtained by wisdom, by knowledge. The meaning is that proper understanding or knowledge is needed to prepare the Soma juice. Or, if the word क्रतु (*kratu*) is taken to mean action, it may mean the Soma juice prepared in the sacrificial action. The sense is: such a Soma juice.

सुतं सोमं (*sutaṃ somaṃ*)—The use of the word सुतं (*sutaṃ*) here means the Soma juice that has just now, in this sacrifice, been freshly prepared: the Soma plants crushed, the juice squeezed out, strained and made ready, fit for drinking.

पुरुष्टुत (*puruṣṭuta*)—पुरुभिर्बहुभिः स्तुतेंद्र (*purubhirbahubhiḥ stuteṃdra*): Indra, who is praised by many people. Many people, priests, sacrificers and others, praise Indra.

पिब (*piba*)—The final syllable of this word is made long in the Saṃhitā text for the convenience of the metre. Such usage is frequent in the Ṛgveda. For example, the word पिब (*piba*) in पिबा सोममभि (*pibā somamabhi*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-17-1); पिबा सोममिंद्र मंदतु (*pibā somamiṃdra maṃdatu*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-22-1); पिबा सोमं मदाय (*pibā somaṃ madāya*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-95-3); पिबा सोमं महते (*pibā somaṃ mahate*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-116-1); the verse **NOTE**

### Printed p. 11

The word श्रुधि (*śrudhi*) too is used with a long final syllable in verses such as श्रुधी नो अग्ने सदने (*śrudhī no agne sadane*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-11-9), श्रुधी न इंद्र (*śrudhī na iṃdra*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-26-1), श्रुधी हवमिंद्र शूर (*śrudhī havamiṃdra śūra*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-148-5), श्रुधी हवं तिरश्चाशि (*śrudhī havaṃ tiraścāśi*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-95-4), and so on. There are many such examples.

तातृपिं (*tātṛpiṃ*)—तर्पकमाप्यायनकारिणमिमं सोमं (*tarpakamāpyāyanakāriṇamimaṃ somaṃ*): the Soma that causes satisfaction; that is, the Soma juice which, after being drunk, settles in the belly, cools it and makes joy arise. There are many descriptions that drinking Soma juice produces satisfaction or joy and also intoxication.

वृषस्व (*vṛṣasva*)—जठरे सिंच । यथा नाभेरधो न पतति तथा धारयेति भावः । (*jaṭhare siṃca | yathā nābheradho na patati tathā dhārayeti bhāvaḥ |*) In this verse it is plainly said पिब (*piba*), "drink the Soma juice"; is not the use of the word वृषस्व (*vṛṣasva*) then useless? The answer: since Indra is famous as extremely fond of drinking Soma juice, the use of the two words पिब (*piba*) and वृषभ (*vṛṣabha*) **NOTE** is also proper, to stress plainly: "when drinking Soma, drink as much as you wish, so that it does not fall below the navel, and drink so that the inner part of the belly is well soaked and refreshed."

"O Indra, praised by singers (स्तवान (*stavāna*)), lord of the विश् (*viś*), that is, of the Maruts (विश्वते (*viśvate*)): together with all the gods worthy of sacrifice, प्र तिरः (*pra tiraḥ*), make grow vigorously this sacrifice of ours, which is धितावानं (*dhitāvānaṃ*)." वानं (*vānaṃ*) is the oblation, because it is shared out (वन्यते संभज्यते (*vanyate saṃbhajyate*)); धितं (*dhitaṃ*) means placed; so the sacrifice is one in which the oblation has been placed, that is, in which the oblation has been gathered. "Make it grow" means: complete it by accepting the oblation.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

विश्वते (*viśvate*)—विशां मरुतां पते (*viśāṃ marutāṃ pate*): Indra, the leader of the Marut gods. There is a very important companionship between the Marut gods and Indra. We have explained this at length in Ṛ. Saṃ. Part 8, page 172, and Part 13, page 210.

The eternal companionship of Indra and the Maruts is well known from the Śruti. The relation of king and subjects between Indra and the Maruts, the offering of oblations to Indra together with the Maruts in the principal sacrificial acts, and the Maruts' helping Indra when he fights with the demon Vṛtra, are described in many places in the Saṃhitās and the Brāhmaṇas.

क्षत्रं वा इंद्रो विशो मरुतो विशा वै क्षत्रियो बलवान् भवति ॥ (*kṣatraṃ vā iṃdro viśo maruto viśā vai kṣatriyo balavān bhavati ‖*) (Śa. Brā. 4-3-3-6).

Indra is the king of kṣatriya splendour, the Maruts are the subjects; it is indicated here that the king becomes powerful only through the support of the subjects, and that through the help of the Maruts Indra's valour increased.

The Bṛhaddevatā explains this adjective, which indicates the relation of these deities: wherever Indra together with the Maruts is addressed, he is called मरुत्वान् (*marutvān*).

धितानानं (*dhitānānaṃ*)—वन्यते संभज्यते इति वानं हविः । धितं निहितं हविर्यस्य तं । संभृतहविष्यमित्यर्थः । (*vanyate saṃbhajyate iti vānaṃ haviḥ | dhitaṃ nihitaṃ haviryasya taṃ | saṃbhṛtahaviṣyamityarthaḥ |*) The word वानं (*vānaṃ*), formed from the root वन षण संभक्तौ (*vana ṣaṇa saṃbhaktau*), means a thing prepared. The sense is: the action, that is, the sacrifice, in which such a thing, that is, an oblation, has been set down to be offered to the gods. In the sacrifice the oblation is prepared and kept ready to offer to the gods. The meaning of the word is: a sacrifice furnished with such an oblation.

प्रति (*prati*)—Accept the oblation we offer and make this sacrifice grow, make it complete.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

चंद्रासः (*caṃdrāsaḥ*)—The word चंद्र (*caṃdra*), formed from the root चदि आह्लादने (*cadi āhlādane*), means delightful, giving joy. This word is an adjective of the word सोमाः (*somāḥ*).

इंदवः (*iṃdavaḥ*)—The word इंदु (*iṃdu*), formed from the root उंदी क्लेदने (*uṃdī kledane*), means oozing, dripping. When the Soma plants are crushed with a stone and the juice is taken out, the Soma juice drips little by little, just as juice oozes when sugarcane is pressed; so it is the custom to call freshly pressed Soma juice इंदु (*iṃdu*) or इंदवः (*iṃdavaḥ*) (plural). Since it has just been pressed, the sense "shining" can also be given.

क्षयं (*kṣayaṃ*)—The dwelling-place. Here the dwelling-place means Indra's belly. After Indra drinks the Soma juice it goes into his belly and settles there; hence that stomach is designated here as क्षयं (*kṣayaṃ*), the dwelling-place.

इमे सुताः (*ime sutāḥ*)—These Soma juices that we are pressing now in the sacrifice; that is, not juices pressed earlier. The sense is: the Soma juice freshly pressed now.

सोमाः सुताः (*somāḥ sutāḥ*)—These are words ending in the nominative plural. The earlier forms of these words, सोमासः सुतासः (*somāsaḥ sutāsaḥ*), are also much used in the Ṛgveda.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

गिर्वणः (*girvaṇaḥ*)—गीर्भिर्वननीय (*gīrbhirvananīya*). One who is described by words of praise. Yāska has given the explanation (Ni. 6-14) that a god is called गिर्वणा (*girvaṇā*) because people serve him with songs. That is, the word गिर्वणा (*girvaṇā*) means a god; for by praising him with words of praise and obtaining his favour, he is called गिर्वणा (*girvaṇā*), a god.

मधोः धाराभिः अज्यसे (*madhoḥ dhārābhiḥ ajyase*)—Here the word मधु (*madhu*) means the intoxicating Soma juice, that is, the juice that gives joy. Although the word मधु (*madhu*) by itself means only sweet or joy-giving, since Soma juice has these qualities, using the word मधु (*madhu*) to denote Soma juice is a special custom in the Ṛgveda. धाराभिः (*dhārābhiḥ*) means falling from above or flowing in a stream. When the Soma plants are pressed, the juice pours down from them as water pours down, or, so poured, it flows away and collects in the large wooden vessel, the द्रोण (*droṇa*) or कलश (*kalaśa*), kept for it. The Soma juice that flows and collects in this way is described here by the words मधोः धाराभिः (*madhoḥ dhārābhiḥ*). The seer prays Indra: "Wet your belly with such Soma juice and refresh it."

त्पादातं इत् (*tpādātaṃ it*)—त्वया शोधितं विशदीकृतमेव (*tvayā śodhitaṃ viśadīkṛtameva*). Purified or made clear by you. The commentator says that the word दातं (*dātaṃ*) is formed here from the root दैप् शोधने (*daip śodhane*) and explains the meaning accordingly. But what the food (oblation) purified by Indra is, is not clear. **NOTE**

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

(continued) However, the meaning "given" or "made clear" for दातं (*dātaṃ*) fits to a fair extent. That is, the sense is: this food in the form of an oblation, which we have obtained through Indra's favour.

यशः (*yaśaḥ*)—अन्नं (*annaṃ*). The word यशः (*yaśaḥ*) is read among the 101 names of water (Ni. 2-24), among the twenty-eight names of food (Ni. 3-9) and among the twenty-eight names of wealth (Ni. 3-9). Here the commentator has taken the word यशः (*yaśaḥ*) in the sense of food and explained accordingly. **NOTE**

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

The meaning of this Ṛk does not fit without supplying words. Therefore the commentator has taken the words वनिनः (*vaninaḥ*) and अक्षिता (*akṣitā*) in their component senses. The word वनिनः (*vaninaḥ*), formed from the root वन संभक्तौ (*vana saṃbhaktau*), he explains as "of the sacrificer who attains or worships (the deity)", and the word अक्षिता (*akṣitā*) as indicating "imperishable or complete oblation-materials such as Soma". Although such an interpretation seems necessary to make this Ṛk clear, one should note that the Ṛk can be explained in other ways. But it should be understood that only Indra's drinking of Soma is clearly stated in this Ṛk.

पीत्वी (*pītvī*)—पीत्वा (*pītvā*). Having drunk; here the word पीत्वी (*pītvī*) is used instead of पीत्वा (*pītvā*). Likewise in the Ṛk
उभे सहस्वती भूत्वी सपत्नीं मे सहावहै । (*ubhe sahasvatī bhūtvī sapatnīṃ me sahāvahai |*)
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-145-5)
the word भूत्वी (*bhūtvī*) is used instead of भूत्वा (*bhūtvā*). But such usages are not frequent in the Ṛgveda.

सोमस्य (*somasya*)—सोमं (*somaṃ*). Here the genitive case is used in place of the accusative. Therefore the word सोमस्य (*somasya*) must be given the accusative sense, "Soma, the Soma juice". Or सोमस्य (*somasya*) may be explained as "a small portion of the prepared Soma juice". This meaning is the more suitable. Such usages are a peculiarity of the Ṛgveda literature.

Adrivaḥ—the meaning is वज्रिन् (*vajrin*), that is, O Indra, who bear the thunderbolt weapon. Yāska, explaining the meaning of the mantra
यदिंद्र चित्र मेहनास्ति त्वादातमद्रिवः । राधस्तन्नो विदद्वस उभयां स्त्या भर ॥ (*yadiṃdra citra mehanāsti tvādātamadrivaḥ | rādhastanno vidadvasa ubhayāṃ styā bhara ‖*)

He thus gives the etymology. That is: because it splits everything, being very hard like a mountain rock, adri means the thunderbolt weapon; or else the word adri may be derived from the root अन भक्षणे (*ana bhakṣaṇe*) ("to eat"). In this context, as a hard stone "eats" other things, that is, destroys them or crushes them to powder, so the thunderbolt is a weapon of hard nature like such a stone. On this point the example is ते सोमाद इति ह विज्ञायते (*te somāda iti ha vijñāyate*); the idea is that as the stones used for crushing Soma plants grind the Soma plants and make the juice flow out, so the thunderbolt, of hard nature, destroys enemies. One who holds such a thunderbolt in his hand is called adrivān. Since the thunderbolt is Indra's weapon, adrivān means Indra. The word adrivaḥ is the vocative singular. ##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

Naḥ hotā ṛtviyaḥ sattaḥ—here naḥ may be given the meaning "our", or else, supplying the word यज्ञे (*yajñe*), the meaning

### Printed p. 26
"in our sacrifice" may be stated. For the word ṛtviyaḥ the commentator explains प्राप्तकालः सन् (*prāptakālaḥ san*), "having come at the right time". That is: the sacrifice begins in the morning; the Prātaḥsavana as well, that is, the rite of pressing the Soma juice, takes place in the morning itself. Therefore the priest called Hotṛ, who invokes the gods in the sacrifice, has come and sits on the sacrificial ground at the proper time, not late, that is, in the morning itself, in order to do his duty; such is the idea.

Tistire barhiḥ ānuṣak—[Sāyaṇa:] परस्परसंबद्धं यथा भवति तथा बर्हिःष्वासन्नेषु स्त्रीण मभूत् (*parasparasaṃbaddhaṃ yathā bhavati tathā barhiḥṣvāsanneṣu strīṇa mabhūt*) ("spread on the seats of the oblations in such a way that the blades are joined to one another"). In this sacrifice the darbha grass called barhiḥ, cut evenly, has been spread joined together, so that the gods may come to the sacrificial ground and sit; that is, the darbhas are not spread far apart. They are joined to one another, laid layer upon layer, one layer over another, spread so that the seat is soft. Three layers are thus spread one over the other and the seat is prepared to be soft. This is called barhiḥ. For the word ānuṣak Yāska says: आनुषगिति नामानुपूर्वस्यानुषक्तं भवति (*ānuṣagiti nāmānupūrvasyānuṣaktaṃ bhavati*); and the Nigama also is स्त्रणंति बर्हिरानुषक् (*straṇaṃti barhirānuṣak*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-45-1), (Ni. 6-14). Ānuṣak means mutually, or without a gap, joined one to another; the idea is "not sparse, pressed close". To show that this word is used when darbha is spread pressed close together, Yāska cites the Ṛk स्तृणंति बर्हिरानुषक् (*stṛṇaṃti barhirānuṣak*).

Adrayaḥ ayujran—ग्रावाणः परस्परं संगता अभूवन् (*grāvāṇaḥ parasparaṃ saṃgatā abhūvan*) ("the stones have come together with one another"): the stones for pressing the Soma juice have been placed one beside another; such is the idea.

Now everything is ready in the sacrifice we perform. The priest called Hotṛ has come at the right time to invoke the gods, has sat in his place and is ready. The darbhas have been spread close together, and a soft seat is ready for the gods to come to the sacrificial ground and sit. The stones for crushing the Soma plants and pressing the juice have been placed close by. Since everything is thus ready, O Indra, he is prayed to come quickly to our sacrificial ground to drink the Soma.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

"O Indra, the hymns (*mati*) made by us lick you, the great one, the drinker of Soma, the lord of strength (*śavas*)." रिहंति (*rihaṃti*) is लिहंति (*lihaṃti*) (r and l are not distinguished). The illustration is वत्सं न (*vatsaṃ na*): as mother cows lick the calf, so. मतयः (*matayaḥ*) is from the root *man* (to know). उरुं (*uruṃ*) is from *ūrṇuñ* (to cover), with the sense "great" (Uṇ. Sū. 1-32). वत्सं (*vatsaṃ*) is from *vad* (to speak clearly): the calf is one who constantly "speaks" to its mother; a young cow-child.

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- मतयः (*matayaḥ*)—(O Indra,) the praises made by us
- उरुं (*uruṃ*)—you of mighty form
- सोमपां (*somapāṃ*)—drinker of Soma juice
- शवस्य (*śavasya*)—of strength
- पतिं (*patiṃ*)—lord
- इंद्रं (*iṃdraṃ*)—you, who are Indra
- मातरः (*mātaraḥ*)—cows
- वत्सं न (*vatsaṃ na*)—as they lovingly lick their calves
- रिहंति (*rihaṃti*)—lick (become one with you)

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, these praises made by us lick you, who are of mighty form, the drinker of Soma juice, the lord of strength, in the form of Indra, as cows lovingly lick their calves. That is, they become one with you and bring you delight.

##### English translation (H. H. Wilson)

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

मतयः (*matayaḥ*)—The word मतयः (*matayaḥ*) is formed from the root *man jñāne* and is read among the names of the wise (Ni. 3-9). But here the word *mati* means intellect or mantra. That is, hymn-sentences that we recite with understanding.
शवस्य पतिं (*śavasya patiṃ*)—The word *śavas* is read among the one hundred and one names of water (Ni. 2-24) and among the twenty-eight names of strength (Ni. 3-9). Here it is used in the sense of strength. "Lord of strength" means exceedingly strong.
मातरः वत्सं न रिहंति (*mātaraḥ vatsaṃ na rihaṃti*)—यथा धेनवो वत्सं लिहंति तद्वत् । (*yathā dhenavo vatsaṃ lihaṃti tadvat |*) Just as cows lovingly lick their calves, so the hymns we make lick you (Indra); that is, reach you. The meaning is that the hymns we make with great reverence touch you, Indra, and bring you love and delight. As cows have love for their calves, so we have love (devotion) for Indra; with such love we make our hymns. This is the purport of the illustration.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

सः (*saḥ*)—Here the word *tad* is used instead of *yuṣmad*. That is, the commentators give the word सः (*saḥ*) the meaning त्वं (*tvaṃ*), "you". For the verb मंदस्व (*maṃdasva*), "be gladdened", in this sentence is in the second person, so even though the subject word सः (*saḥ*) is in the third person, it is proper to give it the sense of the second person.
राधसे (*rādhase*)—The word *rādhas* is read among the twenty-eight names of wealth beginning with *magham*, *rekṣas* (Ni. 3-9), and Yāska has given the etymology "*rādha* is a name for wealth; by it they succeed" (Ni. 4-4).
अंधसः (*aṃdhasaḥ*)—The word *andhas* is read among the names of food (Ni. 3-9). As we said before, the word *andhas* is used here to denote Soma juice.
स्तोतारं निदे न करः (*stotāraṃ nide na karaḥ*)—त्वं स्तोतारं मां निंदायै विषयभूतं मा कार्षिः । (*tvaṃ stotāraṃ māṃ niṃdāyai viṣayabhūtaṃ mā kārṣiḥ |*) O Indra, do not let me, who am praising you, become an object of blame. That is, let no one reproach me.

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> हे इंद्र घृतस्नू श्रमजनितजलप्रस्रवणयुक्तौ केशिना केशिनौ स्कंधप्रदेशे
> लंबमानकेशयुक्तौ हरी आसद आसदनीयं बर्हिरभिलक्ष्य सुखे सुखकरे रथेऽर्वांच मस्तदभिमुखं त्वा त्वां वहतां ॥ अर्वांचं । अंचतेः क्विन्युगिदचामिति
> नुम् । वहतां । वहेर्लोटि रूपं । तसो लसार्वधातुकस्वरे कृते धातुस्वरः ।
> आसदे । सदेः कृत्यार्थे केन्प्रत्ययः । नित्सरः । कृदुत्तरपदप्रकृतिस्वरः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Indra, let your two horses, घृतस्नू (*ghṛtasnū*), which have a flow of water produced by exertion, and केशिना (*keśinā*), which have hair hanging down on the shoulder region, bring you (वहतां (*vahatāṃ*)) facing us (अर्वांचं (*arvāṃcaṃ*)), in the comfortable chariot, toward the grass-seat (बर्हिः (*barhiḥ*)) on which one is to sit (आसदे (*āsade*)).

##### प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)

- इंद्र (*iṃdra*)—O Indra
- धृतस्नू (*dhṛtasnū*)—your horses with bodies shining like ghee (or covered with sweat) **NOTE**
- केशिना (*keśinā*)—with attractive hair on the neck
- आसदे (*āsade*)—fit for sitting
- बर्हिः (*barhiḥ*)—toward the seat of darbha grass
- सुखे (*sukhe*)—comfortable
- रथे (*rathe*)—in the chariot
- अर्वांचं (*arvāṃcaṃ*)—facing us
- त्वा (*tvā*)—you
- वहतां (*vahatāṃ*)—let them bring

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, let your horses, with bodies shining like ghee (or covered with sweat) and with attractive hair on the neck, bring you facing us in the comfortable chariot toward the darbha-grass seat fit for sitting.

##### English translation (H. H. Wilson)
May your long-maned and sleek-coated steeds bring you down in your easy chariot to sit upon the sacred grass.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

धृतस्नू (*dhṛtasnū*)—श्रमजनितजलप्रस्रवणयुक्तौ (*śramajanitajalaprasravaṇayuktau*). Since the word dhṛta is read among the names of water (Ni. 2-24), dhṛta means water, the water of exertion, that is, sweat. Because horses run fast over a long distance, their bodies are soaked with sweat. In the same way here, because Indra's horses have brought Indra from far away to our place of sacrifice, their bodies are drenched with sweat. That condition is described here by the word धृतस्नू (*dhṛtasnū*). **NOTE**
केशिना (*keśinā*)—केशिनौ । स्कंधप्रदेशे लंबमानकेशयुक्तौ हरी । (*keśinau | skaṃdhapradeśe laṃbamānakeśayuktau harī |*) It is natural for horses to have long hair on the neck. In the same way, Indra's horses are described here as having long hair on the neck.

सुतं आ गहि (*sutaṃ ā gahi*)—toward the Soma juice that we have prepared; that is, the idea is "come near us to drink Soma."
