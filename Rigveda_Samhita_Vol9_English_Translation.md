## Pīṭhike (Preface) — Kannada, printed pp. i–?

### Printed p. i  (PDF 8)

In this part of the Ṛgsaṃhitā there are the Sūktas from Maṇḍala 1, Sūkta 113 to Sūkta 121 (9 Sūktas; my first reading "130 / 18" was wrong — the contents table, printed pp. vii–xiii, runs 113–121 and the Pīṭhike itself mentions five Aśvin Sūktas, 116–120). The deities of these Sūktas are Rātri, Uṣas, Rudra, Sūrya, the Aśvins and Indra. More than half of this Adhyāya is devoted to the Aśvins. The account given in Skandasvāmin's bhāṣya on the Ṛks addressed to the Aśvins is not found in Sāyaṇa's bhāṣya. All the Sūktas relating to these deities are accompanied by *upākhyānas* (legends). Skandasvāmin has explained all these legends and has indicated the stories of persons who obtained help of many kinds from the Aśvins. Sāyaṇa, who is later than he, and other later writers have many points in their commentaries that are not in Sāyaṇa's bhāṣya. Therefore in the explanation of this part Skandasvāmin's bhāṣya has been used freely. It is a great misfortune that his bhāṣya is not available in full.

It is a settled matter that Skandasvāmin is earlier than Sāyaṇa, who belongs to the fourteenth century. There are many proofs that Harisvāmin, who wrote a bhāṣya on the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa, was his disciple.

> श्रीमतोऽवन्तिनाथस्य विक्रमस्य क्षितीशितुः ।
> धर्माध्यक्षो हरिस्वामी व्याख्यां कुर्वे यथामति ॥

> *śrīmato 'vantināthasya vikramasya kṣitīśituḥ |*
> *dharmādhyakṣo harisvāmī vyākhyāṃ kurve yathāmati ‖*

"I, Harisvāmin, the Dharmādhyakṣa (chief of justice) of the illustrious Vikrama, lord of Avanti, ruler of the earth, write the commentary according to my understanding." Harisvāmin says in the introduction to his bhāṣya that he is the one who adorns the office of chief of justice at the court of Vikramāditya, the Avantinātha and emperor, and that he writes an exposition of the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa as best he can. And again:

> यः सम्राट् कृतवान् सप्त सोमसंस्थास्तथर्क्श्रुतम् ।
> व्याख्यां कृत्स्नाध्यापयेन्नाम्ना श्रीस्कन्दस्वाम्यस्ति मे गुरुः ॥ [?]

> *yaḥ samrāṭ kṛtavān sapta somasaṃsthāstatharkśrutam |*
> *vyākhyāṃ kṛtsnādhyāpayennāmnā śrīskandasvāmyasti me guruḥ ‖ [?]*

"My teacher is Śrī Skandasvāmin — the one who wrote the *prayoga* of the Soma sacrifices and a commentary on the Ṛgveda, and whom all scholars, and the emperor himself, [honour]" (the Kannada says: the teacher of the Soma-yāgas' procedure and the author of the Ṛgveda bhāṣya, my guru, honoured by all the śāstra-scholars and by the emperor). The date of Harisvāmin too is known from his own words:

> यदा दी(=ब्दा)नां कलेर्जग्मुः षट्त्रिंशच्छतानि वै ।
> चत्वारिंशत्समाश्चान्यास्तदा भाष्यमिदं कृतम् ॥

> *yadā dī(=bdā)nāṃ kalerjagmuḥ ṣaṭtriṃśacchatāni vai |*
> *catvāriṃśatsamāścānyāstadā bhāṣyamidaṃ kṛtam ‖*

"When 3,600 years of the Kali (age) and forty more years had passed, then this bhāṣya was composed" — he states his own date (Kali 3640, i.e. as the Kannada says "three thousand six hundred and forty years"). By this, according to the reckoning of historical researchers, it corresponds to about 5[3]8 [?] of the Christian era. At that time Yaśodharman was ruler of Mālava, and Harisvāmin …

… (Yaśodharman) was the patron, Vikramāditya. Yaśodharman, in C.E. 5[3]2 [?], defeated the Hūṇas (Huns) and consolidated his kingdom. Harisvāmin, who lived under his protection, must have written the bhāṣya in 5[3]8 [?]. From the evidence above it may be said that his teacher Skandasvāmin was at the end of the fifth or the beginning of the sixth century C.E.

The Eighth Adhyāya begins with the Sūkta addressed to Uṣas, the dawn: "**idaṃ śreṣṭhaṃ jyotiṣāṃ jyotiḥ, bhāsvatī netrī sūnṛtānām, uṣā divo duhitā, yāvayad-dveṣā ṛtejāḥ**" etc. In the descriptions of these (Ṛks) Uṣas is more excellent and more splendid than all the planets and constellations; she is the cause of the pleasant and truthful transactions of speech; she is born in heaven; she is the life-giver of all beings.

> इदं श्रेष्ठं ज्योतिषां ज्योतिः भास्वती नेत्री सूनृतानाम् ।

In the Ṛgsaṃhitā there are more than twenty Sūktas addressed to Uṣas. In these hundred-odd Ṛks the description and praise of this goddess awaken the poetic genius of the Sūkta-seer. But in Ṛks such as "**punaḥ-punar jāyamānā purāṇī, devī yuvatiḥ**" etc. there is mixed together a kind of agitation along with the joy that is plainly expressed. Having praised Uṣas, who gives consciousness and light to the whole world, and fills living beings with enthusiasm, and having described beauty, the seer then, after manifesting the world, seems to show in most places an anxiety about the fact that Uṣas, who is for ever in the state of youth in the light, (while) before her all beings, becoming old and dying in a short time. Along with the enjoyment of beauty there is also direct experience. About the goddess Uṣas Prof. Max Müller has said some thought-provoking remarks, so they are quoted here (English, as printed):

What we simply call the sun-rise, brought before their eyes every day, the riddle of the riddles, the riddle of existence. The days of their life sprang from that dark abyss which

Five Sūktas of this Adhyāya are addressed to the Aśvins. The Sūktas praising these deities in the Ṛgsaṃhitā number more than fifty altogether. Their distinguishing mark is a dual-natured form in many respects; things compared to them are also of this dual form. "*grāvāṇeva, gṛdhreva, brahmāṇeva* (2-39-1 [?]) *meneiva tena śumbhamāne dampatīva kratuvidā janeṣu* (2-39-2 [?]) *śṛṅgeva, śaphāviva, cakravākeva* (2-39-3 [?]) *hastāviva, pādeva* (5 [?]) *oṣṭhāviva, nāseva, karṇāviva* (6 [?])" — thus they are always compared with things that are of two parts. They are called ever-young (6-62-10 [?]); ancient (6-62-5 [?]); beautiful (6-62-? [?]); of honey-colour (8-?-? [?]); lords of splendours (8-22-4 [?]); and extremely vigorous (6-?-? [?]); thus in many other ways they are described. In describing their various qualities, when the particular topics of separate Ṛks are explained, their greatness will be set out in detail.

### Printed p. iv  (PDF 11)

Of the endless qualities of the Aśvin deities the distinguishing mark is this: they protect devotees who have fallen into calamity and grant them what they desire. Though Indra and the other deities also help their devotees, the help of the Aśvins is more special than all. Indra and the others are famed for freeing devotees caught by enemies in battle and giving them protection; the Aśvins are famed for freeing them from every kind of calamity and giving their protection. Because these are deities connected with the sea, they saved Bhujyu, son of Tugra, who was in distress from shipwreck, by providing an excellent boat. Because they are physicians of the gods they cured devotees afflicted by many kinds of disease. The story of the ṛṣi Cyavāna, who received from them such help and, after the destruction of his old age, obtained youth, is told in the Śatapatha and other Brāhmaṇas. In the same way, in many places in the Ṛgsaṃhitā are described the occasions on which they helped hundreds of devotees, Vimada, Atri and others.

In this Adhyāya the epithet of Uṣas, *ṛtapāḥ* (1-113-12 [?]), and the description of the nature of the Aśvins, *ṛtāyan* (1-11?-?? [?]), show the similarity of qualities each shares with the other. *Ṛtapāḥ* means "protectress of truth or of the sacrifice" (*satyasya yajñasya vā pālayitrī*) — one who guards truth or the sacrifice. *Ṛtāyan* means (*satyam ātmana icchan*) — one who wishes truth for himself, i.e. one who is truthful in soul and truthful in conduct.

The word *ṛta* indicates the order of the universe, both physical and moral. Mitra and Varuṇa, chief among the Ādityas, are the guardians of this law; that they have the position of carrying on the world's affairs by this law and in the keeping of it is described in the Sūktas of the second Adhyāya of the second Aṣṭaka (as printed) [?], which concern them. How the deities Uṣas and the Aśvins follow this law and in what ways they help the world to follow it is explained here, in brief.

> द्वादशारं नहि तज्जराय वर्वर्ति चक्रं परि द्यामृतस्य ।
> आ पुत्रा अग्ने मिथुनासो अत्र सप्त शतानि विंशतिश्च तस्थुः ॥

> *dvādaśāraṃ nahi tajjarāya varvarti cakraṃ pari dyāmṛtasya |*
> *ā putrā agne mithunāso atra sapta śatāni viṃśatiśca tasthuḥ ‖*
> (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-164-11)

"The wheel of Ṛta with twelve spokes (months) revolves ceaselessly round the sky" — in this Ṛk, the wheel called *ṛta*, which has twelve spokes (months), is described as revolving constantly in the atmosphere. That Uṣas follows the path of this Ṛta by its rule —
