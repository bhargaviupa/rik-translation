## Sūkta 29 (Maṇḍala 6)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> अथ सप्तमोऽध्याय आरभ्यते । षष्ठस्य मंडलस्य तृतीयेऽनुवाके पंच
> सूक्तानि व्याकृतानि । इंद्रं व इति षडृचं षष्ठं सूक्तं भरद्वाजस्यार्षं त्रैष्टुभमैंद्रं । अनुक्रम्यते च । इंद्रं षडिति । गतो विनियोगः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Sāyaṇa says that the seventh adhyāya now begins. In the third anuvāka of the sixth maṇḍala five sūktas have been expounded. The sixth sūkta of this series, of six Ṛks, beginning इंद्रं व (*iṃdraṃ va*), has Bharadvāja as its seer, the metre Triṣṭubh, and Indra as its deity. The Anukramaṇī also says so (इंद्रं षट् (*iṃdraṃ ṣaṭ*)). The use in ritual (viniyoga) has already been stated.

Translation (Anuvāda): Now the exposition of the seventh adhyāya of the fourth aṣṭaka is begun. In the third anuvāka of the sixth maṇḍala five sūktas have so far (in the previous adhyāya) been explained. Now the sixth sūkta, "Indraṃ vo naraḥ", is begun. In it there are six Ṛks. The seer of this sūkta is Bharadvāja; the deity is Indra; the metre is Triṣṭup. In the Anukramaṇī it is said at the beginning, "Indraṃ ṣaṭ". The use in ritual (viniyoga) has been told before.

Sūkta 29. Maṇḍala 6. Anuvāka 3. Sūkta 29. Aṣṭaka 4. Adhyāya 7. Varga 1. Number of Ṛks in the sūkta: 6. Seer: Bharadvāja Bārhaspatya. Deity: Indra. Metre: Triṣṭup.

> इंद्रं॑ वो॒ नरः॑ स॒ख्याय॑ सेपुर्म॒हो यंतः॑ सुम॒तये॑  ॒
> चकानाः ।  
> म॒हो हि दा॒ता वज्र॑हस्तो॒ अस्ति॑ म॒हामु॑ र॒ण्वमव॑से  
> यजध्वं ॥ १ ॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> इंद्रं । वः । नरः । सख्याय । सेपुः । महः । यंतः । सुमतये । चकानाः ।  
> महः । हि । दाता । वज्रहस्तः । अस्ति । महां । ऊं इति । रण्वं । अवसे। यजध्वं ॥१॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> हे यजमाना वो युष्माकं संबंधिनो नरो नेतार ऋत्विजः सख्याय
> सखिभावार्थमिंद्रं सेपुः । परिचरंति । स्पृशंति वा । कथंभूताः । महो महांति
> स्तोत्राणि यंत उपयंतः प्राप्नुवंतः । कुर्वंत इत्यर्थः । तथा सुमतये ।
> सुमतिः शोभनानुग्रहात्मक तदीया बुद्धिः । तां चकानाः कामयमानाः ॥
> क्रियाग्रहणं कर्तव्यमिति कर्मणः संप्रदानसंज्ञा । चकाना इति कामयतेश्छांदसं
> रूपं ॥ यद्वा । महो महत्कर्म यंतोऽनुतिष्ठंतः सुमतये सुमतिं
> शोभनां स्तुतिं चकानाः शब्दयंतः ॥ कै गै शब्द इत्यस्माल्लिटः कानचि रूपं ॥ हि
> यस्माद्वज्रहस्तो वज्रपाणिरिंद्रो महो महतो धनस्य दातास्ति भवति तस्माद्रण्वं
> रमणीयं महामु महांतमेवावसे रक्षणाय यजध्वं । हविर्भिः पूजयत ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Sāyaṇa says: O sacrificers, your priests (the leaders, the ṛtvijs) serve Indra, or touch him, for friendship, that is, to become his friends. They do so offering great hymns, approaching and obtaining them, that is, making them; and desiring his good favour, a kindly mind of his towards them. Or else, performing a great rite and desiring (making resound) a good praise. Since Indra, the thunderbolt in his hand, is the giver of great wealth, therefore worship him alone, the delightful, the great, with oblations, for protection.

- इंद्रं (*iṃdraṃ*)—Indra
- सेपुः (*sepuḥ*)—they worship
- वज्रहस्तः (*vajrahastaḥ*)—Indra, the bearer of the thunderbolt
- महः (*mahaḥ*)—to venerable wealth
- दाता (*dātā*)—as the giver
- आस्ति (*āsti*)—he is
- रण्वं (*raṇvaṃ*)—the delightful
- महामु (*mahāmu*)—the great-souled Indra himself

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O sacrificers, the priests who are your relations worship Indra, offering great hymns for the gaining of friendship with Indra **NOTE**, and desiring excellent understanding. Indra, the bearer of the thunderbolt, is the giver of venerable wealth. Worship Indra himself, the delightful and great-souled, for protection.

##### English translation
Your priests, (oh worshippers), propitiate Indra for his friendship, offering great (praise), and desirous of his favour; for the wielder of the thunderbolt is the giver of vast (wealth); worship him, therefore, who is mighty and benevolent, (to obtain) his protection.

#### Ṛk 2

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> आ यस्मि॒न्हस्ते॒ नर्या॑ मिमि॒क्षुरा रथे॑ हिर॒ण्यये॑ रथे॒ष्णाः ।  
> आ र॒श्मयो॒ गभ॑स्तोः स्फू॒रयो॒राध्व॒न्नश्वा॑सो॒ वृष॑णो  ॒
> युजानाः ॥ २ ॥

##### ॥ पदपाठः ॥ (Pada-pāṭha)

> आ । यस्मिन् । हस्ते । नर्याः । मिमिक्षुः । आ । रथे । हिरण्यये । रथेऽस्थाः ।  
> आ । रश्मयः । गभस्योः । स्फूरयोः । आ । अध्वन् । अश्वासः । वृषणः । युजानाः ॥२॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> यस्मिन्निंद्रे नर्या नृभ्यो हिता राय आ मिमिक्षुः आसिच्यंते आपूर्यंते ॥
> व्यत्ययेन कर्मणि कर्तृप्रत्ययः ॥ इंद्रे कुत्रेति चेत् । हस्ते तदीये बाहौ ।
> यद्वा । एतत्समानाधिकरणमिंद्रस्य विशेषणं । हस्ते हंतर्यस्मिन्निंद्र इत्यर्थः ।
> उक्तं यास्केन । हस्तो हंतेः प्राशुर्हनने । नि. १-७ । इति । यद्वा । ईद्वशे
> यॆस्मिन्निंद्रे वर्तमाना राय आ मिमिक्षुः आसिंचंति अपूरयंति स्तोत्यन् ।
> यश्चेंद्रो रथेष्णा रथे स्थाता तत्स्वभावः सन् हिरण्यये सुवर्णमये रथ आ
> शिष्ठति । यद्वा । रथेष्णा रथेवस्थिता रायो धनानि हिरण्मये रथे तिष्ठन् य इंद्र
> अ सिंचति अभिवर्षति । यस्य च स्फूरयोः स्कूलायोर्विस्तृतयोगर्भस्तोः ।
> बाहुनामैतत् । बाह्वो रश्मयोऽभीशव आ सिच्यंते आयम्यंते । यस्य
> चाश्वासोऽश्वा वृषणः सेक्तारो युवानो युजाना रथे युज्यमाना अध्वन्नॆध्वनि
> मार्ग आ मिमिक्षुः आसिंचंति अपूरयंति । आगच्छंतीत्यर्थः । तमिंद्रं स्तुम इति
> शेषः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Sāyaṇa says: in which Indra the riches that are good for men are poured in, that is, are filled (an active form used in a passive sense by a change of voice). If one asks where in Indra, it is in his hand, that is, his arm. Or else this word is an adjective of Indra in the same case: the slayer, Indra in whom. Yāska has said: "hasta is from han, quick in striking" (Ni. 1-7). Or else, the riches that rest in this Indra are poured in, that is, filled, by the praisers. And the Indra who is by nature a stander in the chariot sits in a golden chariot. Or else, the riches, standing as if in the chariot, remain in the golden chariot, and that Indra pours, showers. And of whom the thick, extended arms (gabhasti is a name of the arm) have the reins, the bridles, poured in, that is, held. And of whom the horses, showerers and young, yoked to the chariot, come, fill, on the road. We praise that Indra: this is the part to be supplied.

##### English translation

You who, by might, by strength, are the overpowerer of enemies, the bearer of the thunderbolt,

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, Bharadvāja offers worship at your feet for the gaining of prosperity. O Indra, leader of rites, you, by your strength the overpowerer of enemies, the bearer of the thunderbolt, the possessor of wealth to be given, wearing a praiseworthy form that ever moves about, so as to be seen by all, have become revolving like the sun.

##### English translation
They offer adoration at your feet to acquire prosperity, for you are the overthrower of enemies by (your) strength, the wielder of the thunderbolt, the bestower of donations ; leader (of rites), you are like the rolling sun, wearing in the sight (of all) a graceful and ever-moving form.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

And when this Soma has been pressed, the leaders, the priests who are makers of the brahma, that is, of the food of the nature of the oblation, praising Indra with hymns and reciting (pronouncing) the uktha-verses and the śastras, become most eminently near to the gods. The construction is: that Soma became mixed with Indra.

##### विशेषविषयगळु (Special Topics)

नू चित् (*nū cit*): the particle नू चित् (*nū cit*) is used when the matter of "new" and "old" is indicated. Yāska has given the explanation on this point:
नूचिदिति निपातः पुराणनवयोर्नूचेति च । अद्याचिन्नू चित्तदपो नदीनां । अद्य च पुरा च तदेव कर्म नदीनां । नू च पुरा च सदनं रयीणां रयिरिति धननाम रातेर्दानकर्मणः ॥ (*nūciditi nipātaḥ purāṇanavayornūceti ca | adyācinnū cittadapo nadīnāṃ | adya ca purā ca tadeva karma nadīnāṃ | nū ca purā ca sadanaṃ rayīṇāṃ rayiriti dhananāma rāterdānakarmaṇaḥ ‖*) (Ni. 4-17).
That is: having said that the particle नूचित् (*nūcit*) is used in the sense of new and old, he has given as an example the sentence in the present Ṛk, अद्याचिन्नु चित्तदपो नदीनां (*adyācinnu cittadapo nadīnāṃ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-30-3). The action of the river, that is, the action of flowing, is of one kind both in former times and now. That is, the way the river used to flow in former times, so it flows now also: this is the meaning. And having said that the words नू च (*nū ca*) also are used in this same sense, for it he gives:
नू च पुरा च सदनं रयाणां जातस्य च जायमानस्य च क्षामं । सतश्च गोपा भवतश्च भूरेर्देवा अग्निं धारयन् द्रविणोदां ॥ (*nū ca purā ca sadanaṃ rayāṇāṃ jātasya ca jāyamānasya ca kṣāmaṃ | sataśca gopā bhavataśca bhūrerdevā agniṃ dhārayan draviṇodāṃ ‖*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-96-7).
In this Ṛk, in the first pāda, the words नू च (*nū ca*) are used. O Agni, named Draviṇoda, you are, in former times and now, the resting place of wealth: this is the meaning.

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> अभूरेक इति पंचर्चमष्टमं सूक्तं भरद्वाजस्य सुहोत्रस्यार्षं ।

> शंसनीयानि । तथा पंचमारण्यकं । अभूरेको रयिपते रयाणामित्यष्टौ
> सूक्तानि । ऐ. आ. ५-२-२ । इति ॥ पृष्ठस्य षष्ठेऽहनि निष्केवल्य इदं सूक्तं निविद्वानं।
> सूत्रितं च । एंद्र याहुप नः प्र घा न्वस्याभूरेक इति निष्केवल्यं । आ. ६० ।

> च । अभूरेको रयिपते रयाणामिति सामसूक्तानि । आ. ८-७ ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

Sāyaṇa says: the sūkta of five Ṛks beginning अभूरेक (*abhūreka*) is the eighth sūkta, with Suhotra of the Bharadvāja family as its seer. The penultimate Ṛk is Śakvarī and the remaining three are Triṣṭubh. The deity is Indra. The Anukramaṇī says: अभूरेकः सुहोत्रस्तु चतुर्थी शक्वरी (*abhūrekaḥ suhotrastu caturthī śakvarī*). In the niṣkevalya of the Mahāvrata the eight sūktas beginning with this one are to be recited; likewise the fifth Āraṇyaka says: अभूरेको रयिपते रयाणामित्यष्टौ सूक्तानि (*abhūreko rayipate rayāṇāmityaṣṭau sūktāni*) (Ai. Ā. 5-2-2). On the sixth day of the Pṛṣṭha rite this sūkta is the one with the nivid in the niṣkevalya. It is stated in the sūtra: एंद्र याहुप नः प्र घा न्वस्याभूरेक इति निष्केवल्यं । (*eṃdra yāhupa naḥ pra ghā nvasyābhūreka iti niṣkevalyaṃ |*) (Ā. 60) **NOTE**. In the Viśvajit, in the Acchāvāka's śastra at the midday pressing, this is to be recited as the Sāmasūkta. It is stated in the sūtra: अभूरेको रयिपते रयाणामिति सामसूक्तानि (*abhūreko rayipate rayāṇāmiti sāmasūktāni*) (Ā. 8-7).

Translation (Anuvāda): This sūkta "Abhūr ekaḥ" is the eighth sūkta in the third anuvāka. In it there are five Ṛks. The seer of this sūkta is a person named Suhotra of the Bharadvāja gotra; Indra is the deity; the Ṛk before the last, that is, the fourth Ṛk of this sūkta, is in the Śakvarī metre; the remaining four Ṛks are in the Triṣṭup metre. In the Anukramaṇī it is said: अभूरेकः सुहोत्रस्तु चतुर्थी शक्वरी (*abhūrekaḥ suhotrastu caturthī śakvarī*). In the rite called Mahāvrata, the use of the eight sūktas from this sūkta onward, for the niṣkevalya-śastra mantras, is indicated in the fifth Āraṇyaka of the Aitareya Āraṇyaka by the sentence अभूरेको रयिपते रयाणामित्यष्टौ सूक्तानि (*abhūreko rayipate rayāṇāmityaṣṭau sūktāni*) (Ai. Ā. 5-2-2). On the sixth day of the Pṛṣṭha sacrifice also, this sūkta is to be recited, inserted among the niṣkevalya-śastra mantras as a nivid-bearing sūkta; this is set forth by the sūtra of the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra, ऐंद्र या ह्युप नः प्र घान्वस्याभूरेक इति निष्केवल्यं (*aiṃdra yā hyupa naḥ pra ghānvasyābhūreka iti niṣkevalyaṃ*) (Ā. 8-1). In the Viśvajit rite also, at the time of the midday pressing, in the śastra-mantras recited by the priest called Acchāvāka, this sūkta is to be recited as a Sāmasūkta; this is set forth by the sūtra of the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra, अभूरेको रयिपते रयाणामिति सामसूक्तानि (*abhūreko rayipate rayāṇāmiti sāmasūktāni*) (Ā. 8-7).

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The sūkta of five Ṛks beginning अपूर्व्या (*apūrvyā*) is the ninth sūkta; it is in the Triṣṭubh metre and addressed to Indra. On the second Chandoma day this sūkta belongs to the Niṣkevalya litany. It is also laid down in the sūtra: अपूर्व्या पुरुतमानि तां सु ते कीर्तिं (*apūrvyā purutamāni tāṃ su te kīrtiṃ*) (Ā. 8-7). The application of this sūkta to the Niṣkevalya litany in the Mahāvrata is also stated.

Anuvāda: This sūkta, beginning with अपूर्व्या (*apūrvyā*), is the ninth sūkta in the third anuvāka. It has five Ṛks. The seer of this sūkta is Suhotra, the seer of the preceding sūkta; the deity is Indra; the metre is Triṣṭup. In the Anukramaṇī it is stated as beginning with अपूर्व्या (*apūrvyā*). That this sūkta is applied to the Niṣkevalya-śastra mantras on the day of reciting the second Chandoma mantras is set out in the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra by the sūtra अपूर्व्या पुरुतमानि तां सु ते कीर्तिं (*apūrvyā purutamāni tāṃ su te kīrtiṃ*) (Ā. 8-7). It has been said earlier that this sūkta is applied to the Niṣkevalya-śastra mantras also in the rite called Mahāvrata.

## Sūkta 33 (Maṇḍala 6)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> य ओजिष्ठ इति पंचर्चं दशमं सूक्तं शुनहोत्रस्यार्षं त्रिष्टुभमैंद्रं ।

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The sūkta of five Ṛks beginning य ओजिष्ठः (*ya ojiṣṭhaḥ*) is the tenth sūkta, with Śunahotra as its seer; it is in the Triṣṭubh metre and addressed to Indra. Anuvāda: This sūkta, beginning with य ओजिष्ठः (*ya ojiṣṭhaḥ*), is the tenth sūkta in the third anuvāka. It has five Ṛks. The seer of this sūkta is Śunahotra, born in the Bhāradvāja lineage; the metre is Triṣṭup. In the Anukramaṇī it is said: य ओ जिष्ठः शुनहोत्रस्तु (*ya o jiṣṭhaḥ śunahotrastu*). In the rite called Mahāvrata this sūkta is applied to the Niṣkevalya-śastra mantras.

## Sūkta 34 (Maṇḍala 6)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> सं च त्वे जग्मुरिति पंचर्चमेकादशं सूक्तं शुनहोत्रस्यार्षं । सं च त्वे
> इत्यनुक्रांतं ॥ तृतीये छांदोमिकेऽहनीदमादिके द्वे सूक्ते । सूत्रितं च । सं च
> त्वे जग्मुरिति सूक्ते । आ. ८-७ । इति ॥ महाव्रतेऽपि निष्केवल्य उक्तो विनियोगः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The sūkta of five Ṛks beginning सं च त्वे जग्मुः (*saṃ ca tve jagmuḥ*) is the eleventh sūkta, with Śunahotra as its seer; the Anukramaṇī records it as सं च त्वे (*saṃ ca tve*). On the third Chandoma day two sūktas beginning with this one are to be recited. It is laid down in the sūtra सं च त्वे जग्मुरिति सूक्ते (*saṃ ca tve jagmuriti sūkte*) (Ā. 8-7). The application to the Niṣkevalya litany in the Mahāvrata has also been stated.

### Printed p. 38

Anuvāda: This sūkta, beginning with सं च त्वे जग्मुः (*saṃ ca tve jagmuḥ*), is the eleventh sūkta in the third anuvāka. It has five Ṛks. The seer of this sūkta is Śunahotra; Indra is the deity; the metre is Triṣṭup. In the Anukramaṇī it is stated as सं च त्वे (*saṃ ca tve*). That this sūkta and the following ones are to be recited on the day of reciting the third Chandoma mantras is explained in the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra by the sūtra सं च त्वे जग्मुरिति सूक्ते (*saṃ ca tve jagmuriti sūkte*) (Ā. 8-7). In the rite called Mahāvrata also the sūkta is applied to the Niṣkevalya-śastra mantras.

##### ॥ संहितापाठः ॥ (Saṃhitā-pāṭha)

> सं च॒ त्वे ज॒ग्मुर्गिर॑ इंद्र पू॒र्वीर्वि च॒ त्वद्यं॑ति वि॒भो॑  ॒
> मनीषाः ।  
> पु॒रा नू॒नं च॑ स्तु॒तय॒ ऋषी॑णां पस्पृ॒ध्र इंद्रे॒ अध्यु॑क्था॒र्का ॥ १ ॥

> हे इंद्र त्वे त्वयि पूर्विर्बहो गिरः स्तुतयश्च सं जग्मुः । संगच्छंते ।
> त्वत्त्वत्तो विभ्वो विभवो विस्तृता मनीषाः स्तोत्यणां मतयश्च वि यंति । विविधं

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Indra, many words, that is, praises, come together in you. From you wide-ranging thoughts of the singers, that is, their minds, go forth in many ways. In earlier time, and today, of the seers…

### Printed p. 39

> द्रियार्थदर्शिनां भरद्वाजादीनां स्तुतयः स्तोत्राणींद्रेऽध्यधिकं पस्पृध्रे ।
> अस्पर्धंत । अहमहमिकयेंद्रस्य स्तवने त्वरिता बभूवुरित्यर्थः । तथोक्तार्था ।

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The sense of the sentence begun before: the praises, that is, the hymns, of the seers who see beyond the senses, Bharadvāja and the others, vie with one another in excess for Indra (पस्पृध्रे (*paspṛdhre*) means "they vied"). The meaning is that each, saying "I first, I first", became eager in praising Indra. In the same sense the Ṛk speaks of the ukthārkā: ukthā is the śastra (recitation), and its forms, the arkas, the instruments of worship, that is, the hymns, also vied.

- इंद्र (*iṃdra*)—O Indra
- त्वे (*tve*)—in you
- पूर्वीः (*pūrvīḥ*)—of many kinds
- गिरः (*giraḥ*)—praises

- विभ्वः (*vibhvaḥ*)—wide-ranging
- मनीषाः (*manīṣāḥ*)—the minds of the singers
- वि यंति (*vi yaṃti*)—unfold in many ways
- पुरा (*purā*)—in former time
- नूनं च (*nūnaṃ ca*)—and now
- ऋषीणां (*ṛṣīṇāṃ*)—of the seers, Bharadvāja and the others
- स्तुतयः (*stutayaḥ*)—the hymns
- इंद्रे (*iṃdre*)—in praising Indra

- पस्पृध्रे (*paspṛdhre*)—vie
- उत्थार्का (*utthārkā*)—the ukthā mantras, instruments of worship (also vie)

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, praises of many kinds come together in you alone. The minds of the singers, wide-ranging as they are, unfold in many ways from you alone. Now and in former time the hymns of the singers, Bharadvāja and the others, vie in excess in the very praising of Indra. The ukthā mantras, instruments of worship, likewise vie.

##### English translation

May he always be one to be praised.

- भूत् (*bhūt*)—may he be

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

##### English translation

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> यमिंद्रं धीतयः कर्माणि परिचरणानि न हिंसंति न बाधंते। वाणीर्वाण्यः
> स्तुतयश्च यं न बाधंते । दातुमसमर्थः पुरुषोऽन्यकृतैः परिचरणैः स्तुतिभिश्च
> भृशं खिद्यतेऽस्य चंद्रस्य बहुधनत्वादात्तृत्वाच्च तस्मिन्प्रयुज्यमानानि परिचरणानि
> स्तोत्राणि च बाधहेतवो नाभूवन्नित्यर्थः । किं तर्हित्यत आह । तमिंद्रं वर्धयंतीर्वर्धयंतोऽभि नक्षंति । अभिगच्छंति । अपि च गिर्वणसं गिरां संभक्तारमिंद्रं
> शतं शतसंख्याकाः स्तोतारो यदि गृणंति स्तुवंति । यद्यदि च सहस्रं सहस्रसंख्याकाः स्तोतारः स्तुवंति तत्सर्वं स्तोत्रमस्मा इंद्राय शं सुखकरं भवति ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The Indra whom the dhītayaḥ, that is, rites and services, do not harm, do not oppress; and whom the vāṇīḥ, the words, the praises, do not oppress. A man who is unable to give is greatly distressed by services and praises made by others. But because of this Indra's abundance of wealth and his being a giver, the services and hymns offered to him do not become causes of distress. What then? Making that Indra grow, they reach him (अभि नक्षंति (*abhi nakṣaṃti*), they go to him). Moreover, if a hundred singers praise Gīrvaṇas, the sharer of praises (गिर्वणसं (*girvaṇasaṃ*)), and if a thousand singers praise him, all that praise is शं (*śaṃ*), that is, productive of happiness, for this Indra.

- धीतयः (*dhītayaḥ*)—the acts of worship
- न हिंसंति (*na hiṃsaṃti*)—do not harm
- वाणीः (*vāṇīḥ*)—the words of praise
- न (*na*)—do not harm
- वर्धयंतीः (*vardhayaṃtīḥ*)—making him grow
- अभि नक्षंति (*abhi nakṣaṃti*)—go and reach
- गिर्वणिसं (*girvaṇisaṃ*)—towards Indra, who is worthy of worship through praise
- शतं स्तोतारः (*śataṃ stotāraḥ*)—hundreds of singers
- यदि गृणंति (*yadi gṛṇaṃti*)—if they offer praise and extol
- सहस्रं (*sahasraṃ*)—thousands of singers

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

Whichever Indra neither the acts of worship nor the words of praise harm, going to that Indra and making him grow, all those praises reach him. The hundreds and thousands of praisers who extol that Indra and offer him praises of many kinds, all those praises are productive of happiness for that Indra.

##### English translation
All praises contributing to his exaltation proceed to Indra, whom no acts, no words can harm, since hundreds and thousands of adorers glorify him who is entitled to praise, and so afford him gratification.

> मानेनेंद्राय त्वा वृत्रघ्न इत्यादिमंत्रसाध्येन युक्तो मिमिषो वसतीवर्येकधनाख्याभिरद्भिरासिक्तः सोमोऽस्मै ॥ विभक्ति व्यत्ययः ॥ अस्मिन्निंद्रे न्ययामि ।
> नियतोऽभूत् । यद्वा । अस्मा इंद्रायैतत्तोत्रं ब्रवाणीति शेषः । दिवि
> द्युलोकेऽर्चेवार्कः सूर्य इव मासा । लुप्तोपममेतत् । माति परिच्छिन्नत्तीति
> मासश्चंद्रमाः । स इव मिमिषो वृष्ट्युदकानां सेक्ता य इंद्रो वर्ततेऽस्मिन्निंद्रेऽभिषुतः सोमो नियम्यते स्म । किंच तमिंद्रं यज्ञैर्यजनसाधनैः
> पुरोडाशादिभिर्हविर्भिः सार्धं हवनानि स्तोत्राणि सत्रा सह नावृधुः । अवर्धयन् ।

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

"On this day", that is, on the day of the pressing of Soma, अर्चेव (*arceva*), as if with a hymn that is the instrument of worship, मासा (*māsā*), with a measure, that is, joined with a mantra to be accomplished by the words beginning "Indrāya tvā vṛtraghne", the Soma, sprinkled with the waters called vasatīvarī and ekadhanā, is held fast in this Indra (a change of case is to be understood: अस्मै (*asmai*) for "in this"). Or else the meaning is: "To this Indra I would speak this hymn", the words "I would speak" being supplied. Another explanation: दिवि (*divi*), in the heavenly world, अर्चेव (*arceva*), like the sun; मासा (*māsā*) (the elliptical simile), the moon, which measures; like him, the sprinkler of rain and waters, who is Indra, in this Indra the pressed Soma is held fast. Moreover, the hymns together with the oblations, the puroḍāśa cakes and the rest, which are the means of sacrifice, made that Indra grow together.

> तत्र दृष्टांतः । धन्वन्धन्वनि मरुदेशेऽभि अभिमुखं सं यत् सम्यग्यत्यो गच्छंत्य

> फलस्य विलंबमसहमान ऋषिरनयापींद्रं पृच्छति । हे इंद्र कर्हि स्वित्कदा

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The seer, unable to bear the delay of the fruit, asks Indra with this Ṛk also. O Indra, "when indeed" (कर्हि स्वित् (*karhi svit*)) would that be, that you bring together (नीळयासे (*nīḷayāse*)) with our men the enemy men, and with our heroes, that is, our sons, the enemy heroes and sons? The root नीळयतिः (*nīḷayatiḥ*) has the sense of joining. Such a joining in battle, when would it be? so runs the question. Moreover, when will you win the battles for us? And when will you win in addition (अधि जयासि (*adhi jayāsi*)) the cattle that are found among the enemies who approach the cow-pen, cattle that bear the three threefold products (त्रिधातु (*tridhātu*): milk, curds and ghee)? O Indra, when will you place among us pervading (स्वर्वत् (*svarvat*)) wealth (द्युम्नं (*dyumnaṃ*), that is, riches)?

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

It is also taught in the sūtra: "सत्रा मदासो यो जात एवाभूरेक इति सामसूक्तानि (*satrā madāso yo jāta evābhūreka iti sāmasūktāni*)" (Ā. Sū. 8-7). In the Mahāvrata also its use in the Niṣkevalya is stated.

##### Anuvāda

This sūkta beginning "Satrā madāsaḥ" is the thirteenth sūkta in the third anuvāka. It has five Ṛks. The seer of this sūkta is Nara; the deity is Indra; the metre is Triṣṭup. In the Anukramaṇikā it is said to begin with "Satrā". That this sūkta is used for the Niṣkevalya śastra mantras on the fifth day of the Pṛṣṭha sacrifice is explained by the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra, in the sūtra प्रेदं ब्रह्मेंद्रो मदाय सत्रा मदास इति निष्केवल्यं (*predaṃ brahmeṃdro madāya satrā madāsa iti niṣkevalyaṃ*) (Ā. Sū. 7-12). That in the śastra mantras that the priest called Praśāstṛ recites at the midday pressing of the Viśvajit iṣṭi this sūkta is to be recited as a Sāma-sūkta is explained by the Āśvalāyana Śrauta Sūtra, in the sūtra सत्रा मदासो यो जात एवाभूरेक इति सामसूक्तानि (*satrā madāso yo jāta evābhūreka iti sāmasūktāni*) (Ā. Sū. 8-7). It was said before that this sūkta is used for the Niṣkevalya śastra mantras in the Mahāvrata also.

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

O Indra, your intoxications (मदासः (*madāsaḥ*)) produced by the drinking of Soma are सत्रा (*satrā*), which is a name for "truth": truly they are beneficial to all people (विश्वजन्याः (*viśvajanyāḥ*)). Or else: सत्रा (*satrā*) is a name for "great", and the construction is "your intoxications are great". Moreover, the riches (रायः (*rāyaḥ*)), the masses of wealth that are yours and are पार्थिवासः (*pārthivāsaḥ*), that is, found in the three worlds designated by the earth, they too truly are beneficial to all people: this is the construction. Further, you truly are (अभवः (*abhavaḥ*), you become) the distributor (विभक्ता (*vibhaktā*)), the giver, of foods (वाजानां (*vājānāṃ*)). Or else the construction is "of great foods". Since you hold strength (असुर्यं (*asuryaṃ*)) among the gods, for that reason you act so: this is the meaning.

- अध (*adha*)—and
- पार्थिवासः (*pārthivāsaḥ*)—those that are in the three worlds
- ये रायः (*ye rāyaḥ*)—whatever riches of yours there are (those also)
- सत्रा (*satrā*)—truly beneficial to the world
- यत् (*yat*)—by which greatness of yours
- देवेषु (*deveṣu*)—among the gods
- असुरं (*asuraṃ*)—strength
- धारयथाः (*dhārayathāḥ*)—you hold (for that very reason)
- सत्रा (*satrā*)—truly
- वाजानां (*vājānāṃ*)—of foods

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, the satisfaction born of your drinking of Soma brings benefit to the whole world. Your riches, spread through the three worlds, are truly beneficial to the world. By that greatness of yours by which you hold strength among the gods, for that very reason you are truly the distributor of foods.

##### English translation

The worshipper (जनः (*janaḥ*)), the sacrificer, continually and eminently worships (अनु प्र येजे (*anu pra yeje*)) the strength (ओजः (*ojaḥ*)) of this Indra. Moreover, the sacrificers truly place him before them (अनु दधिरे (*anu dadhire*)) in order to perform a heroic deed. Or else the construction is "the great Indra". And further, to him who seizes (सूमगृभे (*sūmagṛbhe*)) the enemies that go on without interruption, to the destroyer (दुधये (*dudhaye*); the root has the sense of harming: दुधिर्हिंसाकर्मा (*dudhirhiṃsākarmā*)) of them, and to the one who goes against the enemies (अर्वते (*arvate*)), to that Indra they perform (वृंजंति (*vṛṃjaṃti*), accomplish) the rite (क्रतुं (*kratuṃ*)) consisting of service. For what purpose? For the slaying (वृत्रहत्ये (*vṛtrahatye*)) of Vṛtra, the obstructing enemy. **NOTE**

The sacrificer always worships the strength of Indra. The sacrificers, in order to perform heroic deeds, worship Indra truly, setting him as the model. The Maruts, the companions of Indra, the virile powers, the strengths and the horses of Indra serve Indra together. As rivers enter the ocean, the recited mantras full of strength and the praises go and join the widely pervading Indra.

- एकः (*ekaḥ*)—the unique
- राजा (*rājā*)—you have become the ruler

##### भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)

O Indra, you who are praised by all, cause to flow the stream, in the form of a flood, of wealth that gives joy to many and is the cause of dwelling. You, the one without a second

> स तु श्रु॑धि॒ श्रुत्या॒ यो दु॑वो॒युद्यार्न भूमा॒भि रायो॑  ॒

> असो॒ यथा॑ नः॒ शव॑सा चका॒नो यु॒गेयु॑गे॒ वय॑सा॒ चेकि॑तानः ॥ ५ ॥

> असः । यथा । नः । शवसा । चकानः । युगेऽयुगे । वयसा । चेकितानः ॥ ५ ॥

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> हे इंद्र श्रुत्या श्रुत्यानि शोतव्यानि स्तोत्राणि तु क्षिप्रं श्रुधि ! शृणु ।
> यस्त्वं दुवोयुरस्मदीयं परिचरणमात्मन इच्छन् अर्योरेः संबंधीनि
> रायो धनानि भूम बहुतराणि द्यौर्न सूर्य इवाभि भवसि स त्वं श्रुधीत्यन्वयः ।
> अपि च शवसात्मीयेन बलेन स्तोतव्येन युक्तश्चकानः स्तूयमानो युगे युगे
> काले काले वयसा हनिर्लक्षणेनान्नेन चेकितानः प्रज्ञायमानस्त्वं यथा येन
> प्रकारेण नोऽस्माकमसाधारणोऽसि तथासः । स्याः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

"O Indra, listen quickly to the praises that are fit to be heard (श्रुत्या (*śrutyā*) is explained as श्रुत्यानि शोतव्यानि (*śrutyāni śotavyāni*), praises to be heard); you who, desiring our service, overcome like the sun the abundant riches belonging to the enemy, be that one and hear." The construction is: you who do so, listen. Further: you, endowed with your own strength and being praised, and made known age after age by the offered food, the oblation; as you are not common but peculiarly ours, so may you be. **NOTE**

## Sūkta 37 (Maṇḍala 6)

##### ॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya)

> आर्वाग्रथमिति पंचर्चं चतुर्दशं सूक्तं भरद्वाजस्यार्षं त्रैष्टुभमृंद्रं।
> आर्वाग्रथमित्यनुक्रांतं ॥ महाव्रते निष्केवल्य उक्तो विनियोगः ॥

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The sūkta beginning आर्वाग्रथमिति (*ārvāgrathamiti*) has five Ṛks and is the fourteenth sūkta. Its seer is Bharadvāja (Ārṣa), its metre is Triṣṭubh, and its deity is Indra. The Anukramaṇī records it with the words आर्वाग्रथमित्यनुक्रांतं (*ārvāgrathamityanukrāṃtaṃ*). Its use, as stated before, is in the Niṣkevalya (śastra) of the Mahāvrata.

##### Anuvāda

This sūkta beginning "अर्वाग्रथं (*arvāgrathaṃ*)" is the fourteenth sūkta in the third anuvāka. It has five Ṛks. Bharadvāja is the seer of this sūkta, Indra is the deity, and the metre is Triṣṭup. In the Anukramaṇikā it is said to begin "Arvāgratham". It has already been stated that this sūkta is used for the Niṣkevalya-śastra mantras in the Mahāvrata sacrifice.

##### Gist of the bhāṣya

The horses yoked to the chariot (रथ्यासः (*rathyāsaḥ*)), going about on every side (आसस्राणासः (*āsasrāṇāsaḥ*)), moving straight (ऋज्यंतः (*ṛjyaṃtaḥ*), going directly), should carry towards our oblation, which is to be heard of (श्रवः (*śravaḥ*)), Indra, who stands in the chariot with fair wheels (सुचक्रे (*sucakre*)) and acts like strength itself (शवसानं (*śavasānaṃ*)).
