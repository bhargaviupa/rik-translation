## Preface (Pīṭhike)

When the sūktas or ṛks of the Ṛgveda are recited in the performance of sacrificial and other rites, and in the rites such as marriage prescribed in the Gṛhya-sūtras, it is the practice handed down from the time of the seers to state first the ṛṣi, deity and metre of the mantras and their application (viniyoga), and only then to recite the mantras concerned. The ṛṣis of a sūkta, the Vedic scholars say, are the seers (draṣṭṛ) of that sūkta. Western scholars say that the respective ṛṣis themselves are the authors who composed the respective sūktas. Ordinarily a sūkta made up of many ṛks has one ṛṣi. In some cases different ṛks within a single sūkta have different ṛṣis. Likewise there are many sūktas standing under the name of a single ṛṣi.

As regards the deities, the same arrangement applies. That is, a sūkta ordinarily has one deity; or else different ṛks of a single sūkta have different deities, and there are many sūktas addressed to one and the same deity. Among the deities the chief ones are Agni, Indra, the Aśvin deities, Uṣas, Āditya, the Marut deities, Savitṛ, the Viśvedevas, Pavamāna Soma, and Dyāvāpṛthivī. Besides these there are other deities such as Rudra, Viṣṇu, Pūṣan, Bṛhaspati and Prajāpati. Besides these deities, things said to be inanimate are also regarded as deities of certain ṛks: आपः (*āpaḥ*) (water), night, ग्रावाणः (*grāvāṇaḥ*) (the stones that crush the Soma juice), the herb, the sacrificial post, knowledge, the rivers, दानस्तुतिः (*dānastutiḥ*), the bow, the bowstring, the quiver, the chariot, and so on. How inanimate things become deities is explained by the commentator (Sāyaṇa) in his introduction to the commentary:

अचेतनेऽर्थबंधनादिति । ओषधे त्रायस्वैनं, शृणोत ग्रावाण इत्यादावचेतने द्रव्ये चेतनोचितरक्षणश्रवणाद्यर्थं बध्नाति । स चायुक्तः । नन्वभिमानिव्यपदेश इति वैयासिकशास्त्रे सूत्रितत्वादोषध्याद्यभिमानिचेतनदेवतात्र निवक्ष्यतां ॥ (*acetane 'rthabaṃdhanāditi | oṣadhe trāyasvainaṃ, śṛṇota grāvāṇa ityādāvacetane dravye cetanocitarakṣaṇaśravaṇādyarthaṃ badhnāti | sa cāyuktaḥ | nanvabhimānivyapadeśa iti vaiyāsikaśāstre sūtritatvādoṣadhyādyabhimānicetanadevatātra nivakṣyatāṃ ‖*)

That is, in the ṛks that speak of inanimate things such as the herb and the pressing stone, those inanimate things themselves are not addressed; the presiding deities of those things are praised. Example: when the herb is addressed, it is not the inanimate plants and the like but the deity presiding over them, Soma, who is meant, for there is the Śruti sentence सोम ओषधीनामधिपतिः (*soma oṣadhīnāmadhipatiḥ*). The same is to be understood for other things; so he explains. Some raise the objection that deities such as Agni and the Maruts are likewise inanimate. Not only does the above explanation apply to this objection too, but it is also noteworthy that the Vedāntins say that the parts of one chief deity, the lord of all the deities, are what have become manifest in the forms of Agni and the rest. On this subject Yāska says:

अग्निः पृथिवीस्थानो वायुर्वेंद्रो वांतरिक्षस्थानः सूर्यो द्युस्थानः । तासां महाभाग्यादेकैकस्या अपि बहूनि नामधेयानि भवंति । अपि ना कर्मपृथक्त्वाद्यथा होताध्वर्युर्ब्रह्मोद्गातेत्यस्य एकस्य सतः । अपि ना पृथगेव स्युः पृथग्नि स्तुतयो भवंति ॥ (*agniḥ pṛthivīsthāno vāyurveṃdro vāṃtarikṣasthānaḥ sūryo dyusthānaḥ | tāsāṃ mahābhāgyādekaikasyā api bahūni nāmadheyāni bhavaṃti | api nā karmapṛthaktvādyathā hotādhvaryurbrahmodgātetyasya ekasya sataḥ | api nā pṛthageva syuḥ pṛthagni stutayo bhavaṃti ‖*) (Ni. 7-5).

The chief purport is this: although Agni, Indra and Āditya are praised as distinct deities, Agni on the earth, Vāyu or Indra in the atmosphere, and Āditya or Sūrya in heaven, yet, just as in the sacrificial act the officiating priests called Hotṛ, Adhvaryu, Brahman and Udgātṛ are but limbs of the sacrifice, so Agni and the other deities are only parts or limbs of one chief deity (the Supreme Self). Those who praise them, according to their respective qualities, regard them as separate deities and praise them so. And so on—

Yaska, in the Daivata-kanada of his Nirukta asserts that there are;
in the Veda three divinities. Agni on the earth ; Vayu or
Indra, in the sky ; and Surya (Sun) in heaven ; of each of whom
there are many appellations expressive of his greatness and of
the variety of his functions. All the Deities are but parts of one
Atman or Soul, subservient to the diversification of his praises
through the immensity and variety of his attributes. The
Anukramanika goes further, and affirms that the reis but one
Deity, the great soul, quoting in support of this doctrine,
a passage which applies only to the Sun (सूर्य आत्मा जगतस्तस्थुषश्च (*sūrya ātmā jagatastasthuṣaśca*)
Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-115-1) who is there called " the soul of all that moves
or immovable.
Thus we see that in invoking the different phenomena of nature,
the Vedic Rishis had evidently in view the Supreme Energy

underlying them all. It is not the rain or lightning that is
worshipped, but the Deity or the Divine power that rules them
and this Divine Power is designated by the Vedic Rishis as
Parjanya.
The fire that we see before us and which is so useful to the people
is not the Deity who is invoked by the Rishis—but the Divine
Power which is designated by them as Agni “who bursts forth
from the wood (from the araṇis) when the fire is churned with a stick.”
It is thus clear that the Vedic Rishis did not merely worship
the natural phenomena but the Energy under-lying them all
which they denominated by various names, such as, Surya,
Agni, Indra &c. To them either Indra only or Agni or Surya,
or all of them, together with the other Powers of Nature, did
not complete the conception of God. Nor was any of them
regarded by them as the Supreme Ruler of the Universe,
Omniscient and Omnipotent. They represented merely the
various Energies of the All-Powerful Providence manifested to
humanity through diverse natural phenomena. From this it becomes clear what the seers' intention was with regard to the deities.

On the metre we have spoken earlier. Praising the deities and singing their qualities with metrical ṛks and mantras is more delightful than praising them with sentences in prose, and it is a means of expressing fully the devotion in the mind and a clear idea. In praising with the many kinds of metres the ṛṣis have shown very well their maturity, their power and capacity in presenting a subject, and their apt illustrations.

I wish to say a word or two about the viniyoga. When a particular deity is praised, reciting the ṛks that relate to that deity is what is called viniyoga. In this order of application there is a regular method. For example: in a sacrifice, when an oblation is made to Agni, the mantras describing Agni's qualities are recited first.

Mantras of this kind are called निवित् (*nivit*) or निविद्वान (*nividvāna*). Then, at the invocation, one ṛk addressed to Agni is recited; it is called the पुरोनुवाक्या (*puronuvākyā*) mantra. Having invoked the deity with this पुरोनुवाक्या (*puronuvākyā*) mantra, the mantra (ṛk) by which, with another ṛk of the same deity, an oblation is made to the deity thus invoked is called the याज्या (*yājyā*) mantra. Thus three kinds of mantras are recited one after another: first the निवित् (*nivit*) mantra, then the पुरोनुवाक्या (*puronuvākyā*), then the याज्या (*yājyā*). There is no rule that the निवित् (*nivit*) mantra must be a single ṛk. Ordinarily, in the case of Agni especially, there are twelve of these mantras. The याज्या (*yājyā*) and पुरोनुवाक्या (*puronuvākyā*) mantras must each be a single ṛk. Different deities have different याज्या (*yājyā*) and पुरोनुवाक्या (*puronuvākyā*) mantras. Besides these mantras, there are the प्रातरनुवाक (*prātaranuvāka*) mantras: in the sacrifice, on the day before the Soma pressing, after the last part of the night, before the time of dawn, certain mantras are recited. These mantras must consist of ṛks of the seven metres beginning with Gāyatrī, and they must also be addressed to the respective deities. They must be recited according to a prescribed number. Such recitation is called the प्रातरनुवाक (*prātaranuvāka*) mantra. Among these there are the varieties आग्नॆयक्रतु (*āgnèyakratu*), आश्विनिक्रतु (*āśvinikratu*) and उषस्यक्रतु (*uṣasyakratu*). Further, the mantras to be recited by the Hotṛ are called शस्त्र (*śastra*) or उक्षमंत्र (*ukṣamaṃtra*). Among these there are the varieties आज्यशस्त्र (*ājyaśastra*), प्रउगशस्त्र (*praugaśastra*), मरुत्वतीयशस्त्र (*marutvatīyaśastra*), वैश्वरदेवशस्त्र (*vaiśvaradevaśastra*), अग्निमारुतशस्त्र (*agnimārutaśastra*) and निष्केवल्यशस्त्र (*niṣkevalyaśastra*). The Sāmaveda mantras are sung by the priest called Uddātṛ. These mantras are called स्तोत्र (*stotra*). Among them there are the varieties बहिष्पवमानस्तोत्र (*bahiṣpavamānastotra*), आज्यस्तोत्र (*ājyastotra*), माध्यंदिनपवमानस्तोत्र (*mādhyaṃdinapavamānastotra*), आर्भवस्तोत्र (*ārbhavastotra*), अग्निष्टोमसाम (*agniṣṭomasāma*) and पृष्ठस्तोत्र (*pṛṣṭhastotra*). For all these there are different, specified mantras, which must be recited on the respective occasions. In the same way, in the different sacrifices, the mantras addressed to the respective deities must be recited according to the occasion. This order and the like has been set forth in sequence for the mantras belonging to the Ṛgveda by the great sage Āśvalāyana in the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra. The priest called Hotṛ must recite these. The Yajurveda mantras to be recited by the Adhvaryu, and their application, have been described by Āpastamba and other ṛṣis in their own Śrauta-sūtras. The priests called Adhvaryu, students of the Yajurveda, recite these on the respective occasions and carry out the sacrificial rite. In the same way the Sāmavedins sing, in the manner of Sāma singing, the mantras stated in their Śrauta-sūtras. All this is the application of the mantras used in the Śrauta rites.

In the domestic (gṛhya) rites, that is, in marriage and similar rites, the students of the respective śākhās must recite, on the respective occasions, the mantras suited to the respective rites, according to the rule stated in the Gṛhya-sūtras of their own śākhā. These are the mantras used in the domestic rites.

Besides these, in rites done for desired ends, in hostile (ābhicārika) rites, and in worship of deities and the like, the mantras addressed to the respective deities are to be recited according to the occasion. For all such rites no specific sūtras have been composed. The mantras are to be applied to the respective rites according to their meaning.

The mantras used in the Śrauta rites such as sacrifices are addressed to the respective deities, and their meaning accords with the rite. In the domestic rites too it is the same; but in certain rites of non-ancient origin and in worship of deities, since there are no proper mantras, the practice has come into use of employing such mantras, even where the meaning is different, on the ground that the word alone suffices. For example: it may be said that in ancient times there was no worship, homa and so on of the nine planets. For among the nine planets, other than the names Sūrya, Candra and Bṛhaspati, the other planetary deities are not treated in the Ṛgveda. That is, the deities Aṅgāraka, Budha, Śukra, Śanaiścara, Rāhu and Ketu are not in the Veda. The mantras used for them contain only the present name of the respective deity, while the meaning is of another kind. For example, for the planet Ketu, in the ṛk

केतुं कृष्णन्न केतवे पेशोमर्या अपेशसे । समुषद्भिरजायथाः ॥ (*ketuṃ kṛṣṇanna ketave peśomaryā apeśase | samuṣadbhirajāyathāḥ ‖*)

although the word केतु (*ketu*) is recited, the word केतु (*ketu*) there has the meaning "action" or "intelligence" (प्रज्ञा (*prajñā*)), and is not the planet Ketu; the same rule applies to the other planets. With regard to the gods Rāma, Kṛṣṇa and others who have come into use in modern, that is, non-ancient (non-Vedic) times, since there are no mantras for them in the Veda either, the practice may have come into vogue that any mantra suited to the occasion may be recited. Gradually, without much regard to the primacy of the meaning of mantras, primacy has been given to the words alone, and the practice has come into vogue that, whatever the meaning of mantras may be, it suffices to pronounce them according to rule without any difference of accent and letters. Following that, in a large part of the mantras the application does not accord with their meaning. In some cases the application is said to be लैंगिक (*laiṃgika*). There, "deities stated in the linga" (लिंगोक्तदेवतॆगळु (*liṃgoktadevatègaḷu*)) is to be understood as the deities mentioned in that ṛk. For example:

या गुंगूर्यासिनीवाली या राका या सरस्वती । इंद्राणीमह्व ऊतये वरुणानीं स्वस्तये ॥ (*yā guṃgūryāsinīvālī yā rākā yā sarasvatī | iṃdrāṇīmahva ūtaye varuṇānīṃ svastaye ‖*)
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-32-8)

For this ṛk the deity is given as लिंगोक्तदेवताः (*liṃgoktadevatāḥ*); here "stated in the linga" means the female deities mentioned in this ṛk: Gungū, Sinīvālī, Rākā, Sarasvatī, Indrāṇī and Varuṇānī. लिंगोक्त (*liṃgokta*) has the general sense "stated in the mantra".

Yet with the weighty aim that through such a great undertaking all people, scholars and laymen alike, may gain growth in knowledge and become worthy of God's grace, His Highness Śrī Jayachamarajendra Wodeyar Bahadur, G.C.B., G.C.S.I., Lord of the throne of Karnataka, a lover of Vedic learning, himself a scholar, beloved of his subjects and devoted to God, is encouraging with his generous patronage the publication of this work, which requires great expense; this must be called the merit of the whole multitude of the people of Karnataka.
