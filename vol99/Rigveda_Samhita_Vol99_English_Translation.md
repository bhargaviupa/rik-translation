*Gist:* When the sister vessels (ladles) of the altar-earth, filled with ghee, stand near, Agni the Adhvaryu rejoices at their arrival as barley rejoices at rain.

**प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)**

यदि (*yadi*)—when. मातु: (*mātu:*)—of the earth, which is the maker of the world and has the form of the altar. स्वसा (*svasā*)—the ladle vessels (Juhū) that are sisters to her. धृतं (*dhṛtaṃ*)—clarified butter (ājya). भरंति (*bharaṃti*)—filling themselves with. उप अस्थित (*upa asthita*)—are placed near the fire (then),

तासां (*tāsāṃ*)—of those ladle vessels. आगतौ (*āgatau*)—at the time of arrival. अध्वर्युः (*adhvaryuḥ*)—Agni, who desires the sacrifice or who is named Adhvaryu. यवः (*yavaḥ*)—the barley grain. वृष्टीव (*vṛṣṭīva*)—as if indicating joy at the time of the coming of rain. मोदते (*modate*)—rejoices.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

When the ladle vessels, which are sisters to the earth in the form of the altar, filled with ghee, come near Agni, then Agni, who desires the sacrifice, rejoices greatly, as barley shows its joy at the time of the coming of rain.

**Printed English translation**

When the sister,(vessel), bearing the clarified butter, is placed near the maternal(altar), the Adhwaryu(Agni) rejoices at their approach, like barley(at the fall of) rain.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

In this Ṛk the words मातु: स्वसा (*mātu: svasā*) have no clearly stated meaning, but the commentator has fitted in the meanings "altar" and "Juhū" according to the context and explained the sense. For the word *mātṛ* he gives the various meanings mother, maker, altar, world, earth, according to context. Here he gives the meaning "sacrificial altar", and for the word स्वसा (*svasā*) he explains "Juhū, the wooden ladle for offering ājya, which is like a sister to the altar". Although the sense fits well with this kind of explanation, there remains doubt whether these words are in fact used in these senses.

अध्वर्युः धृतं भरंती आगतौ यवः वृष्टीव मोदते (*adhvaryuḥ dhṛtaṃ bharaṃtī āgatau yavaḥ vṛṣṭīva modate*)—the comparison stated in this illustration fits well. When the priest called Adhvaryu fills the offering ladles with ājya (ghee) in order to offer it in the fire and comes near the fire, the joy that arises in Agni is described: as the barley plants welcome with great joy the rain that they need, so Agni too becomes joyful.

*Gist:* Agni himself, as priest, performs the priestly office for his own purpose; we then fully offer praise, sacrifice and oblation.

**प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)**

(अयमग्निः (*ayamagniḥ*)—this Agni) स्वः (*svaḥ*)—himself. स्वाय (*svāya*)—his own. धायसे (*dhāyase*)—for the work, or for holding the oblations. ऋत्विक् (*ṛtvik*)—in the form of the priests, the Hotṛ and others, described before. ऋत्विजं (*ṛtvijaṃ*)—the work to be performed by the Hotṛ and others. कृणुतां (*kṛṇutāṃ*)—let him perform. वयं (*vayaṃ*)—we also. आत् (*āt*)—immediately after that. स्तोमं (*stomaṃ*)—the praise. यज्ञं च (*yajñaṃ ca*)—and the sacrifice that depends on it. अरं (*araṃ*)—fully. वनेम (*vanema*)—we worship. ररिम (*rarima*)—we also offer the oblation.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

Let this Agni himself, in the form of the priest, perform for his own work the work to be performed by the Hotṛ and others. Immediately after that we too fully worship the praise and the sacrifice that depends on it (we perform them), and we also offer the oblation.

*Running head: Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya-sahitā, Maṃ. 2, A. 1, Sū. 5, p. 504.*

**Printed English translation**

May he, the ministering priest, discharge the office of priest at his own ceremony, and may we worthily repeat(his) praise, and offer(him) sacrifice.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

सः ऋत्विक् ऋत्विजं कृणुतां (*saḥ ṛtvik ṛtvijaṃ kṛṇutāṃ*)—in this sacrifice which Agni himself performs, the wish is expressed that, as said before, he himself discharge the duties of the many priests who take part in the sacrifice, the Hotṛ, Potṛ, Praśāstṛ and others, and bring the sacrifice to completion. The use of the words ऋत्विक् ऋत्विजं (*ṛtvik ṛtvijaṃ*) here indicates all the priests, the Hotṛ and the rest. That is, Agni himself (in the form of each respective priest) performs the rites that all of them perform; the meaning is that Agni, who has the form of a divine priest, performs the rites that the human priests perform.

स्तोमं यज्ञं चादरं वनेमा ररिमा वयं (*stomaṃ yajñaṃ cādaraṃ vanemā rarimā vayaṃ*)—if, as said above, Agni himself performs the sacrificial rites of all the priests, what is left for us to do? The answer: we too recite hymns and the like in the sacrifice and praise the gods, and offer oblations and the like; thus the acts to be performed by us (the sacrificers and others) are also explained.

## Sūkta 6 (Maṇḍala 2)

*The sixth sūkta.*

**Sāyaṇa's introduction to the sūkta** (Sanskrit, transcribed; see the Anuvāda below for the sense):

> इमां म इत्यष्टर्चं षष्ठं सूक्तं सोमाहुत्यार्षमाग्नेयं गायत्रं ।
> इमां मे गायत्रं हीत्यनुक्रांतं ॥ प्रातरनुवाकाश्विनशस्त्रयोगायत्रे छंदस्यस्य
> विनियोगः । सूत्रितं च । इमां मे अग्ने समिधमिमामिति त्रयाणामुत्तमामुद्ध-
> रेत् । आ. ४-१३ । इति ॥ अपराह्नि क्यामुपसदीमां मे अग्ने समिधमिति तिस्र ऋच-
> स्त्रिरभ्यस्ता नव सामिधेन्यः कृत्वा विनियोक्तव्याः । अथोपसदिति खंडे सूत्रितं । इमां
> मे अग्ने समिधमिमामिति तु सामिधेन्यः । आ- ४-८ । इति ॥

*Gist:* *imāṃ me* is the sixth sūkta, of eight ṛks, Gāyatrī metre, deity Agni, seer Somāhuti Bhārgava; used in the Prātaranuvāka and Āśvina-śastra, and as Sāmidhenī verses.

**Anuvāda (Kannada)**

This sūkta *imāṃ me* is the sixth sūkta in the first anuvāka. It has eight ṛks. The seer is the same Somāhuti Bhārgava who was the seer of the preceding sūkta; the deity is Agni; the metre is Gāyatrī. In the Anukramaṇī it is stated "*imāṃ me gāyatraṃ hi*". It is explained by the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra rule इमां मे समिधमिमामिति त्रयाणामुत्तमामुद्धरेत् (*imāṃ me samidhamimāmiti trayāṇāmuttamāmuddharet*) (Āś. 4-13) that this sūkta is applied to the Gāyatrī ṛks at the time of reciting the Prātaranuvāka and the mantras of the Āśvina-śastra. Also, in the Upasad sacrifice performed in the afternoon, the three ṛks of this sūkta beginning इमां मे अग्ने समिधं (*imāṃ me agne samidhaṃ*), each recited three times, are to be used as nine Sāmidhenī ṛks; this is explained by the rule इमां मे अग्ने समिधमिमामिति तु सामिधेन्यः (*imāṃ me agne samidhamimāmiti tu sāmidhenyaḥ*) (Āś. 4-8) in the section called Athopasad of the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra.

Metre: Gāyatrī (printed छंद‘ (*chaṃda‘*) गायत्री (*gāyatrī*)).

as a homa, these are the small dry twigs fallen beneath sacrificially suitable trees such as the aśvattha (peepal). They are thinner than a little finger and four to six inches long. **Printed English**

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

सूक्तेन (*sūktena*)—सुष्टु उक्तेन (*suṣṭu uktena*): well recited, that is, a hymn or Ṛk that praises the qualities of the gods well. In the Ṛgveda a group of many such Ṛks is called a sūkta. In sum, sūkta means a hymn of praise.

ऊर्जो नपात् (*ūrjo napāt*)—बलस्य नप्तः (*balasya naptaḥ*): when fire is churned out of the araṇi sticks, Agni arises only by churning with great force; therefore Agni is addressed with the words सहसस्पुत्र (*sahasasputra*) [as supplied] and ऊर्जोनपात् (*ūrjonapāt*).

अश्वमिष्टे (*aśvamiṣṭe*)—व्यापकयज्ञ (*vyāpakayajña*): one who pervades the sacrifice. If the word अश्व (*aśva*) is understood as derived from the root अशू व्याप्तौ (*aśū vyāptau*), the above meaning results. Or आशु क्रियामणं कर्म अश्वं (*āśu kriyāmaṇaṃ karma aśvaṃ*) [as supplied]: the word अश्वं (*aśvaṃ*), formed from अशु (*aśu*) meaning "quickly", also means a sacrifice performed quickly. Here both meanings are possible.

अया (*ayā*)—आनया (*ānayā*) [as supplied]: for the convenience of the metre the na of अनया (*anayā*) is dropped, giving आया (*āyā*). Likewise the a of आत्मना (*ātmanā*) is dropped and the form त्मना (*tmanā*) is used in many places. Such usages are numerous in the Ṛgveda.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

मघवा (*maghavā*)—the word मघ (*magha*) is read among the names of wealth (Nirukta 3-9). There is also the explanation मघमिति धननामधेयं महंतेर्दानकर्मणः (*maghamiti dhananāmadheyaṃ mahaṃterdānakarmaṇaḥ*) (Nirukta 1-7), which indicates the act of giving. The word मघ (*magha*), derived from the root मंहति (*maṃhati*) [as supplied], means wealth; मघवान् (*maghavān*) means one who has wealth. This word is used also of other deities such as Agni and Indra; because its use is most frequent in connection with Indra, in modern Sanskrit the word मघवत् (*maghavat*) has no meaning other than Indra.

सूरिः (*sūriḥ*)—the word सूरि (*sūri*) is read among the names of praisers (Nirukta 2-19). This word has the meanings intelligent, wise, clever, inspirer, and so on.

The commentator has given as an example, indicating this same sense, the Ṛk स होता सेदु दूत्यं चिकित्त्वा अंतरीयते (*sa hotā sedu dūtyaṃ cikittvā aṃtarīyate*) (Ma. Saṃ. 4-8-4) [reference as supplied]. But in the commentary on that Ṛk the idea that he enters the hearts of men and knows what is in their minds is not indicated. Instead, for the words अंतः ईयते (*aṃtaḥ īyate*) he explains the sense अंतर्द्यावापृथिव्योर्मध्य ईयते गच्छति (*aṃtardyāvāpṛthivyormadhya īyate gacchati*), that is, he goes (moves about) between heaven and earth (earth and atmosphere). Therefore the Ṛk given here as an example does not establish the meaning of the present Ṛk.

जन्म उभया विद्वान् (*janma ubhayā vidvān*)—उभयविधानि जन्मानि यष्टणां यजमानानां यष्टव्यानां देवानां च संबंधीनि च जानन् (*ubhayavidhāni janmāni yaṣṭaṇāṃ yajamānānāṃ yaṣṭavyānāṃ devānāṃ ca saṃbaṃdhīni ca jānan*)—here Agni is described as knowing the two kinds of births. The commentator explains this as knowing the birth, or order of origin, of the sacrificing worshippers and of the gods who are worshipped in the sacrifice.

दूतो जन्मेव मित्रः (*dūto janmeva mitraḥ*)—here an illustration is given. जनेभ्यो हितो विशेषेण मित्रेभ्यो हितो दूत इव । स यथा प्रजानां चित्तवृत्तिज्ञानाय राज्ञा प्रेरितस्तासां मनो जानाति तद्वत् त्वमप्यस्माकमंतरीयस इत्यर्थः (*janebhyo hito viśeṣeṇa mitrebhyo hito dūta iva | sa yathā prajānāṃ cittavṛttijñānāya rājñā preritastāsāṃ mano jānāti tadvat tvamapyasmākamaṃtarīyasa ityarthaḥ*). Agni is praised as being like a messenger who is a well-wisher of people, and especially of those who are friends. How? In the world a messenger sent by the king, moving among the people to learn their minds, learns their wishes and reports them to the king; in the same way Agni too enters the minds of men and, like a well-wisher, makes their heart's desire known to the gods to whom it is directed: this is the intention.

#### Ṛk 8

**॥ संहितापाठः ॥**

> स वि॒द्वा आ च॑ पिप्रयो॒ यक्षि॑ चिकित्सॆ आनु॒षक् ।
> आ चा॒स्मिन्त्स॑त्सि ब॒र्हिषि॑ ॥ ८ ॥

**॥ पदपाठः ॥**

> सः । विद्वान् । आ । च । पिप्रयः । यक्षि । चिकित्वः । आनुषक् ।
> आ । च । अस्मिन् । सत्सि । बर्हिषि ॥ ८ ॥

**॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥**

> हे अग्ने स त्वं विद्वान् विशेषाभिज्ञः सन् आ च पिप्रयः । सर्वतोऽस्मान्सू-
> रय च कामैः । हे चिकित्सश्चेतनावन् आनुषक् अनुक्रमेण यक्षि । यज देवान् ।
> आसत्सि । आसीद चास्मिन् स्त्रीणे बर्हिषि ॥

*Gist:* Agni, knowing, satisfy us on every side, worship the gods in order, and sit on this spread sacred grass.

**प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)**

सः (*saḥ*)—(O Agni) you. विद्वान् (*vidvān*)—knowing everything. आ च (*ā ca*)—in every way. पिप्रयः (*piprayaḥ*)—fulfil our desired aims. चिकित्स्यः (*cikitsyaḥ*)—you, who are wise. आनुषक् (*ānuṣak*)—in order. यक्षि (*yakṣi*)—worship (the gods). अस्मिन् (*asmin*)—spread here. बर्हिषि च (*barhiṣi ca*)—and on the seat of darbha grass. आ सत्सि (*ā satsi*)—sit down.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

O Agni, you who know all, fulfil our desired aims in every way, and worship the gods in order. Sit down also on the darbha-grass seat spread here.

**Printed English**

Agni, you are all-wise; fulfil(our desires): you, who are intelligent, worship( the gods) in order; and sit down on this sacred grass.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

आनुषक् (*ānuṣak*)—the meaning is "in order". For this word Yāska has said: आनुषगिति नामानुपूर्वस्यानुषक्तं भवति । स्तृणंति बर्हिरानुषक् इत्यपि निगमो भवति (*ānuṣagiti nāmānupūrvasyānuṣaktaṃ bhavati | stṛṇaṃti barhirānuṣak ityapi nigamo bhavati*). That is, he says that आनुषक् (*ānuṣak*) is a synonym of the words अनुपूर्व (*anupūrva*) and अनुक्रम (*anukrama*), which give the sense "one after another", and he has given the example स्तृणंति बर्हिरानुषक् (*stṛṇaṃti barhirānuṣak*):

आ घा ये अग्निमिंधते स्त्रणंति बर्हिरानुषक् । येषामिंद्रो युवा सखा ॥ (*ā ghā ye agnimiṃdhate straṇaṃti barhirānuṣak | yeṣāmiṃdro yuvā sakhā ‖*) (Mi. Saṃ. 8-45-1) [abbreviation as supplied]

In this example it is said: first they kindle the fire; afterwards they spread the darbha grass called barhis as a seat. Here kindling the fire is one act; spreading the darbha grass is another act. After the first act, kindling the fire, the second act, spreading the darbha grass, is performed. When acts are done in this way, one after another in order, the word आनुषक् (*ānuṣak*) is used to indicate that order. In the present Ṛk also there are three acts of Agni: fulfilling our desired aims, worshipping the gods, and sitting on the darbha grass. The word आनुषक् (*ānuṣak*) is used here so that they be done knowing the order.

*The seventh sūkta.*

## Sūkta 7 (Maṇḍala 2)

**Sāyaṇa's introduction to the sūkta** (Sanskrit, transcribed; see the Anuvāda below for the sense):

> श्रेष्ठं यविष्टेति षडृचं सप्तमं सूक्तं सोमाहुत्यार्षमाग्नेयं
> गायत्रं ॥ प्रातरनुवाकाश्विनशस्त्रयोर्गायत्रे छंदस्यस्य विनियोगः पूर्वसूकु
> एवोक्तः ॥ श्रवणाकर्मण्याद्या विनियुक्ता ॥

*Gist:* *śreṣṭhaṃ yaviṣṭha* is the seventh sūkta, six ṛks, Gāyatrī, deity Agni, seer Somāhuti Bhārgava; used in the Prātaranuvāka and Āśvina-śastra; Ṛk 1 in the Śravaṇākarman.

**Anuvāda (Kannada)**

This sūkta *śreṣṭhaṃ yaviṣṭha* is the seventh sūkta in the first anuvāka. It has six ṛks. The seer of this sūkta is Somāhuti Bhārgava; the deity is Agni; the metre is Gāyatrī. It was said earlier (in the introduction of the preceding sūkta itself) that this sūkta is applied to the Gāyatrī ṛks at the time of reciting the Prātaranuvāka and the Āśvina-śastra mantras. The first Ṛk of this sūkta is applied in the Śravaṇākarman.

**॥ संहितापाठः ॥**
