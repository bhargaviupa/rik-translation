*Gist:* When the sister vessels (ladles) of the altar-earth, filled with ghee, stand near, Agni the Adhvaryu rejoices at their arrival as barley rejoices at rain.

**प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)**

यदि (*yadi*)—when. मातु: (*mātu:*)—of the earth, which is the maker of the world and has the form of the altar. स्वसा (*svasā*)—the ladle vessels (Juhū) that are sisters to her. धृतं (*dhṛtaṃ*)—clarified butter (ājya). भरंति (*bharaṃti*)—filling themselves with. उप अस्थित (*upa asthita*)—are placed near the fire (then),

तासां (*tāsāṃ*)—of those ladle vessels. आगतौ (*āgatau*)—at the time of arrival. अध्वर्युः (*adhvaryuḥ*)—Agni, who desires the sacrifice or who is named Adhvaryu. यवः (*yavaḥ*)—the barley grain. वृष्टीव (*vṛṣṭīva*)—as if indicating joy at the time of the coming of rain. मोदते (*modate*)—rejoices.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

When the ladle vessels, which are sisters to the earth in the form of the altar, filled with ghee, come near Agni, then Agni, who desires the sacrifice, rejoices greatly, as barley shows its joy at the time of the coming of rain.

**Printed English translation**

When the sister,(vessel), bearing the clarified butter, is placed near the maternal(altar), the Adhwaryu(Agni) rejoices at their approach, like barley(at the fall of) rain.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

In this Ṛk the words मातु: स्वसा (*mātu: svasā*) have no clearly stated meaning, but the commentator has fitted in the meanings "altar" and "Juhū" according to the context and explained the sense. For the word *mātṛ* he gives the various meanings mother, maker, altar, world, earth, according to context. Here he gives the meaning "sacrificial altar", and for the word स्वसा (*svasā*) he explains "Juhū, the wooden ladle for offering ājya, which is like a sister to the altar". Although the sense fits well with this kind of explanation, there remains doubt whether these words are in fact used in these senses.

अध्वर्युः धृतं भरंती आगतौ यवः वृष्टीव मोदते (*adhvaryuḥ dhṛtaṃ bharaṃtī āgatau yavaḥ vṛṣṭīva modate*)—the comparison stated in this illustration fits well. When the priest called Adhvaryu fills the offering ladles with ājya (ghee) in order to offer it in the fire and comes near the fire, the joy that arises in Agni is described: as the barley plants welcome with great joy the rain that they need, so Agni too becomes joyful.

**प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)**

(अयमग्निः (*ayamagniḥ*)—this Agni) स्वः (*svaḥ*)—himself. स्वाय (*svāya*)—his own. धायसे (*dhāyase*)—for the work, or for holding the oblations. ऋत्विक् (*ṛtvik*)—in the form of the priests, the Hotṛ and others, described before. ऋत्विजं (*ṛtvijaṃ*)—the work to be performed by the Hotṛ and others. कृणुतां (*kṛṇutāṃ*)—let him perform. वयं (*vayaṃ*)—we also. आत् (*āt*)—immediately after that. स्तोमं (*stomaṃ*)—the praise. यज्ञं च (*yajñaṃ ca*)—and the sacrifice that depends on it. अरं (*araṃ*)—fully. वनेम (*vanema*)—we worship. ररिम (*rarima*)—we also offer the oblation.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

Let this Agni himself, in the form of the priest, perform for his own work the work to be performed by the Hotṛ and others. Immediately after that we too fully worship the praise and the sacrifice that depends on it (we perform them), and we also offer the oblation.

**Printed English translation**

May he, the ministering priest, discharge the office of priest at his own ceremony, and may we worthily repeat(his) praise, and offer(him) sacrifice.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

सः ऋत्विक् ऋत्विजं कृणुतां (*saḥ ṛtvik ṛtvijaṃ kṛṇutāṃ*)—in this sacrifice which Agni himself performs, the wish is expressed that, as said before, he himself discharge the duties of the many priests who take part in the sacrifice, the Hotṛ, Potṛ, Praśāstṛ and others, and bring the sacrifice to completion. The use of the words ऋत्विक् ऋत्विजं (*ṛtvik ṛtvijaṃ*) here indicates all the priests, the Hotṛ and the rest. That is, Agni himself (in the form of each respective priest) performs the rites that all of them perform; the meaning is that Agni, who has the form of a divine priest, performs the rites that the human priests perform.

स्तोमं यज्ञं चादरं वनेमा ररिमा वयं (*stomaṃ yajñaṃ cādaraṃ vanemā rarimā vayaṃ*)—if, as said above, Agni himself performs the sacrificial rites of all the priests, what is left for us to do? The answer: we too recite hymns and the like in the sacrifice and praise the gods, and offer oblations and the like; thus the acts to be performed by us (the sacrificers and others) are also explained.

## Sūkta 6 (Maṇḍala 2)

*The sixth sūkta.*

**Sāyaṇa's introduction to the sūkta** (Sanskrit, transcribed; see the Anuvāda below for the sense):

> इमां म इत्यष्टर्चं षष्ठं सूक्तं सोमाहुत्यार्षमाग्नेयं गायत्रं ।
> इमां मे गायत्रं हीत्यनुक्रांतं ॥ प्रातरनुवाकाश्विनशस्त्रयोगायत्रे छंदस्यस्य
> विनियोगः । सूत्रितं च । इमां मे अग्ने समिधमिमामिति त्रयाणामुत्तमामुद्ध-
> रेत् । आ. ४-१३ । इति ॥ अपराह्नि क्यामुपसदीमां मे अग्ने समिधमिति तिस्र ऋच-
> स्त्रिरभ्यस्ता नव सामिधेन्यः कृत्वा विनियोक्तव्याः । अथोपसदिति खंडे सूत्रितं । इमां
> मे अग्ने समिधमिमामिति तु सामिधेन्यः । आ- ४-८ । इति ॥

*Gist:* *imāṃ me* is the sixth sūkta, of eight ṛks, Gāyatrī metre, deity Agni, seer Somāhuti Bhārgava; used in the Prātaranuvāka and Āśvina-śastra, and as Sāmidhenī verses.

**Anuvāda (Kannada)**

This sūkta *imāṃ me* is the sixth sūkta in the first anuvāka. It has eight ṛks. The seer is the same Somāhuti Bhārgava who was the seer of the preceding sūkta; the deity is Agni; the metre is Gāyatrī. In the Anukramaṇī it is stated "*imāṃ me gāyatraṃ hi*". It is explained by the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra rule इमां मे समिधमिमामिति त्रयाणामुत्तमामुद्धरेत् (*imāṃ me samidhamimāmiti trayāṇāmuttamāmuddharet*) (Āś. 4-13) that this sūkta is applied to the Gāyatrī ṛks at the time of reciting the Prātaranuvāka and the mantras of the Āśvina-śastra. Also, in the Upasad sacrifice performed in the afternoon, the three ṛks of this sūkta beginning इमां मे अग्ने समिधं (*imāṃ me agne samidhaṃ*), each recited three times, are to be used as nine Sāmidhenī ṛks; this is explained by the rule इमां मे अग्ने समिधमिमामिति तु सामिधेन्यः (*imāṃ me agne samidhamimāmiti tu sāmidhenyaḥ*) (Āś. 4-8) in the section called Athopasad of the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra.

Metre: Gāyatrī (printed छंद‘ (*chaṃda‘*) गायत्री (*gāyatrī*)).

as a homa, these are the small dry twigs fallen beneath sacrificially suitable trees such as the aśvattha (peepal). They are thinner than a little finger and four to six inches long. **Printed English**

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

सूक्तेन (*sūktena*)—सुष्टु उक्तेन (*suṣṭu uktena*): well recited, that is, a hymn or Ṛk that praises the qualities of the gods well. In the Ṛgveda a group of many such Ṛks is called a sūkta. In sum, sūkta means a hymn of praise.

ऊर्जो नपात् (*ūrjo napāt*)—बलस्य नप्तः (*balasya naptaḥ*): when fire is churned out of the araṇi sticks, Agni arises only by churning with great force; therefore Agni is addressed with the words सहसस्पुत्र (*sahasasputra*) [as supplied] and ऊर्जोनपात् (*ūrjonapāt*).

अश्वमिष्टे (*aśvamiṣṭe*)—व्यापकयज्ञ (*vyāpakayajña*): one who pervades the sacrifice. If the word अश्व (*aśva*) is understood as derived from the root अशू व्याप्तौ (*aśū vyāptau*), the above meaning results. Or आशु क्रियामणं कर्म अश्वं (*āśu kriyāmaṇaṃ karma aśvaṃ*) [as supplied]: the word अश्वं (*aśvaṃ*), formed from अशु (*aśu*) meaning "quickly", also means a sacrifice performed quickly. Here both meanings are possible.

अया (*ayā*)—आनया (*ānayā*) [as supplied]: for the convenience of the metre the na of अनया (*anayā*) is dropped, giving आया (*āyā*). Likewise the a of आत्मना (*ātmanā*) is dropped and the form त्मना (*tmanā*) is used in many places. Such usages are numerous in the Ṛgveda.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

मघवा (*maghavā*)—the word मघ (*magha*) is read among the names of wealth (Nirukta 3-9). There is also the explanation मघमिति धननामधेयं महंतेर्दानकर्मणः (*maghamiti dhananāmadheyaṃ mahaṃterdānakarmaṇaḥ*) (Nirukta 1-7), which indicates the act of giving. सूरिः (*sūriḥ*)—the word सूरि (*sūri*) is read among the names of praisers (Nirukta 2-19). This word has the meanings intelligent, wise, clever, inspirer, and so on.

The commentator has given as an example, indicating this same sense, the Ṛk स होता सेदु दूत्यं चिकित्त्वा अंतरीयते (*sa hotā sedu dūtyaṃ cikittvā aṃtarīyate*) (Ma. Saṃ. 4-8-4) [reference as supplied]. But in the commentary on that Ṛk the idea that he enters the hearts of men and knows what is in their minds is not indicated. Instead, for the words अंतः ईयते (*aṃtaḥ īyate*) he explains the sense अंतर्द्यावापृथिव्योर्मध्य ईयते गच्छति (*aṃtardyāvāpṛthivyormadhya īyate gacchati*), that is, he goes (moves about) between heaven and earth (earth and atmosphere). Therefore the Ṛk given here as an example does not establish the meaning of the present Ṛk.

जन्म उभया विद्वान् (*janma ubhayā vidvān*)—उभयविधानि जन्मानि यष्टणां यजमानानां यष्टव्यानां देवानां च संबंधीनि च जानन् (*ubhayavidhāni janmāni yaṣṭaṇāṃ yajamānānāṃ yaṣṭavyānāṃ devānāṃ ca saṃbaṃdhīni ca jānan*)—here Agni is described as knowing the two kinds of births. The commentator explains this as knowing the birth, or order of origin, of the sacrificing worshippers and of the gods who are worshipped in the sacrifice.

दूतो जन्मेव मित्रः (*dūto janmeva mitraḥ*)—here an illustration is given. जनेभ्यो हितो विशेषेण मित्रेभ्यो हितो दूत इव । स यथा प्रजानां चित्तवृत्तिज्ञानाय राज्ञा प्रेरितस्तासां मनो जानाति तद्वत् त्वमप्यस्माकमंतरीयस इत्यर्थः (*janebhyo hito viśeṣeṇa mitrebhyo hito dūta iva | sa yathā prajānāṃ cittavṛttijñānāya rājñā preritastāsāṃ mano jānāti tadvat tvamapyasmākamaṃtarīyasa ityarthaḥ*). Agni is praised as being like a messenger who is a well-wisher of people, and especially of those who are friends. How? In the world a messenger sent by the king, moving among the people to learn their minds, learns their wishes and reports them to the king; in the same way Agni too enters the minds of men and, like a well-wisher, makes their heart's desire known to the gods to whom it is directed: this is the intention.

**प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)**

सः (*saḥ*)—(O Agni) you. विद्वान् (*vidvān*)—knowing everything. आ च (*ā ca*)—in every way. पिप्रयः (*piprayaḥ*)—fulfil our desired aims. चिकित्स्यः (*cikitsyaḥ*)—you, who are wise. आनुषक् (*ānuṣak*)—in order. यक्षि (*yakṣi*)—worship (the gods). अस्मिन् (*asmin*)—spread here. बर्हिषि च (*barhiṣi ca*)—and on the seat of darbha grass. आ सत्सि (*ā satsi*)—sit down.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

O Agni, you who know all, fulfil our desired aims in every way, and worship the gods in order. Sit down also on the darbha-grass seat spread here.

**Printed English**

Agni, you are all-wise; fulfil(our desires): you, who are intelligent, worship( the gods) in order; and sit down on this sacred grass.

### Printed p. 517

*Running head: Aṣṭaka 2, Adhyāya 5, Varga 28, Ṛgveda-saṃhitā, p. 517.*

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

आनुषक् (*ānuṣak*)—the meaning is "in order". For this word Yāska has said: आनुषगिति नामानुपूर्वस्यानुषक्तं भवति । स्तृणंति बर्हिरानुषक् इत्यपि निगमो भवति (*ānuṣagiti nāmānupūrvasyānuṣaktaṃ bhavati | stṛṇaṃti barhirānuṣak ityapi nigamo bhavati*). That is, he says that आनुषक् (*ānuṣak*) is a synonym of the words अनुपूर्व (*anupūrva*) and अनुक्रम (*anukrama*), which give the sense "one after another", and he has given the example स्तृणंति बर्हिरानुषक् (*stṛṇaṃti barhirānuṣak*):

आ घा ये अग्निमिंधते स्त्रणंति बर्हिरानुषक् । येषामिंद्रो युवा सखा ॥ (*ā ghā ye agnimiṃdhate straṇaṃti barhirānuṣak | yeṣāmiṃdro yuvā sakhā ‖*) (Mi. Saṃ. 8-45-1) [abbreviation as supplied]

In this example it is said: first they kindle the fire; afterwards they spread the darbha grass called barhis as a seat. Here kindling the fire is one act; spreading the darbha grass is another act. After the first act, kindling the fire, the second act, spreading the darbha grass, is performed. When acts are done in this way, one after another in order, the word आनुषक् (*ānuṣak*) is used to indicate that order. In the present Ṛk also there are three acts of Agni: fulfilling our desired aims, worshipping the gods, and sitting on the darbha grass. The word आनुषक् (*ānuṣak*) is used here so that they be done knowing the order.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

O Agni, most youthful, kinsman of the priests, giver of a dwelling: bring for us wealth that is most praiseworthy, brilliant and desired by many.

**Printed English translation**

Youngest(of the gods), Agni, descendant of Bharata, granter of dwellings, bring(to us) excellent, splendid, and enviable riches.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

भारत (भारत (*bhārata*)) — The word भारत (*bhārata*) is read among the names of priests (Ni. 3-18). भारत (*bhārata*) means a priest (ṛtvij). The word is derived from the root भृञ् भरणे (*bhṛñ bharaṇe*) and, according to Skandasvāmin's explanation, यज्ञद्वारेण नृन् संभरतीति (*yajñadvāreṇa nṛn saṃbharatīti*) — he has the sacrifice performed and by that means lifts up men (the sacrificers); therefore this priest is called Bhārata. Durgācārya, the commentator on the Nirukta, explains भ्रियंते दक्षिणाभिस्ते इति भारताः (*bhriyaṃte dakṣiṇābhiste iti bhāratāḥ*): since they cause the sacrifice to be performed and obtain satisfaction through the fees (dakṣiṇā) and the like, these priests are called Bhāratas. Thus the word *bharata* has two kinds of etymological sense.

*Gist:* Let no enemy, of gods or of men, prevail over us; "pūraya" — protect us from that twofold hatred.

**प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)**

(O Agni) देवस्य (*devasya*) — one belonging to the company of the gods, or मर्त्यस्य च (*martyasya ca*) — belonging to the company of men; आरातिः (*ārātiḥ*) — the enemy; नः (*naḥ*) — us; मा ईशत (*mā īśata*) — let him not be able to withstand and defeat; उत (*uta*) — and; तस्याः (*tasyāḥ*) — of those two kinds; द्विषः (*dviṣaḥ*) — from enemies; पर्षि (*parṣi*) — protect us.

**Printed English translation**

Let no enemy prevail against us, whether of god or man; protect us from both such foes.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

अरातिः (*arātiḥ*) — The bhāṣya says: अरातिरदानं । यद्वा छंदस्यरातिशब्दः शत्रुवाची स्त्रीलिंगो भवति । (*arātiradānaṃ | yadvā chaṃdasyarātiśabdaḥ śatruvācī strīliṃgo bhavati |*) That is, a man who gives nothing to anyone, or who does not give oblations and the like to the gods, is called *arāti*. Or, in the Veda the word *arāti* is used especially in the sense of "enemy"; then the word *arāti* is feminine, as the commentator explains. Yāska in the Nirukta gives two kinds of meaning: अरातयोऽदानकर्माणः (*arātayo 'dānakarmāṇaḥ*) — those who do not give are *arātis* (Ni. 3-11); or अरातीः—अमित्रानदानानिति वा (*arātīḥ—amitrānadānāniti vā*) — that is, enemies who are not friends, or those who do not give (Ni. 11-1).

देवस्य (*devasya*) — For the word *deva* Yāska has given the explanation देवो दानाद्वा, दीपनाद्वा, द्योतनाद्वा, द्युस्थानो भवतीति वा (*devo dānādvā, dīpanādvā, dyotanādvā, dyusthāno bhavatīti vā*): he who gives, who shines, who is radiant with brilliance, or who dwells in heaven; therefore he is called *deva* (Ni. 7-15).

मर्त्यस्य (*martyasya*) — This word is derived from the root मृज् प्राणत्यागे (*mṛj prāṇatyāge*); म्रियंत इति मर्त्याः (*mriyaṃta iti martyāḥ*) — those who die are *martya*, so men are called *martya*. The gods are said to be *amartya*. In the Ṛgveda the use of the words *martya* (*marta*) and *amartya* is frequent.

**॥ संहितापाठः ॥**

O Agni, as (people) cross without effort small brooks or streams flowing with a little water, so may we also, by your favour, obtain the power to defeat all our enemies.

Let us, through your favour, overwhelm all our foes, like torrents of water.

धारा उदन्या इव (*dhārā udanyā iva*) — Rivers flowing deep and swift with abundant water cannot easily be crossed by people. But a small stream with only a little water, flowing gently, people pass over without effort. Likewise, the prayer is expressed here that we may defeat our enemies without effort.

भारत (*bhārata*) — The word भारत (*bhārata*) is read among the names of priests (Ni. 3-19). Here the commentator explains the word भारत (*bhārata*) as ऋत्विजां पुत्रस्थानीय (*ṛtvijāṃ putrasthānīya*), "O Agni, who are like a son to the priests". वशाभिः उक्षभिः अष्टापदीभिः आहुतः (*vaśābhiḥ ukṣabhiḥ aṣṭāpadībhiḥ āhutaḥ*) — वशाभिर्वंध्याभिर्गोभिः उक्षभिः सेक्तृभि-र्बलिवर्द्यैः अष्टापदीभिर्गर्भिणीभिश्चाहुतः (*vaśābhirvaṃdhyābhirgobhiḥ ukṣabhiḥ sektṛbhi-rbalivardyaiḥ aṣṭāpadībhirgarbhiṇībhiścāhutaḥ*). Sāyaṇa explains: O Agni, you are worshipped by the barren cows we offer you, by bulls capable of impregnating, and by cows with young. Although animals are of these three kinds, the offering of cows as oblations into the fire is nowhere found. Or the words वशाभिः (*vaśābhiḥ*) and the rest need not mean cows; other animals fit for sacrifice, such as goats, must be what is meant here. The word अष्टापदीभिः (*aṣṭāpadībhiḥ*) means an animal with eight feet: the four natural feet of a pregnant animal and the four feet of the calf in the womb together make eight. Therefore the word अष्टापडीभिः (*aṣṭāpaḍībhiḥ*) (as printed; the earlier form has अष्टापदीभिः (*aṣṭāpadībhiḥ*)) is used to designate a female animal with young.

योगान् रथान् (*yogān rathān*)—this can be understood either as "praise the horses and the chariots" or as "praise the chariots to which horses are yoked".

वाजयन्निव (*vājayanniva*)—the word वाजः (*vājaḥ*) is read among the names for food (Ni. 3-9) and among the names for battle (वाजे (*vāje*)) (Ni. 3-9). Here the meaning "food" is taken, and the illustration is given of a man who desires food.

नु (*nu*)—नु इत्येषोऽनेककर्मा । इदं करिष्यतीति हेत्वपदेशे । कथं नु करिष्यतीत्यनुपृष्टे (*nu ityeṣo 'nekakarmā | idaṃ kariṣyatīti hetvapadeśe | kathaṃ nu kariṣyatītyanupṛṣṭe*) (Ni. 1-4). The particle नु (*nu*) has many meanings. In इदं नु करिष्यति (*idaṃ nu kariṣyati*) the word नु (*nu*) has the sense of a reason ("therefore"); in कथं नु करिष्यति (*kathaṃ nu kariṣyati*) it is interrogative. Likewise अथाप्युपमार्थे भवति (*athāpyupamārthe bhavati*)—the word नु (*nu*) is also used when a comparison is indicated. For example:

अक्षो न चक्र्योः शूर बृहन्प्रते मह्ना रिरिचे रोदस्योः । वृक्षस्य नु ते पुरुहूत वया व्यू ३ तयो रुरुहुरिंद्र पूर्विः ॥ (*akṣo na cakryoḥ śūra bṛhanprate mahnā ririce rodasyoḥ | vṛkṣasya nu te puruhūta vayā vyū 3 tayo ruruhuriṃdra pūrviḥ ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-24-3, as printed: "ಮ. ಸಂ.")

The Nirukta-writer has cited this Ṛk and explained नु (*nu*) in the sense of comparison: "like a tree, or like the branches of a tree" (वृक्षस्येव ते पुरुहूत-शाखाः (*vṛkṣasyeva te puruhūta-śākhāḥ*)). Likewise,

नू मर्तो दयते सनिष्यन्यो विष्णव उरुगायाय दाशत् । प्र यः सत्राचा मनस्सा यजात एतावंतं नर्यमाविवासात् ॥ (*nū marto dayate saniṣyanyo viṣṇava urugāyāya dāśat | pra yaḥ satrācā manassā yajāta etāvaṃtaṃ naryamāvivāsāt ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-100-1, as printed: "ಮ. ಸಂ.")

in Ṛks of this kind नु (*nu*) has the sense of क्षिप्रं (*kṣipraṃ*), "quickly"; and in

नू इंद्र शूर स्तवमान ऊती ब्रह्मजूतस्तन्वा नावृधस्व । उप नो वाजान्मिमाह्युप स्तीन्यूयं पात स्वस्तिभिः सदा नः ॥ (*nū iṃdra śūra stavamāna ūtī brahmajūtastanvā nāvṛdhasva | upa no vājānmimāhyupa stīnyūyaṃ pāta svastibhiḥ sadā naḥ ‖*)

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-19-11)

and similar Ṛks the sense "now, at once" (अद्य (*adya*)). And, as the Nirukta-writer says, नु इत्येषोऽनेककर्मा (*nu ityeṣo 'nekakarmā*) (Ni. 1-4), the word नु (*nu*) is used in still other senses. In the present Ṛk the meaning is "quickly, soon".

**Printed English translation**

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

स्वः न भानुना (*svaḥ na bhānunā*)—the meaning is "as the sun shines with his rays". The word स्वः (*svaḥ*) is read among the common names (sādhāraṇa-nāmāni), स्वः पृश्निः नाकः (*svaḥ pṛśniḥ nākaḥ*) and so on. साधारणान्युत्तराणि षट् दिवश्चादित्यस्य च (*sādhāraṇānyuttarāṇi ṣaṭ divaścādityasya ca*) (Ni. 2-13): that is, the six words स्वः पृश्निः नाकः गौः विष्टपं नभः (*svaḥ pṛśniḥ nākaḥ gauḥ viṣṭapaṃ nabhaḥ*) are common designations, both as synonyms of the heavenly world and as referring to the sun; so the Nirukta-kāra states. Then, explaining what the root of the word स्वः (*svaḥ*) is in the sense of the sun, and how the word is derived, he says:

स्वरादित्यो भवति सु अरणः सु ईरणः स्वृतो रसान् स्वृतो भासं ज्योतिषां । स्वृतो भासेति वा (*svarādityo bhavati su araṇaḥ su īraṇaḥ svṛto rasān svṛto bhāsaṃ jyotiṣāṃ | svṛto bhāseti vā*) (Ni. 2-14).

The Nirukta-kāra states that the sun has this name because he is very far-travelling, or because he thoroughly scatters and destroys darkness, or because he enters into the juice (rasa) and becomes inward with it, or because he spreads his light in the orb of radiance and becomes one with it, or because he pervades the universe in the form of light; and for this very reason (एतेन द्यौर्वाख्याता (*etena dyaurvākhyātā*)) the heavenly world also has the name स्वः (*svaḥ*).

स्वरादित्यो भवति स एनानि सारयति (*svarādityo bhavati sa enāni sārayati*) (Ni. 5-4).

Because he makes the days move, too, the sun is called स्वः (*svaḥ*). Where the word is used in the Ṛgveda:

प्रत्यज् देवानां विशः प्रत्यज्जुदेषि मानुषान् । प्रत्यज् विश्वं स्वर्दृशे (*pratyaj devānāṃ viśaḥ pratyajjudeṣi mānuṣān | pratyaj viśvaṃ svardṛśe*) (Ma. Saṃ. 1-50-5) [as printed]

in such Ṛks the word स्वः (*svaḥ*) means the heavenly world (svarga).

त्मना वहंतो दुरो व्यृण्वन्नवंत विश्वे स्व १ र्दृशीके (*tmanā vahaṃto duro vyṛṇvannavaṃta viśve sva 1 rdṛśīke*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-69-10)

in such Ṛks the word स्वः (*svaḥ*) means "in the sky, in the atmosphere" (नभसि (*nabhasi*)).

दानो अग्ने बृहतो दाः सहस्रिणो दुरो न वाजं श्रुत्या अपा वृधि । प्राची द्यावापृथिवी ब्रह्मणा कृधि स्व १ र्ण शुक्रमुषसो वि दिम्युतुः (*dāno agne bṛhato dāḥ sahasriṇo duro na vājaṃ śrutyā apā vṛdhi | prācī dyāvāpṛthivī brahmaṇā kṛdhi sva 1 rṇa śukramuṣaso vi dimyutuḥ*) (Pu. Saṃ. 2-2-7) [as printed]

in such Ṛks the word स्वः (*svaḥ*) has been explained as the sun. In the same way the words स्वर्वत् (*svarvat*) and the like, formed with suffixes such as वत् (*vat*), are also to be explained according to the context. For example:

या इंद्र भुज आभरः स्वर्ना असुरेभ्यः । स्तोतारमिन्मघवन्नस्य वर्धय ये च त्वे व्यक्तबर्हिषः (*yā iṃdra bhuja ābharaḥ svarnā asurebhyaḥ | stotāraminmaghavannasya vardhaya ye ca tve vyaktabarhiṣaḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-97-1)

In this Ṛk—सुखवान्, स्वर्गवान् ना अथवा स्वशब्दः सर्वपर्यायः । सर्वं भूतजातं आत्मन एवोत्पन्नत्वात्तद्वान् (*sukhavān, svargavān nā athavā svaśabdaḥ sarvaparyāyaḥ | sarvaṃ bhūtajātaṃ ātmana evotpannatvāttadvān*). Since the word स्वः (*svaḥ*) has the meanings happiness, heaven or "all", स्वर्वान् (*svarvān*) means one who has happiness, one who dwells in heaven, or one who has obtained everything. When it is an epithet of Indra, all these meanings fit; and again:

तं शिशीता स्वध्वरं सत्यं सत्वानमृत्वियं । उतो नु चिद्य ओहत आंडा शुष्णस्य भेदत्यजैः स्वर्वतीरपो नभंतामन्यके समे (*taṃ śiśītā svadhvaraṃ satyaṃ satvānamṛtviyaṃ | uto nu cidya ohata āṃḍā śuṣṇasya bhedatyajaiḥ svarvatīrapo nabhaṃtāmanyake same*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-40-11)

In such passages, when सर्वतीः अपः (*sarvatīḥ apaḥ*) [sic, as printed] is an epithet of water, दिव्यानि (*divyāni*) means water produced in the heavenly world. This epithet also describes the qualities of other deities such as Agni.

ज्योतिष्मतीमदितिं धारयत् क्षितिं स्वर्वतीमा सचेते दिवे दिवे जागृवांसा दिवेदिवे (*jyotiṣmatīmaditiṃ dhārayat kṣitiṃ svarvatīmā sacete dive dive jāgṛvāṃsā divedive*) (Ma. Saṃ. 1-136-3) [as printed]

Here स्वर्णती (*svarṇatī*) [as printed] is an epithet of Aditi. Here it is explained as यज्ञनिष्पत्तिद्दारा स्वर्गवतीं (*yajñaniṣpattiddārā svargavatīṃ*) [as printed], as if so interpreted.

आ ते शुष्मो वृषभ एतु पश्चादोत्तरादधराद पुरस्तात् । आ विश्वतो अभि समेत्वर्वाजिंद्र द्युम्नं स्वर्वद्धेह्यस्मे (*ā te śuṣmo vṛṣabha etu paścādottarādadharāda purastāt | ā viśvato abhi sametvarvājiṃdra dyumnaṃ svarvaddhehyasme*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-19-9)

In such Ṛks the meaning सुखयुक्तं धनं (*sukhayuktaṃ dhanaṃ*) ("wealth accompanied by happiness") is also indicated. In the present Ṛk the meaning is the sun.

भानुना (*bhānunā*)—since the word भानुः (*bhānuḥ*) is read among the twelve names of day (ahar-nāmāni), वस्तोः द्यौः (*vastoḥ dyauḥ*) and so on, it means the shining day. भानुः भासते हैतत् ज्योतिषा (*bhānuḥ bhāsate haitat jyotiṣā*): because it shines by its own light, it is explained here as the rays of the sun.

अत्रिं (*atriṃ*)—For this word, formed from the root अद भक्षणे (*ada bhakṣaṇe*) ("ad", to eat), the commentator has given two kinds of meaning. अत्रिं (*atriṃ*)—one who eats (burns) and destroys enemies is अत्रिः (*atriḥ*): because he devours, or burns up and destroys, enemies he is called Atri; or

according to the Śruti sentence अद्यत्तेऽत्ति च भूतानि । तस्मादन्नं तदुच्यत (*adyatte 'tti ca bhūtāni | tasmādannaṃ taducyata*) ("it is eaten and it eats beings; therefore it is called food"), a thing fit to be eaten is called food. Because he eats (consumes) such food, or the food in the form of the oblation, he is called Atri: thus two kinds of meaning are stated. Hence the sense is that Agni is the destroyer of enemies, or the eater of food in the form of the oblation.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

In this ṛk the seer Gṛtsamada praises Agni, describing him with various epithets.

होता (*hotā*)—the one who invokes the gods, that is, Agni, who brings the gods invited to the sacrifice to the sacrificial altar. The word होतृ (*hotṛ*) is used in the sacrifice in the sense of human hotṛ priests, in the sense of Agni who accomplishes the oblation, that is, performs the homa, and in the sense of Agni who invokes the gods. Here it is said to mean Agni who invokes the gods.

त्वेषः (*tveṣaḥ*)—the grown, that is, Agni blazing up with fuel, wood and the like and burning fiercely. When fire is churned in the two fire-sticks it is very small, and is therefore said to be in the form of a child. The same Agni, when set up in the āhavanīya and other fires and burning large with wood and the like and with oblations of ghee, is said to be grown. The word त्वेषः (*tveṣaḥ*) is formed from the root त्विष् (*tviṣ*) (to shine) and indicates one who shines resplendently.

सुदक्षः (*sudakṣaḥ*)—the word दक्षः (*dakṣaḥ*) is read [text: परित (*parita*), as supplied] among the twenty-eight names of strength such as ओजः (*ojaḥ*) and पाजः (*pājaḥ*) (Ni. 3-9); so सुदक्षः (*sudakṣaḥ*) means one of very great strength.

अदब्धव्रतप्रमतिः (*adabdhavratapramatiḥ*)—अदब्बान्यहिंसितानि व्रतानि कर्माणि यस्याः सादब्धव्रता प्रमतिः प्रकृष्टा बुद्धिर्यस्य स तथोक्तः (*adabbānyahiṃsitāni vratāni karmāṇi yasyāḥ sādabdhavratā pramatiḥ prakṛṣṭā buddhiryasya sa tathoktaḥ*). That is, the rites of Agni, namely the auxiliary acts of the sacrifice such as invoking the gods and carrying the oblation, are unharmed, that is, of a nature that does no harm to anyone. The sense is: Agni, who has the excellent knowledge that the rites he performs are of such a nature. Compound words of more than two members (compound words) are very rare in the Ṛgveda. In this word three members, अदब्ध (*adabdha*) + व्रत (*vrata*) + प्रमति (*pramati*), join to make one compound; such usages are very scarce.

वसिष्ठः (*vasiṣṭhaḥ*)—सर्वस्य वासयितृतमः (*sarvasya vāsayitṛtamaḥ*): the best among those who provide a dwelling-place for all; or one who has an excellent dwelling-place.

सहस्रंभरः (*sahasraṃbharaḥ*)—सहस्रस्यानेकविधस्य भर्ता । विहरणेन नानाविधरूपस्य धारक इत्यर्थः । यद्वा सहस्रस्य सर्वस्य भर्ता (*sahasrasyānekavidhasya bhartā | viharaṇena nānāvidharūpasya dhāraka ityarthaḥ | yadvā sahasrasya sarvasya bhartā*). Here the word सहस्र (*sahasra*) means "many"; Agni [the supplied text ends here, mid-sentence, at the foot of printed p. 540].

By the sentence जातवेदो निधीमहि (*jātavedo nidhīmahi*) the sense is indicated that we priests place Agni on the northern altar. अग्ने विश्वेभिः स्वनीक देवैरूर्णावंतं प्रथमः सीद योनिमिति विश्वैरेवैनं तद्देवैः सहासादयति । कुलायिनं धृतवंतं सवित्र इति कुलायमिव (*agne viśvebhiḥ svanīka devairūrṇāvaṃtaṃ prathamaḥ sīda yonimiti viśvairevainaṃ taddevaiḥ sahāsādayati | kulāyinaṃ dhṛtavaṃtaṃ savitra iti kulāyamiva*)
ह्येतदज्ञे क्रियते यत्पैतुदारवाः परिधयो गुग्गु लूर्णास्तुकाः सुगंधितेजनानीति यज्ञं नय यजमानाय साध्विति यज्ञमेव तदृजुधा प्रतिष्ठापयति ॥ (*hyetadajñe kriyate yatpaitudāravāḥ paridhayo guggu lūrṇāstukāḥ sugaṃdhitejanānīti yajñaṃ naya yajamānāya sādhviti yajñameva tadṛjudhā pratiṣṭhāpayati ‖*)

After that the hotṛ recites the fifth ṛk—

(5) अग्ने विश्वेभिः स्वनीक देवैरूर्णावंतं प्रथमः सीद योनिं । (*agne viśvebhiḥ svanīka devairūrṇāvaṃtaṃ prathamaḥ sīda yoniṃ |*)
कुलायिनं धृतवंतं सवित्रे यज्ञं नय यजमानाय साधु ॥ (*kulāyinaṃ dhṛtavaṃtaṃ savitre yajñaṃ naya yajamānāya sādhu ‖*)
(Mu. [sic as printed] Saṃ. 6-15-16)

The main sense of the first half of this ṛk: "O Agni, you who are endowed with armies in the form of flames, come, as the chief of all the gods, and sit in this navel-place called the northern altar." By the use of the word विश्वेभिः (*viśvebhiḥ*) here it is indicated that all the gods are established together with Agni; that is, the sense is that Agni, together with all the gods, will sit on the northern altar, which is like a bird's nest, made soft by spreading strands of wool. Being on the northern altar, the navel region, which is abundantly supplied with firewood and the like and sprinkled with ghee, ... the sacrificer's sacrificial rites (text breaks off at the end of this page)

### Printed p. 547

*Running head: Aṣṭaka 2, Adhyāya 6, Varga 1, Ṛgveda-saṃhitā, p. 547.*

... "perform well" is the sense of the second half. Here the words कुलायिनं धृतवंतं (*kulāyinaṃ dhṛtavaṃtaṃ*) in the third pāda suggest a bird's nest. That is, a bird builds its nest by weaving together small twigs of the khadira or devadāru tree, gum (resin), woollen threads, and a kind of fragrant grass. The navel-place of Agni is compared here to that. How? In building a nest the bird uses twigs; here the logs called paridhi are placed around Agni. Likewise, in place of gum, the fragrant substances such as ghee placed for the homa; in place of woollen threads, sacrificial animals such as the goat; in place of grass, the fuel-sticks (samidh) are prepared and placed around Agni. Thus the northern altar (the fire-pit) established for Agni is described by comparison with a bird's nest. The prayer expressed here is that Agni, established in the northern altar which is like such a bird's nest, may perform well the sacrificial acts of the sacrificer.

सीद होतः स्व उ लोके चिकित्वानित्यग्निर्वै देवानां होता तस्यैष स्वो लोको यदुत्तरवेदीनाभिः । सादया यज्ञं सुकृतस्य योनाविति (*sīda hotaḥ sva u loke cikitvānityagnirvai devānāṃ hotā tasyaiṣa svo loko yaduttaravedīnābhiḥ | sādayā yajñaṃ sukṛtasya yonāviti*)
यजमानो वै यज्ञो यजमानाय्नैवैतामाशिषमाशास्ते । देवावीर्देवान् हविषा यजास्यग्ने बृहद्यजमाने वयो धा इति प्राणो वै (*yajamāno vai yajño yajamānāynaivaitāmāśiṣamāśāste | devāvīrdevān haviṣā yajāsyagne bṛhadyajamāne vayo dhā iti prāṇo vai*)
वयः प्राणमेव तद्यजमाने दधाति ॥ (*vayaḥ prāṇameva tadyajamāne dadhāti ‖*)

Thereafter the hotṛ proceeds and, in this part of the rite, recites the sixth ṛk, which is to be said—

(6) सीद होतः स्व उ लोके चिकित्वान्तादया यज्ञं सुकृतस्य योन् । (*sīda hotaḥ sva u loke cikitvāntādayā yajñaṃ sukṛtasya yon |*)
देवावीर्देवान् हविषा यजास्यग्ने बृहद्यजमाने वयो धाः ॥ (*devāvīrdevān haviṣā yajāsyagne bṛhadyajamāne vayo dhāḥ ‖*)
(Ma. Saṃ. 3-29-8)

Agni is the priest called hotṛ of the gods; and the navel of the northern altar is Agni's own place. Therefore the prayer in the first pāda is: "O Agni, you are the accomplisher of the homa, the hotṛ of the gods; being such, sit in your own place, the northern altar." In the second pāda the word यज्ञ (*yajña*) means the sacrificer who performs the sacrifice. The meaning of the second pāda is: "O Agni, establish this sacrificer in the place of those who perform meritorious acts, the virtuous (sukṛta)." In the second half, the word वयः (*vayaḥ*) means life-breath (prāṇa). Therefore: "O Agni, dear to the gods, worship and satisfy the gods with the oblation, and bestow (that is, increase) life-breath, that is, length of life, in the sacrificer who performs the sacrifice." According to the Śruti sentence (यावदस्मिन् शरीरे प्राणो वसति तावदायुः (*yāvadasmin śarīre prāṇo vasati tāvadāyuḥ*), "as long as the life-breath dwells in this body, so long is there life"), as long as the life-breath

*Running head: Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya-sahitā, Maṇḍala 2, Anuvāka 1, Sūkta 9, p. 548.*

remains in the body a man has life; since this is said, the word prāṇa is used here in the sense of "life". The purport is: "Grant long life to the sacrificer."

नि होता होतृषदने विदान इत्यग्निर्वै देवानां होता तस्यैतद्दोतृषदनं यदुत्तरवेदीनाभिः । त्वेषो दीदिना असदत्सुदक्ष इत्यासन्नू हि (*ni hotā hotṛṣadane vidāna ityagnirvai devānāṃ hotā tasyaitaddotṛṣadanaṃ yaduttaravedīnābhiḥ | tveṣo dīdinā asadatsudakṣa ityāsannū hi*)
स तर्हि भवति । अदब्धव्रतप्रमतिर्वसिष्ठ इत्यग्निर्वै देवानां वसिष्ठः । सहस्रं (*sa tarhi bhavati | adabdhavratapramatirvasiṣṭha ityagnirvai devānāṃ vasiṣṭhaḥ | sahasraṃ*)
भरः शुचिजिह्वो अग्निरित्येषा ह वा अस्य सहस्रंभरता यदेनमेकं संतं (*bharaḥ śucijihvo agnirityeṣā ha vā asya sahasraṃbharatā yadenamekaṃ saṃtaṃ*)
बहुधा विहरंति प्र ह वै साहस्रं पोषमाप्नोति य एवं वेद ॥ (*bahudhā viharaṃti pra ha vai sāhasraṃ poṣamāpnoti ya evaṃ veda ‖*)

The hotṛ recites, as the seventh ṛk—

(7) नि होता होतृषदने विदानस्त्वेषो दीदिना असदत्सुदक्षः । (*ni hotā hotṛṣadane vidānastveṣo dīdinā asadatsudakṣaḥ |*)
अदब्धव्रतप्रमतिर्वसिष्ठः सहस्रंभरः शुचिजिह्वो अग्निः ॥ (*adabdhavratapramatirvasiṣṭhaḥ sahasraṃbharaḥ śucijihvo agniḥ ‖*)
(Ma. Saṃ. 2-9-1)

this mantra. Agni is the hotṛ of the gods. The place of the navel of the northern altar is the place called hotṛṣadana, where this divine hotṛ Agni sits: this is the meaning of the first pāda. The second pāda means that this Agni, who is radiant and skilled in sacrificial acts, is seated in this northern-altar navel called hotṛṣadana. Since Agni carries the oblation to the gods, he has obtained the best place in heaven, the gods' own abode. This Agni, whose mind is bent on unharmed sacrifices and the like, is one who has an excellent dwelling: this is the meaning of the third pāda. The meaning of the word सहस्रंभर (*sahasraṃbhara*) is that, although Agni appears in a single form, he is present in all, the priests and the rest, and performs the duty of each, and so bears many forms; or, that Agni, being in many sacrificial houses and fulfilling the sacrifices of many sacrificers, bears many forms. Because his tongue (flame) is purified by receiving the oblation and the like, Agni is called शुचिजिह्व (*śucijihva*). It is praised here that such an Agni gives the sacrificer nourishment by the thousand, in cattle, gold and the like.

त्वं दूतस्त्वमु नः परस्पा इत्युत्तमया परिदधाति त्वं वस्य आ वृषभ प्रणेता । (*tvaṃ dūtastvamu naḥ paraspā ityuttamayā paridadhāti tvaṃ vasya ā vṛṣabha praṇetā |*)
अग्ने तोकस्य नस्तने तनूनामप्रयुच्छन्विद्यद्बोधिं गोपा इति अग्निर्वै (*agne tokasya nastane tanūnāmaprayucchanvidyadbodhiṃ gopā iti agnirvai*)
देवानां गोपा अग्निमेव तत्सर्वतो गोप्तारं परिदत्त आत्मने च (*devānāṃ gopā agnimeva tatsarvato goptāraṃ paridatta ātmane ca*)
यजमानाय च यत्रैवं विद्वानेतया परिदधात्यथो संवत्सरीणामे- (*yajamānāya ca yatraivaṃ vidvānetayā paridadhātyatho saṃvatsarīṇāme-*)
नैतां स्वस्तिं कुरुते ॥ (*naitāṃ svastiṃ kurute ‖*)

Next the hotṛ recites the last, the eighth ṛk, and brings the Agni-praṇayana rite to its close—

(8) त्वं दूतस्त्वमु नः परस्पास्त्वं वस्य आ वृषभ प्रणेता । (*tvaṃ dūtastvamu naḥ paraspāstvaṃ vasya ā vṛṣabha praṇetā |*)
अग्ने तोकस्य नस्तने तनूनामप्रयुच्छन् दीद्यद्वोधि गोपाः ॥ (*agne tokasya nastane tanūnāmaprayucchan dīdyadvodhi gopāḥ ‖*)
(Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-9 [the reference is cut off in the supplied text]) (Ma. Saṃ. 9-2)

This is the last ṛk in the Agni-praṇayana rite. Generally the last ṛk of a rite is called the paridhāna (covering) of that rite. According to the Śruti sentence अग्निर्देवानां दूत आसीत् (*agnirdevānāṃ dūta āsīt*) ("Agni was the messenger of the gods"), the meaning of the first pāda is: "O Agni, you are the messenger of the gods; you are our protector." Having recited this, the hotṛ recites the mantra that marks the completion of the Agni-praṇayana rite. "O Agni, you are the best among gods or among men; you have your dwelling in all places; you are the impeller in many rites; watching over our sons, grandsons and the rest, and over ourselves, so that no mishap may befall, you protect us." This is the main sense of the ṛk. Agni is the protector even of the gods; he brings protection to us and to the sacrificer. The man who knows this obtains protection from Agni for a whole year and lives in well-being.

ता एता अष्टावन्वाह रूपसमृद्धा एतद्वै यज्ञस्य समृद्धं यद्रूपसमृद्धं (*tā etā aṣṭāvanvāha rūpasamṛddhā etadvai yajñasya samṛddhaṃ yadrūpasamṛddhaṃ*)
यॆत्कर्म क्रियामाणमृगभिवदति । तासां त्रिः प्रथमामन्वाह त्रिरुत्तमां (*yètkarma kriyāmāṇamṛgabhivadati | tāsāṃ triḥ prathamāmanvāha triruttamāṃ*)
ता द्वादश संपद्यंते द्वादश वै मासाः संवत्सरः संवत्सरः प्रजापतिः (*tā dvādaśa saṃpadyaṃte dvādaśa vai māsāḥ saṃvatsaraḥ saṃvatsaraḥ prajāpatiḥ*)
प्रजाप्रत्यायतनाभिरेवाभी राध्योति य एवं वेद त्रिः प्रथमां त्रिरुत्त- (*prajāpratyāyatanābhirevābhī rādhyoti ya evaṃ veda triḥ prathamāṃ trirutta-*)
मामन्वाह यज्ञस्यैव तद्बर्सो नह्यति स्थेन्ने बलायाविस्रंसाय ॥ (*māmanvāha yajñasyaiva tadbarso nahyati sthenne balāyāvisraṃsāya ‖*)
(Ai. Brā. 5-2)

In this way the hotṛ recites the eight ṛks stated above. All of these together form a rite that has obtained a complete form. By saying at the proper times mantras that describe and explain the meaning of the respective acts, the sacrificial act is helped to be completed in full. Of these eight mantras the hotṛ recites the first and the last ṛks three times each. Thereby there come to be twelve ṛks in all in this rite. Twelve months make one year. Prajāpati is of the nature of the year. Therefore whoever knows the nature and the order of these mantras, which are established in Prajāpati, will prosper. Because the hotṛ recites the first and the last ṛks three times each, it is as though the two ends of the sacrifice were tied firmly so that they do not slip and fall.

The Agni-praṇayana rite is concluded.

**Printed English translation**

Showerer(of benefits), Agni, be our messenger(to the gods); our preserver from evil; the conveyer to us of wealth; the protector of our sons and grandsons, and of our persons; understand(our prayer), ever heedful and resplendent.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

ದೂತಃ (*dūtaḥ*)—In accordance with the Śruti sentence अग्निर्देवानां दूत आसीत् (*agnirdevānāṃ dūta āsīt*) ("Agni was the messenger of the gods"), Agni is said to be the messenger between gods and men, because he performs such tasks as invoking the gods in the sacrificial rites of the human sacrificers, conveying to them the oblations offered in the fire, and presenting to the gods the hymns and prayers of the ṛtvij priests.

ಪರಸ್ಪಾಃ (*paraspāḥ*)—अस्माकं आपद्भ्यः पारयिता पाता च (*asmākaṃ āpadbhyaḥ pārayitā pātā ca*): one who rescues and protects us from calamities of many kinds and from fear of enemies.

ವೃಷಭ (*vṛṣabha*)—अभिमतफलवर्षक (*abhimataphalavarṣaka*): one who showers the desired objects on us like rain, that is, one who gives; this is the meaning.

ತೋಕಸ್ಯ, ತನೇ (*tokasya, tane*)—The word तोक (*toka*) is read among the fifteen words for offspring (Ni. 3-2); therefore तोक (*toka*) means a son. The word तनय (*tanaya*) is read in the same place and also means a son; but when तनय (*tanaya*) or तन (*tana*) occurs, especially when both words occur in one ṛk, it is customary to explain it as grandson. **विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

उत्तानायां अजनयन् सुसूतं (*uttānāyāṃ ajanayan susūtaṃ*)—*Uttānā* means the lower one of the araṇis (fire-sticks) used for churning out Agni. It is made of the śamī tree. It is broad like a plank, with a slight hollow in the middle. The upper araṇi is made of the wood of the aśvattha tree and is shaped like a stick; it is placed on the lower araṇi of śamī wood and churned, with the help of a rope, as curds are churned. The mouth (or face) of the lower araṇi is always turned upward. Therefore Sāyaṇa explains the word उत्तानायां (*uttānāyāṃ*) here as "in the lower araṇi of śamī wood, which faces upward, of the two araṇis". सुसूतं (*susūtaṃ*) means सुष्टु प्रेरितं (*suṣṭu preritaṃ*), "well urged": that is, the Agni that lay in a state of sleep (slumber) in the araṇis was urged on (awakened) and brought into being by the Adhvaryu and the other priests; this is the chief sense. The Brāhmaṇa sentence शमीगर्भादग्निं मंथति । एषा वा अग्नेर्यज्ञिया तनूः (*śamīgarbhādagniṃ maṃthati | eṣā vā agneryajñiyā tanūḥ*) (Tai. Brā. 1-1-9-1) supports this same sense.

भुवत् अग्निः पुरुषेशासु गर्भः (*bhuvat agniḥ puruṣeśāsu garbhaḥ*)—पेश इति रूपनाम । बहुरूपास्वोषधीषु गर्भो भुवत् । अंतर्वर्तमानतया गर्भ इव भवेत् (*peśa iti rūpanāma | bahurūpāsvoṣadhīṣu garbho bhuvat | aṃtarvartamānatayā garbha iva bhavet*). The word *peśa* is listed among the names of gold (Ni. 2-10) and among the names of form (Ni. 3-13), and there is also an etymology: पेश इति रूपनाम पिंशतेर्विपिशितं भवति (*peśa iti rūpanāma piṃśatervipiśitaṃ bhavati*) (Ni. 8-12). The word *peśa* is derived from the root *piṃś*, which gives the sense of "unfolded, developed", and has the meaning "form". In the present Ṛk, although पुरुषेशासु (*puruṣeśāsu*) would by the sense of its parts mean "in many forms", the commentator here says "in plants of many forms". Since Agni burns plants, trees and the like, it is customary to say that Agni lies hidden in their womb, that is, in their interior (inside). Or, since Agni is produced from the araṇis, which are modifications of trees, one may also explain that Agni dwells in the womb of trees and the like.

शिरिणायां चित् आक्तुना अपरीवृतः (*śiriṇāyāṃ cit āktunā aparīvṛtaḥ*)—The word शिरिणा (*śiriṇā*) is listed among the names of night (Ni. 2-18). This word is derived from the root शीज् स्वप्ने (*śīj svapne*) and is explained as शीर्यंतेऽस्यां भूतानीति शिरिणा रात्रिः (*śīryaṃte 'syāṃ bhūtānīti śiriṇā rātriḥ*) or शाययति प्राणिनः इति शिरिणा (*śāyayati prāṇinaḥ iti śiriṇā*): because living beings sleep at this time, *śiriṇā* means night. Besides this, the word अक्तुः (*aktuḥ*) is also listed among the names of night (Ni. 2-18). This word *aktu* is derived from the root अंजू व्यक्तिम्रक्षणकांतिगतिषु (*aṃjū vyaktimrakṣaṇakāṃtigatiṣu*) and is explained as अज्यते सिच्यतेऽस्यामवश्यायेन जगत् (*ajyate sicyate 'syāmavaśyāyena jagat*): since the whole world is covered by this (darkness), night is called *aktu*. Or, taking the sense of motion, गच्छति वा प्रतिदिनं अक्तुः (*gacchati vā pratidinaṃ aktuḥ*): because it arrives every day, night is called *aktu*. Since in this Ṛk both words शिरिणायां (*śiriṇāyāṃ*) and अक्तुना (*aktunā*) denote night, the commentator explains शिरिणायां (*śiriṇāyāṃ*) as "in the night" and अक्तुना (*aktunā*) as "by darkness or gloom". The sense is: Agni, who is not enveloped, that is, not touched, by the deep darkness of such a night. Since Agni is always shining, he has no contact with the darkness that exists in the night. The chief sense is that such an Agni, endowed with excellent knowledge, shines at night by his own radiance.

> डाशादिलक्षणेन हविषा धृतेन च जिघर्मि ॥ प्रति हैष सर्वाणि भूतानि क्षेति पृथुं

**प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)**

विश्वा (*viśvā*)—all. भुवनानि (*bhuvanāni*)—worlds. प्रतिक्षियंतं (*pratikṣiyaṃtaṃ*)—who pervades in the form of Vaiśvānara. पृथुं (*pṛthuṃ*)—who has greatness. तिरश्चा (*tiraścā*)—moving everywhere. वयसा (*vayasā*)—by form. बृहंतं (*bṛhaṃtaṃ*)—who has grown great. अन्नैः (*annaiḥ*)—by foods in the form of oblations. व्यचिष्ठं (*vyaciṣṭhaṃ*)—who is pervaded. रभसं (*rabhasaṃ*)—who is powerful. दृशानं (*dṛśānaṃ*)—who is seen with all such qualities. अग्निं (*agniṃ*)—Agni. हविसा (*havisā*) (as supplied; the Pada reads हविषा (*haviṣā*))—with the oblation. धृतेन (*dhṛtena*) (as supplied; the Saṃhitā reads घृतेन (*ghṛtena*))—with ghee. जिघर्मि (*jigharmi*)—I cause to shine.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

I offer oblation and ghee to Agni, who pervades all the worlds in the form of Vaiśvānara, has greatness, has grown great in a form moving everywhere, is pervaded by the food that is the oblation, and is powerful.

**Printed English translation**

I offer oblations with butter to Agni, who spreads through all regions, vast, expanding, with all-comprehending form, nourished(with sacrificial) food, powerful, conspicuous.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

प्रतिक्षियंतं भुवनानि विश्वा (*pratikṣiyaṃtaṃ bhuvanāni viśvā*)—प्रति ह्येष सर्वाणि भूतानि क्षेति (*prati hyeṣa sarvāṇi bhūtāni kṣeti*). This Agni dwells in all living beings in the form of Vaiśvānara. That is, as Vaiśvānara fire he dwells in herbs, forest trees, creepers, trees and the like; as the digestive fire (*jāṭharāgni*) in the classes of beings such as men; as terrestrial fire in the earth; as the fire of lightning (flash) in the atmosphere; and as the sun in heaven. Therefore it is said that he dwells in all worlds, भुवनानि विश्वा (*bhuvanāni viśvā*), and in all living beings.

पृथुं तिरश्चा वयसा बृहंतं (*pṛthuṃ tiraścā vayasā bṛhaṃtaṃ*)—महांतं सर्वतः प्रवर्तमानेन पेशसा प्रवृद्धं (*mahāṃtaṃ sarvataḥ pravartamānena peśasā pravṛddhaṃ*). Because Agni spreads and extends in all places (in forms such as the forest fire), he is here described as having a very extensive and great form.

व्यचिष्ठं अन्नैः रभसं दृशानं (*vyaciṣṭhaṃ annaiḥ rabhasaṃ dṛśānaṃ*)—हविर्लक्षणैरन्नैर्वाप्तं बलवंतमेवंगुणवत्तया दृश्यमानं (*havirlakṣaṇairannairvāptaṃ balavaṃtamevaṃguṇavattayā dṛśyamānaṃ*). Growing strong through oblations, firewood, kindling sticks, clarified butter and the like, and becoming powerful, that is, blazing with flames and with radiance, visible to our eyes and facing us: such is the Agni meant. The sage describes that he will offer oblation and ghee into such an Agni.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

I offer ghee in the form of a sprinkling to Agni, who pervades everywhere and comes toward the sacrifice; may Agni enjoy that ghee, offered together with a hymn free from anything harmful. **Printed English translation**

I offer oblations to Agni, who is present at all(sacrifices); may he accept(them), offered with an unhesitating mind,(to him) who is the refuge of man, endowed with any form that may be desired, blazing with a radiance(that it is) not(possible) to endure.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

आ विश्वतः प्रत्यंचं (*ā viśvataḥ pratyaṃcaṃ*)—the meaning is: Agni, who is everywhere and who comes toward the sacrifice.

अरक्षसा मनसा (*arakṣasā manasā*)—बाधकरहितेन मनसा, स्तोत्रेण सह (*bādhakarahitena manasā, stotreṇa saha*). That is, with a mind free from evil thought, with a good mind, with a mind fixed on Agni, or together with a hymn.

मर्यश्रीः (*maryaśrīḥ*)—मर्यैर्मर्त्यैः श्रयणीयो भजनीयः (*maryairmartyaiḥ śrayaṇīyo bhajanīyaḥ*). One who is worshipped by men, or one who is praised.

स्पृहयद्वर्णः (*spṛhayadvarṇaḥ*)—यादृशं वर्णं यजमानः स्पृहयति तादृशवर्णोपेतः (*yādṛśaṃ varṇaṃ yajamānaḥ spṛhayati tādṛśavarṇopetaḥ*). The sacrificer who worships Agni desires Agni to have a certain colour or form; Agni assumes just that form, that is, he assumes the form the sacrificer needs. Such is the sense.

न अभिमृशे (*na abhimṛśe*)—अतितेजस्वितया अभिमर्शनीयो न भवति (*atitejasvitayā abhimarśanīyo na bhavati*). Because Agni shines with excessive radiance, his radiance cannot be endured; this is the sense.
