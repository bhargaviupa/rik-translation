## MINOR findings (exact words)

- **M1, p. 10, Bhāvārtha:** the Kannada has "(ನಾವು ನಿನ್ನನ್ನು ಸ್ತುತಿಗಳಿಂದ ಪರಿಚಯಿಸುತ್ತೇವೆ)" and the English has "(we make you known by praises)". The context (ಸೇವಿಸುತ್ತದೆ, "serves") points to ಪರಿಚರಿಸುತ್ತೇವೆ, "we serve/attend on you". The literal reading of the printed word is defensible, but there is no note. Suggested wording: "(we serve you with praises) *[printed ಪರಿಚಯಿಸುತ್ತೇವೆ, "make known", as supplied]*".
- **M2, p. 11:** the Kannada has "ಸುಷುಮ್ನಾನಾದಿಯ ರಶ್ಮಿಯೂ" and the English has "the ray of the Suṣumnā (nāḍī) etc.". The "(nāḍī)" is an interpretation the text does not give (Suṣumna is a solar ray here). Suggested wording: "the Suṣumna ray and the others".
- **M3, p. 76, Special Topics:** the Sanskrit gloss "ಯಜ್ಞೇ ದೇವಾಕ್ ಕಾಮಯಿಮಾನಾಃ ಋತ್ವಿಜಃ ಅಧ್ವರ್ಯಾದಯಃ ಧೃತೇನ ತ್ವಯಿ ಸಿಂಚಂತಿ" appears only as English ("the priests, the Adhvaryu and others, desiring the gods in the sacrifice, pour ghee on you"). It needs the Devanagari and IAST layers via `⟦ ⟧`.
- **M4, p. 205, grammar:** the Kannada has "ವಿಪ್ ಶ್ಲು ವಿಕರಣನಿಮಿತ್ತವಾಗಿ ಧಾತುವಿಗೆ ದ್ವಿತ್ವಾದಿ" and the English has "the lengthened/reduplicated form arises through the substitute of the affix (… "ವಿಪ್ ಶ್ಲು" …) so the root is doubled". This is unclear. Suggested wording: "on account of the vikaraṇa ślu (printed "ವಿಪ್ ಶ್ಲು"), reduplication etc. of the root".
- **M5, p. 418, grammar:** the Kannada has "ಉಭಯಪದಗಳಲ್ಲಿಯೂ ಆಮ್ಯದಾತ್ತಸ್ವರ ಬರುತ್ತದೆ" and the English has "the āmredita-like udātta accent on both members". "Āmredita" is a guess. The garbled word most likely stands for ಆದ್ಯುದಾತ್ತ (Pā. 6-2-140: both members keep their accent). Suggested wording: "the (initial-)udātta accent on both members (printed ಆಮ್ಯದಾತ್ತ, as supplied)".
- **M6, p. 552:** the Kannada has "ವಾಜ್ಞಾಮಗಳಲ್ಲಿಯೂ (ನಿ. ೨-೨೩)" and the English has "among the वाज्ञाम (*vājñāma*) names [so printed]". The meaning is not given. On p. 11 the same word (ವಾಜ್ನಾಮ) is translated "names of speech". Suggested wording: "among the names of speech (printed ವಾಜ್ಞಾಮ) (Ni. 2-23)".
- **M7, p. 694:** the Kannada page ends "ಈ ಅರ್ಥವನ್ನು ವಿಶದವಾಗಿ ವಿವರಿಸುವ—". The English adds "The commentator has given, as examples, the Ṛks which explain this sense in detail—", which is the sentence from p. 695, and p. 695 repeats it. This is text moved from the next page, not new content. Suggested wording: "Explaining this sense clearly—" and leave the rest to p. 695.
- **M8, p. 820:** the Kannada has "ಚಾತುರ್ವಿಂಶಿಕವೆಂಬ ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತನಾಲ್ಕನೆಯ ದಿವಸ ಮಾಡುವ ಯಜ್ಞದ ಮಾಧ್ಯಂದಿನಸವನದಲ್ಲಿ" and the English has "On the twenty-fourth day of the sacrifice, called Chāturviṃśika, in the midday pressing". Suggested wording: "In the midday pressing of the sacrifice called Cāturviṃśika, performed on the twenty-fourth day". The p. 853 wording of the same rite is fine.
- **Pattern (format, not meaning):** many Sanskrit quotations in Special Topics have only two layers, Devanagari and IAST, with no English rendering. Examples are the RV verses on pp. 11, 148 and 160, Śa. Brā. on pp. 440 and 650, and Tai. Saṃ. on p. 866. On pp. 10 and 300 the translator added a declared gloss. CLAUDE.md §2.1 asks for three layers on every citation. Usually the Kannada paraphrase that follows gives the sense, so meaning is not lost, but the rule is applied inconsistently. Many Sanskrit glosses of single words (e.g. p. 266 "पृथिव्यां अंतरिक्षे विस्तीर्णे तेजः …") also have no English layer.

## ERRORs, with proposed corrected English

**E1, p. 77: Aitareya Brāhmaṇa quotation is shortened (omission).** The Kannada prints the passage in full (5 lines):
"ಅಂಜಂತಿ ತ್ವಾಮಧ್ವರೇ ದೇವಯಂತ ಇತ್ಯನ್ವಾಹ ಅಧ್ವರೇ ಹ್ಯೇನಂ ದೇವಯಂತೋಽಂ / ಜಂತಿ ವನಸ್ಪತೇ ಮಧುನಾ ದೈವ್ಯೇನೇತ್ಯೇತದ್ವೈ ಮಧು ದೈವ್ಯಂ ಯದಾಜ್ಯಂ, / ಯದೂರ್ಧ್ವಸ್ತಿಷ್ಠಾ ದ್ರವಿಣೇಹ ಧತ್ತಾತ್ ಯದ್ವಾ ಕ್ಷಯೋ ಮಾತುರಸ್ಯಾ ಉಪಸ್ಥ ಇತಿ / ಯದಿ ಚ ತಿಷ್ಠಾಸಿ ಯದಿ ಚ ಶಯಾಸೈ ದ್ರವಿಣಮೇವಾಸ್ಮಾಸು ಧತ್ತಾದಿತ್ಯೇವ / ತದಾಹ । (ಇ. ಬ್ರಾ. ೨-೨)".
The English has only "अंजंति त्वामध्वरे देवयंत इत्यन्वाह अध्वरे ह्येनं देवयंतोऽं (…) ... यदि च तिष्ठासि … तदाह", with the note "(The passage is given in the Kannada text in full; here only its beginning and end are written in the three-layer form…)". The note discloses the cut, but the cut itself breaks the rule that a Brāhmaṇa quotation is quoted in full.
**Correction:** quote all five lines through `⟦ ⟧` (Devanagari + IAST): अंजंति त्वामध्वरे देवयंत इत्यन्वाह अध्वरे ह्येनं देवयंतोऽंजंति वनस्पते मधुना दैव्येनेत्येतद्वै मधु दैव्यं यदाज्यं, यदूर्ध्वस्तिष्ठा द्रविणेह धत्तात् यद्वा क्षयो मातुरस्या उपस्थ इति यदि च तिष्ठासि यदि च शयासै द्रविणमेवास्मासु धत्तादित्येव तदाह । (Ai. Brā. 2-2, printed "ಇ. ಬ್ರಾ."). Keep the existing English sense as the third layer.

**E2, p. 77: Nirukta paraphrase quotation is shortened (omission, not disclosed).** The Kannada prints it in full:
"ಅಂಜಂತಿ ತ್ವಾಮಧ್ವರೇ ದೇವಾ ಕಾಮಯಮಾನಾ ವನಸ್ಪತೇ ಮಧುನಾ ದೈವ್ಯೇನ ಚ ಧೃತೇನ ಚ ಯದೂರ್ಧ್ವಃಸ್ಥಾಸ್ಯಸಿ ದ್ರವಿಜಾನಿ ಚ ನೋ ದಾಸ್ಯಸಿ ಯದ್ವಾ ತೇ ಕೃತಃ ಕ್ಷಯೋ ಮಾತುರಸ್ಯಾ ಉಪಸ್ಥೇ ಉಪಸ್ಥಾನೇ".
The English has "अंजंति त्वामध्वरे देवा कामयमाना (…) ... यद्वा ते कृतः क्षयो मातुरस्या उपस्थे उपस्थाने". The middle (वनस्पते मधुना दैव्येन च धृतेन च यदूर्ध्वःस्थास्यसि द्रविजानि च नो दास्यसि) is dropped, and the "..." looks as if it were printed. It is not: the print has no dots here, while p. 31's dots are genuine.
**Correction:** quote it in full, in three layers. English: "They anoint you in the sacrifice, desiring the gods, O Vanaspati, with the divine honey and with ghee; whether you will stand upright, and you will give us riches (printed ದ್ರವಿಜಾನಿ), or whether your dwelling is made in the lap of this mother, in her presence."

## What was not verified

- No scans: misreadings shared by the Kannada OCR and the English cannot be detected. Printed English translations were compared only with the supplied text of them.
- The Sanskrit bhāṣya blocks were not read in full. Only the numbers, names and references cited in the English gists were checked against them (pp. 231, 722, 879, and others by eye). The Devanagari/IAST conversion and the accents were not audited.
- Pages 630–631 have no per-page file in either output (the page numbering skips them in both files). This was noticed but not investigated.
- Whether E1/E2-style shortening recurs elsewhere: a grep for "..." inside Devanagari quotations found only pp. 31 (dots printed in the source), 77 (E1, E2) and 78 (a line broken at the end of a page). The grep may not catch every pattern.
