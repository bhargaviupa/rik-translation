Gist: the passage gives the story of Viśvāmitra, who takes the wealth, comes to the confluence of the Vipāś and the Śutudrī, and praises the rivers so that they become fordable; the other (Bharatas) follow him.

**Anuvāda (Kannada).** Thus an old story is told. "Viśvāmitra" means that this seer is a friend to all, or that all are his friends; this is the etymological sense of the word. This seer was the priest ([sic] the supplied text reads पुरोतनागिद्दनु (*purotanāgiddanu*), evidently for *purohita*) of the king named Sudāsa, the son of Pijavana. Once, taking the wealth given by that king and setting out to go to his hermitage, he came to the confluence of the rivers Vipāś and Śutudrī (the present Beas and Sutlej of the Punjab). Thieves who wished to seize the wealth he carried were following him. Knowing this, Viśvāmitra tried by some means to cross these rivers that lay across his path, but they were very deep with the water of the flood and he could not. Then the seer prayed to the rivers and praised them, that they should reduce their water and make it convenient for him to pass; this is said in the 33rd sūkta (Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-33) and in verses 9-11 of Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-53.

This seer Viśvāmitra in the sūkta above, that is, in Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-33-5, calls himself the son of Kuśika. In the Rāmāyaṇa, the Bhārata and other Purāṇas it is said that there was enmity and ill-will between Viśvāmitra and the seer Vasiṣṭha. There is some support for this in a few places in the Ṛgveda ([sic] the supplied text reads आधांवू (*ādhāṃvū*), evidently for आधारवू (*ādhāravū*), "support"). That will be discussed in the Pīṭhike (introduction) of this part.

The names of Viśvāmitra and of this seer's descendants are read in the mantras Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-1-21; 3-18-4; 3-53-13; 10-89-17; Atharvaveda 18-3-6; 18-4-54; Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa 3-15-1 and others. In the story of Śunaḥśepa the part of Viśvāmitra is very important; we have described it at length in Part 2 of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā. That Viśvāmitra is a principal seer is described in Aitareya (printed ऐतरेय वर्णक (*aitareya varṇaka*), "Aitareya varṇaka") 2-2-1; Ai. Brā. 6-18-1; 6-20-3; Tai. Saṃ. 2-2-1-2; 3-1-7-3; 5-2-3-4; Kāṭhaka Saṃhitā 16-19; 20-9; Maitrāyaṇīya Saṃhitā 2-7-19; Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇa 15-1; 26-14; 28-1,2; 29-3; Pañcaviṃśa Brāhmaṇa 14-3-12; Bṛ. U. 2-2-4; Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa 3-3-13; 3-15-1 and other places.

In the Purāṇas and the like Viśvāmitra is said to have been a king who became a Brāhmaṇa by austerity. In the Ṛgveda there is no support at all for this. But in the Nirukta (Ni. 2-24) it is said that Viśvāmitra was the son of a king named Kauśika; and in the Pañcaviṃśa Brāhmaṇa also (Paṃ. Vi. Brā. 21-12-2) Viśvāmitra is said to have been a king. In the Ṛgveda alone there is no support for this.

**Anuvāda (Kannada).** In the third maṇḍala of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā, in the first anuvāka, beginning with सोमस्य (*somasya*), there are twelve sūktas. Among them the sūkta beginning प्र य आरुः (*pra ya āruḥ*) is the seventh; it has eleven ṛks (as printed here; the head-lines below give 20). As Viśvāmitra is the seer of this whole third maṇḍala, he is the seer of this sūkta also. The metre is Triṣṭubh. The deity is Agni. As it has been said before that the three sūktas beginning प्रत्यग्निरुषसः (*pratyagniruṣasaḥ*) (the 5th, 6th and 7th sūktas) are applied to the recitation of the Prātaranuvāka mantras and the Āśvina śastra mantras, and as this is the third of those three sūktas, the same application given above holds for this sūkta also.

Sūkta—7. Maṇḍala—3. Anuvāka—1. Sūkta—7. Aṣṭaka—3. Adhyāya—1. Varga—1, 2. Number of ṛks in the sūkta—20. Ṛṣi—Viśvāmitra. Devatā—Agni. Chandas—Triṣṭubh.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

The rays of Agni, the blue-backed and all-supporting, going out from Agni, have pervaded everywhere the world and the parents, व्याघ्रापृथिविगळन्नु (*vyāghrāpṛthivigaḷannu*) ([sic] as supplied; evidently *dyāvāpṛthivī*, heaven and earth); in the same way the flowing and worship-worthy rivers also are pervaded. The all-pervading heaven and earth, so that sacrifices may go on in abundance, grant long life to Agni and abundant food to the sacrificer, and सिद्धरिसुत्तवॆ (*siddharisuttavè*) (as supplied; the sense seems to be "work together") with Agni.

**Printed English translation**

The (rays) of the blackbacked, all-sustaining (Agni) have arisen, and pervaded the parents (earth and heaven) and the flowing rivers ; the surrounding parents co-operate with him, and bestow long life for the sake of assiduous worship.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

धासेः— सर्वस्य धारयितुः । (*dhāseḥ— sarvasya dhārayituḥ |*) The author of the Nirukta reads the word धासिः (*dhāsiḥ*) among the twenty-eight names of food (Ni. 3-9). Sāyaṇa has explained the sense as "Agni bears everything". He also gives the meaning that the rays of this Agni shine greatly.

मातरा विविशुः सप्तवाणीः (*mātarā viviśuḥ saptavāṇīḥ*): the word वाणी (*vāṇī*) is read in the Nirukta among the twenty-seven names of speech (Ni. 2-23). Here the meaning "rivers" is given, and it is explained that the rays of Agni pervade the rivers. The sense that the word सप्त (*sapta*) is not a numeral is indicated here; the word is explained as "of pervading nature", and the nature of the rays is described. Though the word मातरा (*mātarā*) is not stated among the names of heaven and earth in the Nirukta, here the word is explained as heaven and earth, and it is described that the rays of Agni pervade heaven and earth (earth and the middle region). The fact that heaven and earth, like mother and father, are the nourishers of all is stated in the ṛks.

परिक्षिता पितरा—परितो वर्तमाने पितरौ द्यावापृथिव्यौ । (*parikṣitā pitarā—parito vartamāne pitarau dyāvāpṛthivyau |*) The meaning is: heaven and earth (earth and the middle region), who are the mother and father of the world, pervade everything, and they co-operate in the world with Agni.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

The red-coloured flames of Agni, the bestower of desired boons, serving as his vehicles, pervade even the firmament and give delight to Agni; this Agni resorts to the rivers, which carry sweet water and are luminous. Thus, O Agni, a voice in the form of praise serves you, who desire to dwell in the firmament, the abode of the waters, and who are the cause of flames (we make you known by praises).

**प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)**

rayīṇām — (in the midst) of riches; rayivit — the one who supplies the best wealth; cikitvān — the wise one; patiḥ — the lord of horses, Agni; sīm — in every way; suyamāḥ — easily controlled; bhavantīḥ (yāḥ) — you who are mares (O horses), whom; ā arohat — he mounts; tāḥ — those horses; nīlapṛṣṭhaḥ — he whose back is dark blue; purudhapratīkaḥ — he whose limbs are scattered in many ways (Agni); atasasya — of the ceaseless going; dhāseḥ — for the nourishment (of it); pra āvāsayat — makes them rest.

(Printed as supplied: शीं (*śīṃ*) for sīm, पुरुषप्रतीकः (*puruṣapratīkaḥ*) for purudhapratīkaḥ, अतसस्य- सतत (*atasasya- satata*) with a stray hyphen; left as supplied.)

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

O mares, the vehicles of Agni! Agni, who bestows the most excellent wealth, who is wise, and who is the lord of horses, mounts you in every way, you who are easily controlled. But that dark-backed Agni, of scattered limbs, in order that the nourishment needed for the unwearied going from you may reach you, in advance makes you rest.

**Printed English**

Their lord has mounted on his well-trained mares; the sagacious selector of treasures, the blackbacked, manylimbed (Agni) has granted them a place of rest for the cherising [sic] of (their) rapid speed.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

रयीणां रयिवित् (*rayīṇāṃ rayivit*) — धनानां मध्यॆ विशिष्टधनस्य लंभयिता (*dhanānāṃ madhyè viśiṣṭadhanasya laṃbhayitā*). The word *rayi* denotes water (Ni. 2-24) and also wealth (Ni. 3-9); and since it is said, at "nū ca purā ca sadanaṃ rayīṇām", "*rayi* is a name for wealth, from *rā*, in the sense of giving" (Ni. 4-17), it conveys the meaning wealth. The meaning is that Agni obtains for those who perform the sacrifice all that is known as wealth.

पुरुधप्रतीक: (*purudhapratīka:*) — पुरुशब्द इह संख्यावाची । बहुधा विक्षिप्तांगः (*puruśabda iha saṃkhyāvācī | bahudhā vikṣiptāṃgaḥ*). It is explained that Agni pervades all places.

धासेः ताः प्र अवासयत् (*dhāseḥ tāḥ pra avāsayat*) — पोषणार्थं आश्वा: प्रकर्षेण वासयेति स्म (*poṣaṇārthaṃ āśvā: prakarṣeṇa vāsayeti sma*). Because Agni is the lord of the horses, he is described as the helper by whom the horses (for their nourishment) may grow,

चिकित्त्वा (*cikittvā*) (as printed; the bhāṣya has चिकित्वान् (*cikitvān*)) — knowing. The meaning is that Agni has spiritual knowledge.

सीं (*sīṃ*) — everywhere, in all regions. The authors of the Nirukta, on the word सीं (*sīṃ*), say सीमिति परिग्रहार्थियो वा पादपूरणे वा (*sīmiti parigrahārthiyo vā pādapūraṇe vā*): the particle *sīm* is used in the sense of "taking in all round" and also as a verse-filler. Then, at विशीमतः सुरुचो-वेन आवः व्यवृणोत् सर्वत आदित्यः (*viśīmataḥ suruco-vena āvaḥ vyavṛṇot sarvata ādityaḥ*) (as supplied), they explain *sīm* as सर्वतः (*sarvataḥ*) (on all sides), and सीम्न: सीमतः सीमतो मर्यादातः सीमा मर्यादा (*sīmna: sīmataḥ sīmato maryādātaḥ sīmā maryādā*) as limit, boundary.
