Gist: the passage gives the story of Viśvāmitra, who takes the wealth, comes to the confluence of the Vipāś and the Śutudrī, and praises the rivers so that they become fordable; the other (Bharatas) follow him.

**Anuvāda (Kannada).** Thus an old story is told. "Viśvāmitra" means that this seer is a friend to all, or that all are his friends; this is the etymological sense of the word. This seer was the priest ([sic] the supplied text reads पुरोतनागिद्दनु (*purotanāgiddanu*), evidently for *purohita*) of the king named Sudāsa, the son of Pijavana. Once, taking the wealth given by that king and setting out to go to his hermitage, he came to the confluence of the rivers Vipāś and Śutudrī (the present Beas and Sutlej of the Punjab). Thieves who wished to seize the wealth he carried were following him. Knowing this, Viśvāmitra tried by some means to cross these rivers that lay across his path, but they were very deep with the water of the flood and he could not. Then the seer prayed to the rivers and praised them, that they should reduce their water and make it convenient for him to pass; this is said in the 33rd sūkta (Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-33) and in verses 9-11 of Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-53.

This seer Viśvāmitra in the sūkta above, that is, in Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-33-5, calls himself the son of Kuśika. In the Rāmāyaṇa, the Bhārata and other Purāṇas it is said that there was enmity and ill-will between Viśvāmitra and the seer Vasiṣṭha. There is some support for this in a few places in the Ṛgveda ([sic] the supplied text reads आधांवू (*ādhāṃvū*), evidently for आधारवू (*ādhāravū*), "support"). That will be discussed in the Pīṭhike (introduction) of this part.

The names of Viśvāmitra and of this seer's descendants are read in the mantras Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-1-21; 3-18-4; 3-53-13; 10-89-17; Atharvaveda 18-3-6; 18-4-54; Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa 3-15-1 and others. In the story of Śunaḥśepa the part of Viśvāmitra is very important; we have described it at length in Part 2 of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā. That Viśvāmitra is a principal seer is described in Aitareya (printed ऐतरेय वर्णक (*aitareya varṇaka*), "Aitareya varṇaka") 2-2-1; Ai. Brā. 6-18-1; 6-20-3; Tai. Saṃ. 2-2-1-2; 3-1-7-3; 5-2-3-4; Kāṭhaka Saṃhitā 16-19; 20-9; Maitrāyaṇīya Saṃhitā 2-7-19; Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇa 15-1; 26-14; 28-1,2; 29-3; Pañcaviṃśa Brāhmaṇa 14-3-12; Bṛ. U. 2-2-4; Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa 3-3-13; 3-15-1 and other places.

In the Purāṇas and the like Viśvāmitra is said to have been a king who became a Brāhmaṇa by austerity. In the Ṛgveda there is no support at all for this. But in the Nirukta (Ni. 2-24) it is said that Viśvāmitra was the son of a king named Kauśika; and in the Pañcaviṃśa Brāhmaṇa also (Paṃ. Vi. Brā. 21-12-2) Viśvāmitra is said to have been a king. In the Ṛgveda alone there is no support for this.

**Anuvāda (Kannada).** In the third maṇḍala of the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā, in the first anuvāka, beginning with सोमस्य (*somasya*), there are twelve sūktas. Among them the sūkta beginning प्र य आरुः (*pra ya āruḥ*) is the seventh; it has eleven ṛks (as printed here; the head-lines below give 20). As Viśvāmitra is the seer of this whole third maṇḍala, he is the seer of this sūkta also. The metre is Triṣṭubh. The deity is Agni. As it has been said before that the three sūktas beginning प्रत्यग्निरुषसः (*pratyagniruṣasaḥ*) (the 5th, 6th and 7th sūktas) are applied to the recitation of the Prātaranuvāka mantras and the Āśvina śastra mantras, and as this is the third of those three sūktas, the same application given above holds for this sūkta also.

Sūkta—7. Maṇḍala—3. Anuvāka—1. Sūkta—7. Aṣṭaka—3. Adhyāya—1. Varga—1, 2. Number of ṛks in the sūkta—20. Ṛṣi—Viśvāmitra. Devatā—Agni. Chandas—Triṣṭubh.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

The rays of Agni, the blue-backed and all-supporting, going out from Agni, have pervaded everywhere the world and the parents, व्याघ्रापृथिविगळन्नु (*vyāghrāpṛthivigaḷannu*) ([sic] as supplied; evidently *dyāvāpṛthivī*, heaven and earth); in the same way the flowing and worship-worthy rivers also are pervaded. The all-pervading heaven and earth, so that sacrifices may go on in abundance, grant long life to Agni and abundant food to the sacrificer, and सिद्धरिसुत्तवॆ (*siddharisuttavè*) (as supplied; the sense seems to be "work together") with Agni.

**Printed English translation**

The (rays) of the blackbacked, all-sustaining (Agni) have arisen, and pervaded the parents (earth and heaven) and the flowing rivers ; the surrounding parents co-operate with him, and bestow long life for the sake of assiduous worship.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

धासेः— सर्वस्य धारयितुः । (*dhāseḥ— sarvasya dhārayituḥ |*) The author of the Nirukta reads the word धासिः (*dhāsiḥ*) among the twenty-eight names of food (Ni. 3-9). Sāyaṇa has explained the sense as "Agni bears everything". He also gives the meaning that the rays of this Agni shine greatly.

मातरा विविशुः सप्तवाणीः (*mātarā viviśuḥ saptavāṇīḥ*): the word वाणी (*vāṇī*) is read in the Nirukta among the twenty-seven names of speech (Ni. 2-23). Here the meaning "rivers" is given, and it is explained that the rays of Agni pervade the rivers. The sense that the word सप्त (*sapta*) is not a numeral is indicated here; the word is explained as "of pervading nature", and the nature of the rays is described. Though the word मातरा (*mātarā*) is not stated among the names of heaven and earth in the Nirukta, here the word is explained as heaven and earth, and it is described that the rays of Agni pervade heaven and earth (earth and the middle region). The fact that heaven and earth, like mother and father, are the nourishers of all is stated in the ṛks.

परिक्षिता पितरा—परितो वर्तमाने पितरौ द्यावापृथिव्यौ । (*parikṣitā pitarā—parito vartamāne pitarau dyāvāpṛthivyau |*) The meaning is: heaven and earth (earth and the middle region), who are the mother and father of the world, pervade everything, and they co-operate in the world with Agni.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

The red-coloured flames of Agni, the bestower of desired boons, serving as his vehicles, pervade even the firmament and give delight to Agni; this Agni resorts to the rivers, which carry sweet water and are luminous. Thus, O Agni, a voice in the form of praise serves you, who desire to dwell in the firmament, the abode of the waters, and who are the cause of flames (we make you known by praises).

**प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha)**

Vṛṣṇaḥ — of the one who showers desires; aruṣasya — of the one without enemies (and therefore shining), Agni; śevam — the comfort that has him as refuge; jānanti — (people) come to know; uta — and (those people who know that comfort); bradhnasya — of Agni, of great majesty; śāsane — under the rule; raṇanti — rejoice (and); yeṣām — whichever men; māhinā — greatness-possessing; iḷā gīḥ — (Agni's) speech in the form of praise; gaṇyā — worthy of honour; te — they; divorucaḥ — (dear to heaven) those who bring heaven into light; surucaḥ — of fine brilliance; rocamānāḥ — shining brightly (become).

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

People come to know the comfort that arises from taking refuge in Agni, who grants what is wished for and has no enemies; coming under the rule of that Agni of great majesty, they live happily. Those who regard as worthy of honour the praise relating to Agni, full of great majesty, become exceedingly radiant and guides toward the attainment of heaven.

**Printed English**

Men comprehend the service of the uninjurable showerer (of benefits) and exult in the commands of the mighty (AGNI) : their frequent and earnest hymns of praise, bright and radiant, are illuminating heaven.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

अरुषस्य (*aruṣasya*) — रोषंतीति रुषा हिंसकाः (*roṣaṃtīti ruṣā hiṃsakāḥ*) : the word *ruṣāḥ*, formed from the root *ruṣ*, which conveys "to injure", is explained as "those who injure"; since there are no such injuring enemies, Agni shines. Of these, the seven who make the *vaṣaṭ* call, including those of the Hotṛ group, he has explained by सप्त वषट्कर्तारः (*sapta vaṣaṭkartāraḥ*). पंचाध्वर्यवः (*paṃcādhvaryavaḥ*): the five, including the priests who assist in performing the work of the Adhvaryu, are described as Adhvaryus. गायत्रमेको गायति शक्वरीषूद्घाता (*gāyatrameko gāyati śakvarīṣūdghātā*) ... ब्रह्मैको जाते जाते विद्यां वदति (*brahmaiko jāte jāte vidyāṃ vadati*) ब्रह्मा सर्वविद्यः सर्वं वेदितुमर्हति (*brahmā sarvavidyaḥ sarvaṃ veditumarhati*) ... यज्ञस्य मात्रां विमिमीत एकोऽध्वर्युः (*yajñasya mātrāṃ vimimīta eko 'dhvaryuḥ*) अध्वर्युरध्वरयुरध्वरं युनक्त्यध्वरस्य नेताध्वरं कामयत इति वा (*adhvaryuradhvarayuradhvaraṃ yunaktyadhvarasya netādhvaraṃ kāmayata iti vā*) ... अध्वर इति यज्ञनाम (*adhvara iti yajñanāma*) ध्वरतिर्हिंसाकर्मा तत्प्रतिषेधः (*dhvaratirhiṃsākarmā tatpratiṣedhaḥ*) (the passage is given with its ellipses and punctuation as supplied).

The root *dhvar* conveys the sense "to injure"; since the injury done in a sacrifice is directed to the deity, it is established that it is not injury. Hence the word *adhvara* denotes the sacrifice, the purifying rite. In this rite the so-called Udgātṛ sings the sāman called Gāyatra. The priest called Brahman is the one who knows all the rites performed in the sacrifice; he informs the other priests of the order in which the respective rites are performed. The priest called Adhvaryu is the one who carries out the rites of the sacrifice, or who carries the sacrifice forward, or who desires that the sacrifice proceed until it is completed; thus the Nirukta explains the duties of the Adhvaryu, Brahman and Udgātṛ.

Durgācārya: यदृचैव हौत्रं क्रियते (*yadṛcaiva hautraṃ kriyate*) (Ai. Brā. 5-5-8) एतत्तावदृगध्ययॆनकर्म तस्मिन् होतरि विनियुक्तं (*etattāvadṛgadhyayènakarma tasmin hotari viniyuktaṃ*). Since the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa passage teaches that the Hotṛ performs the rites of the sacrifice with the Ṛgveda itself, all the rites of the sacrifice that belong to the Ṛgveda are the concern of the priest called Hotṛ. साम्योद्गीथं (*sāmyodgīthaṃ*) (Ai. Brā. 5-5-8) — उद्गाता (*udgātā*) तस्मिन्नप्येतत्सामगानकर्म विनियुक्तं (*tasminnapyetatsāmagānakarma viniyuktaṃ*): the Udgītha is to be performed with the Sāmaveda; the one who performs this rite is called the Udgātṛ; the sāman-singing rite connected with the sacrifice is assigned to him.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

O Agni, shining one, who calls the gods to the sacrifices! To you, of great glory, mighty, of many hues (worshipful), showerer of desires, the countless, greatly extended flames become sprinklers by providing room for the casting in of ghee and other oblations; you, the best among those who gladden the worshippers, the wise one, bring to this Vedic rite the gods who are worthy of worship in the sacrifices, and heaven and earth.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

**देव होतः (*deva hotaḥ*)** — "the shining one, the invoker of the gods". Here Agni is described as blazing with abundant radiance and as the one who invites the gods to the sacrifice. In the passage "अस्य वामस्य फलितस्य होतुः (*asya vāmasya phalitasya hotuḥ*)" [as supplied] the word Hotṛ was explained as "the one who is invoked". For the word होतुः (*hotuḥ*) in "होतुश्चित्पूर्वे हविरद्यमाशत (*hotuścitpūrve haviradyamāśata*)" Durgācārya explains "होतुरपि अग्नेः (*hoturapi agneḥ*)": of Agni, who is in the form of a human Hotṛ; and (he states that) the greatness of the gods is described in four ways: the mantras are of the kinds पौरुषविध्यं (*pauruṣavidhyaṃ*), अपौरुषविध्यं (*apauruṣavidhyaṃ*), कर्मार्थात्मोभयविध्यं (*karmārthātmobhayavidhyaṃ*) and नित्यमौभयविध्यं (*nityamaubhayavidhyaṃ*) [all as supplied], and by these kinds the glory of the gods is made manifest. The gods are endowed with a power consisting of abundant lordship (aiśvarya) and act in the manner fitting their respective duties; the seers, beholders of the mantras, saw this directly and praised each in his own way; and the glory of the gods is described as mūrtā (having form), amūrtā (formless), ekadhā (of one kind), dvidhā (of two kinds) and bahudhā (of many kinds). Here Agni's power is described as his having attained identity with the human Hotṛ who invokes the gods.

**चित्राय (*citrāya*)** — "नानाविधवर्णाय यद्वा देवैश्चायनीयाय पूज्याय (*nānāvidhavarṇāya yadvā devaiścāyanīyāya pūjyāya*)". Sāyaṇa gives two meanings: first, that Agni has many kinds of colours through his rays; second, that Agni, receiving the oblation-portions even from the gods, is worshipful. The Nirukta-writers explain the word citra as "चायनीयं महनीयं पूजनीयं (*cāyanīyaṃ mahanīyaṃ pūjanīyaṃ*)", giving the sense of "worshipful, deserving honour".

**चित्रस्य रश्मयः वृषायंते (*citrasya raśmayaḥ vṛṣāyaṃte*)** — सर्वतः व्याप्ताः ज्वालाः वृषेवाचरंति सेचका इव भवंति । होतारः ज्वालाभिः समिद्धमग्निं दृष्ट्वा सोमाज्यपयःप्रभृतीनि हवींषि तत्र प्रक्षिपंतीति ज्वालानां वृषत्वं । यद्वा षष्ठ्यर्थे चतुर्थी । तथा च महतो अत्यस्य चित्रस्य वृष्टो अग्नेः बहवो रश्मयः वृषेवाचरंति । वृष्टुत्पादनद्वारा पर्जन्यवदाचरंति । (*sarvataḥ vyāptāḥ jvālāḥ vṛṣevācaraṃti secakā iva bhavaṃti | hotāraḥ jvālābhiḥ samiddhamagniṃ dṛṣṭvā somājyapayaḥprabhṛtīni havīṃṣi tatra prakṣipaṃtīti jvālānāṃ vṛṣatvaṃ | yadvā ṣaṣṭhyarthe caturthī | tathā ca mahato atyasya citrasya vṛṣṭo agneḥ bahavo raśmayaḥ vṛṣevācaraṃti | vṛṣṭutpādanadvārā parjanyavadācaraṃti |*) (The supplied text reads "ಚಿತ್ರಬ್ದಕ್ಕೆ" and "ಚಿತ್ರಸ್ಯ ರಶ್ಮಯಃ" at this point, and "ವೃಷ್ಟೋ ಅಗ್ನೇಃ" in the quotation; all as supplied.) This is the same bhāṣya passage already summarised under the Ṛk: the flames spread on all sides act as showerers (sprinklers), since the Hotṛs, seeing Agni kindled by the flames, cast Soma, ghee, milk and other oblations into it; or the dative is in the sense of the genitive, so that the many rays of the great, wonderful, showering Agni act like a rain-giver.

The various flames of Agni, or the rays described in this mantra, spreading everywhere and fulfilling the wishes of the sacrificers, act like Indra, who fulfils the wishes of those who serve him. The Hotṛ and other priests, pouring Soma juice, ghee (clarified butter), oblations and the like into the well-kindled fire, obtain their desired ends; therefore Sāyaṇa explains, as one meaning, that the flames of Agni grant what is desired and, like Indra, bestow wishes. In the second meaning, the dative case in चित्राय (*citrāya*) stands in the sense of the genitive, so that it is चित्रस्य ज्वालाः (*citrasya jvālāḥ*): the flames of the worshipful Agni, shining in many ways and accepting the oblation-portions offered by the sacrificers, become rain-producers (since they cause rain to fall) and do the work of Indra, the production of rain. Hence he explains वृषायंते वृषेवाचरंति (*vṛṣāyaṃte vṛṣevācaraṃti*). Thus the sense is given in two ways: because Agni's flames, kindled and widely spread, accept the oblations offered by the sacrificers and fulfil their wishes, or because, endowed with many colours, they produce rain, they do for Agni and for the world the work of Indra, the fulfilment of desired ends.

**मंद्रतरः (*maṃdrataraḥ*)** — "यजमानस्य मादयितृतमः (*yajamānasya mādayitṛtamaḥ*)". Because Agni fulfils the wishes of the sacrificers, he makes them joyful and exhilarated; this is the meaning. It is said: "मंद्रा मदना हर्षकरी तर्पयित्री वा लोकस्य (*maṃdrā madanā harṣakarī tarpayitrī vā lokasya*)" (Ni. 11-30).

**महः देवा, रोदसी इह आ वह (*mahaḥ devā, rodasī iha ā vaha*)** — By this sentence, since Agni also performs the Hotṛ's function (inviting the gods to the sacrifice), he is prayed to invite to the sacrificial rite the gods who are worshipful and worthy of sacrifice, and also heaven and earth. Addressing Agni, the prayer is that he destroy the sacrificer's sins by the greatness of his abundant flames.

The Nirukta-writers explain the word Agni thus: अग्निः कस्मात् अग्रणीर्भवति अग्रं यज्ञेषु प्रणेयते अंगं नयति सन्नममानः (*agniḥ kasmāt agraṇīrbhavati agraṃ yajñeṣu praṇeyate aṃgaṃ nayati sannamamānaḥ*). Durgācārya explains the analysis अग्रं नयति अग्रणीः भवति (*agraṃ nayati agraṇīḥ bhavati*): the word अग्र (*agra*) means "chief"; सर्वेष्टर्थेषु असावात्मानं अग्रं नयति सेनां वा अग्रे नयति अग्निर्वैदेवानां सेनानीः इति श्रुतेः (*sarveṣṭartheṣu asāvātmānaṃ agraṃ nayati senāṃ vā agre nayati agnirvaidevānāṃ senānīḥ iti śruteḥ*). He takes the first place in all necessary duties; or, being the commander of the gods' armies, Agni leads the gods' army. This is one kind of meaning. Secondly, in sacrifices the fire is prepared first, before all necessary duties; hence the meaning अग्रं प्रणीयते इति अग्निः (*agraṃ praṇīyate iti agniḥ*). Thirdly, wherever he enters he himself is chief and brings all else under his control; hence अंगं नयतीति अग्निः (*aṃgaṃ nayatīti agniḥ*). In all worldly and Vedic works, and even in grass and wood, when Agni has entered and is connected, he keeps the position of being chief; whatever is connected with Agni stays subordinate to him. This is the purport.

The teacher Sthaulāṣṭhivi [sic, as supplied] says अकोपनो भवति न स्नेहयति (*akopano bhavati na snehayati*), and describes Agni's quality: he makes the thing that joins him (that is connected with him) take another form. The teacher Śākapūṇi explains the formation of the word Agni: it is from इतात् (*itāt*), अक्ताद्वा दग्धाद्वा नीतात् (*aktādvā dagdhādvā nītāt*), that is, from the three roots इण् गत् (*iṇ gat*), अंजू व्यक्तिश्लक्ष्ययोः (*aṃjū vyaktiślakṣyayoḥ*), दह भस्मीकरणे (*daha bhasmīkaraṇe*), with the element नी (*nī*) from the root णीज् प्रापणे (*ṇīj prāpaṇe*) added, the form अग्नि (*agni*) results. The Nirukta-writers quote the Ṛk

> ⟦अग्निमीळे पुरोहितं यज्ञस्य देवमृत्वजं । होतारं रत्नधातमं ॥⟧ (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-1-1)

> *⟦agnimīḷe purohitaṃ yajñasya devamṛtvajaṃ | hotāraṃ ratnadhātamaṃ ‖⟧ (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-1-1)*

and say that this Agni is called the terrestrial fire, while the other forms of Agni, such as Jātavedas, are spoken of as middle and highest. In this terrestrial fire the sacrificers invoke the other fires according to the respective rites, to suit their sacrifice. Hence Agni has the identity of all the gods; the Brāhmaṇa sentence अग्निः सर्वा देवताः (*agniḥ sarvā devatāḥ*) and

But: यस्तु सूक्तं भजते यस्मै हविर्निरुप्यते आयमेव सोऽग्निः। निपातमेवैते उत्तरे ज्योतिषी एतेन नामधेयेन भजेते (*yastu sūktaṃ bhajate yasmai havirnirupyate āyameva so 'gniḥ| nipātamevaite uttare jyotiṣī etena nāmadheyena bhajete*) (Ni. 2-7-18). The Agni who is chiefly praised in the various rites, and for whom oblations are prepared with Vedic mantras, is spoken of as the terrestrial fire. The other fires, though in the position of highest and middle (the lightning fire, Jātavedas), are not so spoken of in the world by sentences such as अग्निमानय (*agnimānaya*) ("bring fire"); and अग्नियेषु सूक्तेषु हविः संप्रदानेषु स्तुत्यत्वेन भोक्तृत्वेन चैतदेवोपासीत (*agniyeṣu sūkteṣu haviḥ saṃpradāneṣu stutyatvena bhoktṛtvena caitadevopāsīta*): this terrestrial fire, which is praised in the hymns that describe Agni and is the recipient in the giving of oblations, is to be worshipped, so the injunction says. तस्मादग्निशब्देन पार्थिवस्य ज्योतिषः मुख्यस्संबंधः गौण इतरयोर्मध्यमोत्तमयोः (*tasmādagniśabdena pārthivasya jyotiṣaḥ mukhyassaṃbaṃdhaḥ gauṇa itarayormadhyamottamayoḥ*). ## Sūkta 8 (Maṇḍala 3)

**The eighth sūkta**

**Pīṭhikā (Introduction)**

In this sūkta the sacrificial post (yūpa) is praised. Since the setting up of the yūpa and its consecrations have special importance in the sacrifice, they will be explained here in a special manner. The yūpa is the wooden pillar planted in the sacrifice for binding the animal. It is also called vanaspati; in the Ṛgveda the word vanaspati is the one more used. The word yūpa is used in only five Ṛks, namely यूपः (*yūpaḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-51-14); यूपवाहाः (*yūpavāhāḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-162-6); यूपव्रस्काः (*yūpavraskāḥ*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-162-6); यूपेव (*yūpeva*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-33-3); यूपात् (*yūpāt*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 5-2-7); nowhere else. In the Yajurveda and the like the use of the word yūpa is frequent. Yāska too gives no nirvacana of the word yūpa. Regarding the word vanaspati, when explaining the Ṛk मेद्यंतु ते वह्नयो येभिरीयसे (*medyaṃtu te vahnayo yebhirīyase*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-37-3), he says:

वनस्पत इत्येनमाहैष हि वनानां पाता वा पालयिका वा । वनं वनोशेः । (*vanaspata ityenamāhaiṣa hi vanānāṃ pātā vā pālayikā vā | vanaṃ vanośeḥ |*) (Ni. 8-3)

Durgācārya, commenting on this, says:

यस्मादेषः वनानां वृक्षादीनां अंतर्गतोऽपि समर्थोऽपि दग्गुं न तानि दहति तस्मादयं तेषां पाता रक्षिता पालयिता वेति । (*yasmādeṣaḥ vanānāṃ vṛkṣādīnāṃ aṃtargato 'pi samartho 'pi dagguṃ na tāni dahati tasmādayaṃ teṣāṃ pātā rakṣitā pālayitā veti |*)

That is, Agni, although he is inside trees and the like, does not burn them, and so is called their protector. Here vanaspati is explained as Agni. We have explained this matter at length in commenting on the Āprī sūkta सुसमिद्दो न आ वह (*susamiddo na ā vaha*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-13) (see Ṛgveda Saṃhitā Part 2, pp. 576 and 577). Or, following the derivation वनं वनोतेः (*vanaṃ vanoteḥ*) of the word vana, he says that the tree (vṛkṣa) is used for various purposes, the word being formed from the root vanati/sambhajati ("to resort to, to serve").

Regarding the yūpa there is an old story in the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa (Ai. Brā. 2-1). Its account is given here:

यज्ञेन वै देवा ऊर्ध्वाः स्वर्गं लोकमायंस्तेबिभयुरिमं नो दृष्ट्वा मनुष्याश्च ऋषयश्चानु प्रज्ञास्यंतीति तं वै यूपेनैवायोपयिंस्तं यद्यूपेन्वैवायेपयंस्तद्यूपस्य यूपत्वं तमवाचीनाग्रं निमित्योर्ध्वा उदायंस्ततो वै मनुष्याश्च ऋषयश्च देवानां यज्ञवास्त्वभ्यायन्यज्ञस्य किंचिदेषिष्यामः प्रज्ञात्या इति ते वै यूपमेवाविंदन्न वाचीनाग्रं निमितं ते विदुरनेन वै देवा यज्ञमयूयुपन्निति तमुत्थायोर्ध्वं न्यमिन्वंस्ततो वै ते प्रयज्ञमजानन्न स्वर्गं लोकं । तद्यद्यूप ऊर्ध्वो निमिषायते यज्ञस्य प्रज्ञात्यै स्वर्गस्यलोकस्यानुख्यात्यै ॥ (*yajñena vai devā ūrdhvāḥ svargaṃ lokamāyaṃstebibhayurimaṃ no dṛṣṭvā manuṣyāśca ṛṣayaścānu prajñāsyaṃtīti taṃ vai yūpenaivāyopayiṃstaṃ yadyūpenvaivāyepayaṃstadyūpasya yūpatvaṃ tamavācīnāgraṃ nimityordhvā udāyaṃstato vai manuṣyāśca ṛṣayaśca devānāṃ yajñavāstvabhyāyanyajñasya kiṃcideṣiṣyāmaḥ prajñātyā iti te vai yūpamevāviṃdanna vācīnāgraṃ nimitaṃ te viduranena vai devā yajñamayūyupanniti tamutthāyordhvaṃ nyaminvaṃstato vai te prayajñamajānanna svargaṃ lokaṃ | tadyadyūpa ūrdhvo nimiṣāyate yajñasya prajñātyai svargasyalokasyānukhyātyai ‖*)

(Explanation.) In former times the gods performed the sacrifice called Jyotiṣṭoma and by its power went to the world of heaven. Seeing this sacrificial procedure of the gods, the gods feared that men and seers too would perform sacrifice in the same way and come to heaven, and so they devised a means. To bewilder men and seers, the gods planted a sacrificial post in their sacrifice. Because this post was planted so that the sacrifice might seem fully performed, it got the name yūpa (yūpa meaning "obstruct, make it not come"). When the gods were going to heaven they planted the top (tip) of the yūpa upside down. Afterwards men and seers came there, looked, and found only the yūpa planted upside down. What is its use? Why was it planted? None of these details of the sacrifice were understood. Thinking that the gods must have planted the yūpa upside down in order to deceive them, that is, so that the details of the sacrifice might not be known to them, they pulled it out and planted it correctly, with the top upward. Thereupon [the printed page ends here; the sentence continues on the next page]. ("Mamaṣyarū", sic, for "men", is as supplied.)

Thereupon men and seers came to know the order of the sacrifice and the like, and they too performed the sacrifice duly and obtained the world of heaven. Therefore, so that the sacrificial rite may proceed correctly and heaven may be gained by it, there is the injunction that the yūpa must be planted with its top upward.

> प्रहरति द्विषते भ्रातृव्याय वधं योऽस्य स्तृत्यस्तस्मै स्तरै ।
> वज्रो वै यूपः स एष द्विषतो वध उद्यतस्तिष्ठति तस्माद्दाप्येतर्हि
> यो द्वेषि तस्याप्रियं भवत्यमुष्यायं यूपोऽमुष्यायं

> *praharati dviṣate bhrātṛvyāya vadhaṃ yo 'sya stṛtyastasmai starai |*

> *yūpa iti dṛṣṭvā |*

The yūpa is said to be a part of the thunderbolt weapon (vajra). On this subject there is the śruti sentence of the Taittirīya of the Yajurveda: इंद्रो वृत्राय वज्रं प्राहरत्स श्रेधा व्यभवत्स्फुतीयं यूपस्तृतीयं (*iṃdro vṛtrāya vajraṃ prāharatsa średhā vyabhavatsphutīyaṃ yūpastṛtīyaṃ*). The thunderbolt has eight edges; therefore the yūpa must be made with eight edges (corners or angles). Just as one kills one's enemy with the thunderbolt, so the yūpa too is a cause of the destruction of the enemy. The yūpa stands upright, facing upward, for the slaying of the foe. Therefore, if an enemy of the sacrificer who performs the sacrifice sees the yūpa standing upright, evil will certainly befall him. (The supplied text reads "ಯೂದವನ್ನು" in the first sentence of this page and "ಸ್ಫುತೀಯಂ" for the quotation; printed as supplied.)

> खादिरं यूपं कुर्वीत स्वर्गकामः खादिरेण वै यूपेन देवाः
> स्वर्गं लोकमजयंस्तथैवैतद्यजमानः खादिरेण यूपेन स्वर्गं
> लोकं जयति ॥

> *khādiraṃ yūpaṃ kurvīta svargakāmaḥ khādireṇa vai yūpena devāḥ*
> *svargaṃ lokamajayaṃstathaivaitadyajamānaḥ khādireṇa yūpena svargaṃ*
> *lokaṃ jayati ‖*

It is the custom to make this yūpa from various kinds of tree; there is a rule that if it is made from such and such a tree, such and such a fruit is obtained. That is explained here. Sacrificers desiring heaven must make the yūpa from the particular tree called khādira. The gods made the yūpa of khādira wood in their sacrifice and so obtained the world of heaven. Therefore sacrificers who desire heaven, if they sacrifice with a khādira yūpa, will obtain the world of heaven.

> बैल्वं यूपं कुर्वीतान्नाद्यकामः पुष्टिकामः समां समां वै
> बिल्वो गृभीतस्तदन्नाद्यस्य रूपमामूलाच्छाखाभिरनुचितस्तत्पुष्टेः

> *bailvaṃ yūpaṃ kurvītānnādyakāmaḥ puṣṭikāmaḥ samāṃ samāṃ vai*

> *puṣyati prajāṃ ca paśūṃśca ya evaṃ vidvānpailvaṃ yūpaṃ kurute ‖*

One who desires food (nourishment) and prosperity must make the yūpa from the bilva tree, because the bilva tree bears fruit every year. The bilva tree is a sign of food and prosperity. From the root to the tip all its branches produce increase every year, that is, it keeps growing large; therefore this tree is a sign of prosperity.

Among these bilva trees there is a special kind called the śrīvṛkṣa or jyotirvṛkṣa. If one makes the yūpa from such a tree and sacrifices, splendour arises. The sacrificer who knows this and sacrifices after making the yūpa from this particular kind of tree becomes full of splendour and the foremost (esteemed) among his own people.

One who desires splendour (tejas) and spiritual radiance (brahmavarcasa) must make the yūpa from the palāśa (mutta) tree. The palāśa tree is the source of all the trees of the forest (vanaspati), and among them is the most full of splendour and of spiritual radiance. Therefore any man who knows this and uses in the sacrifice a yūpa made of palāśa wood becomes endowed with splendour and with spiritual radiance. Its meaning is: "O yūpa, rise up on the earth. Until now you have been lying on the earth. Now rise and stand up, make the sacrifice go forward, and bestow splendour on us." Because the word उच्छयस्व (*ucchayasva*) ("stand up") occurs in this Ṛk, its recital is appropriate to the occasion. By the recital of a mantra whose meaning is so apt to the occasion the sacrifice is well accomplished. For there are many sacrificers who perform sacrifices; the gods go only to the sacrifice of the one who invites them saying "come to my sacrifice, come to mine". The meaning is that the gods go to those Hotṛs who know this matter well and who invite them with mantras and the like.

Once upon a time the animals ran away from the gods, so that the gods could not take them as they pleased. Having gone a little way, they stood there and said to the gods: "O gods, you cannot get us; we are not for you." Hearing this, the gods remembered the sacrificial post (yūpa) they had set up at the sacrifice, and looked at it. The animals, frightened by this, came and stood facing the gods. For this very reason it is still the custom today to tie the animals at a sacrifice so that they stand facing the yūpa. The animals that thus stood quietly, without running away from them, the gods accepted for their food. The animals of the sacrificer who knows this well stand quietly beside the yūpa set up high, without trouble, to be accepted by the gods. Therefore the sacrificer who desires cattle should not pull out the sacrificial post but leave it standing as it is. But on behalf of a sacrificer who desires heaven the Adhvaryu pulls out the post and puts it into the fire. For in former times many sacrificers, after the rite of binding the animal was complete, pulled out the posts and put them into the fire. For it is said: the sacrificer himself is the yūpa, the sacrificer himself is the darbha grass called prastara, and the fire is the womb (place of origin) of the gods. By offering the oblation in this way the sacrificer, with a golden body, goes to heaven, the abode of the gods.

> *pṛthivyai tvāṃtarikṣāya tvā dive tvā śuṃdhatāṃ lokaḥ pitṛṣadano*
> *yavo 'si yavayāsmadveṣo yavayārātīḥ pitṛṇāgṃ sadanamasi*
> *svāveśo 'syagregā netraṇāṃ vanaspatiradhi tvā sthāsyati tasya vittādde-*
> *vastvā savitā madhvānakta supippalābhyasauṣadhībhya uddivagga stabhānāṃta-*
> *rikṣaṃ pṛṇa prathivīmupareṇa dṛgṃha te te dhāmānyutmasi gamadhyè*
> *gāvo yatra bhūriśṛṃgā ayāsaḥ | atrāha tadurugāyasya viṣṇoḥ*
> *paramaṃ padamava bhāti bhūreḥ | viṣṇoḥ karmāṇi paśyata yato vratāni*
> *paspaśe | iṃdrasya yujyassakhā | tadviṣṇoḥ paramaṃ padagṃ sadā paśyaṃti*
> *sūrayaḥ | divīva cakṣurātataṃ | brahmavaniṃ tvā kṣatravanigṃ suprajāva-*
> *nigṃ rāyaspoṣavaniṃ paryūhāmi brahma dṛgṃha kṣatraṃ dṛgṃha*
> *prajāṃ dṛgṃha rāyaspoṣaṃ dṛgṃha parivīrasi pari tvā daivīrviśo*
> *vyayaṃtāṃ parīmag rāyaspoṣo yajamānaṃ manuṣyā aṃtarikṣasya*
> *tvā sānāvava gūhāmi ‖*

The two Anuvākas that are in the form of a Brāhmaṇa for the above mantras, that is, that explain their meaning and application, are in the sixth Kāṇḍa, third Prapāṭhaka of the Taittirīya Saṃhitā; they are explained here with their meaning:

> वैष्णव्यर्चा हुत्वा यूपमच्छैति वैष्णवो वै देवतया यूपः स्वयै-
> वैनं देवतयोच्छैत्यत्यन्यानगां नान्यानुपागामित्याहाति ह्यस्यानेति
> नान्यानुपैत्यर्वाक्त्वा परैरविदं परोवरॆरित्याहार्वाग्न्येनं परैर्विं-
> दति परोवरै स्तं त्वा जुषे वैष्णवं देवयज्याया इत्याह देवय-
> ज्यायै हैनं जुषते देवस्त्वा सविता मध्वानक्तित्याह तेजसैवैनम-
> नक्कोषधे त्रायस्वैनग् स्वधिते मैनग् हिंग्‌सीरित्याह वज्रो वै
> स्वधितिः शांतै स्वधितेर्वक्षस्य बिभ्यतः प्रथमेन शकलेन सह तेजः परा
> पतति यःप्रथमः शकलः इरापतेत्तमप्या हरेश्वतेजसमेवैनमा हरती
> मे वैलोका यूपात्पयतो बिभ्यति दिवमग्रेण मा लेखीरंतरिक्षं
> मध्यॆन मा हिंग्‌सीरिष्याहैभ्य एवैनं लोकेभ्यः शमयति वनस्पते
> शतवल्‌शो वि रोहेत्याव्रश्चने जुहोति तस्मदाव्रश्चनाद्वृक्षाणां
