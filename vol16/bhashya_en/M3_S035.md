## Introduction
The sūkta beginning ⟦तिष्ठा हरी⟧ has eleven Ṛks; it is the sixth sūkta, belonging to Viśvāmitra, in the triṣṭubh metre, addressed to Indra. So says the Anukramaṇikā: ⟦तिष्ठा हरी⟧. In the ⟦अग्निष्टुति⟧, in the Marutvatīya śastra, it is the sūkta in which the nivid is set (⟦निविद्वानं⟧). And it is taught in the sūtra: ⟦तिष्ठा हरी यो जात एवेति मध्यंदिनः⟧ (⟦आ. ९-७⟧), that is, the sūkta beginning ⟦तिष्ठा हरी⟧ and the one beginning ⟦यो जात एव⟧ are for the midday.

Also, in the Ekāha called Kulāyana of Indra and Agni, this too is the sūkta in which the nivid is set, in the Marutvatīya. And it is taught in the sūtra: ⟦तिष्ठा हरी तमु ष्टुही⟧ — thus the midday (⟦आ. ९-७⟧).

In the Daśarātra ⟦समूढे⟧, in the third Chandoma, this sūkta is used in the Marutvatīya śastra. And it is taught in the sūtra: ⟦तिष्ठा हरी प्र मुंदिने इमा उ त्वेति मरुत्वतीयं⟧ (⟦आ. ८-७⟧).

In the Atirātra, in the third paryāya, when the hotṛ's śastra has been concluded, ⟦सत्याद्या⟧ **NOTE**{the word ⟦सत्याद्या⟧ is unclear in the supplied text and in the reference text alike; is it "sati ādyā" or a single word, and what does it qualify?} this Ṛk beginning ⟦तिष्ठा हरी⟧ is the yājyā of the śastra. And it is taught in the sūtra: ⟦विश्वजिते तिष्ठा हरी रथ आ युज्यमानेति याज्या⟧ (⟦आ. ६-४⟧).

## Ṛk 1
Viśvāmitra praises. O Indra, keeping in view the two horses ⟦हरी⟧ that are being yoked (⟦युज्यमाना⟧, glossed as "being yoked together") to the chariot (⟦रथे⟧, glossed as the car), stay (⟦तिष्ठ⟧, with the preverb ā) for some time. The illustration for this is ⟦वायुर्न⟧, "like Vāyu": as Vāyu stays for some time, keeping in view the niyuts (⟦नियुतः⟧), which are the mares being yoked to the chariot, so you do. Then, mounted on the chariot, come (⟦याहि⟧) to us (⟦नो⟧ is "us", ⟦अच्छ⟧ is "having us in view"); that is, come quickly. And having come, you who have been permitted by us (⟦अभिसृष्टो⟧ is glossed "permitted", ⟦अस्मे⟧ is "by us") drink (⟦पिबासि⟧ means "drink") the food (⟦अंधः⟧) that has the form of Soma. ⟦स्वाहा⟧: and this Soma has been given. We, for your delight (⟦ते⟧ is "your", ⟦मदाय⟧ is glossed "for delight"), give (⟦ररिम⟧ means "we give") this Soma, offered with svāhā.

Here, in ⟦याहि वायुर्न नियुतो नो अच्छ⟧, the meaning is "come with the chariot". In the Ṛgveda passage ⟦आपत्तित्पिप्युः स्तर्यो न गावः⟧ (⟦ऋग्वं ७-२३-४⟧) "that is not so", and therefore it is not left out at the time of the Pada-pāṭha. **NOTE**{the connection of ⟦तन्न तथेति⟧ and what is "not left out" is not clear; also the quoted Ṛg-verse is damaged in the supplied text and is left untranslated here because Sāyaṇa does not gloss it}

⟦तिष्ठ⟧: the form of the verb tiṣṭhati in loṭ; the substitute tiṣṭha has the first syllable udātta. ⟦याहि⟧: the form of the verb yāti in loṭ; because the ending hi is marked ṅ, the final syllable is udātta. ⟦वायुः⟧: from the root vā, in the senses of going and gandhana, by the Uṇādi rule beginning ⟦कृवापे⟧ the suffix uṇ; then the augment yuk is added after the ā, by ⟦आतो युक्⟧; the accent is that of the suffix. ⟦पिबासि⟧: the form of the verb pāti in leṭ, with the augment āḍ; since the augment is anudātta, the accent is that of the root. ⟦अंधः⟧: from the root ad, in the sense of eating, by ⟦अदेर्नु० धश्च⟧ the suffix asun, the augment num, and dh as the substitute for the final letter. He explains it: ⟦आद्यते⟧, it is eaten by living beings, or it itself eats (⟦स्वयमत्ती⟧). And so the śruti says: ⟦अद्यतेऽत्ति च भूतानि तस्मादन्नं तदुच्यते⟧ (⟦त्व उ. २-२⟧), "It is eaten, and it eats beings; therefore that is called food." The accent is the nit-accent. ⟦अस्मे⟧: by ⟦सुपां सुलुग⟧ the instrumental ending is replaced by śe. ⟦ररिम⟧: the form of the root rā in liṭ; because the sentence is divided, there is no nighāta; the accent is that of the suffix.

## Ṛk 2
O Indra, to you (⟦पुरुहूताय⟧: called to the sacrifice by many, that is, by numerous sacrificers) I yoke the two (horses) ⟦अजिरा⟧, which are of swift-going nature, ⟦सप्ति⟧, which are of gliding nature, ⟦हरी⟧, the two horses named Hari, at the ends of the yoke of the chariot (⟦रथस्य धूर्षु⟧): ⟦उप⟧ ⟦युनज्मि⟧: I yoke them in such a way. ⟦द्रवत्⟧: "in such a way that that chariot may go"; this is what is to be supplied. ⟦विश्वतः⟧ ⟦चित्⟧: from every side; this ⟦यज्ञं⟧, sacrifice, is ⟦संभृतं⟧, filled with the requisites of the sacrifice, such as ⟦अग्निर्यजुर्भिः सविता स्तोमैरित्यादि⟧, "Agni with the yajus-formulas, Savitṛ with the stomas", and so on. Towards you, Indra, may those two horses carry (⟦उप⟧ ⟦वहातः⟧; ⟦वहातः⟧ is glossed "may they carry well") you here.

⟦धूर्षु⟧: from the root dhurvī, in the sense of harming, the suffix kvip; the dropping of the letter v by the rule ⟦राल्लोप⟧; the lengthening by the rule ⟦र्वोरुप धाया⟧, which concerns r and v and the penultimate; by ⟦सावेकाच⟧ the case-ending is udātta. ⟦युनज्मि⟧: the root yujir, in the sense of joining, of the rudh class; the form in laṭ; the accent is nighāta. ⟦द्रवत्⟧: from the root dru, in the sense of going, the form in the Vedic laṅ; by vyatyaya the final syllable is udātta. ⟦वहातः⟧: the form of the root vah in leṭ, with the augment āḍ.

## Ṛk 3
O Indra, ⟦वृषभ⟧ is "showerer of desires"; ⟦स्वधावः⟧ is "possessing food". Bring up to us (⟦उपो नयस्व⟧ is glossed "bring near to us") the two horses ⟦वृषणा⟧, which are capable of sprinkling, ⟦तपुष्पा⟧, which are protectors from the enemies that torment. ⟦उत⟧ means "also": you also protect (⟦अव⟧ is glossed "protect") this sacrificer (⟦ईं⟧ is "this one"). ⟦शोणा⟧: those two horses of red colour; release them (⟦वि मुच⟧) here, in this place of divine worship. Released, may those two horses graze (⟦ग्रसेतां⟧ is glossed "may they eat the ⟦विघसं⟧"). But you eat (⟦अद्धि⟧ is glossed "eat") every day (⟦दिवेदिवे⟧ is glossed "each day") the ⟦धानाः⟧, roasted barley, ⟦सदृशीः⟧, of one uniform form.

⟦नयस्व⟧: the form of the verb nayati in loṭ. ⟦तपुष्पा⟧: the root pā, in the sense of protecting; the suffix ka after a root ending in ā when there is no preverb; by ⟦सुपां सुलुग⟧ the substitute ḍā; by the rule ⟦इसुसोः सामर्थ्य⟧, which concerns is and us, in the saṃhitā the visarjanīya becomes ṣ; the accent is that in which a second member ending in a kṛt suffix keeps its own accent. ⟦अव⟧: the form of the verb avati in loṭ. ⟦स्वधावः⟧: by ⟦मतुवसोरिति⟧ the letter n is replaced by ru; the accent is nighāta. ⟦ग्रसेतां⟧: from the root grasu, in the sense of eating; the form in loṭ, in the ending ātām; because it stands at the beginning of the pāda there is no nighāta; as to the ending ātām, in the matter of the accent of the sārvadhātuka l-ending, the accent is that of the root. ⟦मुच⟧: the form of the verb muñcati in loṭ; since the teaching of augments is not constant, there is no num. ⟦सदृशीः⟧: when the word samāna stands as the upapada, from the root dṛśir, in the sense of seeing, the suffix kañ by ⟦समानान्ययोश्च⟧; the substitution of sa for samāna by ⟦समानस्य दृग्दश्वतुष्टिति⟧; the feminine suffix ṅīp (⟦जेप्⟧) by the rule beginning ⟦टिडाणञ्⟧; since it is marked p, it is anudātta, and so the accent is that of the root. ⟦अद्धि⟧: from the root ad, in the sense of eating, in loṭ; because the root is of the adādi class, śap is dropped; by ⟦हुरुल्भो हेर्धिरिति⟧ the substitute dhi (for hi); the accent is nighāta. ⟦धानाः⟧: the root ḍudhāñ, in the senses of holding and nourishing; the suffix na by ⟦धाप्नवस्यज्यतिभ्यो न⟧; the accent is that of the suffix.

## Ṛk 4
On the Cāturviṃśika day, in the midday pressing, in the śastra of the Brāhmaṇācchaṃsin, the verse beginning ⟦ब्रह्मणा ते⟧ is the ārambhaṇīyā. And it is taught in the sūtra: ⟦ब्रह्मणा ते ब्रह्मयुजा युनज्म्युरुं नो लोकमनुनेषि विद्वान्⟧ (⟦आ- ७-४⟧), "With the brahman I yoke your two brahman-yoked horses; knowing, you will lead us to the wide world."

O Indra, the two horses are ⟦ब्रह्मयुजा⟧, to be yoked with brahman, that is, with a mantra, ⟦सधमादे⟧. The warriors rejoice (⟦माद्यंति⟧) in it; therefore ⟦माद⟧ is battle (yuddha). ⟦सहमादः⟧ is ⟦सधमादः⟧. In that battle, ⟦सखाया⟧ are the two of equal renown, and therefore ⟦आशू⟧, endowed with swift going: your two horses ⟦हरी⟧, that is, your two horses, I yoke (⟦युनज्मि⟧ is glossed "I join to the chariot") with brahman (⟦ब्रह्मणा⟧ is glossed "with praise"). You, mounting (⟦रथमधितिष्ठन्⟧: the word is glossed "mounted") the chariot (⟦रथं⟧) which is ⟦स्थिरं⟧, very firm, ⟦सुखं⟧, with a good opening, and of such a kind, come for the drinking of Soma. Of ⟦प्रजानन्⟧ and so on: ⟦प्रजानन्नित्याद्यगमत्⟧. **NOTE**{the meaning of this closing remark on ⟦प्रजानन्⟧ is not clear in the supplied text, and the reference text reads the same}

⟦ब्रह्मयुजा⟧: from the root yuj, the Uṇādi suffix kvip, in the sense of the object. ⟦युनज्मि⟧: the form of the root yuj in laṭ; the accent is nighāta. **NOTE**{the reference text has "liṭ" here where the supplied text has "laṭ"} ⟦सधमादे⟧: the root madī, in the sense of joy; the suffix ghañ in the sense of the locus; by the rule ⟦सध मादस्थयोष्पंदसीति⟧ the substitute sadha for saha; the accent is that in which a second member ending in a kṛt suffix keeps its own accent. ⟦रथं⟧: by the rule ⟦अधिशीज्‌स्थासां कर्म⟧ it has the designation karman, object. ⟦सुखं⟧: the root khanu, in the sense of splitting; by ⟦अन्वेभ्योऽपि दृश्यत इति⟧ the suffix ḍa; the accent is that of a second member ending in a kṛt suffix.

## Ṛk 5
O Indra, your two horses ⟦हरी⟧, ⟦वृषणा⟧, which are sprinklers of desires, ⟦वीतपृष्ठा⟧, with lovely back-parts: let the other sacrificers (⟦यजमानासः⟧ is glossed "sacrificers"; ⟦अन्ये⟧ is "others than we") not gladden them (⟦मा नि रीरमन्⟧ is glossed "let them not gladden"). We, with the pressed (⟦सुतेभिः⟧ is glossed "pressed") Soma, make (⟦कृणवाम⟧) ⟦अरं⟧, sufficient, for you; that is, we do so that you are satisfied. You, however, pass beyond (⟦अत्यायाहि⟧) the ⟦शश्वतः⟧, the many others, and come quickly for the drinking of Soma.

⟦वीतपृष्ठा⟧: the root vī, in the senses beginning with kānti, desire; from it the suffix kta; in a bahuvrīhi the accent is that of the first member in its natural form. ⟦रीरमन्⟧: the root ramu, in the sense of play, in its causative, the form in luṅ with caṅ; by the rule ⟦न माज्योग⟧ there is absence of the augment aḍ; the accent is nighāta. ⟦अत्यायाहि⟧: the form of the verb yāti in loṭ; by the rule ⟦गतिर्गताविति⟧ the preceding word has nighāta. ⟦कृणवाम⟧: the form of the verb kṛṇoti in loṭ; the accent is nighāta.

## Ṛk 6
⟦तवायं सोम⟧ is, in the midday pressing, the prasthita-yājyā of the Neṣṭṛ. And it is taught in the sūtra: ⟦तनायं सोमस्य मेह्यर्नाजिंद्राय सोमाः प्रदिवः⟧ (⟦आ. ५-५⟧).

O Indra, this Soma is yours, pressed for you. You (⟦त्वं⟧) come (⟦इहि⟧ is glossed "come") ⟦अर्वाज्⟧, facing the Soma. And having come, being ⟦सुमनाः⟧, of good mind, drink (⟦पाहि⟧ is glossed "drink") this Soma (⟦अस्य⟧ is glossed "this Soma") abundantly (⟦शश्वत्तमं⟧ is glossed "abundantly"). Having sat down, that is, having taken your seat, on the barhis (⟦बर्हिषि⟧), ⟦प्रवृद्धे⟧, grown, in this sacrifice, hold (⟦दधिष्ट⟧ is glossed "hold") in the belly (⟦जठरे⟧) this Soma ⟦इंदुं⟧, which is of a moistening nature. Hold it in such a way that it does not go below the navel. **NOTE**{what ⟦प्रवृद्धे⟧ qualifies, the barhis or the sacrifice, is not clear}

In the passage ⟦अंगिरोभिरागहि यज्जियेभिः⟧ (⟦ऋग्वॆ. १०-१४-५⟧), "Come with the Aṅgirases who belong to the sacrifice", here it is not an epithet of the sacrifice, or it is of a form other than the barhis; "that": therefore it is not left out. **NOTE**{the sentence ⟦तदित्यतो नोत्सृज्यते⟧ is unclear: what is "not left out" and why}

⟦इहि⟧: the root iṇ, in the sense of going; the form of it in loṭ; the accent is nighāta. ⟦शश्वत्तमं⟧: because it belongs to the group beginning with uñcha, the final syllable is udātta. ⟦पाहि⟧: the root pā, in the sense of drinking, the form in loṭ; by the rule ⟦बहुलं छंदसीति⟧ śap is dropped; the accent is nighāta. ⟦निषद्य⟧: the form of the root sad in lyap; by the rule ⟦सदेरप्रतेरिति⟧ the change to ṣ; the accent is the lit-accent. ⟦दधिष्ट⟧: the form of the verb dadhāti in loṭ; by the rule ⟦छंदस्युभयथेति⟧, since the ending thās is ārdhadhātuka, there is the augment iṭ; the accent is that of the suffix. **NOTE**{the reference text has only the word here and lacks this explanation of the augment} ⟦इंदुं⟧: the root undī, in the sense of moistening; by the rule ⟦उन्पेरिच्चादेरित्यु⟧ the suffix u; the substitution of i for the initial letter of the root; because of the continuation of "nit", the first syllable is udātta.

## Ṛk 7
O Indra, for your sitting the barhis has been spread, ⟦स्तीर्णं⟧, extended. And Soma has been pressed (⟦सुतः⟧ is glossed "pressed") for you. For your two horses ⟦हरिभ्यां⟧, for eating (⟦अत्तवे⟧ is glossed "for the sake of eating"), the ⟦धानाः⟧, roasted barley, have been made (⟦कृताः⟧ is glossed "prepared"). To you ⟦तदोकसे⟧, for whom that barhis is the abode, the dwelling (⟦ओक⟧ is glossed "dwelling"), ⟦पुरुशाकाय⟧, who are praised by many, or alternatively who have many helpers, ⟦वृष्णे⟧, the showerer of desires, ⟦मरुत्वते⟧, the Maruts being the divine people, who are possessed of them: to you of this kind the oblations (⟦हवींषि⟧), Soma, ghee and so on, have been given (⟦राता⟧ is glossed "given") by us. The meaning is: partake of them. **NOTE**{the reference text reads devavitaḥ where the supplied text has ⟦देवविशः⟧; the translation follows the supplied text}

⟦स्तीर्णं⟧: the root stṛñ, in the sense of covering; by the rule ⟦ऋत इद्दातो⟧ the substitution of i; by the rule ⟦रदाभ्यामिति⟧ the n of the niṣṭhā ending; the accent is that of the suffix. ⟦अत्तवे⟧: the root ad, in the sense of eating, in the sense of the infinitive, the suffix taven; the accent is the nit-accent. ⟦तदोकसे⟧: in a bahuvrīhi the accent is that of the first member. ⟦पुरुशाकाय⟧: the root śac, in the sense of clear speech, in the sense of the object the suffix ghañ; by the rule ⟦चजोः कु घिण्यतोरिति⟧ the change to a guttural; the accent is that of a second member ending in a kṛt suffix. ⟦मरुत्वते⟧: by the rule ⟦तसौ मत्त्वर्थ⟧ it has the designation bha, and so there is no change of the letter t to its voiced counterpart; because the suffix is marked p, it is anudātta, and so the accent is that of the nominal stem. ⟦राता⟧: the root rā, in the sense of giving, the suffix kta in the sense of the object; the accent is that of the suffix.

## Ṛk 8
O Indra, the ⟦नरः⟧, the leaders of rites, the Adhvaryus and others, the ⟦पर्वताः⟧, the pressing stones (⟦ग्रावाण⟧), and the ⟦आपः⟧, the waters: all these together have, for you (⟦तुभ्यं⟧ is glossed "for you"), ⟦समक्रन्⟧, made well, this Soma, ⟦मधुमंतं⟧, endowed with sweetness, with ⟦गोभिः⟧, that is, with milk. O ⟦ऋष्व⟧, Indra worth seeing, ⟦सुमनाः⟧, of good mind, ⟦विद्वान्⟧, knowing the rites, knowing ⟦प्रजानन्⟧ the praises ⟦अनु⟧, which are ⟦स्वाः⟧, your own, and ⟦पथ्याः⟧, good in the Vedic path, that is, in the rite: you, drink of it (⟦तस्य⟧); that is, drink this pressed Soma.

⟦अक्रन्⟧: the form of the verb karoti in luṅ; the accent is nighāta. ⟦आगत्य⟧: the form of the root gam in lyap; the accent is the lit-accent. ⟦पाहि⟧: the form of the verb pāti in loṭ.

## Ṛk 9
O Indra, the Maruts ⟦यान्⟧ whom you ⟦अभजः⟧ honoured ⟦सोमे⟧, in the matter of the drinking of Soma; and the Maruts ⟦ये⟧ who ⟦त्वां⟧ ⟦अवर्धन्⟧ made you grow, that is, made you grow in battle by producing encouragement; and the Maruts who ⟦अभवन्⟧ became ⟦ते⟧ your ⟦गणः⟧ troop; the meaning is that they became helpers. O Indra, together with such Maruts (⟦तेभिः⟧ is glossed "with those Maruts"), ⟦सजोषाः⟧, united, desiring (⟦वावशानः⟧ is glossed "desiring") this pressed Soma (⟦एतं⟧ is glossed "pressed"), drink this Soma with the tongue (⟦जिह्वया⟧) of Agni (⟦अग्नेः⟧), which has the form of flame; Agni here is the Āhavanīya, the locus of the oblation. **NOTE**{the reference text reads juhvayā, "with the ladle", where the supplied text has ⟦जिह्वया⟧, "with the tongue"}

⟦अभजः⟧: the root bhaj, in the sense of serving, the form in laṅ; because of the connection with the relative there is no nighāta. ⟦अवर्धन्⟧: the root vṛdhu, in the sense of growth, in its causative, the form in laṅ; by the rule ⟦छंदस्युभयथेति⟧, because the ending jhi is ārdhadhātuka, by the rule ⟦णॆरनिटीति⟧ there is the loss of ṇi; here too, because of the connection with the relative, there is no nighāta. ⟦अभवन्⟧: because the sentence is divided, there is no nighāta. ⟦वावशानः⟧: the root vaś, in the sense of kānti, desire, the form with yaṅluk and the suffix cānaś; the accent is the cit-accent. ⟦पिब⟧: the root pā, in the sense of drinking; the accent is nighāta.

## Ṛk 10
O Indra, ⟦स्वधया⟧: ⟦स्वधा⟧ is strength; it ⟦दधाति⟧ sustains, that is, ⟦पोषयतीति⟧ nourishes, ⟦स्वमात्मानं⟧ itself. With that strength. ⟦चित्⟧ has the sense of an alternative. Having taken it by force, drink ⟦सुतस्य⟧, the pressed, ⟦सुतमभिषुतं⟧, or the offered. O ⟦यजत्र⟧, Indra who are to be worshipped, or drink (⟦पाहि⟧ is glossed "drink") ⟦अग्नेः⟧ the Soma offered with the tongue (⟦जिह्वया⟧) of Agni, which has the form of flame. O ⟦शक्र⟧, capable Indra, drink the Soma ⟦प्रयतं⟧, set about to be given, ⟦हस्तात्⟧ from the hand of the ⟦अध्वर्योः⟧ Adhvaryu. Or else enjoy (⟦जुषस्व⟧ is glossed "partake") from the ⟦होतुः⟧ hotṛ the ⟦यज्ञं⟧, that which is to be offered, ⟦वषट्कारोपेतं⟧, provided with the vaṣaṭ-call, the portion of the ⟦हविषः⟧ oblation. The meaning is: in any case, you, having come here, drink the Soma being given by us. **NOTE**{the reference text has "sumatam" where the supplied text has ⟦सुतमभिषुतं⟧, and a different word where the supplied text has ⟦वषट्कारोपेतं⟧}

⟦पिब⟧: because the vocative is treated as non-existent, and the verb stands at the beginning of the pāda, there is no nighāta. ⟦अध्वर्योः⟧: "he desires adhvara", with the suffix kyac; by the rule ⟦कव्यध्वर⟧ there is the loss of a; the accent is that of the suffix; then the accent of the single substitute. ⟦प्रयतं⟧: the root yama, in the sense of ceasing; the niṣṭhā suffix in the sense of the object. ⟦शक्र⟧: the root śaklṛ, in the sense of power; by the rule beginning ⟦स्थायितंची⟧ the suffix rak. ⟦हस्तात्⟧: by the rule beginning ⟦असिहसी⟧ the suffix tan; the accent is the nit-accent. ⟦होतुः⟧: from the verb juhoti, the suffix tṛn in its sense of habit; the accent is the nit-accent. ⟦जुषस्व⟧: the root juṣī, in the senses of liking and serving, the form in loṭ; the accent is nighāta.

## Ṛk 11
O Indra, ⟦वाजसातौ⟧: that in which there is the gain (⟦साति⟧) of food (⟦वाज⟧) is ⟦वाजसातिः⟧, the one so called. In ⟦अस्मिन्⟧ ⟦भरे⟧: the warriors bear (⟦बिभ्रति⟧) the goddess of victory by it; thus bhara is battle. In that battle ⟦शुनं⟧, śūna, grown by exertion; or ⟦शुनं⟧ is a name for happiness, bringing happiness. ⟦मघवानं⟧, wealthy; and therefore ⟦इंद्रं⟧ Indra, endowed with unsurpassed lordship; ⟦नृतमं⟧, the greatest leader of the whole world: we would call (⟦हुवेम⟧) you; we Kuśikas would call you for the sacrifice. Likewise ⟦शृण्वंतं⟧, hearing the praise made by us; ⟦उग्रं⟧, terrible to enemies; ⟦समत्सु⟧, in battles; ⟦घ्नंतं⟧, harming, ⟦वृत्राणि⟧, all the Rākṣasas marked by Vṛtra; ⟦संजितं⟧, completely conquering ⟦धनानां⟧ the wealth belonging to enemies: you, for ⟦ऊतये⟧, for protection, we would call. **NOTE**{the reference text reads sūnam where the supplied text has ⟦शूनमुत्साहेन⟧}

⟦शुनं⟧: the root ṭuośvi, in the senses of going and growth; in the niṣṭhā suffix, by the rule ⟦यस्य विभाषेतीट्⟧ there is prohibition of iṭ; because the root belongs to the yajādi group, saṃprasāraṇa; the absence of lengthening is Vedic; the accent is that of the suffix. ⟦हुवेम⟧: the verb hvayati; by the rule ⟦बहुलं छंदसीति⟧ saṃprasāraṇa having been done, and the dropping of śap having been done, the form is in liṅ; the accent is nighāta. ⟦भरे⟧: the root bhṛñ, in the sense of bearing; the suffix gha; because it belongs to the vṛṣādi group, the first syllable is udātta. ⟦वाजसातौ⟧: the root ṣaṇu, in the sense of giving; in the sense of the abstract noun the suffix ktin; by the rule beginning ⟦जनसन⟧ the nasal is replaced by ā; in a bahuvrīhi the accent is that of the first member. ⟦समत्सु⟧: the root ad, in the sense of eating; after sam the suffix kvip; ⟦संभक्षयंति⟧: they consume the lives of the warriors; thus ⟦समदः⟧ are battles; the accent is that of a second member ending in a kṛt suffix. ⟦घ्नंतं⟧: the root han, in the senses of harming and going; in śatṛ, by the rule beginning ⟦गमहन⟧ the loss of the penultimate; by ⟦हो हंतेरिति⟧ the change to gh. ⟦संजितं⟧: the root ji, in the sense of conquering; the suffix kvip.
