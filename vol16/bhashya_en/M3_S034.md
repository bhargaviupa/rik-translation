## Introduction

The sūkta ⟦इंद्रः पूर्विद⟧ ("Indra the breaker of forts"), of eleven Ṛks, the fifth sūkta, is of Viśvāmitra, in the triṣṭubh metre, addressed to Indra. Here is the Anukramaṇikā: ⟦इंद्रः पूर्विदेकादश⟧ (Indra, the breaker of forts, eleven). In the agniṣṭoma, at the midday pressing, in the śastra of the brāhmaṇācchaṃsin, there is the sūkta ⟦इंद्रः पूर्विद⟧. It is taught in the section called ⟦होत्रकाणाम⟧: ⟦इंद्रः पूर्भिदुदु ब्रह्माणि⟧ (Indra the breaker of forts; up, indeed, the prayers) (⟦आ. ५ १६⟧).

On the first day of the pṛṣṭhya and abhiplava ṣaḍahas, at the midday pressing, in the śastra of the brāhmaṇācchaṃsin, three sampāta sūktas stand in place of the ahīna sūkta that is extended to it from the cāturviṃśika. Of these, this is the first: ⟦इंद्रः पूर्विद्य एक इद्यस्तिगृंगः⟧ (Indra the breaker of forts, who alone indeed, who is …) (⟦आ. ७५⟧) **NOTE**{the supplied text has a damaged word at the end of this quotation and the reference text a differently damaged one; which word is intended is not certain, so the last word is left untranslated}.

In the niṣkevalya of the udbhid and valabhid days this sūkta is the nividdhānīya. And it is taught: ⟦इंद्र सोममिंद्रः पूर्विद⟧ (Indra, Soma; Indra, the breaker of forts): thus for the midday (pressing) (⟦आ ९-८⟧).

## Ṛk 1

Viśvāmitra praises. Pūrbhid is the breaker of the cities of the asuras. For that very reason he is Vidadvasu, that is, joined with vasus that make known and proclaim his own greatness. He is ⟦दयमानो⟧, specially harming the enemies, the asuras. And so Yāska says: ⟦विदद्वसुर्दयमानो वि शत्रून⟧, and this expresses the action of harming (⟦नि. ४-१७⟧).

Such an Indra (⟦अतिरत्⟧, that is, he made grow all around) the ⟦दासं⟧. Dāsa is the day, since by it the darkness is destroyed (⟦दस्यते⟧). He made that day grow with his ⟦अर्कैः⟧, that is, with his own worshipful radiances, because Indra is of the nature of the sun. And so the Smṛti says: ⟦चैत्रमासे तपेदिंद्र⟧ (in the month of Caitra Indra gives heat).

Moreover, ⟦ब्रह्मजूतः⟧ means drawn by brahman, that is, by a hymn of praise. Or else: impelled by Brahmā, the creator of the world. ⟦ववृधानः⟧ means growing, with his own body (⟦तन्वा⟧). ⟦भूरिदात्रः⟧: a dātra is a weapon, since by it the head of an enemy is cut (⟦दायते लूयते⟧); so he is possessed of weapons of many kinds. Such an Indra satisfied on all sides (⟦अपृणत्⟧) both ⟦रोदसी⟧, heaven and earth.

Pūrbhid (⟦पूर्भीत्⟧): from the root ⟦भिदिर्⟧, in the sense of splitting, with the suffix kvip.

Atirat (⟦अतिरत्⟧): from the root tṛ, with the suffix śa by vyatyaya (irregular interchange); by the rule ⟦ऋत इद्दातो⟧ the substitution of i for the ṛ. The accent is nighāta.

Dāsa (⟦दासं⟧): the suffix ghañ in the sense of instrument. The accent is that of the marker ñ.

Vidadvasu (⟦विदद्वसुः⟧): from the root vid, which has the causative sense included in it. In the participle śatṛ, since the root belongs to the adādi class, the suffix śap is elided, and so, by the accent of the suffix, the śatṛ is udātta. In the bahuvrīhi compound the accent is that of the first member.

Dayamāna (⟦दयमानः⟧): the root ⟦दय⟧, in the senses of giving, going, protecting, harming and taking. It takes the ātmanepada. The suffix is śānac of laṭ. By the rule ⟦न लोकाव्यय⟧ the genitive is prohibited.

Vavṛdhāna (⟦ववृधानः⟧): the root ⟦वृधु⟧, in the sense of growing. The form is with the suffix kānac. In the Saṃhitā the reduplicate is lengthened because the word belongs to the group beginning with tuj.

Bhūridātra (⟦भूरिदात्रः⟧): the root ⟦दाप्⟧, in the sense of cutting. By the rule beginning ⟦दामी⟧ the suffix ṣṭran comes in the sense of instrument. In the bahuvrīhi compound the first member keeps its own original accent.

Apṛṇat (⟦अपृणत्⟧): the root ⟦पृण⟧, in the sense of pleasing, in laṅ; since it belongs to the tudādi class, the suffix is śa. The accent is nighāta.

Rodasī (⟦रोदसी⟧): the form is made from the root rud with the suffix asun. The accent is that of the marker n.

## Ṛk 2

O Indra, I, adorning you (⟦भूषन्⟧), utter forth (⟦प्रेयर्मि⟧), that is, I pronounce out, for ⟦अमृताय⟧, that is, for the sake of food, your ⟦जूतिं⟧, namely the speech that has praise as its mark, impelled by the mind, of you who are ⟦मखस्य⟧, that is, deserving of honour, to be praised, and ⟦तविषस्य⟧, that is, mighty. You are ⟦पूर्वऽयावा⟧, the one who goes in front, of the ⟦मानुषीणां⟧ ⟦क्षितीनां⟧, that is, the peoples born of Manu, namely men, and also (⟦उत⟧) of the ⟦दैवीनां⟧ ⟦विशां⟧, that is, the creatures connected with the gods.

Makha (⟦मखस्य⟧): the root ⟦मह⟧, in the sense of honouring. By the rule ⟦नहेर्हलोपश्च⟧ (⟦उ. ५-२३⟧), with the words "elision of the final consonant" carried on from it, the suffix kha comes after mah by the rule ⟦महेश्व⟧, together with the elision of the final consonant. A makha is so called because it is honoured (⟦मह्यते⟧), that is, worshipped by all. By the rule ⟦खांतस्याश्मादेः⟧ (⟦फि. १-६⟧) there is final udātta. Although the word begins with ma, it comes about so by the force of the statement.

Taviṣa (⟦तविषस्य⟧): from ⟦तवते⟧ comes the suffix ṭiṣac. The accent is that of the marker c.

Jūti (⟦जूतिं⟧): by the rule beginning ⟦ऊतियूती⟧ the suffix ktin is udātta.

Iyarmi (⟦इयर्मि⟧): the root ⟦अर्ति⟧ belongs to the juhotyādi class. By the rule ⟦आर्तिपिपर्त्योश्च⟧ the reduplicate takes i. By the rule beginning ⟦अभ्यासस्य⟧ comes the substitute iyaṅ. Since it stands at the beginning of the pāda, there is no nighāta. The accent is that of the reduplicated form.

Bhūṣan (⟦भूषन्⟧): the root ⟦भूष⟧, in the sense of adorning, in the form of śatṛ. In the case of śatṛ, by the accent rule for a lasārvadhātuka suffix, the accent is that of the root.

Asi (⟦असि⟧): the form of the root as in laṭ. The accent is nighāta.

Mānuṣī (⟦मानुषीणां⟧): by the rule ⟦मनोर्जातान⟧ the suffix añ, and the augment ṣuk. By the rule beginning ⟦टिड्डाणञ्⟧ comes the suffix ṅīp. The accent is that of the marker ñ.

Daivī (⟦दैवीनां⟧): by the rule ⟦देवाद्यञण⟧ the suffix añ. Here too, as before, the suffix ṅīp. The accent is that of the marker ñ.

Pūrvayāvā (⟦पूर्वऽयावा⟧): from the root ⟦या⟧, in the sense of reaching, by the rule ⟦मनिन्कनिब्बनिप⟧ the suffix vanip. The accent is that of the latter member of a compound ending in a kṛt suffix.

## Ṛk 3

⟦शर्धनीतिः⟧: nayana is nīti, that is, action. He whose nīti, action, is ⟦शर्ध⟧, that is, fully grown, is so called. That Indra ⟦अवृणोत्⟧ Vṛtra, the asura, that is, he obstructed him by force. Likewise ⟦वर्पणीति⟧ is he whose action is the warding off, in battle, of the blows of others. Indra ⟦प्रामिनात्⟧ the ⟦मायिनां⟧; here the genitive stands in the sense of the accusative, so it means he killed the asuras who possess illusion, that is, he slew them utterly. Indra, ⟦उशधक्⟧, that is, desiring the killing of enemies, killed (⟦अहन्⟧, that is, he slew) in the forests the asura who moves about hidden, in such a way that he became ⟦व्यंसं⟧, that is, one whose shoulders are gone. Then, when that night-roaming stealer of cows had been killed, the cows (⟦धेनाः⟧) that were standing inside the ⟦राम्याणां⟧ (the nights), he made manifest (⟦आविरकृणोत्⟧), that is, he made them visible. Ramaṇa, play with women, is rāma; those who are fit for it are the rāmyā, the nights. Concerning the seizure of the nights by the asuras the Taittirīyaka says: ⟦अहर्दॆवानामासीदात्रिरसुराणां तेसुरा यद्देवानां वित्तं वेद्यमासीत्तेन सह रात्रिं प्राविशन्⟧ (the day belonged to the gods, the night to the asuras; the asuras entered the night with whatever wealth of the gods was acquired and to be obtained) and ⟦ये दिवा स्तोतारो राम्यास्तेषां वाजो धेना⟧ (those who by day are praisers, the rāmyas, theirs the voices are dhenās).

Avṛṇot (⟦अवृणोत्⟧): the root ⟦वृञ्⟧, in the sense of choosing, in laṅ. The accent is nighāta.

Śardhanīti (⟦शर्धनीतिः⟧): the root ⟦शर्धति⟧ has the sense of zeal. From it comes ghañ in the sense of the object. In the bahuvrīhi compound the accent is that of the first member.

Aminat (⟦अमिनात्⟧): the root ⟦माञ्⟧, in the sense of harming, in laṅ; by the rule ⟦मीनातेर्निगम⟧ there is shortening. The accent is nighāta.

Ahan (⟦अहन्⟧): the form of the root han in laṅ.

Uśadhak (⟦उशधक्⟧): the root ⟦वश⟧, in the sense of desire. The form is accomplished through its belonging to the group beginning with pṛṣodara.

Āvis (⟦आविः⟧): since it is read in the list of words beginning with svar as final udātta, it is final udātta.

Dhenā (⟦धेनाः⟧): the root ⟦धेट्⟧, in the sense of drinking. By the rule ⟦धेट ई च⟧ (⟦उ. ३-११⟧) the suffix na, and ī as the final substitute. Guṇa. Those who drink (⟦भयंति⟧) her are so called, and so dhenā is speech. In ⟦धेना जिगाति⟧ (⟦ऋग्वे १-२-३⟧) the word is final udātta by the accent of the suffix. Here, by vyatyaya, it is initial udātta.

Akṛṇot (⟦अकृणोत्⟧): the root ⟦कृवि⟧, in the senses of harming and doing. The accent is nighāta.

Rāmya (⟦राम्याणां⟧): the root ⟦रमु⟧, in the sense of playing. In the sense "he who is fit for rāma", by the rule ⟦छंदसि च⟧ the suffix ya; its accent follows. Or else: from the root ram with the causative sense included in it, by the rule ⟦दघ्ना दयश्च⟧ the suffix yak. Yāska says: ⟦परमयति भूतानि नक्तंचारीण्युपरमयतीतराणी⟧ (she makes the creatures that move by night take their pleasure, and makes the others cease) (⟦नि. २-१८⟧). There is an interchange of letters.

## Ṛk 4

Indra, the ⟦स्वर्षाः⟧, the giver of heaven, generating the days (⟦अहानि⟧) by his own radiances, conquered (⟦जिगाय⟧, that is, he conquered the enemies) the ⟦पृतनाः⟧, that is, the armies of others, together with the Aṅgirases, ⟦उशिक्भिः⟧, that is, those who desire battle, being ⟦अभिष्टिः⟧, that is, overpowering. After the killing of the enemies, for ⟦मनवे⟧, that is, for man, namely the sacrificer who is the doer of the rite, he caused the sun, the ⟦केतुं⟧ of the days, that is, the one that makes them known, to shine forth (⟦अरोचयत्⟧); and so, from the killing of the asura who is of the form of darkness, he kindled it intensely. Then, for the great ⟦रणाय⟧, that is, for the lighting up of the battle, he found (⟦अविंदत्⟧, that is, he obtained) ⟦ज्योतिः⟧, that is, light.

Svarṣā (⟦स्वर्षाः⟧): the root ⟦षणु⟧, in the sense of giving, with the suffix kvip. By the rule ⟦जनसनखनां सन्छलो⟧ the substitution of ā. By the rule ⟦सनोतेरन⟧ the substitution of ṣ. By the statement ⟦अहरादीनां सत्यादिषूपसंख्यानम⟧ the visarjanīya becomes ru.

Jigāya (⟦जिगाय⟧): the root ⟦जि⟧, in the sense of conquest, in liṭ. By the rule ⟦सन्नि टोर्जॆ⟧ a letter of the ka-series is substituted for the letter after the reduplicate. The accent is that of liṭ.

Pṛtanā (⟦पृतनाः⟧): the root ⟦पृज्⟧, in the sense of exertion. By the rule ⟦कृपृभ्यां किदिति⟧ the suffix tanan. Pṛtanās are armies, because in them the warriors engage (⟦व्याप्रियंते⟧). Because of the marker n, the word is initial udātta.

Abhiṣṭi (⟦अभिष्टिः⟧): it means ⟦अभिगरिता⟧, one who goes up against. The root ⟦इष⟧, in the sense of going. By the rule beginning ⟦मंत्रे वृष⟧ the suffix ktin is udātta. For it, although it is in the sense of the state, indicates the agent. By the rule beginning ⟦तितुत्रतथ⟧ there is prohibition of the augment iṭ. Because the word belongs to the group beginning with śakandhu, the latter form results. The accent is that of the latter member of a compound ending in a kṛt suffix.

Arocayat (⟦अरोचयत्⟧): the root ⟦रुच⟧, in the sense of shining; the form is in laṅ.

Manu (⟦मनवे⟧): the root ⟦मन⟧, in the sense of knowing. By the rule beginning ⟦श्व स्वस्तिही⟧ the suffix u. Because the marker n is carried on, it is initial udātta.

Avindat (⟦अविंदत्⟧): the root ⟦विद्ल⟧, in the sense of obtaining, in the class with śa; by the rule ⟦मुचादीनाम⟧ the augment nuṃ. Since it stands at the beginning of the pāda, there is no nighāta (⟦पादादित्पादनिघातः⟧).

Raṇa (⟦रणाय⟧): by the statement ⟦क्रियाग्रहणं कर्तव्यं⟧ (⟦म. १-४-३२⟧) the designation sampradāna. The root ⟦रणति⟧ has the sense of sound. By the statement ⟦वशिरन्नोरुपसंख्यानम⟧ the suffix ap. A raṇa is a battle, since in it the warriors make a sound (⟦रणंति⟧) against one another. Because the suffix has the marker p and is therefore anudātta, the accent is that of the root.

## Ṛk 5

That Indra entered the ⟦तुजः⟧, that is, the afflicting, ⟦बर्हणाः⟧, that is, the enemy armies growing through zeal for battle, ⟦नृवत्⟧, that is, like a man; having obtained light, he entered. What did he do? He entered giving (⟦दधानः⟧), that is, bestowing, ⟦पुरूणि⟧, many, ⟦नर्या⟧ riches, that is, riches suitable for those who carry on battle. That Indra, for the praiser (⟦जरित्रे⟧), made known (⟦अचेतयत्⟧, that is, he made them known) these (⟦इमाः⟧) ⟦धियः⟧ that are seen, that is, the dawns; for in them men meditate (⟦ध्यायंत्यासु मनुष्या⟧). Then he increased (⟦प्रातिरत्⟧, that is, he made it grow mightily with his own radiance) this (⟦इमं⟧) ⟦शुक्रं⟧ ⟦वर्ण⟧ of these dawns, which is endowed with brightness. That the word dhī refers to the dawn, there is a Vedic text: ⟦शुक्रवर्णामुदु नो यंसते धियं⟧ (⟦ऋग्वे १-१४३-७⟧).

Tuj (⟦तुजः⟧): the root ⟦तुज⟧, in the sense of harming, with kvip. The accent is that of the root.

Barhaṇā (⟦बर्हणाः⟧): the root ⟦वृहि⟧, in the sense of growing. From it comes yuc (⟦आस्माद्युच्⟧). By the rule ⟦बहुल⟧ yuc comes in the sense of the agent. By vyatyaya it is initial udātta.

Viveśa (⟦विवेश⟧): the form of the root viś in liṭ. The accent is nighāta.

Nṛvat (⟦नृवत्⟧): after the word nṛ, by the rule ⟦तेन तुल्य⟧ the suffix vati. Its accent follows.

Dadhāna (⟦दधानः⟧): the form of the root dhā in śānac. By the rule ⟦अभ्यस्तानामादि⟧ it is initial udātta.

Naryā (⟦नर्या⟧): in the sense "good among men", by the rule ⟦तत्र साधु⟧ the suffix yat. By the rule ⟦यतोऽनाव⟧ it is initial udātta.

Acetayat (⟦आचेतयत्⟧): the root ⟦चिती⟧, in the sense of knowing, with the causative suffix, in laṅ. The accent is that of the augment aṭ.

Dhiyaḥ (⟦धियः⟧): the root ⟦धै⟧, in the sense of thinking. By the rule ⟦अन्वेभ्योऽपि⟧ kvip. From the mention of ⟦दृशि⟧ there is samprasāraṇa. The accent is that of the root.

Imāḥ (⟦इमाः⟧): by the rule ⟦दश्च⟧ (⟦पा. ७-२-१०९⟧) the letter da becomes ma **NOTE**{the reference text gives the number of this rule as 7-2-1-9, the supplied text as 7-2-109}. The accent is that of the single substitute.

Atirat (⟦अतिरत्⟧): from the root tṛ, with śa by vyatyaya. The accent is nighāta.

Āsām (⟦आसां⟧): when idam is used again after a previous mention, before a non-śit suffix the substitute śa comes and it is anudātta. The case ending is anudātta (⟦सुप्ताद⟧). Therefore the whole word is anudātta.

## Ṛk 6

Of this Indra, ⟦महो⟧, that is, great, of such power, the ⟦महानि⟧, that is, praiseworthy, ⟦पुरूणि⟧, that is, many, ⟦सुकृता⟧, that is, well-done, ⟦कर्म⟧, that is, deeds such as the slaying of Vṛtra, they praise (⟦पनयंति⟧, that is, they extol). This cluster of words, ⟦इंद्रस्य कर्म सुकृता पुरूणी⟧, is not abandoned in the Pada recitation, because of the difference of sense here.

Such an Indra, ⟦वृजनेन⟧: vṛjana is strength, since by it enemies are cast away (⟦वर्ज्यंते⟧). With that strength he crushed ⟦सं पिपेष⟧ the ⟦वृजिनान्⟧, that is, the asuras who are of the nature of sin; that is, he ground them thoroughly. Likewise ⟦अभिभूत्योजाः⟧: abhibhūti is overpowering directed at enemies; his strength is in that, so he is abhibhūtyojāḥ, that is, able in the defeat of enemies. Such an Indra crushed the dasyus, that is, the asuras who are wasters, by deceits (⟦मायाभिः⟧, that is, by tricks).

Panayanti (⟦पनयंति⟧): the root ⟦पनते⟧, in the sense of praising; by the rule ⟦गुपूधूपविच्छिपणिपनिभ्य⟧ the suffix āya. The shortening is Vedic. The accent is nighāta.

Vṛjana (⟦वृजनेन⟧): the root ⟦वृजी⟧, in the sense of avoiding. By the rule beginning ⟦कृष्यवृजिमंदिनिधाण्भ्यः क्यु⟧ the suffix kyu. Because it has the marker k there is no guṇa. The accent is that of the suffix.

Vṛjina (⟦वृजिनान्⟧): the root ⟦वृजी⟧, in the sense of avoiding. By the rule ⟦वृजेः किच्च⟧ (⟦उ. २-४७⟧) the suffix inac **NOTE**{the reference text has the suffix as nic where the supplied text has inac}. Vṛjina is sin; those who have it are vṛjina. By its belonging to the group beginning with arśas, the suffix ac. The accent is that of the single substitute.

Pipeṣa (⟦पिपेष⟧): the root ⟦पिष्णु⟧, in the sense of crushing, in liṭ, in ṇal. The accent is nighāta.

Abhibhūtyojāḥ (⟦अभिभूत्योजाः⟧): bhūti is becoming. The suffix ktin in the sense of the state. By the rule ⟦तादौ च नित⟧ the word abhi keeps its original accent **NOTE**{the reference text lacks this sentence and the one before it on bhūti and ktin, which the supplied text has in addition}. The compound is by the rule ⟦सप्तमुसमानपूर्वपदस्य बहुव्रीहि⟧, with the analysis ⟦अभिभूतावोजो यस्य⟧ (he whose strength is in the overpowering). In the bahuvrīhi compound the accent is that of the original of the first member.

## Ṛk 7

Indra is ⟦सत्पतिः⟧, the lord of the good, that is, of the gods, and ⟦चर्षणिप्राः⟧, the fulfiller of the desires of the ⟦चर्षणीनां⟧, that is, of men, by giving the fruit they look for. That Indra, by great (⟦मह्ना⟧, that is, ⟦महता⟧) ⟦युधा⟧, that is, by battle, won ⟦वरिवः⟧: wealth is varivas, since it is greatly chosen by those who seek. Having won such wealth by battle, he gave (⟦चकार⟧, that is, he gave) it to the ⟦देवेभ्यः⟧, that is, to the praisers who are given to ⟦देवन⟧. The wise ⟦विप्राः⟧, the sages (⟦कवयः⟧), that is, the praisers, praise (⟦गृणंति⟧, that is, they extol), with hymns of praise (⟦कर्माण्युक्थेभिः⟧), those deeds of this Indra, such as the slaying of Vṛtra and the many gifts, in the dwelling, that is, in the house, of Vivasvat, that is, of the sacrificer who dwells specially for the sake of rites such as the agnihotra.

Yudhā (⟦युधा⟧): the root ⟦युध⟧, in the sense of fighting. The suffix kvip in the sense of the state, as described in the group beginning with sampad. By the rule ⟦सावेकाच⟧ the case ending is udātta.

Varivas (⟦वरिवः⟧): the root ⟦वृञ्⟧, in the sense of choosing, in the intensive with luk; by the rule ⟦ऋतश्च⟧ the augment rik comes to the reduplicate. From the form ending in that comes asun. By the variability of the usage the ṭi is elided. The accent is that of the marker n.

Cakāra (⟦चकार⟧): the form of the root kṛ in liṭ. The accent is nighāta.

Satpati (⟦सत्पतिः⟧): the form of the root as in śatṛ is sat. By the rule ⟦पत्यावैश्वर्य⟧ the first member keeps its own original accent.

Carṣaṇiprā (⟦चर्षणिप्राः⟧): the root ⟦प्रा⟧, in the sense of filling, with vic.

Vivasvat (⟦विवस्वतः⟧): from the root ⟦वस⟧, in the sense of dwelling, with the prefix vi, the suffix kvip in the sense of the state, as described in the group beginning with sampad. By the rule ⟦तदस्यास्त्यस्मिन⟧ the suffix matup. By the rule ⟦मादुपधाया⟧ its m becomes v. Because the suffix has the marker p and is therefore anudātta, the accent is that of the root.

Gṛṇanti (⟦गृणंति⟧): the root ⟦ग्य⟧, in the sense of sound, in liṭ. The accent is nighāta.

## Ṛk 8

The singers (⟦धीरणासः⟧), who make sound with their praise (⟦धिया⟧, that is, by praise), rejoice (⟦मदंति⟧, that is, they are glad) after Indra, who is ⟦सत्रासहं⟧, that is, the overcomer of great ones such as Vṛtra, and for that very reason ⟦वरेण्यं⟧, that is, to be chosen by all; ⟦सहोदां⟧, that is, the giver of strength to the weak seeker; sharing (⟦ससवांसं⟧) the ⟦देवीः⟧ ⟦अपः⟧, that is, the waters which are given to ⟦देवन⟧, and ⟦स्वः⟧, that is, the heavenly world. The meaning is that they themselves rejoice, following joy. Indra who has given (⟦ससान⟧, that is, he gave) ⟦पृथिवीं⟧, and also (⟦उत⟧) ⟦द्यां⟧, the heavenly world, and ⟦इमां⟧, this atmospheric world, these three worlds, to the dwellers in the respective worlds. The construction is that they rejoice after him.

Satrāsāha (⟦सत्रासाहं⟧): the root ⟦षह⟧, in the sense of enduring. By the rule ⟦छंदसि सह⟧ and because the suffix has the marker ṇ, there is vṛddhi of the penultimate **NOTE**{the reference text has the suffix ṇvi after this rule, which the supplied text lacks}.

Sasavāṃs (⟦ससनांसं⟧): the root ⟦षण⟧, in the sense of sharing. The form is made with kvasu, without iṭ, and with the elision of the letter n. The accent is that of the suffix.

Sasāna (⟦ससान⟧): the root ⟦षणु⟧, in the sense of giving, in liṭ. The accent is that of liṭ.

Madanti (⟦मदंति⟧): the root ⟦मदि स्तुत्यादिष्⟧, in the senses that begin with praise, takes the parasmaipada by vyatyaya. The accent is nighāta.

Dhīraṇāsaḥ (⟦धीरणासः⟧): ⟦रणतीति रणा वाक⟧ (what sounds is raṇā, speech). Those whose raṇā is dhiyā, praise, are dhīraṇāḥ. In the bahuvrīhi compound the accent is that of the original of the first member.

## Ṛk 9

That Indra, who was described in the preceding mantra, gave (⟦ससान⟧) the ⟦अत्यान⟧, that is, the horses; he gave them to the Maruts for the sake of battle. And (⟦उत⟧) he gave ⟦सूर्यं⟧ ⟦ससान⟧, the sun, the Āditya, who illuminates the world; he gave it to beings, for the carrying on of their activity. Likewise ⟦पुरुभोजसं⟧: purubhojas is that which is enjoyed through milk and so on by many, that is, by all people. He gave ⟦गां⟧, the cow that is furnished with food of many kinds in the form of milk and so on, for the success of rites such as the agnihotra; he gave it to them. And (⟦उत⟧) he gave ⟦हिरण्ययं⟧, golden, ⟦भोगं⟧, wealth. Or bhoga is a house, since one enjoys in it. He gave it to the seekers. Then, having killed (⟦हत्वी⟧, that is, having killed) the ⟦दस्यून्⟧, the obstructing asuras, he protected (⟦आवत्⟧) the ⟦आर्यं⟧, that is, the highest ⟦वर्ण⟧, consisting of the three classes, in such a way that there would be no obstruction to the rites.

Sasāna (⟦ससान⟧): the root ⟦षणु⟧, in the sense of giving, in liṭ, in ṇal. The accent is that of liṭ.

Hiraṇyaya (⟦हिरण्ययं⟧): after the word ⟦हिरण्य⟧, in the sense of modification, the suffix is prescribed by the rule ⟦मयड्वैतयो⟧ (⟦पा. ४-३-१४३⟧). In the Veda, by the irregular formation taught in ⟦ऋत्ववास्त्ववास्त्वमाध्वीहिरण्ययानि छंदसी⟧, the m of mayaṭ is elided. The accent is that of the suffix.

Hatvī (⟦हत्वी⟧): from the root han, it is irregularly formed in the sense of ktvā, by the rule ⟦स्नात्वादयश्च⟧. The accent is that of the suffix.

Āvat (⟦आवत्⟧): the form of the root av in laṅ.

## Ṛk 10

Such an Indra gave (⟦असनोत्⟧, that is, he gave) the ⟦ओषधीः⟧, that is, the plants such as grass and shrubs which bear ripe fruit, for the enjoyment of beings. Likewise (⟦अहानि⟧) he granted the days, for the allotment of life. Moreover he gave the ⟦वनस्पतीन्⟧, that is, trees such as khadira and palāśa, for the sacrifice. Likewise he gave ⟦अंतरिक्षं⟧, for movement. Likewise ⟦वलं⟧: vala is the cloud, since by it the sky is covered (⟦संव्रियते⟧). He split (⟦बिभेद⟧, that is, he broke) that cloud, to send forth the water. He drove off (⟦नुनुदे⟧) the ⟦विवाचः⟧, that is, the hostile asuras whose speech is contrary; he afflicted them (⟦बबाधे⟧) for the protection of beings. Then he became the ⟦दमिता⟧, that is, the pacifier, of the ⟦अभिक्रतूनां⟧: kratu is action; those whose action is directed toward battle, face to face, are abhikratu, that is, the stronger enemies. The meaning is that in this way, by doing what is good and avoiding what is harmful, he protected all creatures.

Asanot (⟦असनोत्⟧): the root ⟦षणु⟧, in the sense of giving, in laṅ. The accent is nighāta.

Vanaspati (⟦वनस्पतीन्⟧): by its belonging to the group beginning with pāraskara, the augment suṭ. The word vana is initial udātta by the rule ⟦नब्विषयस्य⟧. The word pati is initial udātta by the accent of the suffix. By the rule ⟦उभे वनस्पत्यादिषु युगपद⟧ both members keep their original accent.

Bibheda (⟦बिभेद⟧): the root ⟦भिदिर्⟧, in the sense of splitting, in liṭ. The accent is that of liṭ.

Nunude (⟦नुनुदे⟧): the root ⟦नुद⟧, in the sense of impelling, in liṭ. There is no nighāta, because the sentence is divided. The accent is that of the suffix.

Damitā (⟦दमिता⟧): the root ⟦दमु⟧, in the sense of calming, in the form of tṛc. The accent is that of the marker c.

Abhikratu (⟦अभिक्रतूनां⟧): in the bahuvrīhi compound the accent is that of the first member.

## Ṛk 11

O Indra, ⟦वाजसात्⟧: vājasāti is that in which there is gain (⟦सातिः⟧) of ⟦वाज⟧, that is, of food. In this ⟦भरे⟧: bhara is a battle, since by it warriors bear (⟦बिभ्रति⟧) the fortune of victory. In that battle we, the Kuśikas, would call you, who are ⟦शुनं⟧, that is, swollen, grown through zeal; or else śuna is a word for happiness, so one who brings happiness; ⟦मघवानं⟧, that is, wealthy, and for that very reason ⟦इंद्रं⟧, that is, endowed with unsurpassed lordship; ⟦नृतमं⟧, that is, the supreme leader of the whole world; we would call you for the sake of the sacrifice (⟦हुवेम⟧). Likewise we would call you for protection (⟦ऊतये⟧), you who hear (⟦शृण्वंतं⟧) the praise made by us; ⟦उग्रं⟧, that is, terrible to enemies; who in battles (⟦समत्तु⟧) are ⟦घ्नंतं⟧, that is, killing, all the rākṣasas that are indicated by the name Vṛtra (⟦वृत्राणि⟧); and you who are, of the ⟦धनानां⟧, that is, of the riches belonging to enemies, ⟦संचितं समग्नेतारं⟧ **NOTE**{the supplied text reads samcitam samagnetaram, the reference text sanjitam samagjetaram, both damaged; the intended words are uncertain, so they are left untranslated}.

Śuna (⟦शुनं⟧): the root ⟦टुओश्वि⟧, in the senses of going and growing, in the past participle; by the rule ⟦यस्य विभाष⟧ the augment iṭ is prohibited. Because it belongs to the group beginning with yaj, there is samprasāraṇa. The absence of lengthening is Vedic. The accent is that of the suffix.

Huvema (⟦हुवेम⟧): from the root hve, by the rule ⟦बहुलं छंदसी⟧ samprasāraṇa takes place, and when the elision of śap has been made, the form is in liṅ. The accent is nighāta.

Bhara (⟦भरे⟧): the root ⟦भृञ्⟧, in the sense of bearing, with the suffix gha. Because it belongs to the group beginning with vṛṣa, it is initial udātta.

Vājasāti (⟦वाजसात्⟧): the root ⟦षणु⟧, in the sense of giving; ktin in the sense of the state. By the rule beginning ⟦जनसन⟧ the substitution of ā for the nasal. In the bahuvrīhi compound the accent is that of the first member.

Samat (⟦समत्तु⟧): the root ⟦आद⟧, in the sense of eating, after the prefix sam, with kvip. The samadas are battles, because they consume (⟦संभक्षयंति⟧) the lives of warriors (⟦योद्धणामायूंशीति⟧). The accent is that of the latter member of a compound ending in a kṛt suffix.

Ghnanta (⟦घ्नंतं⟧): the root ⟦हन⟧, in the senses of harming and going, in the form of śatṛ; by the rule beginning ⟦गमहन⟧ there is the dropping of the penultimate. By the rule ⟦हो हंते⟧ the substitution of gh.

Sañjita (⟦संजितं⟧): the suffix kvip. The supplied text has the word ⟦जिजोयत्⟧ before it **NOTE**{the supplied text has the word jijoyat before kvip here, which the reference text lacks; it is left untranslated}.
