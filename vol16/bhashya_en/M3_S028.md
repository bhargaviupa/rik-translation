## Introduction
The sixteenth sūkta, of six Ṛks, beginning ⟦अग्ने जुषस्वेति⟧, is Viśvāmitra's and is addressed to Agni. On it the Anukramaṇikā says: ⟦अग्ने जुषस्व षट् तृतीयाद्युष्टिक् त्रिष्टुब्बगत्य⟧, "'Agne juṣasva': six Ṛks; those from the third on are Uṣṇih, Triṣṭubh and Jagatī." Because "but in Gāyatrī" was said at the preceding sūkta, this one too is in Gāyatrī. But the third Ṛk is Uṣṇih, the fourth Triṣṭubh, the fifth Jagatī.

The Ṛk ⟦अग्ने जुषस्व⟧ is, at the savanīya puroḍāśa, the anuvākyā of the Sviṣṭakṛt offering that belongs to the morning pressing. And this is laid down in the sūtra: ⟦अथ स्विष्टकृतोऽग्ने जुषस्व नो हविः⟧, "Then, of the Sviṣṭakṛt: 'agne juṣasva no haviḥ'" (⟦आ ५-४⟧). At the Atirātra, when the Āśvina-śastra has been concluded, the puroḍāśa of the Aśvins on two potsherds is to be offered, wholly offered, together with the sets of cups. If they should perform the Sviṣṭakṛt leaving a remainder of the puroḍāśa, then of that Sviṣṭakṛt the Ṛk ⟦पुरोळा अग्न⟧ is the yājyā. ⟦यद्येतस्य पुरोडाशस्य स्विष्टकृता चरेयुः पुरोळा अग्ने पचितः⟧, "If they should proceed with the Sviṣṭakṛt of this puroḍāśa: 'puroḷā agne pacataḥ'" (⟦आ. ६-५⟧), so it is laid down in the sūtra.

At the midday pressing, at the savanīya puroḍāśa, the anuvākyā of the Sviṣṭakṛt is the Ṛk ⟦माध्यंदिने सवन⟧. And this is laid down in the sūtra: ⟦अग्ने जुषस्व नो हविर्माध्यंदिने सवने जातवेदः⟧ (⟦आ. ५-४⟧). At the third pressing the anuvākyā of the Sviṣṭakṛt of the savanīya puroḍāśa is the Ṛk ⟦अग्ने तृतीये सवने⟧. And this is laid down in the sūtra: ⟦अग्ने तृतीये सवने हि कानिष इत्यनुसवनमनुवाक्या⟧, "'agne tṛtīye savane hi kāniṣaḥ': so the anuvākyā, pressing by pressing" (⟦आ. ५-४⟧). The yājyā of the Sviṣṭakṛt of the Aśvins' puroḍāśa is the Ṛk ⟦अग्ने वृधान⟧. And this is laid down in the sūtra: ⟦अग्ने वृधान आहुतिमिति संयाज्ये⟧, "'agne vṛdhāna āhutim': these are the two saṃyājyā verses" (⟦आ. ६-५⟧).

## Ṛk 1
Viśvāmitra praises. ⟦जातवेदः⟧, you in whom wisdom has arisen! ⟦धियावसो⟧: he from whom ⟦वसु⟧, wealth, comes through ⟦धिया⟧, praise, is dhiyāvasu; this is its vocative; you who give wealth in keeping with the rite! O Agni, in ⟦प्रातःसावे⟧, the morning pressing in the Agniṣṭoma, ⟦जुषस्व⟧, partake of, the oblation called puroḍāśa, ⟦पुरोळाशं⟧, which belongs to us (⟦नो⟧).

⟦जुषस्व⟧: it is the form in loṭ from the root juṣī, "in the senses of pleasure and partaking". Because a vocative is treated as if not present, the verb stands at the beginning of the pāda, and so it does not lose its accent. ⟦प्रातःसावे⟧: the root is ⟦सुञ्⟧, "to press". The pressing is sāva: the suffix ghañ comes in the sense of the action. By the rule cited in ⟦थाथघञ् क्ताजबित्रकाणामित्यंतोदात्तत्वं⟧ the last syllable is udātta. ⟦धियावसो⟧: that the case-ending is not elided is Vedic. Because it is a vocative it loses its accent.

## Ṛk 2
⟦यविष्ठ्य⟧, O youngest, O Agni! The ⟦पुरोळाः⟧ ("the adhvaryu and the others offer it in front", and so it is puroḍāḥ, the puroḍāśa) which is ⟦पचतः⟧, cooked on the two potsherds: it is also ⟦परिष्कृतो⟧, adorned by the carrying round of fire and the other acts, ⟦तुभ्यं⟧, for your sake, ⟦ना घ⟧, indeed. This puroḍāśa ⟦जुषस्व⟧, partake of.

⟦पुरोळाः⟧: from the root dāś, "to give", preceded by puras, when the suffix ṇvin has been added in the sense of the object by the rule ⟦श्वेतवहोकशस्सुरोडाशो ण्वैन्⟧, which is stated for the mantra (⟦पा. ३-२-७१⟧), and when the suffix ḍas has been added by ⟦श्वेतवहादीनां डस्पदस्य⟧, "ḍas for śvetavaha and the rest, of a pada" (⟦पा. ३-२-७१-१⟧), then, because the form is given ready made in ⟦अवयाः श्वेतवाः पुरोडाशाश्चॆति⟧**NOTE**{the reference text gives a reference (Pāṇini 8.2.67) after this rule; the supplied text has none}, the letter d becomes ḍ. "They offer it in front", and so it is puroḍāḥ, the puroḍāśa. The letter ḷ in place of ḍ is the tradition of the Bahvṛcas. The accent is that of a final member which is a kṛt formation. ⟦पचतः⟧: from the root ḍupacaṣ, "to cook", comes the suffix atac in the sense of the object by the rule cited in ⟦इत्यस्माद्भृमृदृशीत्यादिना⟧. "It is cooked", and so pacata means "cooked". The accent is that of a suffix marked with c. ⟦घा⟧: by the rule cited in ⟦ऋचितुनुघेत्यादिना⟧ the vowel is long in the Saṃhitā. ⟦परिष्कृतः⟧: by the rule ⟦संपर्युपेभ्यः करोतौ भूषण⟧, "after sam, pari and upa, before the root kṛ, in the sense of adorning"**NOTE**{the reference text gives a reference (Pāṇini 6.1.137) after this rule; the supplied text has none}, the root kṛ takes the augment suṭ. By the rule ⟦गतिरनंतर⟧ the preverb keeps its original accent. ⟦यविष्ठ्य⟧: yaviṣṭhya is just yaviṣṭha. In the rule ⟦पादार्घाभ्यां च⟧ (⟦पा. ५-४-२५⟧)**NOTE**{the supplied text gives this reference after the rule as Pāṇini 5-4-25; the reference text gives it at the end of the sentence as Kāśikā 5.4.25}, the word "and" takes in the suffix yat, in the word's own sense, for vasu and the rest; therefore in yaviṣṭhya there is the suffix yat in the word's own sense. Because it is a vocative it loses its accent.

## Ṛk 3
O Agni! ⟦तिरोअह्न्यं⟧, when the day has disappeared; ⟦आहुतं⟧, offered ⟦आ⟧, on all sides: the oblation called puroḍāśa, ⟦वीहि⟧, eat. In ⟦अध्वरे⟧, in this sacrifice, you, ⟦सहसः⟧ ⟦सूनुः⟧, the son of strength, are ⟦हितः⟧: you have been set down by us in the places of the Āhavanīya and the other fires. "Therefore eat" is to be supplied.

⟦वीहि⟧: the root is vī, "in desire, going and other senses". It is the form in loṭ. Because the ending hi is not marked with p, the last syllable is udātta. ⟦आहुतं⟧: when the suffix kta comes after the root hu in the sense of the object, the preverb keeps its original accent by the rule ⟦गतिरनंतर⟧. ⟦तिरोअह्न्यं⟧: what exists in the day is ⟦अह्न्यं⟧. The suffix yat comes by the rule cited in ⟦भवे छंदसीति⟧, "in the sense of existing there, in the Veda". The time in which what belongs to the day has disappeared is tiroahnya. The meaning is "the time of night". The accusative is by the rule ⟦कालाध्वनोरत्यंतसंयोग⟧, "of words for time and road, when there is unbroken connexion". By the rule cited in ⟦प्रकृत्यांतःपादमित्येजः⟧ the vowel e or o remains in its original state. In a bahuvrīhi the accent is that of the first member.

## Ṛk 4
⟦कवे⟧, O wise one, ⟦जातवेदः⟧! ⟦इह⟧, in this rite called the Agniṣṭoma, and there in the ⟦माध्यंदिने⟧ ⟦सवने⟧, in the pressing that belongs to midday, ⟦जुषस्व⟧, partake of, ⟦पुरोळाशं⟧, the puroḍāśa of the midday pressing. O Agni! In ⟦विदथेषु⟧, in sacrifices, ⟦धीराः⟧, those skilled in the rites, the adhvaryu and the others; ⟦यह्वस्य⟧: "through him the gods go to, reach, the oblations", and so yahva is the great Agni; of you who are ⟦यह्वस्य⟧, great, ⟦भागधेयं⟧, this puroḍāśa which has been set apart as your share, they ⟦न⟧ ⟦प्र⟧ ⟦मिनंति⟧, they do not harm; rather, they present it to you. "Partake of it" is to be supplied.

⟦माध्यंदिने⟧: in the sense "this belongs to midday" the suffix añ comes, because the word belongs to the group beginning with utsa. Because the suffix is marked with ñ there is vṛddhi of the first syllable. And just because it is marked with ñ the first syllable is udātta. ⟦यह्वस्य⟧: from the root yā, "to reach", comes the suffix van in the sense of the instrument, because the form is given ready made in the rule ⟦शेवयह्वजिह्वेति⟧. There are the augment huk and the shortening of the root. "Through him they go", and so yahva is Agni. By an exchange the last syllable is udātta. ⟦भागधेयं⟧: after the word bhāga, in its own sense, comes the suffix dheya by ⟦भागरूपनामभ्यो धेयप्रत्ययो वक्तव्यः⟧, "the suffix dheya is to be stated after bhāga, rūpa and nāman" (⟦पा- ५-४-३६-२⟧). The accent is that of the suffix. ⟦मिनंति⟧: the root miñ, "to harm", takes śnā because it belongs to the class beginning with krī. Before that there is shortening by the rule ⟦मीनातेर्निगम⟧, "of mīnāti, in the Veda". The cerebral ṇ by the rule ⟦हिनु मीना⟧ (⟦पा- ८. ४-१५⟧) does not come about, because all rules are optional in the Veda.

## Ṛk 5
⟦सहसः सूनो⟧, O son of strength, O Agni! In the ⟦तृतीये⟧, the third, pressing you ⟦कानिषः⟧, you desire, the puroḍāśa that is ⟦आहुतं⟧, being offered. ⟦अथ⟧, after that: ⟦अध्वरं⟧, imperishable; ⟦रत्नवंतं⟧: by the word ratna the highest fruit, heaven and the like, is meant; possessing that, giving the fruit; ⟦जागृविं⟧, the cause of wakefulness, for the Soma of the morning is wakeful, a warder-off of sleep: this Soma do you, praised with ⟦विपन्यया⟧, with speech in the form of praise, ⟦धाः⟧, place, among ⟦अमृतेषु⟧, those free from the nature of dying, ⟦देवेषु⟧, the gods, Indra and the rest. ⟦हि⟧ is a filler.

⟦तृतीये⟧: after the word tri, in the sense of an ordinal, comes the suffix tīya by the rule ⟦त्रेः संप्रसारणं⟧, "saṃprasāraṇa of tri" (⟦पा. ५-२-५५⟧). Together with it there is saṃprasāraṇa. The lengthening by the rule ⟦हल⟧ does not take place, because the word cited in ⟦अन्निति⟧ is not carried on into that rule**NOTE**{the supplied text reads "because it is not carried on there"; the reference text reads "because it is carried on there", without the negation, and its editor queries the cited word and adds Pāṇini 6.3.111 — which is meant?}. The accent is that of the suffix. ⟦कानिषः⟧: the root is kanī, "in desire and other senses". In leṭ the suffix sip comes by the rule cited in ⟦सिब्बहुलमिति⟧. Because that is ārdhadhātuka there is the augment iṭ. The "desire" meant is longing. The long ā in place of the short a is by an exchange. Because of the connexion with ⟦हि⟧ the verb does not lose its accent. The accent is that of the root. ⟦विपन्यया⟧: the root is pan, "to praise". The suffix kvip comes in the sense of the action. In the sense "it goes to praise" there is the suffix ka. The accent is that of the suffix. ⟦धाः⟧: it is the form of the root dhā in the Vedic luṅ. Because it stands at the beginning of the pāda it does not lose its accent. ⟦जागृविं⟧: the word jāgṛvi ends in the suffix kvin. The accent is that of a suffix marked with n.

## Ṛk 6
⟦जातवेदो⟧, you in whom wisdom has arisen, O Agni! You, ⟦वृधान⟧, growing by the oblations; ⟦तिरोअह्नं⟧: what exists in the day is ⟦अह्न्यं⟧, the business of the day; the time in which what belongs to the day has disappeared is tiroahnya, the time of night. At that time ⟦जुषस्व⟧, partake of, ⟦आहुतिं⟧, the oblation called puroḍāśa.

⟦आहुतिं⟧: after the root hu comes the suffix ktin in the sense of the object. By the rule cited in ⟦तादौ च निति कृत्यताविति⟧ the preverb keeps its original accent.
