## Introduction

The twelfth sūkta, of nine Ṛks, beginning ⟦इंद्राग्नी⟧, is Viśvāmitra's and is addressed to Indra and Agni. And so it is stated in the Anukramaṇī: ⟦इंद्राग्नी ऐंद्राग्नमिति⟧, "'Indra and Agni': addressed to Indra and Agni". Because of the word tu in ⟦गायत्रं त्विति⟧, "but in Gāyatrī", this sūkta also is in the Gāyatrī metre. Indra and Agni are the deity.

On the Abhiplava days, at the morning pressing, in the śastra of the acchāvāka, this sūkta serves for insertion. It is laid down in the sūtra, in the section ⟦अभिप्लवपृष्ट्याहानीति⟧, "the Abhiplava and Pṛṣṭhya days": ⟦इंद्राग्नी आ गतं ता हुवे⟧, "'O Indra and Agni, come', 'those two I call', 'whose this'" (⟦अ. ७ ५⟧).

In the Agniṣṭoma, in the śastra of the acchāvāka, the triplet beginning ⟦इंद्राग्नी आ गतमिति⟧, "O Indra and Agni, come", is the stotriya. Its first verse is the śastra-yājyā of that same acchāvāka, because it is laid down in the sūtra: ⟦इंद्राग्नी आ गतं सुतमिति याज्या⟧, "'O Indra and Agni, come to the pressed Soma' is the yājyā" (⟦आ. ५ १०⟧).

On the Caturviṃśa day, at the morning pressing, in the śastra of the acchāvāka, the verse "O Indra and Agni, come to the pressed Soma" (⟦आ गतं सुशमिति⟧) is the stotriya of the six-day period. It is laid down in the sūtra: ⟦इंद्राग्नी आ गतमिंद्रे अग्ना नमो बृहत्⟧, "'O Indra and Agni, come', 'to Indra, to Agni, great homage'"**NOTE**{the supplied text quotes the sūtra as indrāgnī ā gatam indre agnā; the reference text has indrāgnī ā gataṃ sutam indre agnā} (⟦७.२⟧).

In the śastra of the acchāvāka at the Agniṣṭoma, the three verses beginning ⟦तोता वृत्रहणा हुव⟧, "I call the two harassers, the slayers of Vṛtra", are used. And it is laid down in the sūtra: ⟦तोशा वृत्रहणा हुव⟧, "the three beginning 'I call the two harassers, the slayers of Vṛtra'"**NOTE**{the supplied text ends this sūtra, like Sāyaṇa's own sentence before it, with ity ādyās tisraḥ; the reference text has iti tisraḥ in the sūtra} (⟦आ. ५-१०⟧).

In the Agniṣṭoma, in the śastra of the acchāvāka, the triplet beginning "O Indra and Agni, around the work" is the anurūpa. And it is laid down in the sūtra: ⟦इंद्राग्नी आ गतं⟧ … ⟦अपसस्परि⟧, "'O Indra and Agni, come to the pressed Soma', 'O Indra and Agni, around the work'" (⟦आ ५-१०⟧).

## Ṛk 1

⟦इंद्राग्नी⟧ is Indra and Agni. O gods Indra and Agni, towards this Soma, which is ⟦सुतं⟧, prepared by the preparations that begin with the pressing, and for that very reason ⟦वरेण्यं⟧, to be chosen, to be resorted to, you two, being called by ⟦गीर्भि⟧, by our words that have the form of praise, ⟦आ⟧ ⟦गतं⟧, come, from ⟦नभो⟧, from the sky, the place of heaven. And having come, ⟦इषिता⟧, impelled, ⟦धिया⟧, in the rite that is being performed by us, you two ⟦अस्य⟧ ⟦पातं⟧: drink this Soma. Or else: ⟦धिया⟧, by our thought, reached, impelled by our devotion, do you two drink this Soma.

⟦इंद्राग्नी⟧: Indra and Agni. Although by ⟦द्वंद्वे घि⟧ (⟦पा. २-२-३२⟧) the word agni would have to be placed first, the word indra is placed first by ⟦अजाद्यदंतं⟧ (⟦पा. २-२-३३⟧). Because the word stands at the beginning of a pāda, the loss of accent is barred. The accent is the one taught in the sixth book. ⟦गतं⟧: the root is gamḷ, sṛpḷ, in the sense of going. When śap has been elided by the rule bahulaṃ chandasi, the m is elided by the rule that begins anudāttopadeśa. It loses its accent. ⟦वरेण्यं⟧: the root is ⟦संभक्तौ⟧, vṛṅ in the sense of resorting to. By ⟦वृज एण्यः⟧ the suffix eṇya comes after vṛṅ. Because it belongs to the group beginning with vṛṣa, it has the udātta on the first syllable. ⟦पातं⟧: ⟦पा पाने⟧, the root pā in the sense of drinking. By the maxim that all rules are optional in the Veda, the substitute piba does not come in. ⟦इषिता⟧: from the root ⟦इष⟧ in the sense of going, in the niṣṭhā, the augment iṭ comes by the rule tīṣasaha. Since iṭ is anudātta by ⟦आगमा अनुदात्ता⟧, "augments are anudātta", the accent of kta is what remains.

## Ṛk 2

O Indra and Agni, ⟦सचा⟧ of ⟦जरितुः⟧, of the praiser, that is, a helper in the attainment of the fruit characterised as heaven and the like; ⟦यज्ञः⟧, being the means of sacrifices such as the Jyotiṣṭoma; ⟦चेतनः⟧, the awakener of the senses, being that which makes them swell: that Soma ⟦जिगाति⟧, goes towards you two. ⟦अया⟧, being called by this speech of ours, which is characterised as praise, ⟦पातं⟧, drink, ⟦इमं⟧, this Soma, which is ⟦सुतं⟧, furnished with the preparations that begin with the pressing.

⟦जिगाति⟧: this root, ⟦गा⟧ in the sense of praising, is used here in the sense of going. In the Veda it belongs to the class beginning with juhoti. By the rule bahulaṃ chandasi the reduplication takes i. It loses its accent. ⟦चेतनः⟧: ⟦चिती संज्ञाने⟧, the root cit in the sense of perceiving. The suffix lyuṭ comes in the sense of the agent, because of the word bahulam in the rule. By ⟦लिती⟧ it has the udātta on the first syllable. ⟦अया⟧: in place of the instrumental ending of the word idam the substitute yāc comes by the rule supāṃ suluk. Where the substitute an would apply, there is elision by ⟦हलि लोपः⟧. ⟦पातं⟧: ⟦पा पाने⟧, the root pā in the sense of drinking. It loses its accent.

## Ṛk 3

By the ⟦जूत्या⟧ of this Soma, which is the means of ⟦यज्ञस्य⟧, of the sacrifice**NOTE**{the supplied text reads asya yajñasya sādhanabhūtasya somasya; the reference text reads yajñasya yajñasādhanabhūtasya somasya, without asya}. Jūti is impelling. For the Soma impels the sacrificer: that the sacrificer, having obtained the means, sets about the rite which is to be accomplished by it, this indeed is its being an impeller. Impelled by that jūti, which has the form of impelling, I ⟦वृणे⟧ you two, Indra and Agni, who are ⟦कविच्छदा⟧, who gratify the poets, the praisers, by bestowing the fitting fruit: with the words "come here to this rite to drink the Soma" I resort to you. ⟦ता⟧: and when they have come, may those two, Indra and Agni, ⟦इह⟧, in this rite, ⟦सोमस्य⟧, by the gift of Soma**NOTE**{the supplied text reads somadānena, by the gift of Soma; the reference text reads somapānena, by the drinking of Soma}, ⟦तृंपतां⟧, be satisfied.

⟦इंद्रं⟧: from the root ⟦इदि⟧ in the sense of supreme lordship; because it is laid down ready-made by the rule beginning ṛjendrāgra as ending in the suffix ran, it has the udātta on the first syllable. ⟦कविच्छदा⟧: ⟦छदि⟧ in the sense of covering; after this root comes kvip. By ⟦सुपां सुलुगिति⟧ the substitute ḍā comes in place of the case-ending. It has the accent by which the second member, ending in a kṛt suffix, keeps its original accent. ⟦जूत्या⟧: ⟦जू⟧ is a root of the sūtras. After it, while "udātta" continues, the udātta on the last syllable results because the word is laid down ready-made by the rule beginning ūtiyūtijūti. By the rule udāttayaṇo halpūrvāt the case-ending is udātta. ⟦वृणे⟧: vṛṅ in the sense of resorting to. The suffix is śnā. By ⟦टित आत्मनेपदानामिति⟧ the final part beginning with the last vowel becomes e. It loses its accent. ⟦ता⟧: the substitute ḍā by the rule supāṃ suluk. ⟦सोमस्य⟧: the root is ⟦अभिषवे⟧, ṣuñ in the sense of pressing. After it comes the suffix man by the rule beginning artistusu. It has the accent of a suffix marked with n. ⟦तृंपतां⟧: ⟦तृप प्रीणने⟧, the root tṛp in the sense of pleasing. In the imperative, because it belongs to the group beginning with muc, the augment num comes in. It loses its accent.

## Ṛk 4

You two, Indra and Agni, who are ⟦तोशा⟧, harassers of enemies; ⟦वृत्रहणा⟧, slayers of Vṛtra, of evil; ⟦सजित्वाना⟧, conquerors together, given to victory in respect of the foe; ⟦अपराजिता⟧, not set at naught by anyone**NOTE**{the supplied text glosses sajitvānā also by parasyāpekṣayā jayaśīlau, which the reference text lacks; the reference text glosses aparājitā also by aparājitau, which the supplied text lacks}; ⟦वाजसातमा⟧, givers of food in the highest degree: you two I ⟦हुवे⟧, I call here to this rite to drink the Soma.

⟦वृत्रहणा⟧: ⟦हन हिंसागत्योः⟧, the root han in the senses of injuring and going. The suffix kvip comes by ⟦ब्रह्मभ्रूणवृत्रेषु क्विबिति⟧. The cerebral ṇ comes by the rule ekājuttarapade ṇaḥ (⟦पा. ८-४-१२⟧). It has the accent by which the second member, ending in a kṛt suffix, keeps its original accent. ⟦हुवे⟧: of the root hvayati, when the elision has taken place by ⟦छंदसीति लुकि सति⟧, by bahulaṃ chandasi, while ⟦ह्वः संप्रसारणमित्यनुवृत्तौ⟧, "saṃprasāraṇa of hvā", continues, the saṃprasāraṇa comes by the rule bahulaṃ chandasi. The substitute uvaṅ comes. The ātmanepada is by interchange. It loses its accent. ⟦सजित्वाना⟧: ⟦जि जये⟧, the root ji in the sense of conquering. The suffix kvanip comes by the rule beginning śīṅ, ⟦क्रुशिरुहिजिक्षिसृधृभ्यः⟧ (⟦उ. ४-११३⟧)**NOTE**{the supplied text gives the Uṇādi reference as 4-113; the reference text gives 4.553}. Because the suffix is marked with k there is no guṇa. Because it is marked with p the augment tuk comes. By ⟦समानस्य छंदस्यमूर्धेति⟧ sa takes the place of samāna. It has the accent by which the second member, ending in a kṛt suffix, keeps its original accent. ⟦अपराजिता⟧: in the compound with the negative, the first member keeps its original accent by the rule tatpuruṣe tulyārtha. ⟦इंद्राग्नी⟧: although by the rule devatādvandve ca both members would keep their original accent, the accent is that of a compound, because of the prohibition ⟦नोत्तरपदेनुदात्तादाविति⟧. ⟦वाजसातमा⟧: ⟦षणु दाने⟧, the root san in the sense of giving. After it comes viṭ by the rule janasanakhanakramagamaḥ. The ā comes by the rule viḍvanor anunāsikasyāt. The suffix tamap comes by ⟦अतिशायने तमबिति⟧. It has the accent by which the second member, ending in a kṛt suffix, keeps its original accent.

## Ṛk 5

O Indra and Agni, the ⟦उक्तिनः⟧ — uktha is the śastra; those who have it, the possessors of the śastra, the hotṛ and the others — ⟦प्र⟧ ⟦अर्चंति⟧ ⟦वां⟧, you two: in this rite they honour you with words that have the form of praise. Likewise the ⟦जरितारः⟧, the praisers, the udgātṛ and the others, who are ⟦नीथाविदः⟧, acquainted with the stotra, skilled in the singing of the sāmans, honour you two in order to attain the desired fruit. I also, for the gaining of ⟦इषः⟧, of food, ⟦आ⟧ ⟦वृणे⟧ you two, Indra and Agni: I resort to you on all sides; the meaning is "I honour you".

⟦वां⟧: for the word yuṣmad in the accusative, because it follows a word, the substitute vām comes by the rule beginning ⟦षष्ठीचतुर्थीद्वितीया⟧, and it is anudātta. ⟦अर्चंति⟧: ⟦अर्च पूजायां⟧, the root arc in the sense of honouring. It loses its accent. ⟦उक्किनः⟧: ⟦वच परिभाषणे⟧, the root vac in the sense of speaking. The suffix thak comes in the sense of the object by the rule beginning pātṝtudivacirici. The saṃprasāraṇa comes by the rule beginning ⟦वचिस्वपी⟧. Uktha is the śastra. "Those who have the uktha": the suffix ini comes by the rule ata iniṭhanau. The elision is by ⟦यस्येति⟧. It has the accent of the suffix. ⟦नीथाविदः⟧: the root nī in the sense of ⟦प्रापणे⟧, leading. The suffix kthan comes by the rule beginning ⟦हनिकुषिनीरमी⟧. Because the suffix is marked with k there is no guṇa. ⟦विद ज्ञाने⟧, the root vid in the sense of knowing. The suffix kvip comes by ⟦क्विप् चेति⟧. It has the accent by which the second member, ending in a kṛt suffix, keeps its original accent. ⟦इंद्राग्नी⟧: because it is a vocative it has the udātta on the first syllable. ⟦वृणे⟧: vṛṅ in the sense of resorting to. It loses its accent.

## Ṛk 6

O Indra and Agni, ⟦दासपत्नीः⟧: "they cause to waste away, they destroy", hence dāsas, destroyers, enemies. Those of which these are the lords, the protectors, are dāsapatnīḥ. ⟦नवतिं⟧, ninety in number, ⟦पुरः⟧, such cities of the enemies, by ⟦एकेन⟧ ⟦कर्मणा⟧, by one effort alone, you two ⟦साकं⟧, together, at one and the same time, ⟦अधूनुतं⟧, shook. "Those two, Indra and Agni, I call" is to be supplied.

⟦नवतिं⟧: of nine tens there is the form nava, and the suffix is ti. By the accent of the suffix it has the udātta on the last syllable. ⟦दासपत्नीः⟧: the root is dasu in the sense of wasting away. "He causes to waste away", hence dāsa. It ends in the suffix ac of the group beginning with pac. Because the suffix is marked with c it has the udātta on the last syllable. "Those of which these are the lords": since it is stated ⟦दासाच्च⟧, "always in sapatnī and the like, and after dāsa" (⟦पा- ४ १-३५-१⟧)**NOTE**{the supplied text gives this reference as pā- 4 1-35-1; the reference text gives it as 4.1.35 with ga. (the gaṇa) in place of the final 1}, the suffix ṅīp comes by the rule vibhāṣā sapūrvasya. In place of the i there is n. In the bahuvrīhi the first member keeps its original accent. ⟦अधूनुतं⟧: ⟦धूञ् कंपने⟧, the root dhū in the sense of shaking. In the imperfect, ⟦स्वादिभ्यः श्नुः⟧, śnu comes after the roots beginning with su. It loses its accent. ⟦एकेन⟧: ⟦इण् गतौ⟧, the root i in the sense of going. The suffix kan comes by ⟦कन्निति⟧, the rule that kan comes after i, bhī, kā, pā, śal, at and marc. Because the suffix is marked with n it has the udātta on the first syllable.

## Ṛk 7

O Indra and Agni, the ⟦धीतयः⟧, the drinkers of the Soma, the hotṛ and the others, ⟦पथ्याः⟧ ⟦अनु⟧, taking as their aim the paths, the ways, of ⟦ऋतस्य⟧, of the fruit of the rite, ⟦परि⟧, on all sides, of ⟦अपसः⟧, of the rite that is being performed by us, ⟦उप प्र यंति⟧: they are present close by, in an eminent way. Therefore do you two come to drink the Soma: this is the sense. Or else: the ⟦धीतयः⟧, the praises, taking as their aim the ways of ⟦ऋतस्य⟧, of the sacrifice, go on all around the rite. Therefore do you two come, as the ones to be praised.

⟦यंति⟧: the root i in the sense of going. The substitute yaṇ comes. It loses its accent. ⟦धीतयः⟧: from the root ⟦धेट्⟧ in the sense of drinking, the suffix ktic comes in the sense of the agent by ⟦संज्ञायामिति⟧, the rule that ktic and kta come in a name. The ī comes by the rule ghumāsthā. Because the suffix is marked with c it has the udātta on the last syllable.

## Ṛk 8

O Indra and Agni, ⟦वां⟧, of you two, the ⟦तविषाणि⟧, the strengths, and the ⟦प्रयांसि⟧, the foods, are ⟦सधस्थानि⟧: they exist without being parted from each other. Likewise aptūrya, the being an impeller by way of the rain, is ⟦हितं⟧, placed, ⟦युवोः⟧, in you two alone. Therefore in all rites, beginning with the drinking of the Soma, Indra and Agni are present only together: this is the sense.

⟦वां⟧: in the genitive dual the substitute vām comes. ⟦सधस्थानि⟧: the root is ⟦ष्ठा⟧ in the sense of the ceasing of motion. The suffix ka comes by ⟦आतोऽनुपसर्गे कः⟧. By the rule sadha mādasthayoś chandasi the substitute sadha comes in place of saha.

## Ṛk 9

O Indra and Agni, you two who are ⟦रोचना⟧ of ⟦दिवः⟧, illuminators, lighters-up of heaven, ⟦वाजेषु⟧, in battles, ⟦परि⟧ ⟦भूषथः⟧: you become adorned all around, on all sides; having defeated the enemies you are victorious on all sides. ⟦वां⟧, of you two, the ⟦वीर्यं⟧, the power alone, ⟦प्र⟧ ⟦चेति⟧, makes known in an eminent way, ⟦तत्तादृशं⟧, such a victory in battle. Or else: you two, ⟦वाजेषु⟧, in battles, paribhūṣathaḥ: you overcome the enemies. The rest is as before.

⟦रोचना⟧: of the root ⟦रुच⟧ in the sense of shining, the suffix yuc comes by the rule anudāttetaś ca halādeḥ. Because the suffix is marked with c it has the udātta on the last syllable. ⟦भूषथः⟧: ⟦भूष भूषणे⟧, the root bhūṣ in the sense of adorning. It loses its accent. ⟦चेति⟧: ⟦चिती संज्ञाने⟧, the root cit in the sense of perceiving. This root has here the sense of the causative included in it. By ⟦बहुलं छंदसीति शपो लुक्⟧, by bahulaṃ chandasi, śap is elided. The elision of the t is Vedic.
