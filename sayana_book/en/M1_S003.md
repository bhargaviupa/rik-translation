## SBh 1.3.0.1

The third sūkta, of twelve Ṛks, is ⟦अश्विना⟧. In it the tṛca addressed to the Aśvins is employed at the Aśvin rite of the morning recitation, the prātaranuvāka. And so it has been laid down in the sūtra: ⟦अथाश्विन एषो उषा प्रातर्युजेति चतस्रोऽश्विना यज्वरीरिषः⟧ (⟦आश्व. श्रौ. ४. १५⟧).

## SBh 1.3.1.1

O Aśvins, you ⟦चनस्यतम्⟧, desire, that is, enjoy, ⟦इषः⟧, foods consisting of oblations. Although the word ⟦चनः⟧ denotes food, there is no fault of repetition together with ⟦इषः⟧, because it is used to indicate desire. Just as there is no repetition in ⟦वक्तव्यमुवाच⟧ and ⟦समूलकाषं कषति⟧ and the like, so here. What kind of foods? ⟦यज्वरीः⟧, those that bring about yoga. What kind of Aśvins? ⟦द्रवत्पाणी⟧, endowed with two hands that run, that hasten, in order to take the oblation. ⟦शुभस्पती⟧, the two protectors of the auspicious rite. ⟦पुरुभुजा⟧, the two of broad arms, or the two great eaters. **NOTE**{Does yoga in the gloss of ⟦यज्वरीः⟧ mean the sacrificial work itself?}

## SBh 1.3.1.2

⟦अश्विना⟧: by the rule ⟦आमन्त्रितस्य⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. १९८⟧) the initial udātta taught in the sixth book. ⟦यज्वरीः⟧: although the foods are only instruments of the sacrifice, with the intention that they are agents in their own operation, as in ⟦असिश्छिनत्ति⟧, the suffix ṅvanip comes by the rule ⟦सुयजोर्ङ्वनिप्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ३. २. १०३⟧); by the rule ⟦वनो र च⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ४. १. ७⟧) the feminine suffix ṅīp; in conjunction with it r is substituted. Because both suffixes are anudātta by the rule ⟦अनुदात्तौ सुप्पितौ⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ३. १. ४⟧), only the accent of the root remains. In ⟦इषः⟧, because the ending śas is anudātta, only the accent of the stem remains. ⟦द्रवत्पाणी⟧ is the vocative dual of those whose two hands are running, hastening. For it the initial udātta of a vocative holds, and not again the nighāta taught in the eighth book, because of the prohibition ⟦अपादादौ⟧.

## SBh 1.3.1.3

If it be said that for ⟦इषः⟧, the earlier word, by the rule ⟦सुबामन्त्रिते⟧ (⟦पा. सू. २. १. २⟧), through its being treated as part of the following word, it is not at the beginning of a pāda, as in ⟦मित्रावरुणावृतावृधौ⟧, no. For there the words are connected with each other by agreement in case. Here, however, ⟦इषः⟧ and ⟦इवत्पाणी⟧ are used without mutual construction, like the words ⟦सरस्वतिशुतुद्रि⟧. ⟦शुभः⟧ is the genitive singular of a word ending in kvip in the abstract sense, because it belongs to the group beginning with sampad, from the root ⟦शुभ शुम्भ दीप्तौ⟧, in the sense of shining. By the rule ⟦षष्ठ्याः पतिपुत्र⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. ३. ५३⟧) the visarjanīya becomes s. When the vocative ⟦पती⟧ of it follows, because it is treated as part of the following word, there is the initial udātta of a vocative, and not again the nighāta of the eighth book. When that is to be done, the preceding vocative ⟦द्रवत्पाणी⟧ is treated as non-existent by the rule ⟦आमन्त्रितं पूर्वमविद्यमानवत्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. १. ७२⟧), so it is at the beginning of a pāda, and therefore, by the prohibition ⟦अपादादौ⟧, there is no nighāta.

## SBh 1.3.1.4

If it be objected that, as in ⟦मित्रावरुणावृतावृधौ⟧, there ought to be a prohibition of treatment as non-existent by the rule ⟦नामन्त्रिते समानाधिकरणे⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. १. ७३⟧), no. For the word ⟦मित्रावरुण⟧ is a general expression, so the prohibition of its being treated as non-existent is fitting; but the word ⟦द्रवत्पाणी⟧ is not so; hence the difference. ⟦पुरुभुजा⟧: the two whose two arms are ⟦पुरू⟧, broad. The initial udātta of a vocative. By the rule ⟦सुपां सुलुक्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ७. १. ३९⟧) the substitute ḍā.

## SBh 1.3.1.5

Or ⟦पुरु⟧, much, ⟦भुञ्जाते⟧, the two eat. Here in ⟦चनस्यतम्⟧: by the rule ⟦चायतेरन्ने ह्रस्वश्च⟧ (⟦उ. सू. ४.६३९⟧), from the root ⟦चायृ पूजानिशामनयोः⟧, in the senses of worship and perceiving, the suffix asun, the shortening of ā, and, drawn in by ca, the augment nuḍ (⟦उ. सु. ४. ६३६⟧); by the rule ⟦लोपो व्योर्वलि⟧ (⟦पा.सू. ६. १. ६६⟧) the elision of y; the word ⟦चनः⟧ is read among the names of food (⟦निरु० ६. १६⟧).

## SBh 1.3.1.6

From that, ⟦तत्⟧, it desires for itself: by the rule ⟦सुप आत्मनः क्यप्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ३. १.८⟧). Because words so ending are roots by the rule ⟦सनाद्यन्ताः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ३. १. ३२⟧), the second person dual of the loṭ. The suffix kyac is udātta by its suffix accent. When a single substitute has been made with śap, it is udātta by the rule ⟦उदात्तेनोदात्तः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. २. ५⟧). **NOTE**{What exactly is the svarita meant by ⟦स्वरितत्त्वम्⟧ in the clause about the following verb and the lasārvadhātuka being anudātta?} And there is no nighāta by the rule ⟦तिङ्ङतिङः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. १. २८⟧), because the preceding vocative is treated as non-existent, so that the verb does not follow a word, or because it is at the beginning of a pāda, so that it is not obtained.

## SBh 1.3.2.1

⟦अश्विना⟧, O Aśvins, you ⟦वनतं⟧, share in, accept, ⟦गिरः⟧, our praises, ⟦धिया⟧, with intelligence joined with devotion. What kind of Aśvins? ⟦पुरुदंससा⟧, of many deeds. Among the twenty-six names of deeds ⟦दंसः⟧ (⟦नि. २. १. ३⟧) is read. ⟦नरा⟧, leaders. ⟦धिष्ण्या⟧, endowed with boldness, or intelligent. What kind of intelligence? ⟦शवीरया⟧, endowed with motion, that is, with unobstructed advance. Of the four vocatives beginning with ⟦अश्विना⟧ there is the initial udātta of a vocative taught in the sixth book. Because they are at the beginning of a pāda, there is no nighāta of the eighth book. For ⟦पुरुदंससा⟧ also is at the beginning of a pāda, because the preceding word is treated as non-existent by the rule ⟦आमन्त्रितं पूर्वमविद्यमानवत्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. १. ७२⟧).

## SBh 1.3.2.2

If it be said that by the rule ⟦नामन्त्रिते समानाधिकरणे⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. १. ७३⟧) the earlier word, being a general expression, and this one a specific expression, are not treated as non-existent, no. Like the word ⟦अश्वि⟧, the word ⟦पुरुदंसः⟧ too, being used by convention of the two Aśvins alone, is a general word; for when it is said that a general expression is not treated as non-existent, it is understood by implication that the following one is a specific expression. If it be said that when both are general expressions, being synonyms, their use together is a repetition, no. For just as the mention of specific qualities, so also the mention of connection with many well-known specific names has a purpose, by its use for praise; for only a repetition without purpose is a repetition. Although ⟦अश्वि⟧ and ⟦पुरुदंसः⟧ both apply to one object, since they are synonyms, with no difference in the ground of application, there is also, from the lack of agreement in case, no prohibition of treatment as non-existent. For agreement in case is the application to one object of words with different grounds of application. If it be said that the ground of ⟦अश्वि⟧ is connection with horses, while that of ⟦पुरुदंसः⟧ is connection with many deeds, so there is a difference of ground of application, no.

## SBh 1.3.2.3

For those two are only grounds of derivation, not grounds of application. If agreement in case were declared by mere difference of the ground of derivation, the same would follow for the words ⟦वृक्ष⟧ and ⟦महीरुह⟧ also. Just for this reason, in ⟦इडे रन्तेऽदिते सरस्वति प्रिये प्रेयसि महि विश्रुत्येतानि ते अघ्निये नामानि⟧ (⟦तै. सं. ७. १. ६. ८⟧), the words ⟦इडे⟧ and the rest, being useful for the praise of the thousandth, are used together, though synonyms, with the sole purpose of praise through connection with many distinct names, by the statement ⟦एतानि ते अघ्निये नामानि⟧. Even if difference of the ground of derivation is intended only through its use for praise, since synonyms are not in agreement in case, there is no prohibition ⟦नामन्त्रिते⟧; and since by ⟦आमन्त्रितं पूर्वमविद्यमानवत्⟧ each preceding word is treated as non-existent, all have the initial udātta taught in the sixth book. So it is in the present case also.

## SBh 1.3.2.4

From the rule ⟦कॄशॄपॄकटिपटिशौटिभ्य ईरन्⟧ (⟦उ. सू. ४. ४७०⟧), with ⟦ईरन्⟧ continuing, by the statement ⟦बहुलवचनादन्यत्रापि⟧, when the suffix īran has been added to the root ⟦शु गतौ⟧, in the sense of going, the word ⟦शवीरया⟧ has the initial udātta because it is marked with n. In the instrumental ⟦धियेत्यत्र⟧, by the rule ⟦सावेकाचः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. १६८⟧) the case ending is udātta. In ⟦वनतमित्यत्र⟧, because śap is marked with p and the second person dual of the loṭ is lasārvadhātuka, only the udātta of the root ⟦वन षण संभक्तौ⟧ remains. And there is no nighāta by ⟦तिङ्ङतिङः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. १.२८⟧), because the preceding vocative is treated as non-existent and so is at the beginning of a pāda. ⟦गिरः⟧: when the ending is anudātta, the accent of the stem remains.

## SBh 1.3.3.1

Here ⟦अश्विना⟧ continues. O Aśvins, ⟦आ⟧ ⟦यातम्⟧, come to this rite. For what purpose? That is said: ⟦सुताः⟧, Somas have been pressed for you; to accept them is to be supplied. What kind of Aśvins? ⟦दस्रा⟧, the two destroyers of enemies. Or, because they are the physicians of the gods, the two destroyers of diseases, by the śruti ⟦अश्विनौ वै देवानां भिषजौ⟧ (⟦ऐ.बा. १. १८⟧). ⟦नासत्या⟧, the two free of asatya, untruth, speaking falsehood. Here Yāska: ⟦सत्यावेव नासत्यावित्यौर्णवाभः। सत्यस्य प्रणेतारावित्याग्रायणः⟧ (⟦निरु. ६. १३⟧). That is, the Nāsatyas are Satyas themselves, says Aurṇavābha; the two leaders of truth, says Āgrāyaṇa. ⟦रुद्रवर्तनी⟧.

## SBh 1.3.3.2

The ground of application of the word ⟦रुद्र⟧ is weeping: ⟦यदरोदीत्तद्रुदस्य रुद्रत्वम्⟧ (⟦तै. सं. १. ५. १. १⟧), say the Taittirīyas; ⟦तद्यद्रोदयन्ति तस्माद्रुद्राः⟧, say the Vājasaneyins. ⟦रुद्रवर्तनी⟧: the two whose way, in the form of a raid, a dhāṭī, is that of the Rudras, the brave warriors who make enemies weep. The sense is: as brave men by a raid make enemies weep, so do these two. ⟦युवाकवः⟧ is an epithet of the pressed Somas: they are mixed with the vasatīvarī waters and the ekadhanā waters. ⟦वृक्तबर्हिषः⟧: those Somas of which the barhis, in the form of the strewing, is ⟦वृक्तानि⟧, free of roots, are ⟦वृक्तबर्हिषः⟧. Or, among the eight names of priests beginning ⟦भरताः⟧, ⟦वृक्तबर्हिषः⟧ (⟦नि. ३. १८, ४⟧) is read. Then the nominative is in the sense of the instrumental; the construction is: pressed by the priests.

## SBh 1.3.3.3

⟦दस्रा⟧: initial udātta by ⟦आमन्त्रितस्य⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. १९८⟧). ⟦युवाकवः⟧: ⟦यु मिश्रणे⟧, the root ⟦यु⟧ in the sense of mixing. ⟦युवन्ति⟧, they mix, are mingled with the vasatīvarī and other substances used in the śrayaṇa; hence ⟦युवाकवः⟧. Although the root ⟦यु⟧ (⟦यौतेः⟧) is not counted in the group ⟦कटिकुष्यादिषु⟧ (⟦उ. सू. ३. ३५७⟧), by the word bahula the suffix kāku. Because it is marked with k, there is no guṇa, and uv is substituted. By the accent of the suffix, ā is udātta. ⟦नासत्यौ⟧: the two in whom there is no untruth.

## SBh 1.3.3.4

By the rule ⟦नभ्राण्नपान्नवेदानासत्या⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. ३. ७५⟧) and so on, because of the retention of the natural form, there is no loss of n of the negative. Because it is at the beginning of a pāda, there is no nighāta, and so the initial udātta of a vocative. ⟦वृक्तबर्हिषः⟧: the Somas of which the strewn barhis is cut, ⟦वृक्तं⟧, free of roots. In the bahuvrīhi, by the accent of the first member, only the accent of the suffix kta remains. In ⟦आ⟧, by the rule ⟦उपसर्गाश्चाभिवर्जम्⟧ (⟦फि.सू. ८१⟧) it is udātta. ⟦रुद्रवर्तनी⟧: by the rule ⟦आमन्त्रितस्य च⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८.१ १९⟧) the nighāta of a vocative.

## SBh 1.3.4.1

⟦चित्रभानो⟧, O Indra of wondrous brightness, ⟦आ⟧ ⟦याहि⟧, come to this rite. ⟦सुताः⟧, pressed, ⟦इमे⟧, these Somas ⟦त्वायवः⟧ are desiring you. ⟦अण्वीभिः⟧: among the twenty-two names of fingers beginning ⟦अग्रुवः⟧, ⟦अण्व्यः⟧ (⟦नि. २. ५, २⟧) is read. The construction is: pressed by the fingers of the priests. Further, these Somas are ⟦तना⟧, always, ⟦पूतासः⟧, purified, pure, because they are cleansed in many ways by the ten-fold strainer. Yāska explains the word Indra in many ways: ⟦इन्द्र इरां दृणातीति वेरां ददातीति वेरां दधातीति वेरां दारयतीति वेरां धारयतीति वेन्दवे द्रवतीति वेन्दौ रमत इति वेन्धे भूतानीति वा तद्यदेनं प्राणैः समैन्धंस्तदिंद्रस्येन्द्रत्वमिति विज्ञायत इदंकरणादिस्याग्रायण इदंदर्शनादित्यौपमन्यव इन्दतेर्वैश्वर्यकर्मण इञ्छत्रूणां दारयिता वा द्रावयिता वा दरयिता च यज्वनाम्⟧ (⟦निरु. १०.८⟧). That is: Indra, because he splits Irā; or because he gives Irā; or because he holds Irā; or because he tears Irā; or because he sustains Irā; or because he runs to the drop (indu); or because he delights in the indu; or because he kindles beings; or, as it is known, because they kindled him with the breaths, that is the Indra-ness of Indra; Āgrāyaṇa says from doing this; Aupamanyava says from seeing this; from the root indati, which has the sense of lordship; or he is the splitter or the driver of enemies, and the one who honours sacrificers.

## SBh 1.3.4.2

This is its meaning. The root is ⟦दॄ विदारणे⟧, splitting. Having Irā, food, in view, for the accomplishment of the water that produces it, he splits the cloud; thus Indra. The root is ⟦डुदाञ् दाने⟧, giving. He gives Irā, food, by producing rain; thus Indra. The root is ⟦धाञ् पोषणार्थः⟧, in the sense of nourishing. Irā is food, the crop that causes satisfaction; he holds it, he nourishes it by the giving of water; thus Indra. To produce Irā he splits the earth by way of the farmer; thus Indra. By the nourishment mentioned before he supports Irā, he establishes it free from destruction; thus Indra. Indu is Soma, the juice of the creeper; for it he runs, he hastens to the sacrificial ground; thus Indra. He delights, he sports in the indu, in the Soma as described; thus Indra. The root is ⟦ञिइन्धी दीप्तौ⟧, in the sense of kindling. He enters within the beings, the bodies of creatures, in the form of life and consciousness, and kindles them; thus Indra. Having this very thing in view, the Vājasaneyins recite: ⟦इन्धो ह वै नामैष योऽयं दक्षिणेऽक्षन्पुरुषः। तं वा एतमिन्धं सन्तमिन्द्र इत्याचक्षते⟧

## SBh 1.3.4.3

⟦परोक्षेण परोक्षप्रिया इव हि देवाः⟧ (⟦बृ. उ. ४. २. २⟧). The words ⟦तद्यत्⟧ and the rest are a sentence of another Brāhmaṇa; the etymology of Indra is stated there; this is to be supplied. Because, ⟦यद्⟧, the worshippers ⟦समैन्धन्⟧, fully kindled, by meditation, this god Indra, in the form of the Supreme Self, together with the breaths, that is, the organs such as speech and the eye, and the winds such as prāṇa and apāna, therefore, ⟦तत्⟧, the name Indra came about. On this view the derivation is in the passive: ⟦इध्यते⟧, he is kindled, he shines. The sage named Āgrāyaṇa holds the etymology ⟦इदंकरणादिन्द्रः⟧. For Indra, in the form of the Supreme Self, makes this world. The sage named Aupamanyava states the etymology ⟦दर्शनादिन्द्र⟧: ⟦इदम्⟧, this, expresses immediacy, for by discrimination one sees the Supreme Self immediately. Having this very thing in view it is recited in the Āraṇyaka section: ⟦स एतमेव पुरुषं ब्रह्म ततममपश्यदिदमदर्शमितीँ३⟧

## SBh 1.3.4.4

⟦तस्मादिदन्द्रो नामेन्द्रो ह वै नाम तमिदन्द्रं सन्तमिन्द्र इत्याचक्षते परोक्षेण परोक्षप्रिया इव हि देवाः⟧ (⟦ऐ. आ. २. ४. ३⟧). The root is ⟦इदि परमैश्वर्ये⟧, in the sense of supreme lordship. Supreme lordship is the form of the world by his own māyā. From connection with it, Indra. With this intention it is heard: ⟦इन्द्रो मायाभिः पुरुरूप ईयते⟧ (⟦ऋ. सं. ६. ४७. १८⟧). When a is dropped from the word ⟦इन⟧, which denotes the lord, the word ⟦इन्⟧, ending in n, results. The root is ⟦दॄ भये⟧, in the sense of fear; and that Supreme Lord is the splitter of enemies, the one who frightens them; thus Indra. The root is ⟦द्रु गतौ⟧, in the sense of going; he makes enemies run, brings them to flight; thus Indra. For the sacrificers, those who perform sacrifices, he is the one who honours them and removes fear. Thus these etymologies are to be seen.

## SBh 1.3.4.5

In ⟦इन्द्र⟧ there is the initial udātta of a vocative. In ⟦आ⟧ the initial udātta, because it is a particle. ⟦चित्रभानो⟧: because it follows a word, the nighāta of a vocative. In the sense 'they desire you', from the word for 'you', by the rule ⟦सुप आत्मनः क्यच्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ३.१.८⟧). ## SBh 1.3.12.1

Sarasvatī is of two kinds: the goddess with a body, and in the form of a river. Of these, in the two previous Ṛks the embodied one was set forth. By this one the river form is set forth. Such a Sarasvatī ⟦प्र⟧ ⟦चेतयति⟧, makes known, powerfully, ⟦महो⟧ ⟦अर्णः⟧, abundant water, ⟦केतुना⟧, by her action in the form of her flow. Further, by her own divine form she ⟦वि⟧ ⟦राजति⟧, illumines distinctly, ⟦विश्वाः⟧ ⟦धियः⟧, all the understandings of the performers; the sense is that she always produces understandings concerning the rite.

## SBh 1.3.12.2

Yāska shows the twofold nature of Sarasvatī: ⟦तत्र सरस्वतीत्येतस्य नदीवद्देवतावच्च निगमा भवन्ति⟧ (⟦निरु. २. २३⟧). Among the hundred and one names of water ⟦अर्णः क्षोदः⟧ (⟦नि. १. १२. १⟧) is read. Yāska explains this Ṛk: ⟦महदर्णः सरस्वती प्रचेतयति प्रज्ञापयति केतुना कर्मणा प्रज्ञया वेमानि च सर्वाणि प्रज्ञानान्यभिविराजति⟧ (⟦निरु. ११. २७⟧). That is: Sarasvatī makes known, makes understood, abundant water, by ⟦केतुना⟧, by action or by wisdom; and she shines over all these understandings.

## SBh 1.3.12.3
