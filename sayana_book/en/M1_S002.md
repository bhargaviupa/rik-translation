## SBh 1.2.0.1

The sūkta beginning ⟦अग्निमीळे⟧ is applied in the morning recitation of the Agniṣṭoma as a śastra; in the same way the tṛcas beginning ⟦वायवा याहि⟧ are applied in the Prauga-śastra. Here this is considered: is the śastra a rite of sanctification, consisting in remembering the deity, or is it a principal rite with an unseen fruit? Regarding this, Jaimini stated the prima facie view (⟦जै. सू. २. १. १३-२९⟧) in the sūtra ⟦स्तुतशस्त्रयोस्तु संस्कारो याज्यावद्देवताभिधानत्वात्⟧. It is heard: ⟦आज्यैः स्तुवते पृष्ठैः स्तुवते प्रउगं शंसति निष्केवल्यं शंसति⟧. There, praising and reciting are the statement of qualities that reside in the possessor of the qualities, as is seen in ⟦इन्द्रस्य नु वीर्याणि प्र वोचम्⟧ (⟦ऋ सं. १. ३२. १⟧). This being so, since by the analogy of the yājyā they state the deity who is the possessor of qualities, the stuta and the śastra must be accepted as being of the nature of sanctification. That the yājyā is of that nature has been decided in the fourth pāda of the tenth chapter (⟦जै. सू. १०. ४ ३९-४१⟧) by the gain of a seen purpose. Here too it is the same. The word ⟦तु⟧ excludes the view that they are principal rites. The siddhāntin refutes that view:

## SBh 1.2.0.2

⟦अर्थेन त्वपकृष्येत देवतानाम्नश्चोदनार्थस्य गुणभूतत्वात्⟧. By the word ⟦तु⟧ he rejects the view that it is a sanctification: on the sanctification view the mantra would be drawn away from its own place according to the purpose. Why? The name of the deity, Indra and so on, which is in the mantra, is subordinate, by the injunction that takes the form of the mantra, to the object, the deity, that is to be conveyed. Therefore where the principal deity is, there the subordinate mantra must be carried. For example, near the Māhendra cup this pragātha is handed down: ⟦अभि त्वा शूर⟧ (⟦ऋ. सं. ७. ३२. २२⟧). It makes known Indra, not Mahendra. Hence in a rite belonging to Indra this pragātha would have to be drawn away. In that case sequence and proximity would be set aside.

The prima facie opponent answers this refutation stated by the siddhāntin: ⟦वशावद्वा गुणार्थं स्यात्⟧. The word ⟦वा⟧ rejects the carrying of the pragātha elsewhere. The mention of the word Indra in the mantra would be for the purpose of indicating the quality of greatness. Just as in ⟦सा वा एषा सर्वदेवत्या यदजा वशा वायव्यामा लभेत⟧ (⟦तै. सं. ३. ४. ३. २⟧), in the rite enjoined by the word ⟦अजावशा⟧, the nigamas joined with the word ⟦छाग⟧ alone also indicate the quality of being a vaśā, so it is here. Therefore in a rite enjoined as possessing the quality of greatness, naming by the word Indra, which lacks the quality, is not contradictory. In the world too we see the word "king" used alone for a great king. The siddhāntin refutes this answer:

## SBh 1.2.0.3

⟦न श्रुतिसमवायित्वात्⟧. What was said, that by the analogy of the vaśā or of the king Indra is suitable as the deity of this cup, is not so, because the deity-ness is concomitant with the taddhita text. In "Māhendra cup", in the sense "this is its deity", the suffix aṇ is prescribed after the word Mahendra by ⟦महेन्द्राद्धाणौ च⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ४. २. २९⟧). Therefore Mahendra alone is the deity, not Indra.

He states an objection against the opposing view: ⟦गुणश्चानर्थकः⟧. If Indra were the deity, then, the sense being understood already by "Aindra cup" alone, the quality of greatness in "Māhendra" would be pointless. The ⟦च⟧ is for combining with the previous reason.

He states another reason: ⟦तथा याज्यापुरोरुचोः⟧. Just as, when the deities Indra and Mahendra are different, the quality of greatness has a purpose, so on this view alone the difference between the yājyā and the puronuvākyā also becomes possible. ⟦एन्द्र सानसिम्⟧ (⟦ऋ. सं. १. ८. १⟧) and so on are the yājyā and the puronuvākyā of Indra; ⟦महाँ इन्द्रो य ओजसा⟧ (⟦ऋ सं. ८. ६. १⟧) and so on are those of Mahendra.

## SBh 1.2.0.4

Regarding the example of the vaśā stated by the prima facie opponent, he states the disparity: ⟦वशायामर्थसमवायात्⟧. For the very vaśā that is heard in the injunctive sentence, the usage with the word ⟦छाग⟧ in the nigamas is not contradictory, because the object characterised by goat-ness inheres in the vaśā; and that is perceived by perception. But the difference between Indra and Mahendra has been established. Therefore the example is unequal. Thus, since on the sanctification view there would arise the drawing away of the pragātha in a rite of Indra, the siddhāntin's view is that the stotra and the śastra are principal rites, in order to prevent that.

Again the prima facie opponent refutes this view: ⟦यत्रेति वार्थवत्त्वात्स्यात्⟧. The word ⟦वा⟧ is for excluding the siddhāntin's view. This alone would be the view: where the rite is Indra's, there the pragātha must be taken. Why? Because of purposefulness. A mantra of Indra is able to make Indra known, so it would be purposeful; but since it is unable to make Mahendra known, purposelessness of the pragātha would result. Therefore it is proper that the stotra and the śastra are sanctificatory rites consisting in making the deity known. Thus the prima facie view stands.

Now he states the conclusion: ⟦अपि वा श्रुतिसंयोगात्प्रकरणे स्तौतिशंसती क्रियोत्पत्तिं विदध्याताम्⟧. By ⟦अपि वा⟧ the sanctification-nature is excluded. The root ṣṭu and the root śaṃs, both of them, in their own context, would enjoin the origination of some principal action. Why? Because of the connection with the śruti. The sense expressed by those two roots is called śruti; its connection is established if they are principal rites. For praise is the statement, chiefly, of the qualities residing in a possessor, making the possessor subordinate. When it is said ⟦यो देवदत्तः स चतुर्वेदाभिज्ञ⟧, all people understand praise. But when the quality is subordinate, praise is not understood.

## SBh 1.2.0.5

When it is said ⟦यश्चतुर्वेदाभिज्ञस्तमाकारय⟧, they do not take it as praise but understand only the primacy of the calling. In the same way in mantras too, when the speaker intends the primacy of the quality, as in "this deity is endowed with such qualities", the principal sense of the root stu is enjoined. On your view, however, the primacy being that of the remembering of the deity, as in "this one endowed with such qualities is the deity", this would not be praise. Hence, by the force of the śruti, these two are principal rites. That being so, since there is no intention of making the deity known, even an Aindra pragātha stays in the Māhendra rite belonging to its own context. If the seen purpose, consisting in remembering the deity, is not obtained, let the purpose then be unseen.

He states another reason for their being principal rites: the distinctness of the words. In ⟦द्वादशाग्निष्टोमस्य स्तोत्राणि द्वादश शस्त्राणि⟧, by the word "twelve" the separateness of the stotras is understood. On the view of making the deity known, since the making-known done by all the groups of mantras is one, the number twelve would not arise. But for principal rites, called the Ājya-stotra, the Pṛṣṭha-stotra and so on, being different, the number twelve is possible. In the same way it is to be applied also to the śastra sentence.

He states the objection against the opposing view: ⟦अनर्थकं च तद्वचनम्⟧. In the praise of Agni it is heard: ⟦आग्नेया ग्रहा भवन्ति⟧. There itself something else is said again: ⟦आग्नेयीषु स्तुवते । आग्नेयीः शंसन्ति⟧. On your view that statement would be pointless,

## SBh 1.2.0.6

because, in the mantras of the stotras and śastras obtained by extension, once the word for the deity is adapted in accordance with the Agni cups, Agni-ness is already accomplished. But on the principal-rite view, since there is no nature of making the deity known, there is no adaptation, and the statement enjoining other Agni mantras is purposeful.

Again he states another reason: ⟦अन्यश्चार्थः प्रतीयते⟧. For it is handed down: ⟦संबद्धे वै स्तोत्रशस्त्रे⟧. A connection holds between two, not of one. Therefore a difference in meaning of the stotra and the śastra is understood. And that is not possible on the sanctification view, because the meaning, consisting in making the deity known, is one. On the principal-rite view, however, the difference of meaning is possible, as the stotra rite and the śastra rite. Although ⟦ष्टुञ् स्तुतौ⟧ and ⟦शंसु स्तुतौ⟧ have the same sense, nevertheless the distinction between them is: ⟦प्रगीतमन्त्रसाध्यं स्तोत्रम्⟧ and ⟦अप्रगीतमन्त्रसाध्यं शस्त्रम्⟧.

He states another reason: ⟦अभिधानं च कर्मवत्⟧. Just as the principal rite is expressed, by the connection with the second case, in ⟦अग्निहोत्रं जुहोति⟧, so it is expressed in ⟦प्रउगं शंसति⟧. Hence, from the resemblance to that, it is a principal rite.

He states another reason:

## SBh 1.2.0.7

⟦फलनिर्वृत्तिश्च⟧. In the supplementary passage of the text of the verse reciting after the stotra, ⟦स्तुतस्य स्तुतमसि⟧ (⟦तै. सं. ३. २. ७. १⟧), only the fruit of the stotra is handed down: ⟦इन्द्रियावन्तो वनामहे धुक्षीमहि प्रजामिषम्⟧ (⟦तै. सं. ३ २. ७. २⟧). A fruit brought about by the deity is not handed down. Hence it is established that it is not a sanctification of the deity but a principal rite.

The purpose of this decision is the absence of adaptation in the modified rites. On the sanctification view, in whichever modified rite there is another deity, the word expressing it would have to be adapted. That it may not be so, it is said to be a principal rite. And this is laid down in a sūtra in the tenth chapter: ⟦ग्रहाणां देवतान्यत्वे स्तुतशस्त्रयोः कर्मत्वादविकारः स्यात्⟧ (⟦जै. सू. १०. ४. ४९⟧).

Here are two summary verses. In "he recites the Prauga" and so on, the question is subordination or primacy. The remembering of the deity is seen; by that, subordination of the stotra and the śastra would follow. If the purpose is remembrance, there is contradiction of the sense, taught by the śruti, of the two roots ⟦स्तौतिशंस्योर्धात्वोः⟧.

## SBh 1.2.0.8

Therefore, even admitting an unseen fruit, primacy is held to be for the sake of the śruti (⟦जै. न्या. २. १. ५⟧).

## SBh 1.2.0.9

In the Agniṣṭoma, on the day of pressing, the hotṛ, sent before sunrise, should recite the morning recitation. This is set out at length in the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa, in the passage beginning ⟦देवेभ्यः प्रातर्यावभ्यो होतरनुब्रूहीत्याहाध्वर्युः⟧ (⟦ऐ. ब्रा. २.१५⟧). And in that morning recitation the sūkta beginning ⟦अग्निमीळे⟧ is included; that has been explained. In the morning pressing, after the taking of the Vaiśvadeva cup, the Prauga-śastra is to be recited by the hotṛ. And that śastra consists of seven tṛcas beginning with ⟦वायवा याहि⟧.

## SBh 1.2.0.10

This is set out at length in the Brāhmaṇa, in the section beginning ⟦ग्रहोक्थम्⟧ (⟦ऐ.ब्रा. ३.१⟧). It is also laid down in a sūtra in the fifth chapter, in the section beginning ⟦स्तोत्रमग्रे शस्त्रात्⟧ (⟦आश्व. श्रौ. ५. १०⟧). Here is the Anukramaṇikā: ⟦वायो वायव्यैन्द्रवायवमैत्रावरुणास्तृचा अश्विना द्वादशाश्विनैन्द्रवैश्वदेवसारस्वतास्तृचाः सप्तैताः प्रउगदेवताः⟧. Its meaning is this. The sūkta beginning ⟦वायवा याहि⟧ has nine ṛks,

## SBh 1.2.0.11

because of the continuation of the word ⟦नव⟧ from ⟦अग्निं नव⟧. In it the first tṛca has Vāyu as its deity, the second has Indra and Vāyu, the third has Mitra and Varuṇa. The sūkta beginning ⟦अश्विना⟧ has twelve ṛks. In it the first tṛca is Aśvin, the second is Aindra, the third is Vaiśvadeva, the fourth is Sārasvata. The seven, Vāyu and so on up to Sarasvatī, who are set forth in those tṛcas, are the deities of the Prauga-śastra. Because of the continuation of Madhucchandas, he is the ṛṣi here. In the same way, by continuation, the metre is Gāyatra. In the Vāyavya tṛca, the first ṛk is one puronuvākyā of the Aindravāyava cup. And this is handed down in the Brāhmaṇa: ⟦वायव्या पूर्वा पुरोनुवाक्यैन्द्रवायव्युत्तरा⟧ (⟦ऐ. ब्रा. २. २६⟧). It is also laid down in the sūtra: ⟦वायवा याहि दर्शतेन्द्रवायू इमे सुता इत्यनुवाक्ये⟧ (⟦आश्व श्रौ. ५ ५⟧).

## SBh 1.2.1.1

⟦दर्शत⟧, O visible one, worth seeing; ⟦वायो⟧, O Vāyu; in this rite ⟦आ⟧ ⟦याहि⟧, come. For you ⟦इमे⟧, these ⟦सोमा⟧, Soma juices, are ⟦अरंकृताः⟧, adorned. The adorning is the preparation, beginning with the pressing. ⟦तेषां⟧ means "those Soma juices". Or else, "a part of them" is to be supplied. ⟦पाहि⟧ means "drink your own share". For the sake of drinking it, ⟦हवम्⟧, our call, ⟦श्रुधि⟧, hear. Here Yāska: ⟦वायवा याहि दर्शनीयेमे सोमा अरंकृता अलंकृतास्तेषां पिब शृणु नो ह्वानम्⟧ (⟦निरु. १०. २⟧).

## SBh 1.2.1.2

In ⟦दर्शत⟧ the Uṇādi suffix atac is added, by the sūtra beginning ⟦भृमृदृशि°⟧ (⟦उ. सू. ३. ३९०⟧). Because of the indicatory c, the word is acute on its final syllable; as a vocative it is unaccented. For the word ⟦सोम⟧, which ends in the suffix man by ⟦अर्तिस्तुसु⟧ (⟦उ. सू. १. १३७⟧) and so on, the nit-accent applies. In ⟦अलम⟧ there is a Vedic substitution of r. In the word ⟦अरंकृत⟧ the final-acute of the compound (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. २२३⟧) is set aside; the accent of the natural accent of the prior member, for a compound with an indeclinable first, comes up (⟦पा. सू. ६. २. २⟧); by ⟦भूषणेऽलम्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. १. ४. ६४⟧) the word ⟦अलम⟧ receives the designation gati; then the natural accent of the latter member ending in a kṛt suffix would come up by ⟦गतिकारक°⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. २. १३९⟧) and so on, but as an exception to that, by ⟦गतिरनन्तरः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. २. ४९⟧), the prior member keeps its natural accent.

## SBh 1.2.1.3

Because it is an indeclinable, the word alam is acute on its first syllable. In ⟦पाही⟧ the non-substitution of piba is Vedic. In ⟦श्रुधी⟧ there is the substitution of dhi for hi, by ⟦श्रुशृणु°⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. ४. १०२⟧) and so on. For what follows a word ending in a verbal ending there is no nighāta. Since by ⟦सेर्ह्यपिञ्च⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ३. ४. ८७⟧) the pit-nature is denied and the unaccented is prevented, the suffix accent results. In ⟦हवम⟧, from the root hvayati, when by ⟦बहुलं छन्दसि⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. ३४⟧) there is saṃprasāraṇa, the word ends in u, and so the suffix ap is added by ⟦ऋदोरप्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ३. ३. ५७⟧). Since it is pit it is unaccented, and the accent of the root remains. In the Saṃhitā, of ⟦श्रुधी⟧ there is lengthening by ⟦अन्येषामपि दृश्यते⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. ३. १३७⟧).

## SBh 1.2.2.1

O ⟦वायो⟧, the ⟦जरितारः⟧, the praisers, the officiating priests and sacrificers, ⟦त्वामच्छ⟧, directing themselves towards you, ⟦उक्थेभिः⟧, with the Ājya, Prauga and other śastras, ⟦जरन्ते⟧, praise. Of what kind? ⟦सुतसोमाः⟧, provided with pressed Soma. ⟦अहर्विदः⟧: the word ⟦अहः⟧ is well known in Vedic usage for a sacrifice such as the Agniṣṭoma that is completed in a single day. The meaning is: knowers of the sacrifices. Among the forty-four verbs of worship, beginning ⟦अर्चति गायति⟧, ⟦जरते ह्वयति⟧ (⟦नि. ३. १४, ८⟧) is read. Since praising is also a particular kind of worship, by propriety the root jarati here has the sense of praising.

## SBh 1.2.2.2

Of the word ⟦अच्छ⟧ there is lengthening in the Saṃhitā by ⟦निपातस्य च⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. ३. १३६⟧). In ⟦सुतसोमा⟧, since it is a bahuvrīhi, the natural accent of the prior member applies (⟦पा. सू. ६. २. १⟧). In ⟦अहर्विद⟧ the compound accent (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. २२३⟧) is set aside; the natural accent of the prior member, ending in the second case, would come up by ⟦तत्पुरुषे तुल्यार्थ°⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. २. २⟧) and so on, but as an exception to that, by ⟦गतिकारकोपपदात्कृत⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. २. १३९⟧), the natural accent of the latter member ending in a kṛt suffix applies.

## SBh 1.2.3.1

O ⟦वायो⟧, your ⟦धेना⟧, speech, ⟦जिगाति⟧, goes, for the sake of drinking Soma, to the ⟦दाशुषे⟧, that is, to the sacrificer who has given. The meaning is: Vāyu says, "O sacrificer, I shall drink the Soma given by you." Of what kind is the speech? ⟦प्रपृञ्चती⟧, making in a high degree a connection with the Soma; the meaning is that it describes the quality of the Soma. ⟦उरूची⟧, going to the many, ⟦उरून्⟧, that is, to many sacrificers; the meaning is that it describes all those who perform Soma sacrifices.

## SBh 1.2.3.2

In ⟦प्रपृञ्चती⟧ the suffix ṅīp is added by ⟦शतुरनुमः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. १७३⟧) and is acute. Among the fifty-seven names of speech, beginning ⟦श्लोकः धारा⟧, ⟦गणः धेना ग्नाः⟧ (⟦नि. १. ११. ३९⟧) is read. Among the one hundred and twenty-two verbs of motion, beginning ⟦वर्तते अयते⟧, ⟦गाति जिगाति⟧ (⟦नि. २. १४. ११३⟧) is read. In ⟦दाशुषे⟧ the fourth case is by ⟦गत्यर्थकर्मणि⟧ (⟦पा. सू. २. ३. १२⟧). In ⟦उरूची⟧, ṅīṣ having been added by reason of its belonging to the Gaura group (⟦पा. सू. ४. १. ४१⟧), the suffix accent results. In ⟦सोमपीतये⟧, although it is not a bahuvrīhi, by vyatyaya the natural accent of the prior member applies.

## SBh 1.2.4.1

The special application of this ṛk as the second puronuvākyā in the Aindravāyava cup has been stated before. O ⟦इन्द्रवायू⟧, O Indra and Vāyu, for you ⟦इमे⟧, these Soma juices, are ⟦सुताः⟧, pressed. Therefore you two, ⟦प्रयोभिः⟧, together with foods to be given to us, ⟦उप⟧ ⟦आ⟧ ⟦गतम्⟧, come near, that is, come towards us. ⟦हि⟧, since ⟦इन्दवः⟧, the Soma juices, ⟦उशन्ति⟧, desire ⟦वाम्⟧, you two, therefore it is proper to come.

## SBh 1.2.4.2

The word ⟦इन्द्रवायू⟧ has the vocative first-acute accent. ⟦प्रयांस्यन्नानि⟧ are foods, because they gratify the eaters. From the root prī, with the causative sense included (⟦पा. सू. ३. १. २६⟧), when the suffix asun is added, the nit-accent results. ## SBh 1.2.7.1

The tṛca ⟦मित्रं हुवे⟧ belongs to Mitra and Varuṇa; in the Gavāmayana, in the Ārambhaṇīya, on the twenty-fourth day, at the morning pressing, it is the stotriya of the Maitrāvaruṇa. There itself it is applied also in the Abhiplava ṣaḍaha. And so by Āśvalāyana, in the section beginning ⟦चतुर्विंशे होताऽजनिष्ट⟧, ⟦मित्रं वयं हवामहे मित्रं हुवे पूतदक्षम्⟧ (⟦आश्व. श्रौ. ७. २⟧) and so on is laid down in a sūtra. He also says, in the section ⟦अभिप्लवपृष्ठ्याहानि⟧, after picking out the additional insertions of the supplement: ⟦मित्रं वयं हवामहे मित्रं हुवे पूतदक्षम्⟧ (⟦आश्व. श्रौ. ७. ५⟧).

## SBh 1.2.7.2

I, in this rite, for the offering of oblation, call ⟦मित्रं⟧, Mitra, ⟦पूतदक्षं⟧, of purified strength. And I call ⟦वरुणं⟧ ⟦च⟧, Varuṇa too, ⟦रिशादसं⟧, the eater of the ⟦रिशानां⟧, the destroyers. Of what kind are Mitra and Varuṇa? They ⟦साधन्ता⟧, accomplish, make, ⟦धियं⟧, the act of raining, ⟦घृताचीं⟧, which brings water, ⟦घृत⟧, to the earth.

## SBh 1.2.7.3

The word ⟦मित्र⟧ is masculine and is acute on the final by the accent of the stem. ⟦हुवे⟧ is from the root hū: when śap is elided by ⟦बहुलं छन्दसि⟧ (⟦पा. सू. २. ४. ७३⟧), then, ⟦ह्वः संप्रसारणम्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. ३२⟧) being continued, saṃprasāraṇa by ⟦बहुलं छन्दसि⟧ (⟦पा.सू. ६.१. ३४⟧), and the substitution of uvaṅ. The nighāta results by ⟦तिङ्ङतिङः⟧ (⟦पा. सू ८. १. २८⟧). The word ⟦पूत⟧ is acute on the final by the suffix accent. In the bahuvrīhi the natural accent of the prior member applies. The word ⟦वरुण⟧, ending in the suffix unan by ⟦कॄवृदारिभ्य उनन्⟧ (⟦उ. सू.३. ३३३⟧), is acute on the first syllable because of its being nit. ⟦रिशाः⟧ are those who injure, enemies.

## SBh 1.2.7.4

The suffix ka is by ⟦इगुपधज्ञाप्रीकिरः कः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ३. १. १३५⟧). It is acute by the suffix accent. One who eats them is ⟦रिशादाः⟧, him. When the suffix asun is added by ⟦सर्वधातुभ्योऽसुन्⟧ (⟦उ. सू. ४, ६२८⟧), the latter member is acute on its first syllable by the nit-accent. By the natural accent of the latter member ending in a kṛt suffix, that alone remains. When the rest is lowered, the lengthening of like vowels is also acute alone, by ⟦एकादेश उदात्तेनोदात्तः⟧ (⟦पा. सू.८.२.५⟧). ⟦धीः⟧ is read among the twenty-six names of action beginning ⟦अपः⟧ (⟦नि. २.१.२१⟧). It is acute on the final by the accent of the stem. One who goes to ghee is ⟦घृताची⟧. The suffix kvin is added by ⟦ऋविग्दधृक्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ३. २. ५९⟧) and so on, and the nasal is dropped by ⟦अनिदिताम्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. ४. २४⟧).

## SBh 1.2.7.5

The suffix ṅīp is by ⟦अञ्चतेश्चोपसंख्यानम्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ४. १. ६. २⟧). When the letter a is dropped by ⟦अचः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. ४. १३८⟧), lengthening follows by ⟦चौ⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. ३. १३८⟧). The word ⟦घृत⟧, setting aside the first-acute of ⟦नब्विषयस्यानिसन्तस्य⟧ (⟦फि. सू. २६⟧), is acute on the final by ⟦घृतादीनां च⟧ (⟦फि. सू. २१⟧).

## SBh 1.2.7.6

The rule ⟦समासस्य⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. २२३⟧) gives the final-acute; as an exception to it, the natural accent of the prior member by ⟦तत्पुरुषे तुल्यार्थं°⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. २. २⟧) is set aside, and by ⟦गतिकारकोपपदात्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. २. १३९⟧) the natural accent of the latter member applies. When the root's letter a, which is acute, is dropped, the acuteness of ṅīp would obtain by ⟦अनुदात्तस्य च यत्रोदात्तलोपः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. १६१⟧), but by ⟦चौ⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. २२२⟧) the final of the prior member is acute.

## SBh 1.2.7.7

⟦साधन्ता⟧. From this root, ⟦राध साध संसिद्धौ⟧, with the causative sense included, in the place of laṭ the substitute śatṛ (⟦पा. सू. ३. २. १२४⟧) is set; setting aside the affix ⟦क्षुं⟧, śap is added by vyatyaya. Because the root is a-ending in the upadeśa, the unaccentedness of the lasārvadhātuka suffix śatṛ follows (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. १८६⟧). When the dual ending and śap are unaccented by ⟦अनुदात्तौ सुप्पितौ⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ३. १, ४⟧), the accent of the root alone remains, by ⟦धातोः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. १६२⟧). The substitution of ā for the case ending is by ⟦सुपां सुलुक्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ७. १, ३९⟧) and so on.

## SBh 1.2.8.1

O ⟦मित्रावरुणौ⟧, you two ⟦आशाथे⟧, that is ⟦आनशाथे⟧, have pervaded ⟦क्रतुम्⟧, this Soma sacrifice which is going on. By what cause? ⟦ऋतेन⟧, by the true fruit, because of its coming to be without fail. The meaning is: to give us the fruit. Of what kind are you two? ⟦ऋतावृधौ⟧. ⟦ऋतमित्युदकनाम⟧ (⟦निरु. २. २५⟧) and ⟦सत्यं वा यज्ञं वा⟧ (⟦निरु. ४. १९⟧), says Yāska. You two are the increasers of any one of water and the rest. For that very reason ⟦ऋतस्पृशा⟧, you two touch water and the rest. Of what kind is the sacrifice? ⟦बृहन्तम्⟧, exceedingly full-grown with its limbs and subsidiary limbs.

## SBh 1.2.8.2

The word ⟦ऋत⟧ is acute on the final because it belongs to the ghṛta group. In ⟦मित्रावरुणौ⟧, "Mitra and Varuṇa" gives ⟦मित्रावरुणौ⟧; the substitution ānaṅ for the prior member is by ⟦देवताद्वन्द्वे च⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. ३. २६⟧). In the sense "the two increasers of ṛta", the suffix kvip is added to the root vṛdh with the causative sense included. The lengthening of the prior member is by ⟦अन्येषामपि दृश्यते⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. ३. १३७⟧). ⟦ऋतस्पृशा⟧: the substitution of ḍā is by ⟦सुपां सुलुक्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ७. १. ३९⟧). Of the three vocatives beginning with ⟦मित्रावरुणौ⟧, because each follows its own prior word, the eighth-book nighāta results by ⟦आमन्त्रितस्य⟧ (⟦पा. . ८. १. १९⟧).

An objection: "For ⟦ऋतेन⟧, by the rule ⟦सुबामन्त्रिते पराङ्गवत्स्वरे⟧ (⟦पा. सू. २. १. २⟧), through the treatment as part of what follows, it enters the vocative; so, since the eighth-book nighāta does not apply, either because it is at the beginning of a pāda or because it does not follow a word, it ought to have the first-acute by ⟦आमन्त्रितस्य च⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. १९८⟧)." If so, no. Since the treatment as part of what follows depends on a word ending in a case ending and a vocative, it is a rule concerning words, by the restriction ⟦समर्थः पदविधिः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. २. १. १⟧).

## SBh 1.2.8.3

And here ⟦ऋतेन⟧ and ⟦मित्रावरुणौ⟧ are not mutually related, because of their connection with the verb ⟦आशाथे⟧ alone; hence they are not mutually capable. But where there is capability through mutual connection, there, by the treatment as part of what follows, the first-acute at the beginning of a pāda does arise, as in ⟦मरुतां पितस्तदहं गृणामि⟧. Because it ends in the suffix uti by ⟦मृग्रोरुतिः⟧ (⟦उ. सू. १. ९४⟧), in ⟦पृश्नियै वै पयसो मरुतो जाताः⟧ (⟦तै. सं. २. २. ११. ४⟧)

## SBh 1.2.8.4

and so on, the word ⟦मरुच्⟧ is acute on the final; yet in ⟦मरुतां पितः⟧, through capability, by the treatment as part of what follows alone, it has become acute on the first syllable. In the case at hand, however, because ⟦ऋतेन⟧ is not capable, there is no treatment as part of what follows. In ⟦ऋतावृधा⟧, where the nighāta of the second vocative is to be made, if it is objected that by ⟦आमन्त्रितं पूर्वमविद्यमानवत्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. १.७२⟧) the first vocative would have to be treated as non-existent, then let it be so. For that very reason, since it does not intervene, the second vocative is lowered by following the first word ⟦ऋतेन⟧ alone.

## SBh 1.2.8.5

As in ⟦इमं मे गङ्गे यमुने⟧ (⟦ऋ. सं. १०. ७५, ६⟧) and so on, even though the word ⟦गङ्गे⟧ is treated as non-existent, since it does not intervene, the lowering of the word ⟦यमुने⟧ is by relying on the word ⟦मे⟧ alone. Moreover, in the case at hand, the vocative ⟦मित्रावरुणौ⟧ is a general expression; as its qualifier the special expression is ⟦ऋतावृधा⟧. Hence, even by the prohibition of the treatment of the former as non-existent, by ⟦नामन्त्रिते समानाधिकरणे सामान्यवचनम्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. १.७३⟧), the lowering of the second is without obstruction.

## SBh 1.2.8.6

An objection: "Even so, since ⟦अपादादौ⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. १. १८⟧) continues, ⟦ऋतावृधे⟧ is at the beginning of the second pāda, so there ought to be no nighāta. For that very reason, in ⟦इमं मे गङ्गे⟧, the word ⟦शुतुद्रि⟧, a vocative that follows a word, is nevertheless at the beginning of a pāda, and hence, there being no nighāta, the first-acute resulted. So here too it ought to be so, or a special reason must be stated."

## SBh 1.2.8.7

It is answered: by the treatment of the word ⟦मित्रावरुण⟧ as part of what follows, by ⟦सुबामन्त्रिते०⟧ (⟦पा. सू. २. १. २⟧), by its entering the following word, ⟦ऋतावृधे⟧ is not at the beginning of a pāda. If it is said, "Then the word ⟦शुतुद्रि⟧ also, in just this way, by the treatment of the preceding word ⟦सरस्वति⟧ as part of what follows, would not be at the beginning of a pāda, and so would be lowered", no. The treatment as part of what follows is a rule concerning words, since it operates depending on a word ending in a case ending and on a vocative. Hence, only when the two are mutually connected can there be treatment as part of what follows, by the restriction ⟦समर्थः पदविधिः⟧ (⟦पा, सू. २. १. १⟧). And the words ⟦शुतुद्रि⟧ and ⟦सरस्वति⟧ are not mutually connected but are connected with ⟦सचत⟧, hence because of incapability there is no treatment as part of what follows. In the case at hand, however, in ⟦मित्रावरुणौ ऋतावृधौ⟧ there is mutual connection of the two through being in apposition, so there is capability, and so there ought to be treatment as part of what follows, as in ⟦मरुतां पितः⟧; this is the difference.

## SBh 1.2.8.8

An objection: "For that very reason, then, since the word ⟦मित्रावरुण⟧ is at the beginning of a pāda by the treatment as part of what follows, the nighāta of the vocative would not result, because of the exclusion by ⟦अपादादौ⟧." If so, no. Where an accent operates depending on a preceding word ending in a case ending and a following vocative, there the treatment as part of what follows by ⟦सुबामन्त्रिते⟧ applies. And the lowering of the word ⟦ऋतावृध⟧ is of this kind. There, since the preceding is not at the beginning of a pāda by the treatment as part of what follows, that operates. But the lowering of the word ⟦मित्रावरुण⟧ depends on the preceding word alone, not on the following vocative, so there is no treatment as part of what follows.

## SBh 1.2.8.9

An objection: "Like the treatment as part of what follows, the nighāta is also a rule concerning words; so, because of the incapability with ⟦ऋतेन⟧, it would not apply to the word ⟦मित्रावरुण⟧ which follows that word." If so, no. By ⟦समानवाक्ये निघातयुष्मदस्मदादेशा वक्तव्याः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. २. १. १. ११⟧), for the nighāta, even though it is a rule concerning words, being in the same sentence is enough, and mutual connection is not required as with the treatment as part of what follows. Enough of this.

⟦क्रतुम्⟧: ⟦कृञः कतुः⟧ (⟦उ. सू. १. ७७⟧). It is acute on the first syllable by the suffix accent. ⟦आशाथे⟧ means ⟦आनशाथे⟧. The lit is in the sense of the present, by ⟦छन्दसि लुङ्लङ्लिटः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ३. ४. ६⟧). The absence of nuṭ is Vedic.

## SBh 1.2.9.1

These two deities, Mitra and Varuṇa, ⟦दधाते⟧, nourish, ⟦नः⟧, our ⟦दक्षं⟧, strength, and ⟦अपसं⟧, rite. Of what kind are they? ⟦कवी⟧, wise. ⟦तुविजातौ⟧, born as benefactors of many. ⟦उरुक्षया⟧, having many dwellings. Among the twenty-four names of the wise, beginning ⟦विप्रः धीरः⟧, ⟦कविः मनीषी⟧ (⟦नि. ३. १५. १०⟧) is read. These two words, ⟦उरु तुवि⟧ (⟦नि. ३. १. १, २⟧), are read among the twelve names of many. Among the twenty-eight names of strength, beginning ⟦ओजः पाजः⟧, ⟦दक्षः वीळु⟧ (⟦नि. २. ९. १३⟧) is read. The word ⟦अपः⟧ is read among the twenty-six names of action (⟦नि. २. १. १⟧).

## SBh 1.2.9.2

⟦मित्रावरुणा⟧. The word ⟦मित्र⟧ is acute on the final by the accent of the stem. The word ⟦वरुण⟧ is acute on the first syllable by the nit-accent. In the dvandva, both remain by ⟦देवताद्वन्द्वे च⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. २. १४१⟧). ⟦तुविजातौ⟧: "born as benefactors of many, in relation to them", so in the genitive compound the compound-final acute results. For in a compound of the fourth case, the natural accent of the prior member would result, by ⟦क्ते च⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. २. ४५⟧). ⟦उरुक्षयौ⟧ are the dwellers of the ⟦उरूणां⟧, the many. From the root ⟦क्षि निवासगत्यो⟧, that in which they dwell is ⟦क्षय⟧. Since it ends in the suffix ac in the locative sense, by ⟦एरच्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ३. ३. ५६⟧), the final-acute would come up by ⟦चितः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १, १६३⟧), but the first-acute is prescribed by ⟦क्षयो निवासे⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. २०१⟧).

## SBh 1.2.9.3

In the compound, however, setting aside the final-acute by ⟦समासस्य⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. २२३⟧), the first-acute of the latter member, which comes up by the natural accent of the latter member ending in a kṛt suffix, is indeed set aside by the final-acute of the rule beginning with thātha; nevertheless the first-acute of the latter member is to be seen by ⟦परादिश्छन्दसि बहुलम्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. २. १९९⟧). ⟦दक्ष⟧ is from the root dakṣ in the sense of vigour, with the suffix ghañ; because of the indicatory ñ it is acute on the first syllable. That by which the fruit is obtained is ⟦अपः⟧, a rite. By ⟦आपः कर्माख्यायां ह्रस्वो नुट्च वा⟧ (⟦उ. सू. ४. ६४७⟧) it ends in asun; although the word ⟦अपः⟧ is acute on the first syllable because of being nit, as in ⟦अपसस्पारे⟧ (⟦ऋ. सं. ६. ६९. १⟧) and so on, here, by vyatyaya, it is acute on the beginning of the suffix.
