## SBh 0.4.126.1

(⟦बृo. उप- ४।४।२⟧) Therefore, since knowledge of the meaning is prescribed just as study is, the Veda must be explained for the sake of knowing its meaning. Without knowledge of the subject, the purpose, the relation and the qualified person, listeners would not take up the work; so the subject and the rest are set out. The subject of the explanation is the Veda that is to be explained. The knowledge of its meaning is the purpose. The relation is that of explanation and thing explained. And the person who seeks knowledge is the qualified person. Although this much is well known, yet if the Veda had no subject and the rest, then the explanation too would have no ultimate subject and the rest. Therefore the four of the Veda are stated.

## SBh 0.4.126.2

In the Veda, in its earlier and later portions respectively, dharma and Brahman are the subject, because these two cannot be obtained in any other way. And so it is laid down as a sūtra in the instruction on the human ends: ⟦धर्मब्रह्मणी वेदकवेद्ये⟧, "dharma and Brahman are to be known through the Veda alone." And in the second sūtra of Jaimini, the knowers of the tradition have stated a twofold restriction: the injunction alone is the means of knowledge in regard to dharma, and the injunction is a means of knowledge only. To establish the "alone" in the first, the fourth sūtra rejects that dharma is an object of perception: ⟦प्रत्यक्षमनिमित्ताविद्यभानोपलम्भनत्वाद्⟧ (⟦जै० १।१।४⟧), "perception (is not the means), because it is the apprehension of ... (things already present)." **NOTE**{the cited sūtra is garbled after its first word; is the intended reading animitta, "not the cause", with vidyamāna-upalambhana, "apprehension of what is present"?}

## SBh 0.4.126.3

Dharma, which comes into being after the performance, does not exist before it; therefore it has no fitness to be perceived. In the later time too, because it has no form and the like, it is not grasped by the senses. For this very reason everyone calls it "unseen". Because it has no inferential mark, it cannot be an object of inference either. If it is said that pleasure and pain are the mark of dharma and adharma, that is granted; but even this relation of mark and thing marked is known only through the Veda. Therefore the injunction alone is the means of knowledge in regard to dharma.

## SBh 0.4.126.4

In the second varṇaka of the third sūtra of Vyāsa, the commentators have explained that Brahman, although an established entity, has scripture alone as its subject; the intention being that Brahman, the cause of the origin and the rest of the world, is known from scripture alone as the means of knowledge. And there is also the śruti: ⟦नावेदविन्मनुते तं बृहन्तम्⟧, "one who does not know the Veda does not think of that great one" (⟦तै० ब्रा ३।१२।९।७⟧). The reasoning there was stated by the earlier teachers thus: ⟦रूपलिङ्गादिराहित्यान् नास्य मानख्तरयोग्यता⟧, "because it lacks form, mark and the like, it has no fitness for another means of knowledge." Therefore, since they cannot be obtained in any other way, the Veda is the subject of dharma and Brahman.

## SBh 0.4.126.5

The knowledge of both of these is the direct purpose of the Veda. Nor should it be suspected that this knowledge ends in no human end, like the knowledge ⟦सप्तद्वीपा वसुमती⟧, "the earth has seven continents", or ⟦राजासौःगच्छति⟧, "that king is going", and the like; for the human end that arises from dharma is praised: ⟦धर्मो विश्वस्य जगतः प्रतिष्ठा⟧, "dharma is the foundation of the whole world; in the world people approach the most righteous man; by dharma they drive away sin; in dharma everything is established; therefore they call dharma supreme" (⟦तै० आ०१०।६३।७⟧). And dharma is a human end also because it is a cause of victory, like a king's help to a weak man.

## SBh 0.4.126.6

And so the followers of the Vājasaneyaka recite in the section on creation: ⟦स नैव व्यभवत्⟧, "He did not flourish; he created a still more excellent form, dharma. That dharma is the kṣatra of the kṣatra; therefore there is nothing higher than dharma; thereby the weaker hopes against the stronger, as through a king. That which is dharma is truth; therefore they say of one who speaks truth, 'he speaks dharma', and of one who speaks dharma, 'he speaks truth'; for both of these are this" (⟦बृ. उप. १ ।४1१४⟧). Also ⟦ब्रह्मविदाप्नोति परम्⟧, "the knower of Brahman attains the supreme" (⟦तै० आ०८।१⟧), and ⟦ब्रह्म वेद ब्रह्मैव भवति⟧, "he who knows Brahman becomes Brahman itself" (⟦मुण्ड० ३।⟧

## SBh 0.4.127.1

⟦२।।⟧). ⟦तरति शोकमात्मवित्⟧, "the knower of the self crosses sorrow" (⟦छा० उप० ७।१।३⟧). In scriptural passages such as these the human end that arises from knowledge of Brahman is well known. The person who seeks knowledge of both is qualified in the Veda; and he is a man of the three classes. For a woman and a śūdra, although the need for knowledge exists, qualification in the Veda is forbidden, because without the initiation there is no study. Knowledge of dharma and Brahman arises for them through the Purāṇas and the like. Therefore men of the three classes are qualified for knowledge of the meaning through the Veda.

## SBh 0.4.127.2

The relation of the Veda with dharma and Brahman is that of what is taught and what teaches; with the knowledge of them, that of what is produced and what produces; with men of the three classes, that of the benefited and the benefactor. Having thus understood the four connected matters, the subject and the rest, let the listeners with attentive minds engage in the explanation of the Veda. To make known the meaning of the very deep Veda, the six aṅgas, śikṣā and the rest, have arisen. For this very reason the followers of the Atharvaveda record in the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad that these are of the nature of the lower knowledge: ⟦द्वे विद्ये वेदितव्ये इति ह स्म यद् ब्रह्मविदो वदन्ति, परा चैवापरा च⟧, "'Two knowledges are to be known', so indeed the knowers of Brahman say, 'the higher and the lower.' Of these the lower is the Ṛgveda, the Yajurveda, the Sāmaveda, the Atharvaveda, śikṣā, kalpa, grammar, nirukta, metre, astronomy. Then the higher is that by which that imperishable is reached" (⟦मुण्ड. १।१।४⟧). Because they are the cause of knowledge of dharma, which is a means, the ritual portions together with the six aṅgas are the lower knowledge. Because they are the cause of knowledge of Brahman, which is the supreme human end, the Upaniṣads are the higher knowledge.

## SBh 0.4.128.3

Now, did Āśvalāyana proceed following the mantra portion, or following the Brāhmaṇa? Not the first: since he has gone beyond it by saying ⟦दर्शपूर्णमासौ तु पूर्व व्याख्यास्यामः⟧, "we shall explain the new-moon and full-moon sacrifices first" (⟦आश्व० सू०१।१।३⟧), for mantras such as ⟦अग्निमीळे⟧ are nowhere applied in the new-moon and full-moon sacrifices. Not the second either: since in the Brāhmaṇa the dīkṣaṇīyā offering is begun with ⟦आग्नावैष्णवमेकादशकपालं निर्वपन्ति दीक्षणीया⟧, "they offer an eleven-potsherd cake to Agni and Viṣṇu as the dīkṣaṇīyā" (⟦ऐत० ब्रा० १ ।१⟧). To this we reply: the mantra portion proceeds in the order of recitation such as the brahmayajña, not in the order of performing sacrifices. And the brahmayajña is itself enjoined: ⟦यः स्वाध्यायमधीयीतैकामष्यृच यजुः साम वा तद् ब्रह्मयज्ञ⟧, "he who recites his own Veda, even a single ṛk, yajus or sāman, that is the brahmayajña" (⟦तै० आ० २।१०।६⟧). Likewise ⟦वाचःस्तोमे पारिप्लवं शंसति⟧, "at the speech-stoma he recites the pāriplava", enjoins that all the ṛks, all the yajus formulas and all the sāmans be recited. **NOTE**{the supplied text reads kharvāṇi, "dwarf", for the yajus formulas where sarvāṇi, "all", seems meant; is this correct?} Likewise, when the Āśvina is about to be performed, it is enjoined: ⟦सूर्यो नोदियादपि सर्वा दाशतयीखव्रूयाद्⟧, "let the sun not rise; he should also recite all the Daśatayī" (⟦आप० श्रौत सूत्र १४।१।२⟧). The particular application of particular mantras, however, Āśvalāyana shows by drawing on the means of knowledge such as direct text, indication and syntactic connection; (the verb "drawing on" is completed at the beginning of the next unit).

## SBh 0.4.129.1

He shows it by drawing on them. Therefore, even in the absence of the order of the mantra portion, there is no contradiction.

## SBh 0.4.129.2

The mantras beginning ⟦ईषे त्वा⟧ are also recited in the very order of the performance of the sacrifice; so Āpastamba and others set out to show their application in that same order. Because they are so recited, in recitation and the like also that same order holds.

## SBh 0.4.129.3

Although the Brāhmaṇa begins with the dīkṣaṇīyā offering, yet, since that offering is a modification of the new-moon and full-moon sacrifices and so depends on them, it is proper for Āśvalāyana to explain them first. Therefore the Kalpa-sūtra, by teaching the performance of the sacrifice together with the application of the mantras, is helpful.

## SBh 0.4.129.4

If it is said: then let Āśvalāyana state the application of the ṛks alone, such as the sāmidhenī verses ⟦प्र वो वाजा⟧; but those such as ⟦नमः प्रवक्त⟧ (⟦अश्वा सूत्र १ ।२⟧) are not recited in the Saṃhitā, so from where are they applied? this is no fault, because the application of those recited in other branches and established in other Brāhmaṇas has to be stated here by the principle of the collecting of subsidiary features; and the experts in the principles hold that one act is the same in all the branches. Therefore, like śikṣā, kalpa too is needed.

## SBh 0.4.129.5

Grammar too is of use, by teaching the base, the suffix and the rest, for settling the form of words and their meaning. ## SBh 0.4.134.2

The word nigama means Vedic speech, since Yāska in various places introduces sentences of the Veda with "there is also a nigama". ## SBh 0.4.134.3

Because in this text, which consists of three portions in five chapters, the meaning of the word is stated independent of any other help, the text is the Nirukta. And its explanation Yāska composed in twelve chapters, beginning with ⟦समाम्नायः समाम्नातः⟧ and ending with ⟦तस्यास्तस्यास्ताद्भाव्यमनुभवत्यनुभवति⟧. That too is called Nirukta, from the derivation that the possible meanings of the parts of each single word are stated there completely. For there, having declared that the classes of words are four, the noun, the verb, the preposition and the particle, and having explained the nature of the particle as "they fall into various meanings", he gave this example: ⟦नेति प्रतिषेधार्थीयो भाषायामुभयमन्वध्यायं नेन्द्र देवममसत⟧, "na has the sense of prohibition in common speech; in the Veda, following the chapters, both: 'they did not regard Indra as a god' has the sense of prohibition", and ⟦दुर्मदासो न सुरायामित्युपमार्थीय⟧, "'like drunkards in liquor' has the sense of comparison" (⟦नि १।४⟧). And although in the world the letter na has only the sense of prohibition, in this text it is understood that in the Veda it is given as an example of both senses, prohibition and comparison. The particular derivations of the various words stated by the author in this way will be cited by us at the very time of explaining the various mantras.

Nor should it be suspected that the derivations have no basis, because for this very purpose of derivation some derivations of words have been stated in the Brāhmaṇas: ⟦तदाहुतीनाम् आहुतित्वम्⟧, "that is the nature of oblations as oblations" (⟦ऐत० ब्रा० १।१।२⟧); ⟦तमिदन्द्रं सन्तमिन्द्र इत्याचक्षत⟧, "him, being this Indra, they call Indra" (⟦ऐत० आ० २।४।३⟧); and ⟦यदप्रथयत् तत् पृथिव्याः पृथिवीत्वम्⟧, "because it spread out, that is the earthness of the earth" (⟦तै० ब्रा० १।१।३।७⟧). For this very reason the author says: ⟦तदिदं विद्यास्थानं व्याकरणस्य कार्त्स्न्य स्वार्थसाधकञ्च⟧, "this branch of knowledge is the completeness of grammar and accomplishes its own purpose" (⟦नि० १।१५⟧). Therefore the Nirukta is useful for understanding the meaning of the Veda.

That these six texts, which help towards the meaning of the Veda, are limbs of the Veda is stated thus in the Śikṣā: ⟦छन्दः पादौ तु वेदस्य हस्तौ कल्पोऽथ पठ्यते । ज्योतिषामयनं चक्षुर्निरुक्तं श्रोत्रमुच्यते ।। शिक्षाघ्राणं तु वेदस्य मुखं व्याकरणं स्मृतम् । तस्मात् साङ्गमधीत्यैव ब्रह्मलोके महीयते⟧, "metre is the two feet of the Veda; kalpa is then declared the two hands; the course of the luminaries is the eye; nirukta is called the ear. Śikṣā is the nose of the Veda; grammar is remembered as the mouth. Therefore, having studied the Veda with its limbs, one is honoured in the world of Brahmā" (⟦पा० शि० ४१-४२⟧). Like the six limbs, the usefulness of the Purāṇas and the rest for knowing the meaning of the Veda is also recalled by Yājñavalkya: ⟦पुराणन्यायमीमांसाधर्मशास्त्राङ्गमिश्रिताः । वेदाः स्थानानि विद्यानां धर्मस्य च चतुर्दश⟧, "the Vedas joined with the Purāṇa, Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, Dharmaśāstra and the limbs are the fourteen seats of knowledge and of dharma" (⟦या० स्मृ० १।३⟧). ⟦द्वतिहाखपुराणाभ्यां वेदं समुपबृंहयेत् । बिभ्येत्यल्पश्रुताद् वेदो मामयं प्रहरेदिति⟧, "one should expand the Veda with the Itihāsa and the Purāṇa; the Veda is afraid of one of little learning, thinking 'he will strike me'" (⟦म० भा० १।१।२६७⟧): so too it is recalled elsewhere. The hymns of the branches such as the Aitareya, Taittirīya and Kāṭhaka, and the narratives such as those of Hariścandra and Naciketas,

## SBh 0.4.136.1

which are useful for understanding dharma and Brahman, are made clear in the various Itihāsa texts. And the creation, maintenance, dissolution and the rest told in the Upaniṣads are made clear in the Purāṇas, the Brāhma, Pādma, Vaiṣṇava and others: ⟦सर्गश्च प्रतिसर्गश्च वंशो मन्वन्तराणि च । वंशानुचरितञ्चेति पुराणं पञ्चलक्षणम्⟧, "creation, secondary creation, genealogy, the periods of the Manus, and the deeds of the lineages: the Purāṇa has five marks" (⟦वि० पु० ३।६।२५⟧)

## SBh 0.4.136.2

from which it is understood that creation and the rest are what the Purāṇas teach. In the Nyāya treatise, because sixteen categories such as the means of knowledge, the object of knowledge, doubt, purpose and example are set out, following it a decision can be made that this sentence is a means of knowledge in this meaning and not the other. The use of the earlier and the later Mīmāṃsā for the Veda's meaning is very clear indeed. In the Smṛtis proclaimed by Manu, Atri, Viṣṇu, Hārīta and others, the rules stated in the Veda, such as the twilight worship, are elaborated. ⟦तदु ह वा एते ब्रह्मवादिनः पूर्वाभिमुखाः सन्ध्यायां गायत्र्याभिमन्त्रिता आपः ऊर्द्ध्व विक्षिपन्ति⟧, "those speakers on Brahman, facing east at twilight, throw water upward, consecrated with the Gāyatrī" (⟦तै० आ० २।२।२⟧), and the like, is the rule of the twilight worship. ⟦पञ्च वा एते महायज्ञाः सतति प्रतायन्ते⟧, "these five great sacrifices are continuously extended" (⟦तै० आ० २।१०⟧), and the like, is the rule of the great sacrifices. In the same way other rules are to be seen. In the way stated, because the Purāṇas and the rest are of use for knowing the meaning of the Veda, it is proper that they are seats of knowledge. When knowledge reinforced by these fourteen seats of knowledge, the Purāṇas and the rest, is to be received, the special qualification of the one qualified is shown by four mantras, by hundreds of other branches. **NOTE**{the words ⟦शाखान्तरशतैश्चतुर्भिर्मन्त्रैः⟧ read "by hundreds of other branches, by four mantras"; is the sense four mantras taken from other branches?} And Yāska cited those mantras (⟦नि० २।४⟧).

## SBh 0.4.136.3

There this is the first mantra: ⟦विद्या ह वै ब्राह्मणमाजगाम गोपाय मा शेवधिष्टेऽश्मस्मि । असूयकायानृजवे ऽयताय न मा ब्रूया वीर्यवती तथा स्याम्⟧, "Knowledge came indeed to the Brāhmaṇa: 'Guard me; I am your treasure. To the scoffer, the crooked, the uncontrolled, do not tell me; so may I be potent.'"

## SBh 0.4.136.4

The deity who presides over knowledge, coming to the Brāhmaṇa, the instructor, the teacher, prayed thus: "O Brāhmaṇa, protect me by not teaching me to one who is not qualified. I am to you like a treasure, a cause of the human ends. Such being I, and you, my instructor, he who bears envy, and he who does not practise knowledge with sincerity, and he who is not regulated in conduct such as bathing and sipping water: to such sham pupils you should not tell me; that being so, abiding in your heart, I would give fruit."

## SBh 0.4.136.5

Now the second mantra: ⟦य आतृणत्यवितथेन कर्णावदुःखं कुर्वन्नमृतं संप्रयच्छन्⟧, "he who pierces the ears with the unfalse, causing no pain, bestowing the immortal, ⟦तं मन्येत पितरं मातरश्च तस्मै न द्रह्येत् कतमञ्जनाह⟧, him one should regard as father and mother; to him one should not do harm in any way."

## SBh 0.4.136.6

Having stated the rule for the teacher in the previous mantra, in this mantra the rule for the pupil is stated. The ⟦वितथम्⟧, the false, is the worldly sentence, ⟦अमृतम्⟧, which is not a human end; **NOTE**{the supplied text reads amṛta, "immortal", beside vitatha, "false"; is anṛta, "untrue", meant?} its opposite

## SBh 0.4.137.1

is the true Vedic sentence, the unfalse. With such a sentence the teacher who ⟦कर्णावातृणत्ति⟧ "pierces the pupil's ears" fills and bores them on all sides. By the force of the prefix and by propriety the root ⟦तृणत्ति⟧ is used in another sense: the meaning is that he who always makes him hear the Veda. Doing what? Causing no pain: for a dull-witted boy who at first cannot take even half a ṛk or a ṛk, he makes him take a word or a part of a quarter-verse, so that there may be no pain. Moreover, bestowing the immortal: because it is the means of reaching immortality, which is divine birth or release, the meaning of the Veda is immortal; he performs the giving of that. ⟦तं⟧, such a teacher, ⟦सम्⟧, the pupil should regard as in the form of the principal mother and father. **NOTE**{the word ⟦सम्⟧ stands before "pupil" and has no clear construction; what is it?} For the previously established mother and father are not principal, being lower, because they give this human body. To such a teacher, in the form of the principal mother and father, he should do not even one harm.

## SBh 0.4.137.2

Now the third mantra: ⟦अध्यापिता ये गुरुं नाद्रियन्ते विप्रा वाचा मनसा कर्मणा⟧, "brāhmaṇas who, having been taught, do not honour the guru in speech, mind or action: ⟦यथैव ते न गुरोर्भोजनीयस्तथैव तान्न भुनक्ति क्षुतं तत्⟧, just as they are not to be enjoyed by the guru, so that which was heard (⟦क्षुतं⟧, as supplied) does not protect them." **NOTE**{the mantra has ⟦क्षुतं⟧, "sneezed", where the gloss has the word for "heard"; is śrutam meant?}

## SBh 0.4.137.3

The lowest brāhmaṇas who, though taught by the guru, do not honour the guru by humble speech, by thinking of his welfare, or by service, are devoid of respect; these sham pupils are not to be enjoyed by the guru, that is, they are not fit to be experienced; the guru does not show favour to them. Just as they are not to be protected by the guru, so that heard word of the Veda, taught by the guru, does not protect those lowest pupils: that is, it does not become fruit-giving. This is the meaning.

## SBh 0.4.137.4

Now the fourth mantra: ⟦यमेव विद्याः शुचिमप्रमत्तं मेधाविनं ब्रह्मचर्योपपन्नम्⟧, "whom you would know as pure, careful, intelligent, endowed with student's discipline, ⟦यस्ते न द्रुह्येत् कतमञ्चनाह तस्मै मा ब्रूया निधिपायब्रह्मन्⟧, who would not do you harm in any way: to him, the guardian of the treasure, O Brāhmaṇa, tell me."

## SBh 0.4.137.5

O teacher, whomever you should know as a principal pupil endowed with qualities such as purity, and moreover the principal pupil who would never at any time harm you: to that principal pupil, the guardian of your treasure, O Brāhmaṇa, you should tell me, the knowledge that is of the form of the Veda. Thus, since this was prayed for by the deity of knowledge, the knowledge of the Veda is to be taught by the teacher to the principal pupil. For that purpose the Ṛgveda is explained by us in accordance with the six aṅgas. Thus ends the introduction to the commentary on the Ṛgveda-saṃhitā.
