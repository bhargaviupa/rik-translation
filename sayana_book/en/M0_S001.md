The well-disposed Vāgīśa and the others, at the beginning of all undertakings, having bowed to whom they **NOTE**{the supplied letters ⟦स्तुस्तृंनमामि⟧ look like two words run together (perhaps "may be" and "I bow to"); is the intended sense "they have accomplished their task, and I bow to"?} accomplished their task: to him, ⟦गजाननम्⟧, the elephant-faced one, I bow. By his side-glance, the king **NOTE**{the supplied letters ⟦तद्रुपदधद् क्ष्णहीपतिः⟧ are damaged; which king is meant, and what is the participle ⟦दधद्⟧ doing with "his form"?} commanded Mādhavācārya to bring out the meaning of the Veda. The two Mīmāṃsās, the earlier and the later, having expounded those two in the most condensed form, the compassionate Mādhavācārya has set out to tell the meaning of the Veda.

## SBh 0.1.89.2

Because of the Adhvaryu's pre-eminence in sacrifices, the Yajurveda was expounded earlier; now, for the sake of the Hotṛ, the Ṛgveda will be expounded. In this, the first chapter is to be heard by tradition. The intelligent man, having been grounded by that much, is able to understand everything.

## SBh 0.1.89.3

Here some say: because the Ṛgveda is recited first everywhere, and so is pre-eminent, its exposition first is proper. Its being first is plain in the Puruṣa hymn: ⟦तस्माद् यज्ञात् सर्वहुत⟧, "from that sacrifice, offered to by all", the ⟦ऋचः⟧, the ṛcs, and the ⟦सामानि⟧, the sāmans, were born; ⟦छन्दांसि जज्ञिरे तस्मात्⟧, the metres were born from that; the ⟦यजु⟧, the yajus, ⟦स्तस्मादजायत⟧, was born from that (⟦ऋ०. १०. ९०. ९⟧). "From that sacrifice" means from the Supreme Lord, spoken of in ⟦सहस्रशीर्षा पुरुष⟧ (⟦ऋ०. १०. ९०. ९⟧) **NOTE**{the same reference is given for both quotations; is the second quotation not from a different verse?}. ⟦यज्ञाद्⟧ means from him who is to be worshipped, honoured; ⟦सर्वहुतः⟧ means from him who is offered to by all. Although Indra and the others are offered to here and there, there is no contradiction, since it is the Supreme Lord alone who abides in the form of Indra and the others.

## SBh 0.1.90.1

And so the mantra text says: ⟦इन्द्रं मित्रं वरुणमग्निमाहुरथो दिव्यः स सुपर्णो गरुत्मान्⟧. They call him Indra, Mitra, Varuṇa, Agni, and he is the divine, well-winged Garutmān.

## SBh 0.1.90.2

⟦एकं सद् विप्रा वहुधा वदन्त्यग्नि यमं मातरिश्वानमाहुः⟧ (⟦ऋ०. १. १६४.४६⟧): the wise speak of the one that is, in many ways; they call it Agni, Yama, Mātariśvan. The followers of the Vājasaneyaka also recite: ⟦तद् यद् इदमाहुरमु यजामु यजेत्येकैकं देवम् एतस्यैव सा विसृष्टिरेष उ ह्येव सर्व्वे देवा इति⟧ (⟦वृ०. १. ४. ६⟧): that which they say, "sacrifice to him, sacrifice to him", of each single god, is only his creation, and he is indeed all the gods. Therefore it is the Supreme Lord alone who is offered to by all.

## SBh 0.1.90.3

The ṛc is pre-eminent not only by being first in recitation, but also because **NOTE**{the supplied letters ⟦यज्ञाङ्गदार्ढ्यण्डेत्वादीप⟧ are damaged; is the intended sense "because it is the cause of firmness of the parts of the sacrifice"?} . And so the Taittirīyas recite (⟦तै. सं. ६।५।१०।३⟧): what is done in the sacrifice with a sāman or a yajus is loose; what is done **NOTE**{the supplied letters ⟦यद्क्त्वातद्दृढ⟧ are damaged; is the intended sense "what is done with a ṛc, that is firm"?}, that is firm.

## SBh 0.1.90.4

Similarly the Brāhmaṇas belonging to all the Vedas, for firmness of belief in what they have stated, cite only a ṛc, saying ⟦तदेतद् ऋचा अभ्युक्त⟧, "this has been said with a ṛc". In the mantra sections also, belonging to the Yajurveda, here and there many ṛcs to be employed by the Adhvaryu are recited. The dependence of all the sāmans **NOTE**{the supplied letters ⟦सास्रा तु सर्वेघाम्ऋगाश्रितत्वं⟧ are damaged; is the intended sense "the dependence of all the sāmans on the ṛc"?} on the ṛc is well known. The followers of the Atharvan too study in their own saṃhitā mostly ṛcs. Therefore, because of **NOTE**{the supplied letters ⟦द्वनत्वाद⟧ are damaged; what quality of the ṛc is meant, "being resorted to" or similar?} by all the other Vedas, its pre-eminence follows. The Chāndogas, giving first place to the Ṛgveda, recite Nārada's words to Sanatkumāra thus: ⟦ऋग्वेदं भगवोऽध्येमि यजुर्वेदं सामवेदमाथर्वणं चेति⟧ (⟦छा० ७११२⟧), "Sir, I study the Ṛgveda, the Yajurveda, the Sāmaveda and the Atharvan". In the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad also it is recited thus: ⟦ऋग्वेदो यजुर्वेदः सामवेदोऽथर्वण इति⟧ (⟦मु० १।१।५⟧). In the Tāpanīya Upaniṣad too, in the sections on the Mantrarāja, they recite the order of study thus: ⟦ऋग्यजुःसामाथर्वाणश्चत्वारो वेदाः साङ्गाः सशाखाश्चत्वारः पादा भवन्ति इति⟧ (⟦नृ० १।१।२⟧), "the four Vedas, Ṛg, Yajus, Sāman and Atharvan, with their limbs and branches, are the four feet". So it is to be illustrated everywhere. Therefore, since the Ṛgveda is pre-eminent, its exposition first is fitting; so they say.

## SBh 0.1.90.5

To them this is said. Granted that in the study of all the Vedas, their recitation through, the brahmayajña, japa and the like, the Ṛgveda alone comes first. But since knowledge of meaning serves the performance of sacrifices, and in that the Yajurveda alone is the principal, its exposition alone is proper first. A certain ṛc itself states that pre-eminence: ⟦ऋचां त्वः पोषमास्ते पुपुष्वान् गायत्रं त्वो गायति शक्वरीषु। ब्रह्मा त्वो वदति जातविद्यां यज्ञस्य मात्रां विमिमीत उत्वः।।⟧ (⟦ऋग्वेदः --10.71.11⟧): one sits, having grown fat, making the ṛcs flourish; one sings the gāyatra in the śakvarī verses; one, Brahmā, speaks the knowledge of what is born; and one measures out the measure of the sacrifice.

## SBh 0.1.91.1

Yāska, the author of the Nirukta, briefly shows the purport of this ṛc: ⟦ऋत्विक्कर्मणां विनियोगमाचष्टे⟧ (⟦नि०१।८⟧), "it describes the assignment of the priests' functions". Again he himself explains the first quarter: ⟦ऋचामेकः पोषमास्ते पुपुष्वान्होतर्गर्चनी⟧ (⟦नि०१।८⟧) **NOTE**{the last word of the supplied quotation, ⟦होतर्गर्चनी⟧, seems damaged; is the intended text "hotā, arcanī"?}. Its meaning: the word ⟦त्व⟧ is a synonym of "one", and qualifies the hotṛ. One priest, named hotṛ, sits at the time of the sacrifice making the ṛcs belonging to his own Veda flourish: having collected into one place the ṛcs recited in different places, he makes "this much is this śastra" **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦चति⟧ before "makes" is unclear; is it a form of "collects" or "adds"?}; this is the flourishing. The word ṛc declares this meaning ⟦अर्चनी⟧: from the derivation of the word ṛc, "that by which a particular deity, a particular action or a particular instrument of it is praised, ⟦अर्ज्यते⟧".

## SBh 0.1.91.2

Now he explains the second quarter: ⟦गायत्रमेको गायति शक्वरीपूद्गाता⟧, "one, the udgātṛ, sings the gāyatra in the śakvarīs. The gāyatra is from the root ⟦गायतेः⟧ in the sense of praise. The śakvarīs are ṛcs, from the root ⟦शक्रोते⟧; as it is known, 'because with these he was able to kill Vṛtra, that is the śakvarī-ness of the śakvarīs'" (⟦कौ- २३।२⟧) **NOTE**{the supplied letters ⟦शङ्कव्यं ऋचः⟧ are damaged; is the intended text "the śakvarīs are ṛcs"?}. Its meaning: one priest named udgātṛ sings the stotra called gāyatra and **NOTE**{the supplied letters the damaged word after "stotra called gāyatra" in the supplied text are damaged; is the intended sense "the ṛcs called śakvarī"?}. Since roots have many meanings, the word gāyatra is produced from the root ⟦गायति⟧, which expresses the act of praise. The word śakvarī is produced from the root ⟦शक्रोति⟧; the derivation is that "with these **NOTE**{the supplied letters ⟦आभिर्व्यम्भि⟧ are damaged; is the intended text "with these ṛcs"?} he is able to kill Vṛtra's enemy", which is known in a certain Brāhmaṇa.

## SBh 0.1.91.3

Now he explains the third quarter: ⟦ब्रह्मा एकः जाते जाते विद्यां वदति, ब्रह्मा सर्व्वविदिति⟧ (⟦नि०१।८⟧), "the Brahmā, one, speaks vidyā in each thing that is born; Brahmā knows all". Its meaning: one priest named Brahmā, in each thing born, that is, at each moment arisen, in a rite commenced such as the carrying forward of the waters, speaks vidyā, that is, permission: addressed with "Brahmā, shall I carry forward the waters?", he gives permission with ⟦ॐ प्रणय⟧, "Om, carry forward". And that Brahmā is acquainted with all the rites spoken of in the three Vedas. Therefore, having seen the propriety, he is able to permit each of these, and to set right any error that occurs. The Chāndogas recite his capability: ⟦एष एव यज्ञस्तस्य मनश्च वाक् च वर्त्तनी । तयोरन्यतरां मनसा रूस्करोति ब्रह्मा, वाचा होताध्वर्युरुद्गाता चान्यतराम्⟧ (⟦छा० ४।१६।१-२⟧): this is the sacrifice; its two paths are mind and speech. Of these two, Brahmā prepares one by mind; the hotṛ, the adhvaryu and the udgātṛ the other by speech. The whole sacrifice should be properly followed up with the mind, to avoid error; and with speech the mantras spoken of in the three Vedas should be recited. There the hotṛ and the others, three, together prepare the path of the sacrifice that consists of speech, while Brahmā alone prepares the whole of the path that consists of mind. Therefore he has this capability.

## SBh 0.1.91.4

Now he explains the fourth quarter: ⟦यज्ञस्य मात्रां विमिमीत एकोऽध्वर्युः⟧, "the Adhvaryu, one, measures out the measure of the sacrifice. ⟦अस्त्यर्युरध्वरयुरध्वरं युनक्ति अध्वरस्य नेतेति⟧" (⟦ऋग्वेदः--10.71.11⟧) **NOTE**{this reference follows the Nirukta passage, whereas the earlier Nirukta passages carry a Nirukta reference; is it a slip of the editors?}. Its meaning: one priest named Adhvaryu ⟦विमिमीते⟧, brings about in a special way, the measure of the sacrifice, that is, its nature. That which is ⟦भीयते⟧, is measured, is made, is the measure, that is, the nature; and that he is its producer is understood from the explanation of the name Adhvaryu: in the word adhvaryu, by the Vedic procedure, the letter a that has been dropped is put back again, and

## SBh 0.1.92.1

the name adhvarayu is to be obtained. "He yokes the sacrifice" is the sense of its parts; "leader of the sacrifice" is the purport. With this very in view Yāska shows the explanation indicating that the Veda of the Adhvaryu is the producer of the sacrifice: ⟦मन्त्रा मननात्, छन्दांसि छादनात्, स्तोमः स्तवनात्, यजुर्यजतेरिति⟧ (⟦नि- ७|१२⟧). This being so, when the body of the sacrifice has been produced in the Yajurveda connected with the Adhvaryu, the two parts needed by it, in the form of stotra and śastra, are completed by the other two Vedas; hence the exposition of the Yajurveda, on which they depend, first is proper. After that, since the sāmans depend on the ṛcs, the exposition of the Ṛgveda is proper first of the two. Therefore the Ṛgveda is now expounded.

## SBh 0.1.92.2

Objection: but there is no Veda at all; how then is there a subdivision of it, the Ṛgveda? Thus: what is this thing called Veda? There is neither a definition nor a proof of it. Nothing is established without these two. For the view of the logicians is that a thing is established by definition and proof.

## SBh 0.1.92.3

If it is said that the definition is: "the last among the particular means of knowledge, perception, inference and scripture, is the Veda" — no, because of over-extension to the smṛtis of Manu and others; for the definition of scripture, "the means of correct indirect experience by the force of convention", is present in these too.

## SBh 0.1.92.4

If it is said that the fault is removed by the qualification "being not of human origin" — no, because the Veda too, being made by the Supreme Lord, is of human origin. If it is said that it is not of human origin because it is not made by a living being possessed of a body — no, because by scriptural texts such as ⟦सहस्रशीर्षा पुरुष⟧ even the Lord has a body.

## SBh 0.1.92.5

If it is said that "not of human origin" is meant merely as the absence of being made by a living being possessed of a body that is the fruit of its actions — no, because the Vedas were produced by particular living beings, **NOTE**{the supplied letters ⟦जीवविशेयैरश्चिवच्चादित्वैर्वेदानामुलूपादितत्वात्⟧ are damaged; is the intended sense "by the particular living beings Agni, Vāyu and Āditya the Vedas were produced"?}. For the scriptural text says (⟦ऐत० ब्रा० ५।५।७⟧): ⟦ऋग्वेद एवाग्नेरजायत यजुर्वेदोवायोः सामवेद आदित्या⟧, "the Ṛgveda was born from Agni, the Yajurveda from Vāyu, the Sāmaveda from Āditya". The Lord's being the maker is to be seen as consisting in his impelling Agni and the others.

## SBh 0.1.92.6

If it is said that the Veda is the mass of words consisting of mantra and brāhmaṇa — no, because even now it has not been determined, of these two, "such is a mantra, such a brāhmaṇa". Therefore there is no definition of the Veda at all.

## SBh 0.1.92.7

Nor do we see a proof of its existence. If it is said that a sentence such as ⟦ऋग्वेद भगवोऽध्येमि यजुर्वेदं सामवेदमाथर्वण चतुर्थ⟧, "Sir, I study the Ṛgveda, the Yajurveda, the Sāmaveda, the Atharvan as the fourth", is the proof — no, because that sentence too, being included in the Veda, would lead to dependence on itself. Surely not even a skilful man can climb on his own shoulder. If it is said that a smṛti sentence such as ⟦वेद एव द्विजातीनां निःश्रेयसकरः परः⟧ (⟦या. स्मृ०. १ ।४०⟧), "the Veda alone is the highest bestower of the supreme good for the twice-born", is the proof — no, because that too has the said scriptural text as its root and is therefore refuted.

## SBh 0.1.92.8

Perception and the rest are unfit even to be suspected. And as for the popular acceptance regarding the Veda,

## SBh 0.1.93.1

it is mistaken, even though universal, like "the sky is blue" and the like. Therefore the existence of a Veda that lacks definition and proof cannot be accepted. This is the prior view.

## SBh 0.1.93.2

Here it is said. Having mantras and brāhmaṇas as its nature is a faultless definition. For this very reason Āpastamba says in the Yajñaparibhāṣā: ⟦मन्त्रब्राह्मणयोर्वेदनामधेय⟧ (⟦आप०परि०३१⟧), "the name Veda belongs to mantra and brāhmaṇa". The nature of these two will be determined later.

## SBh 0.1.93.3

As for "the sentence not of human origin", the kind we intend will also be made clear later. The proofs also, in the form of scripture, smṛti and popular acceptance as stated, are to be seen as regards the existence of the Veda. Just as, though pots, cloths and other substances lack self-luminosity, the self-luminosity of the sun, the moon and the others is not contradictory, so, though men and others cannot climb on their own shoulders, let the Veda, whose power is unblunted, also be a presenter of itself, as it is a presenter of other things. For this very reason those who know the tradition show the unblunted power of the Veda: ⟦चोदना हि भूतंभवन्तंभविष्यन्तंसूक्ष्मव्यवहितविप्रकृष्टनिप्येवं जातीयमर्थ शक्रोति अवगमयितुम⟧ (⟦शा०.भा०१।२⟧): the injunction is able to make known a thing that is past, present or future, subtle, hidden, or remote, **NOTE**{the supplied letters ⟦विप्रकृष्टनिप्येवं⟧ look damaged; is the intended text "remote; a thing of this kind"?} a thing of this kind. That being so, the validity of the Veda, and of the popular acceptance rooted in the Veda, is unavoidable. Therefore it is settled that the Veda, established by definition and proof, cannot be refuted by any Cārvāka or other.

## SBh 0.1.93.4

Objection: let there be some thing called Veda; still it does not deserve exposition, being useless, because it is not a means of knowledge. For the Veda is not a means of knowledge, since its definition is hard to establish there. Thus: some give the definition "a means of knowledge is the instrument of correct experience". Others say "a means of knowledge is that which reaches an object not yet reached". And neither of these two is possible in the Veda. For the Veda consists of mantra and brāhmaṇa. Of these, some mantras do not convey a meaning.

## SBh 0.1.93.5

For example: ⟦अम्यक्सा त इन्द्र ऋष्टिरस्मे सनेम्यभ्वं मरुतो जुनन्ति⟧ (⟦ऋ० १।१६९।३⟧); and another: ⟦यादृश्मिन्धायि धायि तमपस्यया⟧ (⟦ऋ० ५।४४।८⟧).

## SBh 0.1.94.1

Another: ⟦सृण्येव जर्भरी तुर्फरी तू⟧ (⟦ऋ० १०।१०६।६⟧).

## SBh 0.1.94.2

Likewise ⟦पान्तमन्युस्तृपूलप्रभर्म्मा⟧ (⟦ऋ० १०।८९ ।५⟧) and the like are to be given as examples. By these mantras no meaning whatever is understood. When there is no experience at all in these, their correctness and their being a means of it are far removed **NOTE**{the supplied text reads "non-instrumentality" (⟦तदसाधनत्वं⟧); is "instrumentality" intended?}.

## SBh 0.1.94.3

Even if the mantra ⟦अधस्विदासीद् उपरस्विदासीद⟧ (⟦ऋ० १०।१२९।५⟧) conveys something, there is no validity, since it conveys a doubtful meaning, like a statement such as ⟦स्थाणुर्वा पुरुषो वा⟧, "a post or a man".

## SBh 0.1.94.4

The mantra ⟦ओषधे त्रायस्वैनमिति⟧ (⟦तै.सं. १।२।१।१⟧) concerns the darbha grass. ⟦स्वधिते मैन⟧ (⟦तै.सं. १।२।१।१⟧) concerns the razor. ⟦शृणोत ग्रावाण⟧ (⟦तै.सं. १।३।१३।१⟧) concerns the stones. In these, the insentient darbha, razor and stones are heard addressed as if they were sentient. Therefore, since they convey a contradictory meaning, like the sentence ⟦द्वौ चन्द्रमसावि⟧, "there are two moons", they lack validity. And of these two mantras, ⟦एक एव रुद्रो न द्वितीयोऽवतस्थे⟧ (⟦तै.सं. १।८।६।१⟧) and ⟦सहस्राणि सहस्रशोयेरुद्राअधिभूम्याम्⟧ (⟦तै. सं०. ४।५।११।५⟧), since they convey a self-contradiction, like the sentence ⟦यावज्जोवमह मौनी⟧, "I am silent all my life", they lack validity.

śubhikā being placed on the heads of the bridegroom and bride. And since these two mantras restate what is popularly known, they have no reaching of an object not yet reached. Therefore the mantra portion is not a means of knowledge. To this it is said: the meaning of the mantras beginning with ⟦श्रम्यगादि⟧ has been explained by Yāska in the Nirukta book. ## SBh 0.1.101.2

Objection: "if they are for making the meaning plain, a seen purpose is gained" is only a reasoning; we see no Vedic mark that supports it. Raising this doubt, he lays down the answer in the sūtra ⟦लिङ्गोपदेशश्च तदर्थवत्⟧ (⟦जै०. १।२।५१⟧).

## SBh 0.1.101.3

It is heard: ⟦आग्नेय्याग्नीध्रमुपतिष्ठेत⟧. Its meaning: the ṛc whose deity is Agni is this "āgneyī"; with it one should approach the Āgnīdhra's place. Here the Brāhmaṇa, enjoining the approach, does not enjoin by reciting the beginning of a mantra, "with ⟦अग्ने नय⟧ (⟦ऋ० १।१८९।१⟧) one should approach", but enjoins by the mark of its being āgneyī. When in a ṛc Agni is set forth as the chief thing, then Agni is the deity of that ṛc. That being so, the designation ⟦आग्नेय्या⟧, ending in a taddhita suffix that denotes a deity, is justified. Therefore this injunction shows that it depends on the sentence-meaning of the mantra. Hence, since the meaning is intended, the mantra is uttered at the time of application for the sake of conveying the meaning.

## SBh 0.1.101.4

For that very intendedness of meaning he lays down another mark in the sūtra ⟦ऊहः⟧ (⟦जै०. १।२।५२⟧).

## SBh 0.1.101.5

Ūha is the recitation, in a modified rite, of a mantra recited in the model rite, by inserting other fitting words so that the meaning fits. Thus: ⟦अन्वेन माता मन्यतामनु पितानु भ्राता⟧ is the model mantra text concerning an animal (⟦मै०. सं०. ४।१३।४⟧). In the modified rite, when there are two animals, the ūha is ⟦अन्वेनौमाता मन्यताम⟧; when there are many animals, the ūha to be made is ⟦अन्वेनान् माता मन्यताम्⟧. The Brāhmaṇa that explains this mantra is recited thus: ⟦न माता वर्ण्यते नपिता⟧, "neither the mother nor the father is increased" **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦वर्ण्यते⟧ reads "is described"; the next unit speaks of growth, so is a form of "grows" intended?}. Here this is to be considered: is bodily growth denied here, or growth in words?

## SBh 0.1.101.6

Growth in words is the use of the word mother, which ends in the singular, with the dual ending, as in ⟦मातराविति⟧, or with the plural ending, as in "mothers". Of these, bodily growth cannot be denied, since its growth is plain to see according to age, childhood, youth and so on. So only the denial of growth in words remains. Since growth in a special form is denied in the words mother and father, a growth conforming to the meaning is indicated for the other, the word ⟦एनमिति⟧. In that case, if the meaning were not intended, how would the dual for two animals and the plural for many animals be inferred? Therefore mantras have an intended meaning.

## SBh 0.1.101.7

For that very meaning he lays down another mark in the sūtra "and from the injunction-word" (⟦जै० १।२।53⟧).

## SBh 0.1.102.1

The word in the Brāhmaṇa that takes the form of an explanation of a mantra **NOTE**{the supplied letters ⟦व्राह्मणगतःङ्गं⟧ look damaged; what are they?} is called an injunction-word. And it is recited thus: ⟦शत हिमाः शतं वर्षाणि जीव्याः स्म इत्येव एतदाह⟧ (⟦श०. ब्रा०. २।३।४।२१⟧): "may we live a hundred winters, a hundred years", so he says this. ⟦शत हिमा⟧ is the beginning of the mantra to be explained; the rest is the explanation of its purport. But if the mantra had no intended meaning, what purport would be explained in the mantra? Therefore mantras have an intended meaning and are to be uttered at the time of application only to make their own meaning plain.

## SBh 0.1.102.2

Here are two summary verses. Are mantras such as ⟦उरु प्रथस्व⟧ solely a cause of the unseen, or, in sacrifices, do they illuminate the spreading of the puroḍāśa and the like? Since that is illuminated by the Brāhmaṇa also, are the mantras solely a cause of merit? No: because of the seen purpose of the illumination of that; the seen is better than the unseen (⟦जै०. न्या०. १।३।४⟧).

Among these, of the injunctions belonging to the Karmakāṇḍa, such as ⟦जर्त्तिलयवाग्वा वो छेङ्कुमाह गवीधुकयवाग्वावा⟧ (⟦तै. सं. 5.4.3.2⟧) **NOTE**{the supplied letters ⟦वो छेङ्कुमाह⟧ look damaged; is the sense "with a gruel of wild sesame, or with a gruel of gavīdhuka"?}, there is no validity, because by enjoining a substance unfit for activity there is no being a means of correct experience. Here the being a substance of oblation, of forest sesame and forest wheat, is denied. Therefore the injunction of wild sesame and the rest, being contradicted, is no valid means.
