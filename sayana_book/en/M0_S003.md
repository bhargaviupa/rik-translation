Therefore one's own recitation is to be studied. The Veda is ⟦सचिविद्⟧, "the knowing one", since, knowing the labour of the man who studies, it protects him like a friend. Although this is the fruit of the recitation of the brahma-sacrifice, still, since the brahma-sacrifice is impossible without study for the purpose of grasping, its fruit too is obtained. ## SBh 0.3.117.2

Objection: once the Veda has been studied, the knowledge of the injunction of study comes afterwards; when there is knowledge, the engagement in study comes afterwards; so there is mutual dependence. [Answer:] Granted. Therefore those who follow the Guru's [Prabhākara's] view establish the engagement of the boy's study with great effort through teaching by the teacher as agent. But Prakāśātman and others, who follow another view, describe the knowledge of the injunction of study as coming from father and others before study, like the knowledge of the rule for evening worship and the like. Whether the engagement is by the injunction of teaching or by its own injunction, the Veda is to be studied by all who have been initiated. And of that study, its ⟦ह्यार्थत्वम्⟧ **NOTE**{⟦तस्य च अध्ययनस्य ह्यार्थत्वम्⟧ is damaged, perhaps for "having a seen purpose"} and its ending in the grasping of the syllables are laid down in sūtras in the Puruṣārthānuśāsana. We shall cite those sūtras and their commentary. To establish that study has a seen purpose, he states the prima facie view: ⟦अदृष्टार्था त्वधीतिर्विहितत्वात्⟧, "but study is for an unseen purpose, because it is enjoined".

## SBh 0.3.117.3

Since in eating and the like, which accomplish a seen fruit, no injunction is seen, study too, though enjoined, is to be understood as for an unseen purpose. If it is said, "a special unseen has not been heard", he answers: ⟦घृतकूल्याद्यतिदेशः स्वर्गकल्पनं वा⟧, "either a transfer of the streams of ghee and the rest, or the assuming of heaven".

## SBh 0.3.117.4

Having transferred to the obligatory study the commendatory statement on the brahma-sacrifice, on recitation and on study, the ⟦घृतकूल्यादिकं⟧ [streams of ghee and the rest] stated there is to be assumed as the fruit, by the principle of the night-session. But those who do not accept the transfer of the commendatory statement must assume heaven by the Viśvajit principle. When seen fruits, namely the obtaining [of the text] and sanctification, are possible, how is an unseen assumed? To this he says: ⟦अयुक्ते संस्कारप्राप्ती⟧, "sanctification and obtaining are not fitting".

## SBh 0.3.118.1

Because a sanctified self-recitation is not seen to be employed in any sacrifice, and because the obtaining is not itself a human end — this is the meaning. Fearing the objection that the obtaining of the recitation is a human end by being the cause of knowing the sense, he says that, like mantras employed for work such as removing poison, the sentences on Jyotiṣṭoma and the rest, employed as subsidiary to study, have no authority in their own sense: ⟦अन्याङ्गं नार्थप्रभापकम्⟧, "what is subsidiary to another does not make known the sense" **NOTE**{⟦प्रभापकम्⟧ is read as the word for "making known"}. But the sentence that enjoins study, being subsidiary only to the study that it itself enjoins, is authoritative for its own sense; he says: ⟦अध्ययनवाक्यमनन्याद्गम्⟧, "the sentence on study is not subsidiary to anything else". Objection: if so, then since it is for an unseen purpose, and there is no fruit belonging to the recitation that stands as object, the suffix tavya that denotes the object, in ⟦अध्येतव्य⟧, "is to be studied", would be contradicted. To this he says: ⟦सक्तुवत् करणपरिणामः⟧, "as with the roasted-barley flour, a change to the instrument".

## SBh 0.3.118.2

In ⟦सक्तूसुहोति⟧, "he offers the roasted-barley flour", although the flour is understood as principal in the enjoining of the sanctifying of the offering, since the flour, having been offered and burnt to ashes, has no use elsewhere, the principality of the object is given up and a change to the instrument is made: "he offers with the flour". So here too, since sanctification and obtaining belonging to the object are impossible, the sentence must be changed to: "he should study with the self-recitation". Now he states the established view that where a seen fruit exists no unseen one is assumed: ⟦दृष्टे तु नादृष्टम्⟧, "but where there is a seen [fruit], no unseen". What is that seen fruit? He says: ⟦दृष्टौ प्राप्तिसंस्कारौ⟧, "the seen ones are the obtaining and the sanctification". He says that the obtaining of the syllables is a human end by succession: ⟦प्राप्त्यार्थबोधः⟧, "by the obtaining, understanding of the sense"; "arises" is to be supplied. It should not be suspected that the injunction is useless because [the obtaining] is established by agreement and difference, like eating and the like; he says that the injunction is justified for an unseen of restriction, like the pounding: ⟦विधिनिष्पत्त्याः⟧ **NOTE**{⟦विधिनिष्पत्त्याः⟧ is read as "by the fulfilment of the injunction"}. As to what was said, that since the use of the sanctified self-recitation is not seen there is no sanctification, to that he says: ⟦सस्कारसिद्धिः क्रत्वध्ययनविधिक्तोपादानात्⟧, "sanctification is established, because both the sacrifice-injunction and the study-injunction are taken up".

## SBh 0.3.118.3

For the injunctions of sacrifices, requiring knowledge of their object, employ the self-recitation for that knowledge. And the study injunction, by excluding reading from writing and the like, makes known that the self-recitation is ⟦अध्ययनसच्छतत्वं⟧ **NOTE**{⟦अध्ययनसच्छतत्वं⟧ is not a known word; perhaps for "having study as its sanctification"}. So from the taking up of both, that [sanctification] is established.

## SBh 0.3.118.4

Objection: sanctification is a special unseen potency. And that does not belong to the self-recitation, because by the suffix tavya the unseen of the productive effort coloured by study, which is the sense of the base taken up in its own word, is what is denoted.

## SBh 0.3.119.1

Then how is the self-recitation sanctified? To that he says: ⟦तव्यः कर्मगादृष्टवाची⟧, "the tavya denotes the unseen residing in the object".

## SBh 0.3.119.2

Here, since the suffix tavya denotes the object, and the self-recitation, the object, is nearer to the suffix tavya than study, the sense of the base, the suffix tavya states only the unseen that resides in the self-recitation; for although it is a rule that the unseen is produced by the sense of the verbal root, there is no rule that it is coloured by it — this is the idea.

## SBh 0.3.119.3

And as to what was said, "what is subsidiary to another does not make known the sense", that is wrong, since that holds for mantras that are subsidiary to an independent unseen. Here, however, the unseen depends on the self-recitation; and since there is a fruit, the understanding of the sense that is established through the power of the syllables belonging to the self-recitation, there is no fitness in assuming another fruit, and the unseen only supports the authority and does not obstruct it; so he says: ⟦स्वतन्त्राहष्टशेवत्वान्न स्वार्थप्रमा प्रतिबध्यते⟧, "because [the sentence] is subsidiary to an independent unseen, the knowledge of its own sense is not obstructed". Objection: by the roasted-barley principle, with the principality of the object given up, the independent unseen alone would be here too. To this he says: ⟦यथाक्षुतोपपत्तेर्न सक्तुन्यायः⟧, "since it is justified as heard, the roasted-barley principle does not apply" **NOTE**{⟦यथाक्षुतोपपत्तेर्न⟧ is read as "as heard"}.

## SBh 0.3.119.4

In the case of the flour there is no other way, so let the heard [sense] be abandoned and an unheard one assumed; here that is not fitting, because [the contrary] has been shown — this is the meaning. Having thus established that study has a seen purpose, he states the prima facie view to refute that it goes as far as understanding the sense: ⟦वैधमर्थनिर्णयं भट्टगुरू विधेः पुमर्थावसानात्⟧, "the Bhaṭṭa and the Guru [hold] that the determination of the sense is enjoined, because an injunction ends in a human end". Since it is the rule everywhere that an injunction ends in a human end, here too the Bhaṭṭa and the Guru hold that the determination of the sense, being a human end that has a fruit, is impelled by the injunction of study.

## SBh 0.3.119.5

Objection: from a single study, or from study with repetition, the determination of the sense is not found. Fearing this, he says that, so that it may be accomplished, the study injunction will postulate the inquiry that is joined with the determination of the sense: ⟦स विचारमाक्षिपेत्⟧, "it would draw in the inquiry" **NOTE**{⟦सोऽध्ययनविध्नेयरर्थनिश्चयक्तं⟧ is damaged; the sense given is "the study injunction will postulate the inquiry joined with the determination of the sense"}.

## SBh 0.3.119.6

Objection: the rule everywhere is that an injunction impels only what is enjoined by itself and what helps it; this being so, how will the study injunction here draw in what is not of that kind? He answers: ⟦अविधेयानुपकार्याक्षेपोऽवघातावृत्तिवत्⟧, "the drawing in of what is neither enjoined nor a help is like the repetition of the pounding".

## SBh 0.3.119.7

In ⟦व्रीहीन् अवहन्ति⟧, "he pounds the rice", only the pounding is enjoined, not its repetition, since that is not the sense of the verbal root. Nor is that [repetition] a help to the enjoined act, since the pounding is accomplished by a single stroke of the pestle without repetition. Still, for the accomplishment of the fruit, the production of the husked grain, that injunction drew in the repetition; as in this case, so in the matter before us it is to be understood. Objection: even if no understanding of the sense arises for one who studies the Veda alone, it does arise for one who studies the Veda together with the auxiliary sciences beginning with grammar; so for him the injunction would not postulate inquiry — fearing this,

## SBh 0.3.120.1

he says that inquiry is indeed required for removing conflicts that lie in the sense: ⟦साङ्गाध्ययनात् तद्भावे विचारोऽर्थविरोधापनुत्⟧, "even if that [understanding] comes from study with the auxiliaries, inquiry removes conflicts of sense". He states the established view: ⟦प्राप्तेषु गवादिवत् पुमर्थत्वाद् विधिस्तदन्तः⟧, "like cows and the like, once obtained, because it is a human end, the injunction ends there". Just as cows and the like, being causes of milk and the like, the fruit, are sought by men **NOTE**{⟦फलभूतख्यट्पंक्षीरादेर्हेतवो⟧ is damaged; I understand "of milk and the like, the fruit"}, so, since the obtaining of the syllables, which is the cause of understanding a fruitful sense, is also a human end, the study injunction is to be understood as ending in the obtaining of the syllables.

## SBh 0.3.120.2

Objection: if the obtaining of the syllables is a human end because it is impelled by the understanding of a fruitful sense, then, since that understanding is the chief human end, why should the injunction not end in the understanding? To that he says: ⟦फलवद् वोधान्तत्वेऽध्ययनाकार्त्स्न्यम्⟧, "if it ended in a fruitful understanding, study would be incomplete".

## SBh 0.3.120.3

For the fruit of understanding is the performance of rites. That being so, a Brāhmaṇa or other person who has authority for a certain rite, such as the Bṛhaspati-sava, would study only the sentences of that rite, and not the sentences of the Rājasūya and the like, since there is no fruit, such as engagement, in those. On our own side this fault does not arise, he says: ⟦कृत्स्नप्राप्तिर्जपार्थो⟧, "the obtaining of the whole is for the sake of recitation".

## SBh 0.3.120.4

It should not be suspected that if [the injunction] does not convey it, understanding of the sense would not be established; for it is the nature of a means of knowledge to convey its object, and worldly sentences of trustworthy persons are seen to convey even without an injunction. So he says: ⟦लौकवन्नैजो बोध्यः⟧, "like the worldly, the understanding of what is to be understood is its own". Objection: if understanding is the fruit of the injunction, then since the injunction can be made with a desire for understanding in view, a person with authority would be easy to find. Setting aside, as being plain, the reply that a Brāhmaṇa initiated at eight years, desiring the obtaining [of the text], is an easily found person with authority, he faults the desirability of understanding: ⟦सोऽकाम्यः प्राग्वोध्यभानाभानयोः⟧, "that is not to be desired, whether the object of understanding is apparent or not apparent before".

## SBh 0.3.120.5

Since the sense of the Veda, namely Agnihotra and the rest, the object of understanding, is already established as apparent before study through the instruction of father and others, as with the evening worship, that understanding of the sense is not to be desired. When it is not apparent, it cannot be desired, since desire is restricted to an object that is already known.

## SBh 0.3.120.6

Objection: when there is knowledge in general, a wish to know in particular is possible. Or, even when the particular has been learned from the instruction of father and others **NOTE**{⟦पित्राद्युपदेद्याद्रुश्रवगते⟧ is damaged; I translate "learned from the instruction of father and others"}, a desire to understand again, to determine the authority of the knowledge got from instruction, is proper. Fearing this, he says that even so the enjoining of study with the understanding of the sense as its aim is not possible: ⟦उहेशायोगात्⟧, "because an aim is not possible".

## SBh 0.3.120.7

Of the particular knowledges of Agnihotra and the rest, an aim in the particular form, by a single cognition, is not possible, because they are endless; if the aim is in the general form, then only the general would be the fruit of the injunction, not a particular knowledge. Therefore the aim is not proper.

## SBh 0.3.121.1

Objection: if there is no aim of understanding the sense and no ⟦उश्च एरणा⟧ **NOTE**{⟦उश्च एरणाभावे⟧ is damaged; perhaps for "no uttering"}, the Veda would have no purport in its own sense. Fearing this, he says that the purport, which is to be known by indications such as the beginning, is established by the power of the word itself: ⟦तात्पर्यं⟧, "the purport comes from the word".

## SBh 0.3.121.2

Objection: then the uttering of words with the aim of knowing the sense would be useless in the world. Not so, since it is for removing the obstruction ⟦पुरूपसम्बन्धकृतदोपाण्यप्रलिबन्धपरिहारार्थत्वाहु⟧ **NOTE**{the supplied ⟦पुरूपसम्बन्धकृतदोपाण्यप्रलिबन्धपरिहारार्थत्वाहु⟧ is damaged; I read "for removing obstructions by faults produced through connection with a person"}; so he says: ⟦उद्दिश्य उच्चारणं दोपघ्नं लोके⟧, "uttering with an aim removes faults in the world". Objection: if the study injunction does not end in understanding, the science of inquiry would not proceed, for lack of an impeller. Fearing this, he says: ⟦विचार उत्तरविधियुक्त उपपद्यते⟧, "inquiry is justified when it is joined to a later injunction".

## SBh 0.3.121.3

The injunctions of sacrifices, though ⟦साक्त्येदाध्ययनाद्⟧ **NOTE**{⟦साक्त्येदाध्ययनाद्⟧ is damaged; I read "from study of the Veda"} they are known only superficially, being unable to make one perform without a knowledge of the decision, which is established by the removal of conflicts, impel inquiry into the sacrifice for its decision. But the injunction of hearing directly enjoins inquiry into Brahman. This being so, it is fully fitting that the injunction of hearing impels what it itself enjoins, and that the sacrifice injunctions impel what helps what is enjoined. On the view that [inquiry] is impelled by the study injunction, since that injunction ends in the accomplishment of heaven through the sacrifice, the performance of the sacrifice would also be impelled by it, and the sacrifice injunction would fall into uselessness. **NOTE**{⟦तद्विइग्नेः⟧ is damaged; I read "of that injunction"}

## SBh 0.3.121.4

Objection: since the study injunction is obligatory only for the three twice-born classes, if [inquiry] were impelled by it, inquiry too would be obtained for them [only]; not otherwise. Is the obligatory nature of inquiry into sacrifice established for the three classes alone, or that of inquiry into Brahman? The first holds equally on your view too; so he says: ⟦अतो नित्यः क्रतुविचारस्त्रैवर्णिकमात्रस्य⟧, "therefore inquiry into sacrifice is obligatory for the three classes alone". Since a penalty is heard of for not doing them, sacrifices are obligatory for the three classes; this is the meaning **NOTE**{⟦प्रत्यवारुभवणात्⟧ is read as "because a penalty is heard"}. The second is unwanted; he says: ⟦ब्रह्मविचारः पुनः परमहसख्यैव⟧, "but inquiry into Brahman is for the paramahaṃsa alone".

## SBh 0.3.121.5

"Obligatory" is to be carried on.

## SBh 0.3.121.6

Objection: by the reasoning stated, if study ends in grasping the syllables, knowledge of the sense would not be enjoined. Not so, since it is enjoined by another sentence: ⟦ब्राह्मणेन निष्कारणो धर्मः षड्ङ्गो वेदोऽध्येयो ज्ञेयश्च⟧, "by a Brāhmaṇa, without a motive, the dharma, the Veda with its six limbs, is to be studied and known" (the Mahābhāṣya, Paspaśāhnika); that is its injunction. There, by the word "without a motive", the desirability of study and knowing is excluded. Two sayings that prompt a man to engagement in knowing the sense, belonging to another branch of the Veda, the author of the Nirukta, Yāska (⟦नि० १ । १८⟧), thus cited: ⟦अद्यापि लानप्रशंसा भवत्यज्ञाननिन्दा च⟧, "even today there is praise of knowledge and blame of ignorance" **NOTE**{⟦लानप्रशंसा⟧ is read as "praise of knowledge"}: ⟦स्थास्तुरयं भारहारः किलाभूदधीत्य वेदं न विजानाति योऽर्थम्⟧, "this one, who having studied the Veda does not understand the sense, has indeed become a post, a bearer of a load;

## SBh 0.3.122.1

⟦योऽर्थज्ञ इत् सकलं भद्रमश्नुते नाकमेति ज्ञानविधूतपाष्मा⟧, he who knows the sense obtains all good; his evil shaken off by knowledge, he goes to heaven. ⟦यद् गृद्वोतमविज्ञातं⟧, what is learned without being understood, ⟦निगदेनैव⟧, is merely rattled off, ⟦अनग्नाविव शुष्कैधो न तज्ज्वलति कर्हिचित्⟧, never blazes, like dry fuel on a place without fire" (⟦संहि० ब्रा० ३⟧). He who knows the sense of the Veda obtains all good in this world. And, when his sin has been destroyed by that knowledge, he goes after death to heaven. This fruit of knowledge, in this world and the next, the Taittirīyas made clear by citing a mantra and by the Brāhmaṇa that states its purport: ⟦तदेपाशृक्ता⟧, "on this this ṛc is said: ⟦ये अर्वाङुत वा पुराणे वेद विद्वांसमभितो वदन्त्यादित्यमेव ते परिवदन्ति, सर्व्वेऽग्नि द्वितीयं तृतीयं च हसमिति⟧, those who, around the learned man who knows the Veda, whether recent or ancient, speak, revile Āditya himself; all of them [revile] Agni as the second and the Haṃsa as the third".

## SBh 0.3.122.2

⟦यावतीर्वै देवतास्ता सर्व्वा वेदविदि ब्राह्मणे वसन्ति । तस्माद् ब्राह्मणेभ्यो वेदविद्भ्यो दिवे दिवे नमस्कुर्यान्नाश्लीलं कीर्तयेदेता एव देवताः प्रीणाति⟧, "as many deities as there are, all of them dwell in a Brāhmaṇa who knows the Veda. ## SBh 0.3.122.3

The learned man who knows the Veda is a man who knows the sense, and he is of two kinds: one born in recent times, some teacher skilled in the fourteen branches of knowledge, and one born in ancient times, Vyāsa and others. Those men who, endowed with the pride of learning, the pride of wealth and the pride of family, thinking themselves pandits, revile this learned man of both kinds all around, in learning and the rest, all of them revile Āditya first; relative to Āditya they revile Agni as the second; relative to both of those they revile the Haṃsa as the third. The Haṃsa is Vāyu, since it ⟦हन्ति⟧, goes, always.

## SBh 0.3.122.4

And that the Veda-knower is of the form of Agni and the rest is recited: ⟦अग्नेर्वायोरादित्यस्य सायुज्य गच्छति⟧, "he reaches union with Agni, Vāyu and Āditya" (⟦तै० आ० २।१५⟧). Not only these three deities, but all deities dwell in the Veda-knower. Therefore one should do homage daily to Brāhmaṇas who know the Veda, on seeing them or remembering them. When this is so, the man who does homage pleases all the deities, who are the sense of the several mantras, and who, being remembered by the knower of the sense of the Veda, abide in his heart **NOTE**{⟦तत्तन्नत्तत्रार्थभूताः⟧ is damaged; I read "who are the sense of the several mantras"}. Therefore for the knower of the sense of the Veda, who is to be worshipped by beings with the thought that he is all the deities, the obtaining of good in both worlds is fitting.

## SBh 0.3.122.5

But he who, even having studied the Veda, does not understand the sense, that man only carries a burden. "Post" is the illustration. A dry tree whose branches have been cut and of which only the root remains is called by the word "post". As that is used only for fuel, not for flowers and fruit, so for the mere reciter there is only this much, that he is not an outcast; but there is no performance of rites, and no accomplishment of fruit such as heaven. By the word "indeed" [⟦किल⟧] fame in the world is indicated. In the world too the honour that is paid to a reciter, such as ⟦श्रनादि⟧ **NOTE**{⟦श्रनादिपूजा⟧ is unclear; perhaps "of wealth and the like"}, is seen to be exceeded for a learned man.

## SBh 0.3.123.1

Further, that Vedic sentence, taken from the teacher without knowledge of the sense, and uttered again and again only as recitation, never blazes: it does not make its own sense shine; as dry wood thrown into a place without fire does not blaze. This being so, that sentence would not be Veda in the chief sense **NOTE**{⟦सद्वेदवाक्यमभात्वार्याद् गृहीतम्⟧ is damaged; I read "that Vedic sentence, taken from the teacher"}. The etymology of the word "Veda" is: one knows by this a means to a human end beyond the worldly.

## SBh 0.3.123.2

Therefore, to establish Veda-ness in the chief sense, its sense must be known. And here Yāska (⟦नि० १।१९⟧) has cited another ṛc: ⟦उत त्वः पश्यन्न ददर्श वाचमुत त्वः शृवच्छन्न शृणोत्येनाम्⟧, "one, seeing, has not seen Speech; another, hearing, does not hear her;

## SBh 0.3.123.3

⟦उतो त्वस्मै तन्वं विसस्रे जायेव पत्य उशती सुवासाः⟧, but to another she has spread out her body, like a loving, well-clad wife to her husband" (⟦ऋ० १०।७१।४⟧).

## SBh 0.3.123.4

Of this, he shows the purport of the first half: ⟦अप्येकः पश्यन्न पश्यति, वाचमपि च प्रयवन्न शृणोति, एनामिति अविद्वांसमाह अर्धम् इति⟧, "even one, seeing, does not see; and even hearing speech, does not hear her" — he speaks of the one who does not know **NOTE**{the words ⟦अर्धम् इति⟧ after ⟦अविद्वांसमाह⟧ are unclear; perhaps they mark the first half}. This is the meaning: to the one who does not know the sense, the mantra speaks in its first half. One man, who stays at mere recitation, though seeing the speech in the form of the Veda, does not see rightly, because, without the discrimination of singular and plural and the like, even correctness of recitation cannot be achieved.

## SBh 0.3.123.5

In ⟦वायुमेव स्वेन भागधेयेन उप धावति स एव एनं भूतिं गमयति⟧, "he runs to Vāyu alone with his own share; he himself brings him to prosperity", and ⟦आदित्यानेव स्वेन भागधेयेन उप धावन्ति त एव एनं भूतिं गमयन्ति⟧, "they run to the Ādityas alone with their own share; they themselves bring him to prosperity" (⟦तै- सं- ३।१।१।१⟧), and the like, how would one who has not learned grammar settle the reading? ## SBh 0.3.123.6

He shows the purport of the third pāda: ⟦अप्येकस्मै तन्यविसस्र⟧, "even to one she has spread out [her body]". The explanation of "own" and the rest comes later; "knowledge" and the rest is the explanation of the purport. The mantra says that, with this speech in the form of the third pāda, [one] able to make the sense of the Veda shine **NOTE**{⟦धनाशाम्⟧ in ⟦वेदार्थप्रकाशनक्षमं धनाशाम्⟧ is unclear and left untranslated}.

## SBh 0.3.123.7

He shows the purport of the fourth pāda, by "the best speech, a simile": ⟦जायेव पत्ये काम अयमाना सुवासा । ऋतुकालेषु सुवासाः कल्पाणवासा: । कामयमाना⟧, "like a wife to her husband, desiring, well-clad; in the seasons well-clad is auspiciously clad; desiring

## SBh 0.3.124.1

⟦ऋतुकालेषु⟧, in the seasons. As he sees her, he hears: thus is the praise of the one who knows the sense." This is the meaning: by the best speech, which is the fourth pāda, a simile of the sense of the third pāda is stated. The explanation of ⟦उशतीरिल्येतस्य⟧ ["loving"] is ⟦कामयमानेति⟧ ["desiring"]. Though by day, at the time of household work, she is in soiled clothes, still at the times of union she is auspiciously clad. The reason there is ⟦कामयमाना ऋतुकालेषु⟧, "desiring in the seasons". As that husband looks at this wife fully, with respect, and also listens to what she says with a good mind, so this man, endowed with the practice of the fourteen branches of knowledge, rightly sees the secret of the sense of the Veda, and with a good mind accepts the sense stated in the Veda, which has the form of dharma and Brahman. This is the praise, said, of one who knows the sense of the Veda.

## SBh 0.3.124.2

Again Yāska (⟦नि० १।२०⟧) cited another ṛc: ⟦तस्योत्तराभूयसे निर्वचनाय⟧, "for a fuller explanation of that, the next [is cited]": ⟦उत त्वं सख्ये स्थिरपीतमाहुर्नैन हिन्वन्त्यपि वाजिनेषु । अधेन्वा चरति माययैष वाच शुश्रुवो अफलामपुष्पाम्⟧, "and one [they call] long-drunk, firm in friendship; they do not urge him even among the prize-winners; he goes with the deceit of a cow without milk, having heard speech without fruit or flower" (⟦ऋ०१०|७१|5⟧). This is the meaning. After the ṛc cited before, beginning ⟦उत त्वः पश्यन्⟧, a certain ṛc is recited that serves for a fuller explanation of the mantra stated before; it is able to teach that sense to the highest degree. How? That is told. Moreover, they, the knowers, say of one man, skilled in the fourteen branches of knowledge, that, staying in friendship with Speech in the form of the Veda, firmly, he is endowed with the drinking of the nectar of the sense stated in the Veda. In the mantra ⟦सचिविदं सखायम⟧ the friendship of the Veda was cited. Or: staying in the ⟦सव्ये⟧ **NOTE**{⟦सव्ये⟧ here looks like a damaged form of the word for "friendship"; I translate "staying in friendship"} of the Vedas in the world of heaven, they say that he has drunk the nectar to the highest degree. The ⟦वाजिनाः⟧ are the lords of speech, or those bold in assemblies. Among them too they do not ⟦हिम्यन्ति⟧ him who is skilled in the sense of the Veda **NOTE**{⟦हिम्यन्ति⟧ is read as the verb "urge" of the ṛc}; none obtains him, because they are unable to dispute with him. But the other one, who is intent on mere recitation, has heard speech without flower and fruit. The flower is knowledge of the dharma stated in the earlier part; the fruit is knowledge of ⟦ब्राह्मणो⟧ stated in the later part **NOTE**{⟦उत्तरकारष्ठोक्तस्य ब्राह्मणो ज्ञानं फलम्⟧ is read as "knowledge of Brahman stated in the later part is the fruit"}.

## SBh 0.3.124.3

As in the world a flower produces fruit, so knowledge of dharma, such as Vedic recitation, through performance produces the desire for knowledge of Brahman, which is of the nature of fruit; by the śruti ⟦तमेव वेदानुवचनेन ब्राह्मणा विदिषन्ति यज्ञेन दानेन तपसा नाशकेन⟧, "Brāhmaṇas seek to know him by Vedic recitation, by sacrifice, by gift, by austerity, by fasting" (⟦बृo. उप. ४।४। २२⟧). And as fruit is a cause of satisfaction, so knowledge of Brahman is the cause of having done what is to be done, by the śruti ⟦यत् पूर्णानन्दैकबोधस्तद् ब्रह्माहमस्त्वीति कृतकृत्यो भवति⟧, "'that which is full bliss and sole knowing, that Brahman am I' — thus he becomes one who has done what is to be done" (⟦परमहसोपनिषद् ४⟧). The reciter of the Veda who is without such fruit of merit goes about with the deceit of a cow without milk. A cow that has newly calved and gives milk is called ⟦धेनु⟧, by the derivation ⟦धिनोतीतित्युत्पत्त्या⟧, that she pleases, because she is a cause of delight. For the man who is intent on recitation only, the speech in the form of the Veda does not give the milk of knowledge of dharma and Brahman; she is not a milch cow **NOTE**{⟦न दोक्तप्रीत्यधेनुः⟧ is damaged; I read "is not a milch cow that pleases"}. For that very reason this is deceit, of the nature of trickery, because it has the form of a cow like a cow made by a magician. Going about with that deceit he does not obtain the highest human end: this is the meaning. Thus by Yāska the praise of knowledge and the blame of ignorance have been set out at length.

## SBh 0.3.125.1

And by the principle that what is praised is enjoined, an injunction of knowing the sense too, like that of study, is to be admitted.

## SBh 0.3.125.2

Further, in the section of the Nakṣatra offerings, the sentence on the fruit of each offering is recited as the same for the sacrifice and for the knowing of it: ⟦यथा ह वा अग्निर्देवानामन्नादः, एवं ह वा एष मनुष्याणां भवति व एतेन हविषा यजतेय उ चैतदेवं वेद⟧, "as Agni among the gods is the eater of food, so he becomes among men who sacrifices with this oblation, and who knows this thus" (⟦तै० ब्रा० ३।१।४।१⟧). Hence, so that one may have a fruit like that of the sacrifice, knowing it also is enjoined. By this reasoning injunctions of knowing are to be seen in all the Brāhmaṇas.

## SBh 0.3.125.3

Objection: in the sūtra ⟦विद्याप्रशंसेति⟧ (⟦जै० १।२।१५⟧) Jaimini laid down that the fruits of knowing are of the nature of praise. [Answer:] Let it be so, since praise can be made even with a fruit that exists. When the new-moon and full-moon sacrifices are missed, in order to enjoin the Vaiśvānara offering, which is of the nature of an expiation, praise is made with just the existing fruit, heaven: ⟦सुवर्गाय हि लोकायदर्शपूर्णमासाविल्येते⟧, "for the heavenly world indeed are the new-moon and full-moon sacrifices performed" (⟦तै० सं- २।२।५।४⟧).

## SBh 0.3.125.4

And this was cited by the teachers to show that the sentence on the fruit of the knowledge of Brahman has purport also in its own sense: "I accept that the sentence is a commendatory statement, because its purport lies elsewhere; but, since it states the thing as it is, it is not a commendatory statement of a non-existent sense. As the new-moon and full-moon sacrifices are performed for the heavenly world, so also here; it is not a commendatory statement of a non-existent sense, like the śruti that has an evil fame." **NOTE**{⟦हच्छाम्येवार्थवादत्वं वचसोऽन्यपस्त्वतः⟧ and the second verse are damaged in several letters; the verse is rendered by its evident sense}. It should not be suspected that, if the fruit is accomplished by mere knowing, performance would be useless, because this has been answered by the abundance of fruit. And a sūtra of Jaimini is cited here: ⟦फलस्य कर्मनिष्पत्तेस्तेषां लोकवत् परिमाणतः सारतो वा फलविशेषः स्यात्⟧, "the fruit comes from the accomplishment of the act; among these, as in the world, there would be a difference of fruit according to measure or according to quality" (⟦जै० १।२।१७⟧).

## SBh 0.3.125.5

And this was explained by us by the citation ⟦तरति ब्रह्महत्यां योऽ,वमेधेन यजते य उ चैनमेव वेद⟧, "he crosses over the killing of a Brāhmaṇa who sacrifices with the horse sacrifice, and who knows this thus".

## SBh 0.3.125.6

And the Chāndogas recite that in a performance accompanied by knowledge there is a surplus of fruit over a mere performance **NOTE**{⟦केचरतादनुष्ठानाद् विद्यासीइतेऽनुष्ठाने⟧ is damaged; I read "from mere performance, in performance accompanied by knowledge"}: ⟦तेनोभौ कुरुतो यश्चैतदेव वेद यश्च न वेद । नाना तु विद्या । चाविद्या च । यदेव विद्यया करोतिश्रद्धयोपनिषदा तदेव वीर्य्यवत्तरं भवति⟧, "both act by it, he who knows this and he who does not; but knowledge and ignorance are different. Whatever he does with knowledge, faith and the Upaniṣad, that becomes more vigorous" (⟦छा० उप० १ ।१। १०⟧). Although here by the word "knowledge" meditation on the part [of the Sāman] is intended, still the principle is the same in all the meditations.

## SBh 0.3.125.7
