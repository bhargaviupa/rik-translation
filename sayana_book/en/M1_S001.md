## SBh 1.1.0.1

There, the sūkta ⟦अग्निमीळे⟧ is employed in the Āgneya rite (kratu) of the morning recitation (prātaranuvāka). That employment is laid down by Āśvalāyana in the thirteenth section of the fourth chapter: ⟦अवा नो अग्न इति षळग्निमीळेऽग्निं दूतम्⟧. There the sūkta is determined from the mention of the pāda that is deficient, since it is laid down as a definition (⟦आश्व. श्रौ.१. १⟧): ⟦सूक्तं सूक्तादौ हीने पादे⟧. In that sūkta, the first Ṛk is employed as the yājyā of the Sviṣṭakṛt in the second Pavamāna iṣṭi.

## SBh 1.1.0.2

And that employment is laid down in the first section of the second chapter: ⟦साह्वान्विश्वा अभियुजोऽग्निमीळे पुरोहितमिति संयाज्ये⟧. There, from the mention of the complete pāda, it is understood to be a Ṛk, since it is laid down as a definition (⟦आश्व. श्रौ १. १⟧): ⟦ऋचं पादग्रहणे⟧. Likewise, since it is laid down as a definition (⟦आश्व. श्रौ. २.१⟧): ⟦संयाज्ये इत्युक्ते सौविष्टकृती प्रतीयात्⟧, the connection with the Sviṣṭakṛt is determined. There too, since the Ṛk is cited as the second mantra, it is a yājyā. Although the preparatory act (saṃskāra), consisting in recalling the deity, is accomplished by the puronuvākyā ⟦साह्वान्⟧ alone, nevertheless the combination of the yājyā and the anuvākyā has been discussed in the twelfth chapter, in the fourth pāda: "Is the yājyā to be alternative to the puronuvākyā, or combined with it? It is alternative, because the deity is made known by either one alone."

## SBh 1.1.0.3

The conclusion: combination, both from the name "puronuvākyā" and from the statement, since the function of making the deity known is one. If it is objected, "As there is alternation between two pairs, so too between the two that belong to one pair," not so. For the name ⟦पुरोनुवाक्या⟧ cannot be explained without a later yājyā. Moreover, by the direct statement ⟦पुरोनुवाक्यामनूच्य याज्यया जुहोति⟧, "having recited the puronuvākyā, he offers with the yājyā," togetherness is enjoined, preceded by a statement of the difference in the functions of indicating the deity and of giving the oblation. Hence, combination.

## SBh 1.1.0.4

And this sūkta beginning ⟦अग्निमित्यादि⟧ has nine Ṛks, since it is stated in the Anukramaṇikā: ⟦अग्निं नव मधुच्छन्दा वैश्वामित्रः⟧. Madhucchandas, the son of Viśvāmitra, being the seer of this sūkta, is its ṛṣi. The root is ⟦ऋष गतौ⟧, in the sense of going. ⟦सर्वधातुभ्य इन्⟧ (⟦उ. सू. ४. ५५७⟧): the suffix in is added after all roots. ⟦इगुपधात्कित्⟧ (⟦उ. स. ४. ५५९⟧): it is kit after a root whose penultimate vowel is one of the iK group. The Self-existent One, the Veda-Puruṣa, reaches the men who practise austerity for the attainment of the Veda. And so it is heard: ⟦अजान्ह वै पृश्नींस्तपस्यमानान्ब्रह्म स्वयंभ्वभ्यानर्षत्तदृषयोऽभवन्⟧ (⟦तै. आ. २. ९⟧): the Self-existent Brahman approached the Ajas and the Pṛśnis who were practising austerity, and they became ṛṣis. Likewise, with the idea that ṛṣi-hood belongs to one who sees first, by the grace of the Supreme Lord, the Veda that lies beyond the senses, it is recalled in the smṛti: ⟦युगान्तेऽन्तर्हितान् वेदान् सेतिहासान् महर्षयः । लेभिरे तपसा पूर्वमनुज्ञातः स्वयंभुवा⟧: at the end of the age the great seers regained by austerity the Vedas, with their Itihāsas, that had disappeared, having been permitted by the Self-existent One. A sin is recalled for lack of knowledge of the ṛṣi and the rest: ⟦अविदित्वा ऋषिं छन्दो दैवतं योगमेव च। योऽध्यापयेज्जपेद्वापि पापीयाञ्जायते तु सः⟧: whoever teaches or even mutters it without knowing the ṛṣi, the metre, the deity and the application, becomes the more sinful. ⟦ऋषिच्छन्दोदैवतानि ब्राह्मणार्थं स्वराद्यपि । अविदित्वा प्रयुञ्जानो मन्त्रकण्टक उच्यते⟧: one who employs the ṛṣi, the metre, the deities, the Brāhmaṇa-meaning (**NOTE**{is the compound to be taken as "the Brāhmaṇa and the meaning" or as "the meaning according to the Brāhmaṇa"?}) and also the accents and the rest, without knowing them, is called a thorn of the mantra. And the rule of knowing is recalled: ⟦स्वरो वर्णोऽक्षरं मात्रा विनियोगोऽर्थ एव च⟧: accent, letter, syllable, measure, application and meaning too,

## SBh 1.1.0.5

⟦मन्त्रं जिज्ञासमानेन वेदितव्यं पदे पदे⟧: must be known, word by word, by one who wishes to know the mantra.

## SBh 1.1.0.6

Thus. Although the metre of the sūkta beginning ⟦अग्निमित्यादि⟧ is not stated here in the Anukramaṇikā, nevertheless it is stated thus in the paribhāṣā (⟦अनु. १२. १४⟧): ⟦आदौ गायत्रं प्राग्घिरण्यस्तूपात्⟧. The meaning is: in the mantras before those of which Hiraṇyastūpa will be named the ṛṣi, the metre in general is Gāyatra. It is called chandas because it covers, in order to ward off a man's connection with sin. And that is recited in the Āraṇyaka section: ⟦छादयन्ति ह वा एनं छन्दांसि पापकर्मणः⟧ (⟦ऐ. आ. २.५⟧): the metres, verily, cover him from sinful action. Or else chandas is so called because it covers the heat of the fire that is being piled. And the Taittirīyas recite that: ⟦प्रजापतिरग्निमचिनुत स क्षुरपविर्भूत्वाऽतिष्ठत्तं देवा बिभ्यतो नोपायन्ते छन्दोभिरात्मानं छादयित्वोपायन्तच्छन्दसां छन्दस्त्वम्⟧ (⟦तै. सं. ५. ६. ६. १⟧): Prajāpati piled the fire; it stood there becoming sharp as a razor's edge; the gods, afraid, did not approach it; having covered themselves with metres, they approached it; that is the chandas-hood of the metres.

## SBh 1.1.0.7

Or else, it is called chandas because it covers, in order to ward off untimely death. That too is recited in the Chāndogya Upaniṣad: ⟦देवा वै मृत्योर्बिभ्यतस्त्रयीं विद्या प्राविशंस्ते छन्दोभिरात्मानमच्छादयन्यदेभिरच्छादयंस्तच्छन्दसां छन्दस्त्वम्⟧ (⟦छां. उ. १. ४. २⟧): the gods, afraid of death, entered the threefold knowledge; they covered themselves with metres; because they covered themselves with these, that is the chandas-hood of the metres. Likewise it is recited that the word ⟦देव⟧ has as its source the root ⟦दीव्यति⟧, in the sense of shining: ⟦दिवा वै नोऽभूदिति तद्देवानां देवत्वम्⟧, "it became day for us": that is the god-hood of the gods. Hence ⟦दीव्यतीति देवः⟧; the meaning is that he shines by the mantra. In this sūkta Agni is the deity, because he is praised. And so it is stated in the Anukramaṇikā (⟦अनु. १२. १२⟧): ⟦मण्डलादिष्वाग्नेयमैन्द्रात्⟧: at the beginnings of the maṇḍalas, the Āgneya sūktas come before the Aindra. The Lord, the Veda-Puruṣa, speaks the first Ṛk of that sūkta:

## SBh 1.1.1.1

⟦ईळे⟧: I praise the god named Agni. The root is ⟦ईड स्तुतौ⟧, in the sense of praising. The substitution of ⟦ळकार⟧ for ⟦डकार⟧ has come down by the tradition of the students of the Bahvṛca (Ṛgveda) school. And so it is recited: ⟦अज्मध्यस्थडकारस्य ळकारं बह्वृचा जगुः । अज्मध्यस्थढकारस्य ळ्हकारं वै यथाक्रमम्⟧: the Bahvṛcas have sung the ḷ for a ḍ standing between vowels, and, in order, the ḷh for a ḍh standing between vowels.

## SBh 1.1.1.2

Since the mantra is to be employed by the hotṛ, it is understood: "I, the hotṛ, praise." What kind of Agni? ⟦यज्ञस्य⟧ ⟦पुरोहितम्⟧. As the king's purohita carries out what he desires, so Agni also carries out the oblation that the sacrifice requires. Or else, he stands in the form of the Āhavanīya in the eastern part belonging to the sacrifice. Again, what kind? ⟦देवं⟧, endowed with the qualities of giving and the like. Again, what kind? ⟦होतारम्⟧, ⟦ऋत्विजम्⟧. In the sacrifices of the gods, the priest called hotṛ is Agni alone. And so it is heard: ⟦अग्निर्वै देवानां होता⟧ (⟦ऐ. ब्रा. ३. १४⟧): Agni, verily, is the hotṛ of the gods. Again, what kind? ⟦रत्नधातमं⟧: the one who bears most excellently, or nourishes, the treasures that take the form of the fruit of the sacrifice.

## SBh 1.1.1.3

Here Yāska shows, in many ways, the explanation of the word ⟦अग्नि⟧: ⟦अथातोऽनुक्रमिष्यामोऽग्निः पृथिवीस्थानस्तं प्रथमं व्याख्यास्यामोऽग्निः कस्मादग्रणीर्भवत्यग्रं यज्ञेषु प्रणीयतेऽङ्गं नयति संनममानोऽक्नोपनो भवतीति स्थौलाष्ठीविर्न क्नोपयति न स्नेहयति त्रिभ्य आख्यातेभ्यो जायत इति शाकपूणिरितादक्ताद्दग्धाद्वा नीतात्स खल्वेतेरकारमादत्ते गकारमनक्तेर्वा दहतेर्वा नीः परस्तस्यैषा भवत्यग्निमीळे⟧ (⟦निरु. ७, १४⟧). That is: "Now, therefore, we shall go through the deities in order. Agni has the earth as his place; him we shall explain first. Why is Agni agraṇī (the leader)? He is led forward (agram praṇīyate) at the sacrifices; he leads a limb (aṅgaṃ nayati), bending himself well; or he is akno-pana. Sthaulāṣṭhīvi says: he does not dry up (⟦न क्नोपयति⟧), he does not moisten (⟦न स्नेहयति⟧). Śākapūṇi says that he is born from three verbs: from ita (gone), akta (anointed), dagdha (burnt), or nīta (led); he takes the a from the root ⟦एति⟧, the g from ⟦अनक्ति⟧ or from ⟦दहति⟧, and nī comes last. Of him this Ṛk is: ⟦अग्निमीळे⟧." The meaning of this is as follows. Since the characteristic common to all the deities has been stated in general, and, as it is then desired that each one be spoken of specially, therefore we shall speak of them in order. There, Agni, who stands in the earth-world, will be explained first.

## SBh 1.1.1.4

The answer ⟦अग्रणीः⟧ and the rest is to the question: "From what ground of application (pravṛtti-nimitta) is the deity denoted by the word ⟦अग्नि⟧?" ⟦अग्रणीः⟧: he himself leads the army of the gods in front. This is one ground of application of the word ⟦अग्नि⟧. And so another Brāhmaṇa says: ⟦अग्निर्देवानां सेनानीः⟧, "Agni is the leader of the army of the gods." With just this in mind the Bahvṛcas recite in the mantra and the Brāhmaṇa: ⟦अग्निर्मुखं प्रथमो देवतानाम्⟧ (⟦ऐ. ब्रा. १. ४⟧), this is the mantra; ⟦अग्निर्वै देवानामवमः⟧ (⟦ऐ. ब्रा. १, १⟧), this is the Brāhmaṇa. And likewise the Taittirīyas recite: ⟦अग्निरग्रे प्रथम देवतानाम्⟧ (⟦तै. ब्रा. २. ४. ३. ३⟧).

## SBh 1.1.1.5

And ⟦अग्निरवमो देवतानाम्⟧. And the Vājasaneyins recite thus: ⟦स वा एषोऽग्रे देवतानामजायत तस्मादग्निर्नाम⟧: he, that one, was born first of the deities; therefore the name is Agni. In the sacrifices, which take the form of the agnihotra, the iṣṭi, the animal sacrifice and the Soma, he is led forward (⟦अग्रं⟧), from the Gārhapatya toward the place of the Āhavanīya lying in the eastern direction: this is the second ground of application. ⟦संनममानः⟧: bending himself down well, of his own accord, ⟦अङ्गं⟧ he leads his own body, that is, he sends it forth in burning wood and in cooking the oblation: this is the third ground of application. A certain author of the Nirukta, the son of the great seer named Sthūlāṣṭhīvan, explains the word ⟦अग्नि⟧ as ⟦अक्नोपनः⟧.

## SBh 1.1.1.6

There, when it is said ⟦न क्नोपयति⟧, it means: he does not moisten (⟦न स्नेहयति⟧), but rather he makes wood and the like dry. The author of the Nirukta named Śākapūṇi holds that the word ⟦अग्नि⟧ is formed from three roots. From ⟦इतः⟧ the root is ⟦इण् गतौ⟧. From ⟦अक्तः⟧ the root is ⟦अञ्जूव्यक्तिम्रक्षणकान्तिगतिषु⟧. From ⟦दग्धः⟧ the root is ⟦दह भस्मीकरणे⟧. From ⟦नीतः⟧ the root is ⟦णीम् प्रापणे⟧. The word ⟦अग्नि⟧, not depending on the letter a, the letter g and the part ni taken as separate words, takes the letter a from the word ⟦अयन⟧, which is produced from the root ⟦एति⟧. It takes the letter g after making ⟦गकार⟧ the substitute of the ⟦ककारस्य⟧ belonging to the root ⟦अनक्ति⟧. Or else it takes the letter g from the word ⟦दग्ध⟧, produced from the root ⟦दहति⟧. ⟦नीः⟧ is from the root ⟦नयति⟧.

## SBh 1.1.1.7

And that, becoming short, comes last. Then the three roots, uniting, become the word ⟦अग्नि⟧. The sense of the whole is: having gone to the sacrificial ground, he leads his own limb, that is, sends it forth in burning wood and in cooking the oblation. In order to show the praise, chiefly, of that special deity who is the meaning of the word ⟦अग्नि⟧, there is this Ṛk ⟦अग्निमीळे⟧. This Ṛk Yāska has explained thus: ⟦अग्निमीळेऽग्निं याचामीळिरध्येषणाकर्मा पूजाकर्मा वा पुरोहितो व्याख्यातो यज्ञस्य देवो दानाद्वा दीपनाद्वा द्योतनाद्वा द्युस्थानो भवतीति वा यो देवः सा देवता होतारं ह्वातारं जुहोतेर्होतेत्यौर्णवाभो रत्नधातमं रमणीयानां धनानां दातृतमम्⟧. That is: "I praise Agni; I beg Agni. The verb īḍ (īḷ) means entreaty or worship. The purohita has been explained. Of the sacrifice. Deva is so called from giving, or from kindling, or from shining; or he is the one whose place is the sky; whoever is a deva, that is a devatā. Hotāram is the one who calls (hvātṛ); the hotṛ is from the root ⟦जुहोति⟧, says Aurṇavābha. Ratnadhātamam is the one who is the greatest giver of lovely riches."

## SBh 1.1.1.8

(⟦निरु. ७. १५⟧). Its meaning is as follows. That the root ⟦ईडति⟧ has the sense of praise is well known. Relying on the maxim that roots have several meanings, request, entreaty and worship have also been given as its meanings here, because they are suitable. The word ⟦पुरोहित⟧ is explained in the second chapter, after citing the Ṛk ⟦यद्देवापिः शंतनवे पुरोहितः⟧, by ⟦पुर एनं दधति⟧ (⟦निरु. २. १२⟧): they place him in front. And the Taittirīyas, having laid down an animal rite for one who competes for the office of purohita, recite as its fruit ⟦पुर एनं दधते⟧ (⟦ते. सं. २. १. २. ९⟧). The word ⟦देव⟧ denotes any one of the meanings giving, kindling and shining. It is said that this Agni is the giver, the kindler and the illuminator of the sacrifice.

## SBh 1.1.1.9

Although kindling and shining are of one meaning, there is a difference of root. Although Agni has the earth as his place, nevertheless, because he carries the oblation to the gods, he has the sky as his place. Since the words ⟦देव⟧ and ⟦देवता⟧ are synonyms, no deity other than Agni, set forth by the mantra, is to be sought. Since the word ⟦होतृ⟧ is produced from the root ⟦ह्वयति⟧, it means "the one who calls the gods." But the sage named Aurṇavābha holds that the word ⟦होतृ⟧ is produced from the root ⟦जुहोति⟧. And Agni's being a hotṛ is to be seen as his being the substratum of the oblation. The word ⟦रत्न⟧ is read in the second chapter among the twenty-eight names of wealth beginning with ⟦मघम्⟧ (⟦नि.२. १०.७⟧). Its being ⟦रत्न⟧ is because of loveliness. The root ⟦दधाति⟧ here conveys the sense of giving. This is the commentary on the mantra by Yāska, the author of the Nirukta.

## SBh 1.1.1.10

Now the grammatical procedure is stated. After the root ⟦अगि⟧, in the sense of going, by the Uṇādi-sūtra ⟦अङ्गेर्नलोपश्च⟧ (⟦उ. सू. ४. ४९०⟧), the suffix ⟦नि⟧ is added. And the loss of the n that would be obtained by the augment ⟦नुम⟧, because the root has the marker i (⟦पा. सू. ७. १. ५८⟧), takes place. ⟦अङ्गति स्वर्गे गच्छति हविर्नेतुमित्यग्निः⟧: he goes to heaven in order to carry the oblation; therefore he is agni. There, by ⟦धातोः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. १६२⟧), the letter a is udātta. By ⟦आद्युदात्तश्च⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ३. १. ३⟧), the letter i belonging to the suffix is also udātta. By ⟦अनुदात्तं पदमेकवर्जम्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १, १५८⟧), it is obtained that, one of the two being left udātta, the other becomes anudātta. There, when the root accent has first been established, the suffix accent, taught afterwards, is what remains.

## SBh 1.1.1.11

For there is the maxim ⟦सति शिष्टस्वरो बलीयान्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. १५८.९⟧): when there is a conflict, the remaining accent is stronger. Hence the stem ⟦अग्नि⟧ has the final udātta. By ⟦अनुदात्तौ सुप्पितौ⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ३. १. ४⟧), the accusative singular ending ⟦अम्⟧ is anudātta. The prior form (pūrvarūpa) that results from it by ⟦अमि पूर्वः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. १०७⟧) is udātta, since it is laid down ⟦एकादेश उदात्तेनोदात्तः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. २. ५⟧). In the view that the word ⟦अग्नि⟧ is produced from a root, this whole procedure is to be seen. Yāska has shown the two views: ⟦नामान्याख्यातजानीति शाकटायनो नैरुक्तसमयश्च न सर्वाणीति गार्ग्यो वैयाकरणानां चैके⟧ (⟦निरु. १. १२⟧): Śākaṭāyana, and the doctrine of the Nairuktas, says that nouns are produced from verbs; Gārgya, and some of the grammarians, say that not all of them are.

## SBh 1.1.1.12

In the view of Gārgya, since the word ⟦अग्नि⟧ is an indivisible nominal stem, it has the final udātta by ⟦फिषोऽन्त उदात्तः⟧ (⟦फि. सू. १⟧). In the explanations stated before, ⟦अग्रणीः⟧ and the others, the whole procedure of base, suffix and the rest is to be supposed as is fitting. With just this in mind Yāska says: ⟦अथ निर्वचनं तद्येषु पदेषु स्वरसंस्कारौ समर्थौ प्रादेशिकेन गुणेनान्वितौ स्यातां तथा तानि निर्ब्रूयादथानन्वितेऽर्थेऽप्रादेशिके विकारेऽर्थनित्यः परीक्षेत केनचिद्वृत्तिसामान्येनाविद्यमाने सामान्येऽप्यक्षरवर्णसामान्यान्निर्ब्रूयान्नत्वेव न निर्ब्रूयात्⟧ (⟦निरु. २. १⟧). The meaning of this is as follows. "Now, the etymology: in those words in which accent and formation are competent and are accompanied by a local quality, one should explain them accordingly. But where the meaning is not so accompanied, in a non-local modification, one devoted to the meaning should investigate through some similarity of operation. Where even a similarity is not found, one should explain by similarity of syllable and letter. But one should by no means refrain from explaining." That is, among the group of words to be explained, in those words ⟦अग्नि⟧ and the rest in which accent and formation are competent, that is, established by grammar, in the manner stated before. The accent is udātta and the rest; the formation is the suffix ⟦नि⟧ and the rest. Moreover, these two, accent and formation, should be accompanied by a local quality. The locality (pradeśa) is a part of the word, the root ⟦अगि⟧ stated before. The quality that arises there is the meaning in the form of going. They are accompanied by that. One should explain those words ⟦अग्नि⟧ and the rest in that way, in accordance with grammar. And that explanation has been shown by us.

## SBh 1.1.1.13

Now, in contrast to what was stated before, if some meaning intended by oneself is not accompanied, that is, is not found in that word, its explanation is: ⟦अप्रादेशिके विकारे⟧. The modification (vikāra) is a particular action, in the form of leading in front and the like. And that is not expressed here by the locality, that is, by a part of the word ⟦अग्नि⟧; therefore it is non-local. This being so, the man who is ⟦अर्थनित्यः⟧, that is, fixed on the meaning he intends, who is insistent, whose insistence is either to connect it according to a Brāhmaṇa, or as an epithet of another deity: then that man should investigate the meaning he intends through some similarity of operation, and should connect it with that word. Operation (vṛtti) is action. Similarity in that form is resemblance. And by us, relying on the similarity of being an action, in the form of leading in front and the like, the meaning of being the leader in front and the like has been connected. This is the explanation approved by Yāska. Sthaulāṣṭhīvi explains by similarity of syllable. At the beginning of the word ⟦अक्नोपन⟧ there is the syllable in the form of the letter a, for the sake of negation. At the beginning of the word ⟦अग्नि⟧ also there is the letter a. This ⟦अग्निः⟧, ⟦देवः⟧, with the other gods who enjoy oblations, ⟦आ⟧ ⟦गमत्⟧: let him come together in this sacrifice. What kind of Agni? ⟦होता⟧: the accomplisher of the oblation. ⟦कविक्रतुः⟧: the word ⟦कवि⟧ here means one who has gone beyond (⟦क्रान्त⟧), and does not belong to the names for the wise. ⟦क्रतुः⟧ is a name for wisdom or for action. Hence: one whose wisdom has gone beyond, or whose action has gone beyond. ⟦सत्यः⟧: free from falsehood; the meaning is that he surely gives the fruit. ⟦चित्रश्रवस्तमः⟧: ⟦श्रवः⟧ is fame, since it is heard (⟦श्रूयते⟧). He is endowed, in the highest degree, with manifold fame.

## SBh 1.1.5.2

In ⟦कविक्रतुः⟧ and ⟦चित्रश्रवस्तमः⟧, in both, since they are bahuvrīhi compounds, the accent of the original form of the first member (⟦पा. सू. ६. २. १⟧). ⟦सत्सु साधुः सत्यः⟧: good among the good; in ⟦सत्यादशपथे⟧ (⟦पा, सू. ५, ४. ६६⟧) the one with the final udātta was declared irregularly formed by Haradatta.

## SBh 1.1.5.3

For the word ⟦गच्छतु⟧, which ends in the imperative, there is no change to the letter ccha. The loss of the letter u is Vedic. Hence the form becomes ⟦गमत्⟧. The rest is clear. End of the first.

## SBh 1.1.6.1

⟦अङ्ग⟧ is a particle in the sense of turning toward. ⟦अङ्ग⟧ ⟦अग्ने⟧: O Agni, you, for the sacrificer who has given the oblation, ⟦दाशुषे⟧, for his pleasure, ⟦यत्⟧ ⟦भद्रं⟧: the good that you ⟦करिष्यसि⟧ will do, consisting of wealth, house, offspring and cattle, ⟦तत्⟧ that good is ⟦तव⟧ ⟦इत्⟧: yours alone. The remainder is: it is the cause of happiness. ⟦अङ्गिरः⟧: O Agni. And this is ⟦सत्यं⟧: there is no contradiction in it. When the sacrificer has attained prosperity beginning with ⟦विज्ञादिसंपत्तौ⟧ (**NOTE**{is "विज्ञ" as supplied meant here, since the earlier list reads wealth, house, offspring and cattle?}), the happiness is Agni's alone, through the performance of the later sacrifices. The Śāṭyāyanins recite on the meaning of the word ⟦भद्र⟧: ⟦यद्वै पुरुषस्य वित्तं तद्भद्रं गृहा भद्रं प्रजा भद्रं पशवो भद्रम्⟧: whatever a man's wealth is, that is good; houses are good; offspring is good; cattle are good.

## SBh 1.1.6.2

Although ⟦अङ्ग⟧ is a particle, it has the final udātta because it belongs to the group ⟦अभ्यादि⟧ (⟦फि. सू. ८१⟧). By the rule ⟦दाश्वान्साह्वान्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. १२⟧), the suffix ⟦क्वसु⟧ is irregularly formed after the root ⟦दाश दाने⟧. There, the suffix accent (⟦पा. सू. ३. १. ३⟧). It is not to be suspected that the vocative ⟦अग्नि⟧ has the anudātta taught in the eighth book (⟦पा. सू. ८. १. १९⟧), as following a word, because that is excluded by ⟦अपादादौ⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. १. १८⟧). Hence there is just the initial udātta taught in the sixth book (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. १९८⟧). Although for the word ⟦भद्र⟧, being within the sphere of ⟦नब्विषयस्य⟧, the initial udātta would be obtained (⟦फि. सू. २६⟧), it has the final udātta because of the irregular formation, after the root ⟦भदि कल्याणे⟧, with the suffix ⟦रक्⟧.

## SBh 1.1.6.3

In this sentence, because of the use of the relative ⟦य⟧, the nighāta is prohibited by ⟦निपातैर्यद्यदिहन्त⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. १. ३०⟧); then, by the suffix accent of ⟦स्य⟧, what remains gives the word ⟦करिष्यसि⟧ the udātta on the penultimate syllable. In ⟦तव⟧ the initial udātta, by ⟦युष्मदस्मदोर्ङसि⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. २ १ १⟧). Yāska says ⟦अङ्गिरा अङ्गाराः⟧ (⟦निरु० ३. १७⟧). The Aitareyins also recite, in the account of the meditation of Prajāpati on his daughter: ⟦येऽङ्गारा आसंस्तेऽङ्गिरसोऽभवन्⟧ (⟦ऐ. ब्रा. ३. ३४⟧). Therefore, because the sage named Aṅgiras came from him, Agni, who is in the form of the coals, is Aṅgiras. Here, as following a word, the anudātta taught in the eighth book.

## SBh 1.1.6.4

In the Agnīṣoma-praṇayana, the anuvacanīya tṛca beginning ⟦उप त्वाग्ने⟧. And this is recited in the Brāhmaṇa: ⟦उप त्वाग्ने दिवेदिव उप प्रियं पनिप्नतमिति तिस्रश्चैकां चान्वाह⟧ (⟦ऐ. ब्रा. १. ३०⟧): with ⟦उप त्वाग्ने दिवेदिवे⟧ and ⟦उप प्रियं पनिप्नतम्⟧ he recites three and one.

## SBh 1.1.7.1

⟦अग्ने⟧: O Agni, we, the performers, ⟦दिवेदिवे⟧, day by day, ⟦दोषावस्तः⟧, at night and in the day, ⟦धिया⟧, with the mind, ⟦नमः⟧ ⟦भरन्तः⟧, offering homage, ⟦उप⟧, near, ⟦त्वा⟧ ⟦एमसि⟧: we come to you. For ⟦उप⟧, the particle accent (⟦फि. सू. ८०⟧). By ⟦त्वामौ द्वितीयायाः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. १. २३⟧), the anudātta ⟦त्वा⟧ is the substitute for the pronoun for "you." The word ⟦दोषा⟧ denotes night. ⟦वस्तर्⟧ denotes day. In a dvandva compound, because of the group ⟦कार्तकौजपादि⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. २. ३७⟧), it has the initial udātta.

## SBh 1.1.7.2

By ⟦सावेकाचः०⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. १६८⟧), the case ending of ⟦धियो⟧ is udātta. ⟦नम⟧ is a particle. In ⟦भरन्त⟧, when ⟦शप⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ३. १. ६८⟧) is anudātta because it is pit, and because ⟦शतुर्⟧ is lasārvadhātuka (⟦पा. सु. ६. १. १८६⟧), the root accent remains. In ⟦इमसि⟧, the substitution by ⟦इदन्तो मसिः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ७. १. ४६⟧), and the nighāta.

## SBh 1.1.8.1

In the preceding mantra, ⟦त्वामुपैम⟧ was said with reference to Agni. What kind of you? ⟦राजन्तं⟧: shining. ⟦अध्वराणां⟧: of the sacrifices free from harm done by the demons. ⟦गोपां⟧: the protector. ⟦ऋतस्य⟧: of the truth, that is, of the sure-to-come fruit of action; ⟦दीदिविं⟧: the one who illumines again and again, or greatly. Seeing Agni as the support of the offering, the fruit of action, well known from the śāstra, is recalled. ⟦स्वे⟧ ⟦दमे⟧: in his own house, in the sacrificial hall, ⟦वर्धमानम्⟧, growing by the oblations.

## SBh 1.1.8.2

In ⟦राजन्तं⟧ and ⟦वर्धमानम⟧, in both, the root accent remains, as before. For ⟦दीदिवि⟧ the initial udātta by ⟦अभ्यस्तानामादिः⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. १८९⟧). The word ⟦दम⟧, because it belongs to the group ⟦वृषादि⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ६. १. २०३⟧), has the initial udātta.

## SBh 1.1.9.1

In both cases the illustration is this: as, ⟦सूनवे⟧, for the sake of the son, the father is, for the most part, easily reached and joined, so it is here. ## SBh 1.1.9.2

For ⟦न⟧, the substitute for the first-person pronoun, the anudātta by ⟦अनुदात्तं सर्वम्⟧ (⟦पा. सू. ८. १. १८⟧). By ⟦चादयोऽनुदात्ताः⟧ (⟦फि. सू. ८४⟧), ⟦इव⟧ is anudātta.
