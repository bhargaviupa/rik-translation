## SBh 1.155.0.1

The sūkta beginning ⟦प्र वः पान्तम्⟧, of six Ṛks, is the sixteenth sūkta; it is of Dīrghatamas and in the jagatī metre. Because ⟦वैष्णवं हि⟧, "addressed to Viṣṇu indeed", was said at the preceding sūkta, it is addressed to Viṣṇu. And the first triplet has Indra as its deity. The Anukramaṇikā says: ⟦प्र वो जागतं त्वैन्द्रश्चाद्यस्तृचः⟧, "'Pra vaḥ': but in jagatī; and the first triplet is addressed to Indra". Its employment in the reciting beyond the stoma on the Ukthya days of the Abhiplava has been stated.

## SBh 1.155.1.1

O Adhvaryu and the rest, ⟦वः⟧, belonging to you, ⟦पान्तं⟧, whose nature it is to protect, or which is to be drunk, ⟦अन्धसः⟧, the andhas, the food in the form of Soma, ⟦प्र⟧ ⟦अर्चत⟧, prepare it in an excellent way. For whom? For ⟦धियायते⟧, him who desires praises, ⟦महे⟧, the great, ⟦शूराय⟧, the valiant one, Indra, ⟦विष्णवे⟧ ⟦च⟧, and for the pervading god who bears this name. What is the pre-eminence of these two? It is told: ⟦या⟧, which two, Indra and Viṣṇu, ⟦पर्वतानां⟧, of those that possess gratifying, or that possess filling. The word parvata is explained by Yāska thus: ⟦पर्ववान् पर्वतः पर्व पुनः पृणातेः प्रीणातेर्वा⟧, "Parvata is that which has joints; and parvan again is from the root pṝ or from the root prī".

## SBh 1.155.1.2

Of the worlds so characterised, ⟦सानुनि⟧, on the elevated region; the meaning is, in the whole world. Or else, on the elevated region of the clouds, since the word parvata is stated among the names of the cloud. There ⟦तस्थतुः⟧, they stand. Of what kind are those two? ⟦अदाभ्या⟧, not to be harmed, not to be overpowered by others, and for that very reason ⟦महः⟧, great. Or else: mahaḥ is "in splendour", in their own lustre, and it is connected with the word sānuni. For this there is an illustration: ⟦इव⟧, as by ⟦साधुना⟧, by one able to bring one to the desired place, ⟦अर्वता⟧, by a swift horse. As by means of it one mounts a very elevated region, so these two have mounted a high station. Therefore prepare it for those two.

## SBh 1.155.2.1

When there is Soma left over, at the third pressing, this is an optional yājyā. And so it is laid down in the sūtra: ⟦त्वेषमित्था समरणं शिमीवतोरिति वा याज्या⟧ (⟦आश्व. श्रौ. ६. ७⟧), "or the yājyā is the Ṛk beginning 'tveṣam itthā samaraṇaṃ śimīvatoḥ'".

## SBh 1.155.2.2

O ⟦इन्द्राविष्णू⟧, Indra and Viṣṇu, of ⟦शिमीवतोः⟧, of you who have such works as the granting of what is desired, ⟦वां⟧, of you two, ⟦इत्था⟧, in this manner, the ⟦त्वेषं⟧, the blazing, ⟦समरणं⟧, proper going to the place of the sacrifice, ⟦सुतपाः⟧, the sacrificer who has drunk the Soma left over from the offering, ⟦उरुष्यति⟧, protects, honours by sacrifice. Or else: of you who have such works as striking, of you two, the blazing, the bright, endowed with valour, samaraṇa, attended by a proper going to one another, which has the form of the cleaving of the cloud for the granting of rain, the sacrificer protects; the meaning is that he praises it. What is the pre-eminence of these two? It is told: ⟦या⟧, which two, Indra and Viṣṇu, ⟦मर्त्याय⟧, for the man, for the sacrificer who gives the oblation, ⟦प्रतिधीयमानमित्⟧, that which is to be bestowed in return, having the form of the fruit, ⟦असनाम्⟧, the asana, whose nature it is to move, whose nature it is to be given, food and the like, from ⟦कृशानोः⟧, from Agni, ⟦अस्तुः⟧, the thrower of what is desired, or the driver-out of enemies, ⟦उरुष्यथः⟧, you make go on without interruption. The meaning is: having accepted the oblation offered in the fire, you will give the fruit too through his mouth alone.

## SBh 1.155.3.1

⟦ताः⟧, those well-known oblations in the form of Soma, offered by the sacrificer, ⟦ईम्⟧, this, ⟦अस्य⟧, of this Indra, ⟦महि⟧, great, ⟦पौंस्यं⟧, deed of a man, the strength whose form is going to be told, ⟦वर्धन्ति⟧, they make grow. Or ⟦ईम्⟧ is without meaning. And that Indra ⟦नि⟧ ⟦नयति⟧, conveys thoroughly, the power produced by the drinking of Soma to ⟦मातरा⟧, to heaven and earth. For what purpose? ⟦रेतसे⟧, for the capacity of living beings to beget sons and the like, ⟦भुजे⟧, for their enjoyment or their protection. Where? That is told: ⟦दिवः⟧, of the shining one, the sun, ⟦अधि⟧, exceedingly, ⟦रोचने⟧, in the shining orb. The ⟦नाम⟧, the bending, the power established in heaven and earth, the ⟦पुत्रः⟧, the protector of many, all people, ⟦पितुः⟧, from the protector, Viṣṇu, by his favour.

## SBh 1.155.3.2

Or else: ⟦पितुः⟧, from his own begetter, being himself born, the ⟦अवरं⟧, the lower name, termed "grandson", the ⟦परं⟧, the one higher than that, the name "son", and, in relation to both of these, the ⟦तृतीयं⟧, the third name, "father", ⟦दधाति⟧, he bears. This is because of the passage of the Veda ⟦त्रीणि वाव रेतांसि पिता पुत्रः पौत्रः⟧ (⟦तै. सं. ५. ६. ८. ४⟧), "Three indeed are the seeds: father, son, grandson". The meaning of this is as follows. The oblations of Soma offered in the fire reach the orb of the sun and nourish Indra and Viṣṇu, who are other forms of Savitṛ of the twelve selves; those two, being satisfied, rain by means of the cloud. By that rain, by way of the crops and the like, they make the offspring grow in the form of sons, grandsons and the rest. Of such great majesty are Indra and Viṣṇu.

## SBh 1.155.4.1

Of ⟦अस्य⟧, of this one of great majesty, ⟦तत्तत्⟧, this and that ⟦पौंस्यं⟧, manliness, excellence of valour, ⟦गृणीमसि⟧, we praise, we laud. Of what kind is he? Of ⟦इनस्य⟧, the lord of all, ⟦त्रातुः⟧, the protector, ⟦अवृकस्य⟧: a vṛka is one who injures, an enemy and the like; of him who is free from such; ⟦मीळ्हुषः⟧, of the sprinkler, the ever young. Among the deeds of valour that are to be praised he mentions one: ⟦यः⟧, the Viṣṇu who, the ⟦पार्थिवानि⟧, the earth and the rest, or those that possess extension; by the force of the sense it is understood that the rajases are meant. That the three worlds too are denoted by the word pṛthivī has been shown before. Over these, by ⟦त्रिभिरिद्विगामभिः⟧, by three varied goings only, ⟦उरु⟧, in such a way that it is wide, ⟦क्रमिष्ट⟧, he strode; the meaning is that he strode out; ⟦उरुगायाय⟧, for that which is to be widely sung, ⟦जीवसे⟧, for the protection of the three worlds. Or else ⟦उरुगायाय⟧: the fourth case-ending is in the sense of the sixth. Such valour of the urugāya, of Viṣṇu, we praise. For what purpose? For jīvase, for life, for protection.

## SBh 1.155.5.1

Of ⟦अस्य⟧, of this ⟦स्वर्दृशः⟧, the seer of heaven or of everything, Viṣṇu, ⟦द्वे⟧ ⟦इत्⟧ ⟦क्रमणे⟧, two steps only, ⟦मर्त्यः⟧, a man, ⟦अभिख्याय⟧, having proclaimed them by all prosperity and the like **NOTE**{the gloss of abhikhyāya is ⟦सर्वभूत्यादिना प्रख्याय⟧, translated word for word as "having proclaimed by all prosperity and the like": what is meant by its first word?}, ⟦भुरण्यति⟧, goes to, resorts to. The verb bhuraṇyati belongs to the group beginning with kaṇḍū and has the sense of going, because it is read among the verbs of that sense in ⟦भुरण्यति शवति⟧ (⟦नि. २. १४. १६⟧), "bhuraṇyati, śavati". The world of earth because it is well known, and the mid-region because the rain comes from it: so he knows both steps.

## SBh 1.155.5.2

⟦अस्य⟧, of this Viṣṇu, the ⟦तृतीयं⟧, the third step, called the world of heaven, no man whatever, ⟦नकिः⟧, not at all, ⟦आ⟧ ⟦दधर्षति⟧, overpowers by his understanding; the meaning is that he is not able to know it. Not man only, but ⟦वयश्चन⟧, even the Maruts, the goers. Of what kind are they? ⟦पतयन्तः⟧, able to go everywhere; and likewise the ⟦पतत्रिणः⟧, those whose nature it is to fly, Garuḍa and the like, or the winds, are not at all able. The augment aṭ comes to the root dhṛṣ in leṭ. The purport is: because the world of Satya is exceedingly remote, it is beyond the range of all.

## SBh 1.155.6.1

This Viṣṇu, whose self is the sun, ⟦नवतिं⟧ ⟦च⟧, ninety, ⟦साकं⟧, joined with ⟦चतुर्भिः⟧, with four; the meaning is, ninety-four. The divisions of time of this number, by ⟦नामभिः⟧, by his own modes of bending, by particular impulsions, ⟦वृत्तं⟧, in such a way that it becomes round, ⟦व्यतीन्⟧, those whose nature it is to move in various ways, ⟦चक्रं⟧ ⟦न⟧, like a round wheel, a wheel furnished with many spokes: as one makes that whirl by hurling it upon an enemy, so the divisions of time of the said number ⟦अवीविपत्⟧, he makes tremble, he makes whirl. The root is ⟦टुवेपृ कम्पने⟧, ṭuvepṛ in the sense of trembling; it is the form from the stem ending in ṇi, in luṅ, with caṅ.

## SBh 1.155.6.2

Which, then, are they? It is told. The year is one. The half-years are two. The seasons are five. The months are twelve. The half-months are twenty-four. The days-and-nights are thirty. The watches are eight. The lagnas beginning with Meṣa, which come round in a single day, are twelve. Taken together, he has made tremble those of the number ninety with four in addition. But the sun himself also revolves like the others; how is it said that he makes them revolve? This is no fault, because he is another form of Viṣṇu, the fixed one, who makes these revolve. Or else because the revolving of the others depends on his own revolving; therefore it is said that he himself makes them revolve.

## SBh 1.155.6.3

Viṣṇu, who thus has the divisions as his self, is ⟦बृहच्छरीरः⟧, of great body, because in his self as Virāj the bodies of all gods, men and the rest are his own body. He establishes this very greatness of body: ⟦विमिमानः⟧, measuring out his self in various ways, dividing it and setting it up as gods and animals. Or else: measured out by ⟦ऋक्वभिः⟧, by those who have praises, or by those who have mantras. Although he is all-pervading, yet, because he is dependent on devotion, he is measured by praise. ⟦युवा⟧, whose nature it is to mingle everywhere, or the ever young; for that very reason ⟦अकुमारः⟧, not small; Mahāviṣṇu, being such, ⟦प्रत्येति⟧, goes, to the ⟦आहवम्⟧, the call, to the place of the sacrifice.
