## SBh 0.2.103.1

"Because of contradiction there is no authority." The followers of the Aitareya recension read: ⟦आत्मा वा इदमेक ऐवाग्र आसीद्⟧, "In the beginning this was the Self alone" (⟦ऐ०. उ०. १।१⟧). The Taittirīyas read: ⟦असद् वा इदमग्र आसीत्⟧, "In the beginning this was non-being" (⟦तै०.उ०. २.७⟧). This is a contradiction. Therefore the whole injunctive portion of the Veda is without authority. When this objection has been raised, we answer: Let the injunction of ⟦जर्त्तिलादि⟧ and the like indeed be without authority, because what it teaches is not to be performed. But the matter that is to be accepted is enjoined in a later sentence, ⟦अजक्षीरेण जहोति⟧, "He offers with goat's milk" (⟦तै. सं० ५।४।३।२⟧). In praise of that, the ⟦जर्त्तिलादि⟧ and the like are here restated and then censured. It is as when, in praise of cows and horses, the sentence ⟦अपगवो वा अन्ये गोअश्वेभ्यः⟧, "Others than cows and horses are non-cattle" (⟦तै०- ब्रा०. ३।९।१०।२⟧), in the form of an explanatory passage denies that goats and the like are cattle; it is the same here. The objector says: "In that case, since goats and the like really are cattle, the injunction of ⟦जर्सिलादि⟧ and the like, though censured here, might exist somewhere in another recension." Let it be so; its authority too will hold for the student of that recension. It is as with eating another's food: though forbidden in the householder's stage of life, it is authoritative in other stages. By this reasoning, injunction and prohibition that contradict one another are everywhere to be settled by difference of person, just as a difference in the reading of mantras is settled by difference of recension. The Taittirīyas recite the mantra ⟦वायवस्थोपायवस्थेति⟧ (⟦तै०. सं०१।१.१⟧).

## SBh 0.2.103.2

But the followers of the Vājasaneyi recension do not recite the portion ⟦उपायवस्थ⟧ (⟦वा. स०. १।१⟧); on the contrary, the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa restates that portion and rejects it. Likewise, in the Sūktavāka mantra, the Taittirīyas reject the reading of another recension and recite another reading **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦पाठतत्ततरं⟧ is read as pāṭhāntaram, "another reading"; is this right?}. The rejection is this: ⟦यद् ब्रूयात्सूपावसाना च स्वध्यवसाना चेति प्रमायु कोयजमानः स्यादिति⟧, "If he were to say 'sūpāvasānā ca svadhyavasānā ca', the sacrificer would die prematurely." The teaching of the other reading is: ⟦सूपचरणा च स्वधिचरणा चेरयेव ब्रूयाद्⟧, "He should say only 'sūpacaraṇā ca svadhicaraṇā ca'" (⟦तै. सं०. २।६।९।६⟧). In this the arrangement is by difference of the persons who perform the rite **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦अनुष्ठाद्यरुषभेदेन⟧ is read as anuṣṭhātṛpuruṣabhedena, "by difference of the performing persons"; is this right?}.

## SBh 0.2.103.3

The same is to be understood in the case of injunctions. The objection about the Ṣoḍaśin cup and the like suits only you, who have not heard the account of the Mīmāṃsā. In the Pūrva-mīmāṃsā, in the eighth section of the tenth chapter, the option between taking and not taking the Ṣoḍaśin is decided. In the first section of the second chapter, the apūrva, the unseen result, is decided upon, for the sake of a fruit that comes at another time. In the same way, in the Uttara-mīmāṃsā, in the fourth section of the first chapter, in the aphorism ⟦कारणत्वेन चाकाशादिषु यथा व्यपदिष्टोक्तेः⟧ (⟦ब्र०. सू० १।४।१४⟧), the disagreement of the Śruti about the Supreme Self as the cause of the world is removed. In the first section of the second chapter, in the Ārambha section, we have the aphorism ⟦असद्व्यपदेशाम्नेति चेत्, न,⟧, "If it is said that it is not so, because of the statement of non-existence, no, …" (continued in the next unit).

## SBh 0.2.104.1

… ⟦धर्म्मान्तरेण वाक्यशेद्यात्⟧, "… because of the remainder of the sentence, by another property" (⟦ब्र०. सू० २।१।११⟧). In this aphorism it is decided that the word ⟦असच्छव्दख्य⟧ in the Taittirīya sentence does not have the sense of non-existence **NOTE**{the supplied letters ⟦न ज्जपरत्वं⟧ are read as na abhāvaparatvam, "not the sense of non-existence"; is this right?} but the sense of the unmanifest state. Just as Jaimini, in the aphorism on injunction (⟦जै० १।१।२⟧), declared that an injunctive sentence is an authority in regard to dharma, and in the aphorism on origin (⟦जै० १।१।५⟧) established that authority; and as Vyāsa too, in the aphorism that Brahman is the source of the scripture (⟦ब्र०. सू. १।१३⟧), declared the authority of the Vedānta concerning Brahman and established it with aphorisms beginning ⟦तत्तु समन्वयाद्⟧ (⟦ब्र० सू० १।१।३⟧). Therefore for you, who are no Mīmāṃsaka, the argument of the post and the robber stated before is hard to dispose of. Hence the authority of the injunctive portion is well established.

## SBh 0.2.104.2

Jaimini established the authority of the explanatory portion  with great effort. Those aphorisms will be explained. There he first states the opponent's position in an aphorism: ⟦आम्नायस्य क्रियार्थत्वादानर्थक्यमतदर्थानां तस्मादनित्यमुच्यते⟧ (⟦जै०१।२।१⟧). "Since the sacred tradition has action as its purpose, those parts that do not have that purpose are pointless; therefore they are called non-eternal."

## SBh 0.2.104.3

Since the whole of the sacred tradition is directed to teaching action, the explanatory passages, which do not teach action, have no intended meaning of their own. And those explanatory passages are recited thus: ⟦सोऽरोदीत्तद्रूऽद्रस्य रुद्रत्वम्⟧, "He wept; that is the Rudra-ness of Rudra" (⟦तै०. सं०. १।५।१⟧); ⟦स आत्मनो वपामुदखिदत्⟧, "He cut out his own omentum" (⟦तै० सं० २।१।१⟧); and ⟦देवा वै देवयजनमध्यवसाय दिशो न प्राजानन्⟧, "The gods, having settled on the place of sacrifice, did not know the directions" (⟦तै. सं०.६। १।५⟧). Since no intended meaning at all exists for a sentence of this kind, this sentence is called non-eternal. Although, because the Veda has no beginning, there is no non-eternity in its own form, still, because it lacks the eternal [function] characterized by conveying dharma **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦नित्यकाग्नस्य⟧ is read as nityakāryasya; is this right?}, and because it is equal to non-eternal poetic chatter, it is without authority; this is the meaning.

## SBh 0.2.104.4

Objection: even if the explanatory passages cited have no authority in regard to the dharma that is to be performed, let them have authority in regard to their own meaning, since their authority of their own, as conveying that, cannot be set aside. Anticipating this objection, and having in mind that since in certain other explanatory passages a conflict with another means of knowledge is seen, they are without authority, and that by the example of those all explanatory passages are without authority, he states the aphorism: ⟦शास्त्रहष्टविरोधाच्च⟧ (⟦जै०. १।२।२⟧), "And because of conflict with the scripture and with what is seen."

## SBh 0.2.104.5

Conflict is found in explanatory passages in three kinds: conflict with the scripture, conflict with what is seen, and conflict with both the scripture and what is seen. Thus, in ⟦स्तेनम् मनोऽनृतवादिनी वाग्⟧, "The mind is a thief; speech speaks untruth," the mental theft and the verbal speaking of untruth that are heard here conflict with the prohibitory scripture.

## SBh 0.2.105.1

In ⟦तस्माद् धूम एव अग्नेर्दिवा ददृशे नार्द्यिस्तस्मादर्च्चिरेवार्नग्नेक्तं दद्वशे न धूमः⟧ (⟦तै० भ्रा० २।१।२⟧), "Therefore by day only the smoke of Agni is seen, not the flame; therefore at night only the flame is seen, not the smoke," there is conflict with what is seen **NOTE**{the supplied words after ⟦तस्माद् धूम एव अग्नेर्दिवा ददृशे⟧ are damaged; the sense "not the flame" and "only the flame at night" is translated; is this right?}. Likewise in ⟦न चैतद् विद्मो वयं ब्राह्मणा वा स्मोऽब्राह्मणा वा⟧, "We do not know this, whether we are brahmins or non-brahmins" (⟦मै०. सं. १।४।११⟧), there is conflict with direct perception. In ⟦को हि तद् वेद यद्यमुष्मिन्लोकेऽस्ति वा न वा⟧, "Who indeed knows that, whether it exists in yonder world or not" (⟦तै०. सं. ६।१।१।१⟧), there is conflict with both scripture and what is seen. For in the scripture, in ⟦स्वर्गकामो यजेत⟧ and the like, a fruit belonging to the other world is seen. Therefore, because of conflict, the explanatory passages have no authority.

## SBh 0.2.105.2

Objection: even if "He wept" and the like are without authority because they serve no purpose, and "The mind is a thief" and the like because of conflict, yet the explanatory passages that state a fruit differ from both, so let them have authority. Anticipating this, he states the aphorism: ⟦तथा फलाभावात्⟧ (⟦जै० १।२।३⟧), "Likewise, because the fruit does not exist."

## SBh 0.2.105.3

Just as what the explanatory passages state is contrary to another means of knowledge, so the fruit also that they state does not exist. Thus, in connection with the Garga-trirātra it is heard: ⟦शोभतेऽख्य मुखं य एवं वेद⟧, "His face shines, who knows thus" (⟦ता० म० २०।१६।६⟧). In connection with the touching of the Veda at the new-moon and full-moon sacrifices it is heard: ⟦आस्य प्रजायां वाजी जायते य एव वेद⟧, "In his offspring is born a strong one, who knows thus." And we do not find that fruit in [those who] **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦वेदिहरगा⟧ is unclear; I read it as those who study the Veda; is this right?} study the Veda.

## SBh 0.2.105.4

Objection: even if the sentences stating a fruit in this world have no authority because of discrepancy, let the sentences stating a fruit in the other world have authority. Anticipating this, he states the aphorism: ⟦अन्यानर्थक्यात्⟧ (⟦जै० १।२।४⟧), "Because of the pointlessness of the others."

## SBh 0.2.105.5

For thus it is heard: ⟦पूर्णाहुत्या सर्व्वानकामाआप्नोति⟧, "By the full oblation one obtains all desires" (⟦तै- ब्रा० ३।८।१०१५⟧); ⟦पशुरुधयाजी सर्न्वान् लोकानभिजयति⟧, "One who has performed the animal sacrifice conquers all worlds"; and ⟦तरति मृत्युं तरति पाप्मानं तरति ब्रह्महत्यां योऽश्वमेधेन यजते य उ चैनमेव वेद⟧, "He crosses death, crosses sin, crosses the killing of a brahmin, who sacrifices with the horse sacrifice, and who knows it thus" (⟦तै० सं० ५.३.१२.२⟧). There, since all desires would be obtained by the full oblation belonging to the establishment of the fires, the other rites that come later, the Agnihotra and the rest, would be pointless. In the same way, with the performance of the established animal sacrifice, because all worlds are conquered, the Jyotiṣṭoma and the other sacrifices would be pointless. And since the killing of a brahmin and the like are crossed by the mere knowledge of the horse sacrifice, which is gained at the time of study, its performance would be useless. Therefore even the sentences stating a fruit in the other world have no authority.

## SBh 0.2.105.6

Objection: let the sentences stating a fruit not have authority, yet since no conflict is found in prohibitive sentences, let them have authority. Anticipating this, he states the aphorism: ⟦अभागिप्रतिषेधात्⟧ (⟦जै० १. २.५⟧), "Because of a prohibition that has no object." In ⟦न पृथिव्यामग्निश्चेतव्यो नान्तरिक्षे न दिवि⟧ (⟦तै० सं० ५।२।१२।२⟧), "Fire is not to be piled on the earth, nor in the atmosphere, nor in the sky," the atmosphere and the sky are not eligible for the prohibition, since there is no occasion at all for piling there. Then let prohibitions not have authority; but for sentences such as ⟦ववरः प्रावाहणिकामयत⟧ that tell of persons of the past, no conflict is found, so let them have authority. Anticipating this, he states the aphorism: ⟦अनित्यसंयोगात्⟧ (⟦जै० १।२।६⟧), "Because of connection with the non-eternal."

## SBh 0.2.106.1

Since there is connection with a non-eternal thing, in the form of Babara and the like, and since this sentence did not exist before that, it would follow that it is of human origin, like the sentences of Kālidāsa and others. What more need be said? In every way the explanatory passages have no authority at all: this is the opponent's position. He states the established conclusion: ⟦विधिना त्वेकवाक्यत्वात् स्तुत्यर्थेन विधीनां स्युः⟧ (⟦जै० १।२।७⟧), "But because they form one sentence with the injunction, they would be there for the praise of the injunctions."

## SBh 0.2.106.2

The word ⟦तु⟧ ("but") rejects the lack of authority of the explanatory passages. Because explanatory passages such as ⟦वायुर्वै क्षेपिष्ठा⟧, "Vāyu is the swiftest," form one sentence with the injunction ⟦वायव्यं श्वेतमालभेत⟧, "He should offer a white victim to Vāyu" (⟦तै.सं. २ । १।१।१⟧), they have authority in regard to dharma. And it should not be objected that, since the injunctive sentence is complete in its connection of words without needing the explanatory passages, the explanatory passages have no use there. For those explanatory passages would be useful as the praise of injunctions for people who desire a man's engagement in action, and a man who is attracted by praise engages in it.

## SBh 0.2.106.3

Objection: since the explanatory passages are to be disregarded as recited through inattention, what is the use of this effort to make them one sentence with the injunctions? Anticipating this, he says: ⟦तुल्यं च साम्प्रदायिकम्⟧ (⟦जै० १।२।८⟧), "And what comes by tradition is the same."

## SBh 0.2.106.4

Study from the tradition of the teacher, preceded by rules such as avoiding the days when study is forbidden, is what comes by tradition. And that is the same for injunctions and for explanatory passages. Therefore, like the injunctions, the reading of these too is not through inattention. Objection: but unsuitability in the explanatory passages was stated by "And because of conflict with the scripture and with what is seen." Anticipating this, he says: ⟦आप्राप्ता चानुपपत्तिः प्रयोगे हि विरोधः स्याच्छब्दार्थसवप्रयोगभूतस्तस्त्वादुपपद्यते⟧ (⟦जै. १।२।९⟧) **NOTE**{the supplied end of the aphorism is damaged; I read it as śabdārthas tu prayogabhūtas tasmād upapadyate, as translated; is this right?}. "And the unsuitability has not been reached; for there would be conflict in the performance, but the meaning of the words is not part of the performance, so it is suitable."

## SBh 0.2.106.5

In ⟦स्तेन मनः⟧ and the like, unsuitability through conflict with the scripture and so on has been reached **NOTE**{the supplied text says unsuitability ⟦प्राप्ता⟧, "has been reached", while the aphorism just quoted says āprāptā, "not reached"; the supplied word is translated as it stands}, because no performance is stated. For if theft and the like were stated as a performance, there would be conflict with the scripture; but here it is not said as a performance that theft is to be done; only the meaning of the word "theft" is stated. And the meaning of the word is not part of a performance. Therefore, since by the mere statement of the meaning of a word there is no conflict with the scripture, this explanatory passage is quite suitable.

## SBh 0.2.106.6

Objection: what was said, ⟦स्तुत्यर्थेन विधीनां स्युः⟧, "they would be there for the praise of the injunctions," is wrong, because of difference of reference. In ⟦वेतसशाखया चावकाभिश्चाग्नि विकर्षत्यापोवै शान्ता⟧ (⟦तै० सं० ५।४।४।३⟧), "He draws the fire apart with a branch of cane and with avakā plants; the waters are indeed calm," the cane and the avakā are enjoined, while the waters are praised; so there is difference of reference. Anticipating this, he says: ⟦गुणवादस्तु⟧ (⟦जै. १।२।१०⟧), "But it is a statement of a quality."

## SBh 0.2.106.7

The word ⟦तु⟧ ("but") rejects the fault of difference of reference. For here a statement of a quality is intended. Just as in the world Devadatta, whose family is from Kashmir, when the Kashmir lands are being praised, thinks himself praised, so here too, when the waters are praised, the cane and the avakā, being born from the waters, are themselves praised indeed. (Continued in the next unit.)

## SBh 0.2.107.1

… Because the cane and the avakā are born from calm waters, they themselves also are calm, and so they allay what is unfavourable to the sacrificer. A statement of such a [quality] **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦गुह्यस्य⟧ ("secret") is read as guṇasya, "quality"; is this right?} is intended here.

## SBh 0.2.107.2

In "He wept" too, since silver has the form of fallen tears, if silver is given there would be occasion for [weeping] **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦ऐडन⟧ is read as rodana, "weeping"; is this right?} even in the house; therefore, by the prohibition ⟦वर्हिषि रजतं न देयम्⟧, "Silver is not to be given on the sacred grass" (⟦तै०. सं० १।५।१।२⟧), the explanatory passage forms one sentence with the injunction. There the quality, namely the absence of weeping when silver is not given, is intended; and by this quality the injunction, which has the form of preventing the giving of silver, is praised. Although the origin of silver from tears is utterly unreal, still in the way stated the praise of the injunction is accomplished.

## SBh 0.2.107.3

The injunction ⟦यः प्रजाकामः पशुकामः स्थात् स वत प्राजापत्यमजं नूपरमालभेत⟧ (⟦तै०. सं०२|१।१।4,५⟧), "He who desires offspring and cattle should offer to Prajāpati a hornless goat," is praised by the cutting out of his omentum by Prajāpati. For Prajāpati, having cut out even his omentum and offered it into the fire, took for himself the hornless goat that was born from it, and obtained offspring and cattle. Therefore this hornless goat is a provider of offspring and the like. Thus a statement of the quality of the hornless goat is intended here.

## SBh 0.2.107.4

This injunction ⟦आदित्यः प्रायणीयश्चरु⟧ (⟦तै०. सं० ६।१।५|१⟧), "The cooked offering to Āditya is the opening one," is praised by the confusion about the directions, in ⟦दिशो न प्राजानन्⟧, "They did not know the directions." Just as the goddess Aditi here removes even the confusion about the directions and makes a particular direction known, so in the Soma sacrifice, which consists of an assemblage of many kinds of rites, she removes the confusion that is contrary to the performance; what more need be said? Thus a statement of a quality belonging to the deity Aditi is intended here. Whether both the cutting out of his own omentum and the confusion about the directions, merely from settling at the place of sacrifice, are true or not, nothing is lost for us, who accept that the passages are directed to praise. For in the world praise is seen even with a non-existent meaning, as in ⟦शिखा ते वर्धते वत्स गुडच्रीं श्रद्धया पिव⟧, "Your tuft grows, child; drink the gudūcī with faith."

## SBh 0.2.107.5

Now, to the conflict with the scripture that the opponent showed in what he cited, ⟦स्तेनमनोऽनृतवादिनी वाग्⟧, "The mind is a thief; speech speaks untruth," he states the answer in an aphorism: ⟦रूपात् प्रायात्⟧ (⟦जै. १।२।११⟧), "From the form, from what is mostly so."

## SBh 0.2.107.6

To praise this injunction, ⟦हिरण्यं हस्ते भवति अथ गृभ्णाति⟧, "Gold is in the hand; then he takes" (⟦मै०. सं०. ४. ८.२.३⟧), this explanatory passage is spoken. As in the world, in ⟦किमृषिणादेवदश्च एव पूजयितव्यः⟧ **NOTE**{the supplied words ⟦ऽकिमृषिणादेवदश्च⟧ are damaged; I read: "What of the sage? Devadatta alone is to be honoured"; is this right?}, indifference towards the sage is put forward only to praise the honouring of Devadatta, not to prevent the sage from being worthy of honour; so here too, to praise the holding of gold in the hand, thief-nature of the mind and untruth-speaking of speech are put forward. There the meaning of the words is to be construed by way of a statement of a quality. As thieves are of concealed form, so the mind also; thus concealed form is the quality here. Speech mostly speaks untruth; thus being mostly so is the quality here. The hand is not concealed, nor much given to untruth. Therefore holding gold in the hand is excellent: thus it is praised.

## SBh 0.2.107.7

As for the passage ⟦धूम एव अग्नेर्दिवा ददृशे⟧ and the like, cited for conflict with what is seen, he states the answer in an aphorism: ⟦दूरभूयत्तवात्⟧ (⟦जै.१।२।१२⟧), "Because of great distance."

## SBh 0.2.108.1

The two injunctions ⟦अग्निर्ज्योतिर्ज्योतिरग्निः स्वाहेति सायं जुहोति, सूर्यां ज्योतिर्ज्योतिः सूर्य्यः स्वाहेति प्रातरि⟧ (⟦ऐ०.ब्रा०.५।५।६⟧), "In the evening he offers with 'Agni is light, light is Agni, svāhā'; in the morning with 'Sūrya is light, light is Sūrya, svāhā'," are the ones that this explanatory passage serves to praise. Because the flame is not seen by day, therefore the Sūrya mantra alone is to be used in the morning. Because at night only the flame is seen, therefore the Agni mantra is to be used at night and the Sūrya mantra by day. This is the praise of those two mantras. The statement of the non-seeing of smoke and flame has as its ground the quality of great distance. For at a very great distance, on a mountain peak, even trees and the like are not seen distinctly, but there is only an appearance of seeing them, like grass. So too here.

## SBh 0.2.108.2

As for the other passage cited for conflict with what is seen, ⟦न चैतद्विद्यो वयं ब्राह्मणा वा स्मोऽब्राह्मणा वा⟧, "We do not know this, whether we are brahmins or non-brahmins," he states the answer in an aphorism: ⟦अपराधात् कर्तुश् च पुत्रदर्शनम्⟧ (⟦जै० १।२।१३⟧), "Because of an offence, and the son of the begetter is seen."

## SBh 0.2.108.3

This explanatory passage serves to praise the injunction ⟦प्रवरे प्रब्रियमाणे ब्रूयाद् देवाः पितरः⟧, "When the pravara is being chosen, he should say 'Gods, fathers'" (⟦मै० तश्च १।४।११⟧). If the sacrificer were to address the pravara with the mantra beginning ⟦देवाः पितरः⟧, then even a non-brahmin would become a brahmin: thus the addressing is praised. The statement of ignorance, ⟦न चैतद् विद्मः⟧, "We do not know this," is used there with the quality of difficulty of knowing. Where an offence is committed by a woman, there a son is seen even of the begetter, the paramour. Hence, since sons are seen of both the husband and the paramour, it is hard to know what one's own birth is. Because it is used with this intention, there is no conflict with what is seen there. For ⟦न चैतद् विद्मः⟧ is not put forward in order to deny the brahmin-hood of oneself which is seen there.

## SBh 0.2.108.4

As for the other passage cited for conflict with scripture and with what is seen, ⟦को हि तद् वेद यद्यभुष्मिंल्लोकेऽस्ति वा न वा⟧, "Who indeed knows that, whether it exists in yonder world or not," he states the answer in an aphorism: ⟦आकालिकेल्या⟧ (⟦जै० १।२।१४⟧) **NOTE**{the supplied aphorism ⟦आकालिकेल्या⟧ is read as ākālikepsā, "desire for what is later"; the next unit explains ākālika and īpsā; is this right?}.

## SBh 0.2.108.5

⟦दित्त्वतीकाशान् करोति⟧ (⟦तै० सं- ६।१।१।१⟧) is the injunction about the door of the eastern hall **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦दित्त्वतीकाशान्⟧ is unclear and is not translated}. This passage, ⟦को हि तद् वेद⟧ (⟦जै. ब्रा. का. २. १⟧), is the remainder of that injunction. By the avoidance of troubles such as smoke, with a fruit that is perceived, the injunction about the door is praised. But the fruit that consists in the attainment of heaven is ⟦फलमाकालिकम्⟧. ⟦भवमाकालिकं⟧ means "that which belongs to a distant time"; it is not of the present time: this is the meaning. Its ⟦ईप्सा⟧ is the wish to obtain. And that is the reason for the statement of uncertainty in ⟦कोहि तद् वेद⟧. As the history of grandsons, great-grandsons and so on in future time cannot be ascertained, so the attainment of heaven belongs to a future time; thus, through connection with this quality, the statement of uncertainty is made. But the avoidance of smoke and the like is certain, being perceptible: this is the meaning.

## SBh 0.2.108.6

As for the other passage cited for conflict with what is seen, ⟦शौभतेऽस्य सुखं य एवं वेद⟧ (⟦ता. म० ब्रा. २०।१६।६⟧), "His face shines, who knows thus," he states the answer in an aphorism: ⟦विद्याप्रशंसा⟧ (⟦जै० 1.2.15⟧), "Praise of knowledge." This is the remainder of the injunction of the Garga-trirātra. Even the knowledge concerning it is a cause of the beauty of the face, how much more its performance: thus it is praised. As the face becomes beautiful by ornaments of the ear and the like **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦कर्णभारणदिना⟧ is read as karṇabhūṣaṇādinā, "by ear-ornaments and the like"; is this right?} (continued in the next unit),

## SBh 0.2.109.1

so too the face of one who knows thus, expanded by enthusiasm, seems beautified, [and is] **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦क्षिकस्त्रीकृतं⟧ is damaged and is not translated}. Hence, through connection with the quality of resemblance to beauty, it is said ⟦शोभते⟧, "it shines."

## SBh 0.2.109.2

As for the other passage cited for conflict, ⟦आस्य प्रजायां वाजी जायते य एवं वेद⟧ (⟦कृष्‍णयजुर्वेदः. का १. प्रपाठकः ७. 1.7.5⟧), "In his offspring is born a strong one, who knows thus," this too is the remainder of the injunction about addressing the Veda. Here too the praise is to be construed, as before, by the argument "how much more." The son of one who knows, through the father's instruction, becomes learned himself, and then obtains food by accepting gifts. With such a quality in view, ⟦वाजी जायते⟧ (⟦ऋ. ५.१⟧) is said.

## SBh 0.2.109.3

As for the passage cited for the pointlessness of the others, ⟦पूर्णाहुत्या सर्व्वानकामान्दअचाप्नोति⟧, "By the full oblation he obtains all desires," he states the answer in an aphorism: ⟦सर्व्वत्वमाधिकारिकम्⟧ (⟦जै० १।२।१६⟧), "The 'all' belongs to the context."

## SBh 0.2.109.4

This is the remainder of the injunction ⟦पूर्णाहुति जुहुयाद्⟧, "He should offer the full oblation." Since it is the cause of obtaining all desires, this oblation is excellent: thus it is praised. As, in "All brahmins are to be fed," the "all" refers to the brahmins who have come to one's own house.

## SBh 0.2.109.5

In the same way, the "all" is to be seen as referring only to the fruit that is possible in the matter in hand, when the rite is complete in its parts through the full oblation. When the full oblation is absent, the rite consisting of the establishment of the fires is defective in a part. And that defect is made good by the full oblation: this is one desire. When that is made good, the Āhavanīya and the other fires become fit for rites such as the Agnihotra: this is another desire. And by those rites this and that fruit is obtained: this is a further desire. If it is said that such obtaining of all desires exists in the other oblations also, then let it exist. What is lost? By that there is certainly no harm at all to the praise of the full oblation.

## SBh 0.2.109.6

Objection: since the full oblation is a subsidiary, the statement of the fruit connected with it may be praise, as an explanatory passage, because this was decided by the aphorism ⟦द्रव्यसस्कारकर्म्मसुपरार्थत्वात्फलश्रूतिरर्थवादःश्⟧ (⟦जै०४।३।१⟧), "In matters of substance, preparation and rite, since they are for another's sake, the statement of a fruit is an explanatory passage." But the animal-sacrifice sentence enjoins a rite, and the conquest of all worlds is its principal fruit; so the pointlessness of the others cannot be avoided. Anticipating this, he states the answer in an aphorism: ⟦फलस्य कर्मनिष्पत्तेस्तेषां लोकवत् परिमाणतः सारतो वा फलविशेषः स्यात्⟧ (⟦जै० १।२।१७⟧), "Since the fruit is accomplished by the rite, there would be a difference of fruit among them, as in the world, by quantity or by excellence."

## SBh 0.2.109.7

A fruit of the form of conquering one of the worlds, earth, atmosphere or sky, is accomplished by the animal-sacrifice rite. And of those fruits, earth and the rest, by another rite an excess of measure or an excellence is brought about. Hence there would be a difference of fruit, and so there is no pointlessness. ⟦लोकवद⟧ ("as in the world") gives examples for what has been stated. As in the world, having sold rice measured by the khārī for one niṣka and then buying again with another niṣka, there is an excess of quantity; or as with one niṣka mere cloth is obtained, but with two niṣkas excellent fine silk. So an excess of enjoyment, or an excellence of enjoyment, is to be seen by another rite. Also the killing of a brahmin, when it is mental and slight, is crossed by mere knowledge; when it is bodily and great, by the horse sacrifice. Thus there is no pointlessness of the others.

## SBh 0.2.110.1

As for what was cited, ⟦नान्तरिक्षे न दिवि⟧, as a prohibition without occasion, and likewise ⟦बबरः प्रावाहीणः⟧ as connection with a non-eternal, for both he states the answer in an aphorism: ⟦अर्न्त्येयोर्यथोक्तम्⟧ (⟦जै० १।२।१८⟧), "For the last two, as stated."

## SBh 0.2.110.2

For the last two examples the answer is to be seen as the one stated before. The explanatory passage in the form of a censure of piling in the atmosphere and the like is the remainder of the injunction ⟦हिरण्य निधाय चेतव्यम्⟧, "Having laid down gold, it is to be piled" (⟦तै० सं० ५।२।७।१⟧).

## SBh 0.2.110.3

Hence here the answer already given applies: ⟦स्तुत्यर्थेन विधीनां स्युः⟧ (⟦जै०१,२.७⟧). Since there is no occasion for piling in the atmosphere, the censure of it is a restatement of what is always established. By that too the injunction can be praised, because, by the restatement of something established always, the animal-sacrifice injunctions are praised through the swiftness of Vāyu **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦पशुविष्ट्व्येः⟧ is damaged; I read it as paśuvidheḥ, "of the animal-sacrifice injunction"; is this right?}. In ⟦बबरः प्रावाहणिरकामयत⟧ too, a certain non-eternal person, a human named Babara, is not intended, but Vāyu, endowed with the sound "babara" and given to blowing intensely, is the eternal thing intended in practical use. This answer was stated in the last section of the first chapter's first part.

## SBh 0.2.110.4

Therefore, because of the scholarship [brought to bear] **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦पण्डितत्वाद्⟧ means "because of learnedness"; I translate it as it stands, though "because they have been refuted" may be meant; the following ⟦द्वै⟧ is not translated} on the possible faults, the explanatory passages are not without authority. On this there are summary verses: ⟦वायुर्वा इत्येवमादेरर्थवादख्य मानता⟧, "Is the explanatory passage beginning 'Vāyu is …' an authority? ⟦न विधेयेऽस्ति धर्मे किं किं वासौ तत्र विद्यते⟧, Is there not [authority] in the dharma to be enjoined, or what is there? Because of the exhaustion of the mutual need ⟦मिथोऽपेधापरिक्षयात्⟧ of the words of injunction and explanatory passage, ⟦नास्त्येकवाक्यता धर्मे प्रामाण्यं सम्भवेत् कुतः⟧, there is no unity of sentence in regard to dharma; how could authority arise? ⟦विध्यर्थवादौ साकाह्वौ प्राशस्त्वपुरुषार्थयोः⟧, Injunction and explanatory passage have mutual expectancy as to praise and human aim; ⟦तेनैकवाक्यता तस्माद् वादानां धर्ममानता⟧, hence there is unity of sentence, and therefore the explanatory passages are an authority in regard to dharma" (⟦जै०न्या०मा० १।२।१⟧).

## SBh 0.2.110.5

Thus, since there is no cause for lack of authority in the portions of mantra, injunction and explanatory passage that exist in the Veda, and since the authority of those that convey knowledge is accepted as holding of itself, the authority of the whole Veda is established.

## SBh 0.2.110.6

Objection: even so, because the Veda is of human origin, it would be without authority, like a sentence meant to deceive. And Jaimini, in the first section, stated the human origin as the opponent's position in the aphorism: ⟦वेदांश्चैके सन्निकर्षं पुरुषाख्या⟧ (⟦जै० १।१।२७⟧), "And some hold that the Vedas are of recent connection, because of the naming of persons."

## SBh 0.2.110.7

Some disputants hold that the Vedas are of recent connection. The word ⟦च⟧ ("and") is for including works such as the Raghuvaṃśa composed by Kālidāsa and others. These are included here as examples: as the Raghuvaṃśa and the others are recent, so too are the Vedas. The Vedas are not without beginning; for that very reason persons are named as the authors of the Veda. As in the case of ⟦वैयासिकं⟧, "Vyāsa's," the Bhārata, and the Rāmāyaṇa of Vālmīki, Vyāsa and others are named as authors of the Bhārata and the rest, so, since Kaṭha and others are named as the authors of this and that branch of the Veda, saying "the Kāṭhaka, the Kauthuma **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦कौननुम⟧ is damaged; I read Kauthuma}, the Taittirīya," the Vedas are of human origin.

## SBh 0.2.111.1

Objection: even if the Vedas are eternal, the names Kāṭhaka and the like could arise because Kaṭha and others were propagators of the tradition, like a teacher. Anticipating this, he states another argument in an aphorism: ⟦अनित्यदर्शनाच्च⟧ (⟦जै० १ ।१।२८⟧), "And because the non-eternal is seen."

## SBh 0.2.111.2

Non-eternal beings, subject to birth and death, Babara and others, are heard of in the Veda: ⟦बबरः प्रावाहणिरकामयत⟧, "Babara Prāvāhaṇi desired" (⟦तै० सं ७।१।१०।२⟧), and ⟦कुसुरुविन्द औद्दालकिरकायमत⟧, "Kusuruvinda Auddālaki desired" (⟦तै० सं ७।२।२।१⟧). That being so, since before Babara and the others [the Veda] did not exist, the Vedas are non-eternal. The disputed Vedic sentence is of human origin, because it is a sentence, like the sentences of Kālidāsa and others. The word ⟦च⟧ ("and") is for including inferences of this kind **NOTE**{the supplied words ⟦वद् क्त्याद्यनुमान⟧ are damaged; I read vad ityādyanumāna, "such inferences"; is this right?}. He states the established conclusion in the aphorism at ⟦जै० १।१। २९⟧: "But the priority of the word has been stated."

## SBh 0.2.111.3

The word ⟦तु⟧ ("but") rejects the non-eternity of the Vedas. The priority of the word, in the form of the Veda, to persons such as Kaṭha, that is, its beginninglessness, has been stated by the earlier aphorisms themselves. In the aphorism ⟦औत्पत्तिकस्तु शाब्दस्य अर्थेन सम्बन्धः⟧ (⟦जै० १।5⟧), by the word ⟦औत्पत्तिक⟧ the eternity of all words, of the Vedas, of their meanings and of the relation between both was declared, and it was established by the two following sections, the one on the word and the one on the sentence. Then what is the explanation of the names Kāṭhaka and the like? Anticipating this, he states the answer: it is explained by the propagation of the tradition. ⟦आख्या प्रवचनात्⟧ (⟦जै० १।१।३०⟧), "The naming is from the teaching."

## SBh 0.2.111.4

Let this be the explanation of the naming. But what is the answer to the seeing of the non-eternal, Babara and the like, that was stated after that? Anticipating this, he states the answer in an aphorism: ⟦परं तु श्रुतिसामान्यमात्रम्⟧ (⟦जै० १।१।३१⟧), "But the other is only a similarity of words."

## SBh 0.2.111.5

What is the "other", Babara and the like, is only a similarity of words. A human named Babara is not intended here. For what can be spoken of here is Vāyu, who has the sound "babara" and whose nature is to blow.

## SBh 0.2.111.6

Objection: somewhere in the Veda **NOTE**{the supplied words ⟦क्वचिदेकं श्रूयते⟧ are translated as they stand ("something is heard"); is this right?} it is heard: ⟦चनस्पतयः सत्त्रमत्सत⟧, "The trees sat through a sacrificial session," and ⟦सर्दाः सक्त्यासत⟧, "The serpents sat …". There, because trees are insensible, and serpents, though sentient, are without learning, their performance of it is not possible. Hence, because it is like the sentences of a mad person or a child, such as ⟦ज रद्गवो गायति मद्रकाणि⟧, "The old bull sings pleasant songs," the Veda was made by someone. Anticipating this, he states the answer in an aphorism: ⟦कृते चाविनियोगः स्यात् कर्मणः समत्वात्⟧ (⟦जै० १।१।३२⟧), "And if it were made, there would be no application, because the rite is the same."

## SBh 0.2.112.1

If the Jyotiṣṭoma sentence and the like were made by some person, then, once that sentence is made, the application of the Jyotiṣṭoma as a means to heaven would not stand, since a relation of means and end cannot be known by a person. But the application is heard: ⟦ज्योतिष्टोमेन स्वर्गकामो यजेन्⟧, "One who desires heaven should sacrifice with the Jyotiṣṭoma." And it is not like the sentence of a mad person, because, as in an ordinary injunctive sentence, the bhāvanā is understood, furnished with three parts: what is to be brought about, the means, and the manner of doing it. For in the world, in the injunction "He should feed brahmins," when there is the expectation "what, by what, how," it is said: aiming at satisfaction, with rice as the substance, by the way of serving vegetables, soups and the like. Likewise in the Jyotiṣṭoma injunction too, when it is said: aiming at heaven, with Soma as the substance, by the way of helping through the subsidiaries such as the consecration, how could it be like the sentence of a mad person?

## SBh 0.2.112.2

The sentence about the session of trees and the like is also not like that, because that session rite is equal to the Jyotiṣṭoma and the rest. Those who know the rule say: "That word whose purport lies in something has that as its meaning." Since the Jyotiṣṭoma sentence and the like are injunctive, their purport lies in performance; since the sentence about the session of trees and the like is an explanatory passage, its purport lies in praise. And that can be made even with what does not exist. "Even insensible and unlearned ones performed the session; how much more sentient, learned brahmins": thus the praise of the session. The word ⟦च⟧ ("and") adds the refutation, by the non-finding of an author, of the reason, being a sentence, stated by the opponent **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦कडनुपलम्भेन⟧ is read as kartṛ-anupalambhena, "by the non-finding of an author"; is this right?}. Therefore the Veda is not of human origin.

## SBh 0.2.112.3

Here are two summary verses: ⟦पौरुषेय न वा वेदवाक्यं स्यात् पौरुषेयता⟧, "Is the Vedic sentence of human origin or not? It would be of human origin, ⟦काठकादिसमाख्यानाद् वाक्यत्वाच्चान्यवाक्यवत्⟧, because of the naming Kāṭhaka and the like, and because of its being a sentence, like other sentences. ⟦समाख्यानं प्रवचनाद् वाक्यत्वं तु पराहतम्⟧ The naming is from the teaching, and the sentencehood is refuted; ⟦तत्कर्त्रनुपलम्भेन ख्यात्ततोऽपौरुषेयता⟧ because its maker is not found, therefore it is of non-human origin" (⟦जै०न्या०मा०१।२।८⟧).

## SBh 0.2.112.4

Objection: the revered Bādarāyaṇa has laid down in an aphorism that the Veda has Brahman alone as its source: ⟦शास्त्रयोनित्वाद⟧ (⟦वे. १।१|3⟧). The meaning of the aphorism is: because Brahman is the cause of the scriptures, the Ṛgveda and the rest, Brahman is omniscient. Reply: that is childish. By this the [human origin] **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦चेकेवयत्वं⟧ is damaged; I read it as paurūṣeyatvam, "human origin"; is this right?} does not follow, because the Veda was not made by men. Having in view such non-human origin, Bādarāyaṇa himself, in the section on the gods, laid down that in practical use the Veda is eternal like space and the like: ⟦अत एव च नित्यत्वम्⟧, "And for this very reason, eternity" (⟦वे- सू- १।२।२९⟧). And there are a Śruti and a Smṛti on this: ⟦वाचा विरूपनित्यया⟧, "by speech, the Virūpa, the eternal," is the Śruti (⟦ऋ०८।७५।६⟧); ⟦अनादिनिधना नित्या वागुत्सृष्टा स्वयम्भुवा⟧, "Speech without beginning or end, eternal, was sent forth by the Self-existent," is the Smṛti. Therefore, since no suspicion of a fault of the maker arises, the authority of the Veda, which consists of mantra and brāhmaṇa, is established without hindrance.

## SBh 0.2.112.5

Objection: it is not right that the Veda consists of mantra and brāhmaṇa, because the nature of those two cannot be determined. Not so. It is decided in the seventh and eighth sections of the first part of the second chapter.

## SBh 0.2.113.1

He introduces the seventh section: ⟦अहे बुज्जिय मन्त्रं म इति मन्त्रस्य लक्षणम्⟧, "In 'Ahe budhniya, my mantra …', is there a definition of mantra?" ⟦नास्त्यस्ति वास्य नास्त्येतदव्यास्यादेरवारणात्⟧, "Is there one, or not? There is not, since non-pervasion and the like cannot be warded off." ⟦याज्ञिकानां समाख्यानं लक्षण दोषवर्जितम्⟧, "The usage of the sacrificers is the definition free of fault." "They use the word 'mantra' for ⟦ऽनुष्ठानख्यारकादौ⟧, what recalls the performance and the like" (⟦जै०न्या०मा०२।१।७⟧).

## SBh 0.2.113.2

At the establishment of the fires this is recited: ⟦अहे बुध्निय मन्त्रं मे गोपाय⟧, "Ahe budhniya, guard my mantra" (⟦तै० ब्रा० १।२।१।८६⟧).

## SBh 0.2.113.3

There a definition of mantra does not exist, since non-pervasion and over-pervasion cannot be prevented. If it is said that a mantra is what expresses the enjoined meaning, there is non-pervasion, because the mantra ⟦वसन्ताय कपिञ्जलानालभते⟧, "He offers kapiñjala birds to spring" (⟦वा०स० २४।२०⟧), has the form of an injunction. If it is said that a mantra is what is for [mere] **NOTE**{the supplied letters ⟦मननर्द्ध्वेहण⟧ are damaged and are not translated} thinking, there is over-pervasion into the brāhmaṇa. Likewise the definitions "a mantra ends in a verb" **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦एवमत्तिपदान्तो⟧ is read as evaṃ tiṅpadānto; is this right?} and "a mantra ends in the first person" and the like each fail to cover the others. If it is said so, we reply: not so; because the usage of the sacrificers is a faultless definition. And that usage shows the mantra-hood of what recalls the performance and the rest: ⟦उरु प्रथस्थ⟧ (⟦तै० सं- ६।२।७।३⟧) and the like are what recall the performance; ⟦अग्निमीडे पुरोहितम⟧ (⟦ऋ.1.1.1⟧) and the like are of the form of praise **NOTE**{the supplied word ⟦ण्ठतिरूपाः⟧ is read as stutirūpāḥ, "of the form of praise"; is this right?}; ⟦इषेत्वा⟧ (⟦वा- सं- १ ।१⟧) and the like are those that end in ⟦त्वा⟧; ⟦अग्न आयाहि वीतय⟧ (⟦सा० सं- १।१⟧) and the like are those that carry an invocation.

## SBh 0.2.113.4

⟦अग्नीदग्नीन्विहर⟧ (⟦तै० सं- ६।३।१।२⟧) and the like are of the form of a command. ⟦अधः स्विदासीदुपरीख्यदासीद्⟧ (⟦ऋ० १०.१२६.५⟧) and the like are of the form of inquiry. ⟦अम्बेऽम्बिके अम्बालिके न मा नयति कश्चन⟧ (⟦बा- सं० २३।१८⟧) and the like are of the form of lamentation. ⟦पृच्छामि त्वा परमन्तं⟧ ⟦पृथिव्या⟧ (⟦वा० सं० २३।६१⟧) and the like are of the form of question. ⟦वेदिमाहुः परमन्तं पृथिव्याः⟧ (⟦वा २३.६२⟧) and the like are of the form of answer. Others too are to be given as examples in the same way. Among such very different kinds there is, apart from the usage, no other common property of which a definition could be stated. And the use of a definition has been shown by earlier teachers: ⟦ऋषयोऽपि पदार्थानां नान्तं यान्ति पृथक्त्वशः⟧, "Even sages do not reach the end of things by taking them one by one; ⟦लक्षणेनतु सिद्धानायन्ते यान्ति विपश्चितः⟧ **NOTE**{the supplied second half-verse is damaged and is not translated}." Therefore for the experienced the usage, "this is a mantra," is the definition.
