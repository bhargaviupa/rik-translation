Sūkta 63

## Introduction

A brief account of Mitra and Varuṇa.

In the eleven sūktas of this Maṇḍala, from the 62nd to the 72nd, Mitra and Varuṇa are praised. A brief account of these deities is given here.

The companionship of Mitra and Varuṇa is well known. Very often both are praised together. Often there is praise addressed to Varuṇa individually. But it is rare for Mitra to be praised without Varuṇa. As the commentator explains, Mitra is the presiding deity of the day and Varuṇa

### Printed p. 2

of the night; if this is indeed the fact, then the companionship of the two is natural. Commenting on the mantra Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-89-3, Sāyaṇa writes आहरभिमानिनं देवं । मित्रं वा अहरिति श्रुतेः (*āharabhimāninaṃ devaṃ | mitraṃ vā ahariti śruteḥ*); since there is a Vedic sentence showing the identity of Mitra with the day (ahas), he states that Mitra is the deity presiding over the day, and that Varuṇa is known as the one presiding over the night. The word "Varuṇa" is formed from the root "vṛ", which has the sense of covering. This deity binds the wicked with his noose, and the night belongs to Varuṇa. वृणोति पापकृतः स्वकीयैः पाशैरावृणोतीति रात्र्यभिमानिदेवो वरुणः । श्रूयते च-वारुणी रात्रिः । (तै. ब्रा. १-७-१०-१ इति) (*vṛṇoti pāpakṛtaḥ svakīyaiḥ pāśairāvṛṇotīti rātryabhimānidevo varuṇaḥ | śrūyate ca-vāruṇī rātriḥ | (tai. brā. 1-7-10-1 iti)*)

Likewise, in stating the meaning of the mantra 1-141-9, he gives the same etymologies and adds that Aryaman is the one who stands between this pair of deities (आर्यमा उभयोर्मध्यवर्ति देवः (*āryamā ubhayormadhyavarti devaḥ*)). In the explanation of the mantra 1-90-1 (आर्यमा अहोरात्रविभागस्य कर्ता सूर्यः (*āryamā ahorātravibhāgasya kartā sūryaḥ*)) he indicates that the Sun, who makes the division of day and night, is called Aryaman. Again, in the Bhāṣya on the mantra 2-38-8 the nature of Varuṇa is described thus:

योनिं स्थानं निमिषि निमेषे सवितुरस्तमये सति विश्रमार्थं प्राणिभ्यः प्रयच्छति । वरुणस्य रात्रेर्निर्वाहकत्वात् । (*yoniṃ sthānaṃ nimiṣi nimeṣe saviturastamaye sati viśramārthaṃ prāṇibhyaḥ prayacchati | varuṇasya rātrernirvāhakatvāt |*)

Varuṇa is the one who, after sunset, provides living beings with a place of rest. He is the manager of all the affairs of the night.

In 1-35-1, Mitra and Varuṇa are invoked together with the deities Agni, Savitṛ and Night: "For the accomplishment of our welfare I invoke Agni with prayer. For our help I call Mitra and Varuṇa. I call Night, who gives rest to the world." Thus they are praised in company with the other deities. In some places it is also stated that the Sun himself is what the word Mitra denotes (मित्रशब्दस्य सूर‍्यवाचित्वात् (*mitraśabdasya sūryavācitvāt*) Tai. Saṃ. Bhā. 1-8-16-1).**NOTE** In the same context, giving the etymology of the word Varuṇa as well, he says वरुणशब्दस्य अंधकारवदावर्तकवाचित्वात् । (*varuṇaśabdasya aṃdhakāravadāvartakavācitvāt |*); that is, Varuṇa is known by this name because by nature he spreads everywhere like darkness. It is also said: मैत्रं वा अहः । वारुणी रात्रिः ॥ (*maitraṃ vā ahaḥ | vāruṇī rātriḥ ‖*) (Tai. Brā. 1-7-10-1)

न वा इदं दिवा न नक्तमासीदव्याकृतं ते देवा मित्रावरुणावब्रुवन्निदं नो विवासयतमिति । (*na vā idaṃ divā na naktamāsīdavyākṛtaṃ te devā mitrāvaruṇāvabruvannidaṃ no vivāsayatamiti |*) (Tai. Saṃ. 6-4-8-3)

मित्रो अहरजनयद्वरुणो रात्रिं ॥ (*mitro aharajanayadvaruṇo rātriṃ ‖*) (Kṛ. Saṃ. 6-4-8-3)**NOTE**

### Printed p. 3

In the beginning there was no division of the world into day and night; everything was unmanifest. Then the gods said to Mitra and Varuṇa, "Make for us the division of day and night"; whereupon Mitra made the day and Varuṇa the night. So the passage of the Taittirīya Saṃhitā declares.

In the Ṛk-Saṃhitā, in the praise of Varuṇa:

स क्षपः परिषस्वजे न्यु १ स्रो मायया दधे स विश्वं परि दर्शतः । (*sa kṣapaḥ pariṣasvaje nyu 1 sro māyayā dadhe sa viśvaṃ pari darśataḥ |*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-41-3)

This same Varuṇa, of beautiful form, embraces the night. By his power of māyā he creates the day and establishes everything in its own appointed place and time; so he is extolled.

In the Bhāṣya on Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-87-1:

अस्तं गच्छन् सूर्य एव वरुण इत्युच्यते । स हि स्वगमनेन रात्रीर्ज नयति (*astaṃ gacchan sūrya eva varuṇa ityucyate | sa hi svagamanena rātrīrja nayati*)

Sāyaṇa explains that the Sun, when he goes to his setting, is himself called Varuṇa, and that by his own going he creates the night.

अयं वै लोको मित्रः । असौ वरुणः । (*ayaṃ vai loko mitraḥ | asau varuṇaḥ |*) (Śa. Brā. 12-9-2-12)

The Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa declares that this earth is Mitra and the heavenly world is Varuṇa. Thus the description of their qualities takes many forms.

## The excellent qualities of Mitra and Varuṇa

The special qualities of Mitra and Varuṇa, as they are described in the Ṛk-Saṃhitā, are briefly set out here. Of these two deities, Varuṇa is praised as one who can at times come within the sight of the sacrificer.

"I have seen Varuṇa, who is visible to all, with my own eyes. I have also seen with my own eyes his chariot standing upon the earth." (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-25-18)

"When I obtained the sight of Varuṇa, his splendour had a radiance equal to the splendour of Agni." (7-88-2)

"Varuṇa, of firm resolve and of great nature, exercising lordship, sits at ease in his dwelling-place." (1-25-10)

"While he stands clad in a golden mantle and exercises lordship, his attendants, or else other deities, stand around him on every side." (1-25-13)

### Printed p. 4

"The sacrificers, the performers of the sacrifice, who enter the house of Varuṇa and worship him face to face: that house has a thousand doors." (7-85-5)

"In the firm house with a thousand supporting pillars, the radiant Varuṇa dwells together with Mitra. Both are of unobstructed splendour." (राजाना (*rājānā*); अहृणीयमाना (*ahṛṇīyamānā*)) (2-41-5; 5-62-6)

"These two, Mitra and Varuṇa, lords of the world, mount an excellent chariot, drawn by horses, which at sunrise takes a golden colour and at sunset changes into the form of iron, and they set out on their journey." (5-62-8)

"Mounting this chariot and travelling in the highest atmosphere, both of them survey all the heaven and earth completely." (5-62-4, 8; 5-63-1)

"Varuṇa has wide-spreading eyes; therefore he is far-sighted." (1-25-5, 16; 8-90-2)**NOTE**

"This same Varuṇa is thousand-eyed (सहस्रचक्षस् (*sahasracakṣas*))." (7-34-18)**NOTE**

In some places Varuṇa is praised as one whose messenger is golden-hued (हिरण्यपक्षं वरुणस्य दूतं (*hiraṇyapakṣaṃ varuṇasya dūtaṃ*)) (10-123-6).

In still other places the Sun is described as the eye of Mitra and Varuṇa (चक्षुर्मित्रस्य वरुणस्य (*cakṣurmitrasya varuṇasya*)) (1-1-5-1; 7-63-1; 7-61-1; 10-37-1).**NOTE**

Aryaman, who belongs to the very group of the Ādityas, together with Mitra and Varuṇa, is described in many places as "सूरचक्षसः (*sūracakṣasaḥ*)" (for example, 7-66-10).

"Mitra and Varuṇa have hands fit for the most skilful action."

"Varuṇa is designated in many places by the epithet 'Rājā' (king). He is the lord of gods, men and the rest. He is the sovereign of the whole world." (1-24-7, 8; 4-1-2; 5-40-7; 7-64-1; 10-103-9; 10-173-5; 10-132-4)

"त्वं विश्वेषां वरुणासि राजा ये च देवा आसुर ये च मर्ताः (*tvaṃ viśveṣāṃ varuṇāsi rājā ye ca devā āsura ye ca martāḥ*)" (2-27-10)

"Varuṇa is the king of all the worlds (विश्वस्य भुवनस्य राजा (*viśvasya bhuvanasya rājā*))." (5-85-3)

"Moreover, he is the emperor (सम्राट् (*samrāṭ*)) of all beings, moving and unmoving." (7-87-6; 1-25-10; 2-28-6; 5-85-1; 6-68-9; 8-42-1)

"Varuṇa's lordship does not depend on the help of others; he has independent lordship (स्वराट् (*svarāṭ*))." (2-28-1)

"Just as the epithets 'Rājā', 'Samrāṭ' and the like are applied to Varuṇa individually, so there are also many occasions on which they are applied to Mitra and Varuṇa together." (1-71-9; 1-76-1-4; 1-137-1; 5-62-6; 5-63-2, 3, 5; 5-65-2; 5-68-2; 7-64-2; 8-23-30; 8-25-4, 7, 8; 8-101-2; 10-65-5)

Power, kṣatra-vigour, lordship and similar extraordinary qualities are praised as the special qualities of Mitra and Varuṇa. Together with the Ādityas, Mitra and Varuṇa are known as Kṣatriyas. (1-24-6; 1-25-5; 1-136-1; 5-66-2; 5-67-1; 5-68-1, 7; 6-49-1; 6-51-10; 6-67-5, 6; 7-34-11; 7-64-2; 8-25-8; 8-67-1; 8-100-2)

Because of these qualities they are also designated by the epithets "Rudras" and "Asuras" (असुरा तानार्या (*asurā tānāryā*)). Though they have divine qualities, they are terrible. (5-70-2, 3; 7-36-2; 8-25-4; 8-27-20; 7-65-2)

The epithet "Asura", which denotes divine power, is applied more often to Varuṇa. (1-24-14; 2-27-10; 5-85-5, 6; 8-42-1) It is also used of other deities in some places.

More than all the other epithets, the word "Māyā" indicates the most extraordinary quality of Varuṇa. In some places Varuṇa is denoted by the word "Māyin" itself. (6-48-14; 7-28-4; 10-90-10; 11-147-5)**NOTE** In one or two contexts both Mitra and Varuṇa are addressed as "Māyā". (3-61-7; 5-63-4)

By this Asura power and by this power of māyā both of them send down rain, and both are of firm ordinances (dhṛtavrata).

## The individual qualities and acts of Varuṇa

All the acts of Varuṇa relate to the working of the universe. Endowed with boundless intelligence, Varuṇa made heaven and earth and is their upholder; and as lord of all the worlds he has taken possession of them all. These ordinances of Varuṇa are altogether extraordinary. (8-42-1)

The three worlds, the heavenly world, the earth and the atmosphere, all depend on this Varuṇa, and all of them are contained in him. (7-87-5)

### Printed p. 6

This same Varuṇa has caused the Āditya, who is of a revolving nature and of golden form, to shine in the atmosphere. (5-85-2; 6-70 1; 7-86-1; 7-87-6; 8-41-4, 5, 10)

The wind, which pervades the whole atmosphere and makes a deep sound, is the breath of Varuṇa. (7-87-2)

This Varuṇa has made an endless path for the Sun's journey. For the rivers, which flow obedient to his command, he has dug channels. (1-24-8; 2-28-4; 7-87-1)

By the skill of his intelligence the rivers, though they ceaselessly and without break pour their waters into the oceans, cannot fill them and make them overflow. As all the commands of that Varuṇa are firm, so they are unobstructed and not to be transgressed. (5-85-6; 3-54-98)**NOTE**

As mountains stand firm, all these things stand resting on Varuṇa alone. (2-25-8; 1-25-6, 10; 1-44-14; 1-141-9; 2-1-4; 8-25-2)

By the ordinances of this Varuṇa, whose ordinances are firm, the stars shine at night. The radiant moon also shines by the greatness of his ordinances. And this moon and the stars lose their light by day. (1-24-10)

Neither the birds that fly in the atmosphere nor the rivers that flow without break can know the power of this Varuṇa or the force of his wrath. (1-24-6)

His messengers alone, however, can know fully the conditions of the two worlds. (7-87-3; 1-24-13; 6-67-5)

He knows the flights of the birds that travel in the atmosphere, the courses of the ships that move on the oceans, the paths of the winds that reach the most distant regions, and all the affairs of past, future and present time. (1-25-7)

Without the help of Varuṇa no living being can even wink an eye. (2-28-6)

The true dealings of men and their untrue dealings he sees directly, understands, and gives the fruits that are due. (7-49-7)

Varuṇa teaches the secrets of truth to the sage Vasiṣṭha. (7-87-4)

But he does not disclose his secret truth, nor that of Mitra, to one who is dull of mind and without discernment.
