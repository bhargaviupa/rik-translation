# Prompt for the Vol 25 session: Kannada text from the scans by agents, then translation by agents (paste into a new Claude Code session on this repo; Sonnet is enough)

**UPDATE (Nov 26 2026): read `docs/SCAN_PIPELINE.md` first and follow it where it differs from this file.** Read **3 pages per agent** (not 6), add its rules to the transcription prompt (copy the print's letters, no inferred head digits, no `**`, no `sed`), run the free checks after every batch, and after the volume run `python3 tools/rv/scan_repair.py init/step1/crops/vote/rebuild` (OCR, enlarged crops, two independent readers, vote). Vol 25 and Vol 26 part 2 show what the repair costs when the first pass is careless.

You are the dispatcher for **Volume 25** of the Rigveda translation (repo `bhargaviupa/rik-translation`). **No machine-read text of Vol 25 exists**, so this session makes the Kannada text itself from the page scans, with agents that read the page images, **skipping the Saṃhitā, Pada and Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya blocks** (the user's decision of Nov 24 2026: those three layers come from the Wikisource index, where the machine readers made them worst). Then it translates by agents exactly as Vol 24 is made. Nothing here calls the API: agents read the images with the Read tool; no money is spent. One writer, one volume. **Never touch `vol22/`, `vol23/` or `vol24/`.** Read `CLAUDE.md`, the last ten entries of `docs/LESSONS.md`, `docs/prompts/VOL24_AGENT_PROMPT.md` (the translation loop, which you will follow in Phase 2) and `vol21/STATUS.md`.

**What is known about reading scans** (do not forget it): the old scan-based volumes had about a quarter of their numbers wrong and many `[?]` marks (Vol 13: 1,482 `[?]` on 517 of 653 pages); a language model's transcription of Vol 22 got only 51–60% of the words of its Saṃhitā and Pada blocks right (measured against Wikisource), with letters of other scripts in the text, while its Kannada prose looked fine to the user. The accuracy of reading the prose from scans by Sonnet agents has **not been measured**: Phase 1 measures it before anything else is spent.

## Start (once)

Never force-push; no pull request; `main` only when the user says so. `pip install indic-transliteration rapidfuzz openpyxl pillow numpy`; `python3 tools/rv/ws_index.py build` (builds the Wikisource index from `refs/wikisource/raw`; no network).
2. Ask the user to put the scan PDF at `vol25/scans/Rig_Vol25.pdf` (the folder is not committed). Look at it: `pdfinfo`, `pdfimages -list -f 1 -l 3` (the PDF page size of these scans is wrong: the page images are 300 ppi, 2304×3456; `scan_pilot.py` renders at the image's own width). Find by eye (render a few pages at low resolution and read them) the cover, the title page, the contents page ("ವಿಷಯಾನುಕ್ರಮಣಿಕೆ") and **the PDF page where printed p. 1 begins** (for Vol 22 the contents page was PDF 22 and the first sūkta began at the next page).
3. **Scope from the contents page:** which Maṇḍala.Sūkta numbers the volume holds (the contents lists each sūkta and its page; a sūkta number or a preface that names a range can be misread: the contents page and the Wikisource list of sūktas decide). Tell the user the scope and ask them to confirm it before reading further.

## Phase 1: the Kannada text from the scans
**Render** with `python3 tools/rv/scan_pilot.py render 25 vol25/scans/Rig_Vol25.pdf FIRST LAST` (no API): each page becomes two halves, `vol25/work/scan_pilot/p-NNNNa.jpg` (top) and `…b.jpg` (bottom), 1568 px wide, cut at the emptiest line near the middle.

**The transcription agent** (Agent tool, `model: sonnet`, `subagent_type: general-purpose`, foreground, one at a time; **6 pages per agent**). Its prompt, exactly:

> You transcribe six consecutive scanned pages (PDF pages FIRST–LAST) of Vol 25 of a 1950s Kannada edition of the Ṛgveda (Sāyaṇa's Sanskrit commentary with a Kannada commentary). The images are in vol25/work/scan_pilot/: for each page two files, p-NNNNa.jpg (the TOP half) and p-NNNNb.jpg (the BOTTOM half of the same page). Read the images with the Read tool, both halves of a page, and write ONE file per page, vol25/work/scan_pages/pNNNN.md, in the page's reading order, in Kannada script exactly as printed. You do not translate. Rules:
> - First line: the running head exactly as printed (title and page number) on a line of its own. Headings (sūkta, Ṛk number, labels such as ಪ್ರತಿಪದಾರ್ಥ, ಭಾವಾರ್ಥ, ವಿಶೇಷವಿಷಯಗಳು) as printed, one per line.
> - The Sanskrit blocks under the labels ಸಂಹಿತಾಪಾಠಃ, ಪದಪಾಠಃ and ಸಾಯಣಭಾಷ್ಯಂ are NOT read: write the label line, then one line `[[block: samhita]]`, `[[block: pada]]` or `[[block: bhashya]]`. A Sanskrit quotation inside the Kannada commentary is transcribed as printed.
> - Numbers and references (sūtra numbers, verse numbers, page numbers) exactly as printed, in Kannada digits; look-alike digits (೩/೫, ೬/೭, ೮/೯, ೨/೩/೭) must be read with care; where you cannot decide, write the likeliest digit and put [?] right after it.
> - A word or letter you cannot read: write [?]. Never complete anything from memory, never correct what the print says, never skip a line; no remarks of your own; no letters of other scripts (Tamil, Bengali, Telugu, Malayalam are never right here).
> - English paragraphs printed on the page (the edition's English under each verse) are copied as printed. Footnotes at the end of the page after a line `---`.
> Final report in ≤ 5 lines: pages written, number of [?] marks per page, any page you could not read well.

**Pilot first (12 pages, from the middle of the volume, including at least two Ṛk pages with word meanings and one page of Special Topics with many sūtra numbers).** Then **measure, by script, and report to the user** (do not go on before the user says yes):
1. Page heads read: the page numbers run in sequence.
2. Sūkta and Ṛk numbers against the Wikisource index (`python3 tools/rv/ws_index.py get M.S`): every Ṛk number present, in order, and the number of Ṛks of each sūkta equal to the index's.
3. **Word-meaning headwords against the Pada** (the objective check): the words before the dash in the ಪ್ರತಿಪದಾರ್ಥ lines are the Pada words in Kannada script; convert them to Devanagari (`publish/tools/translit/kannada_to_deva.py`), normalise as in `tools/rv/scan_pilot.py compare`, and report the percentage found in the Wikisource Pada of the same Ṛk. Do the same for the Vol 22 file if it is available, so that the two readers are compared.
4. `[?]` count; letters of other scripts (must be 0).
5. Show the user three pages (the image and the transcription) to read by eye; give the tokens used per page.

**Then the whole volume**, batch after batch, with these checks after every batch (cost nothing): page sequence; Ṛk numbers against the index; letters of other scripts; `[?]` per page. A batch that fails a check is repaired by the dispatcher as a logged line in `vol25/review/repair_log.md` or sent again, and the cause is named in the next prompt. Every 5 batches give the user one line (pages done, `[?]` count, headword match, tokens).

**Assemble** the pages into `vol25/html/rig_25.md` in the layout of the supplied files (look at `vol21/html/rig_21.md`: running head line first on each page; `<!-- page 1 -->` before the opening of the body if the first page prints no head). **Pictures:** save the cover and the title page as `vol25/html/figures/fig1_title.png` and `fig2_cover.png` (pictures 1 and 2 of the supplied file; the PDF will use them, REQUIREMENTS A2).

**Fill the blocks from Wikisource** with a shared tool you write: `tools/rv/ws_fill.py 25` (no per-volume tool). For every Ṛk (its address from the sūkta heading and the `ಋಕ್`/number line) it replaces each `[[block: …]]` line by the block in Kannada script, in the layout of the supplied files for that block: the Saṃhitā from `rik.samhita`, the Pada from `rik.pada`, the bhāṣya from the Ṛk's paragraphs (`para`, M.S.R.P) **where the page prints it** (the commentary of two or three Ṛks stands with the last of them: `rik.bh_at`; the print position of the bhāṣya block decides under which Ṛk it is filled), converted with `publish/tools/translit/deva_to_kannada.py`. Wikisource is never edited and never compared; its three known defects (5.5.11, 9.97 Ṛk 10, 10.27 Ṛk 1, REQUIREMENTS B5.6) get a `NOTE`. It prints what it filled and checks by count that every Ṛk has all three blocks, none missing, none twice (REQUIREMENTS D17), and logs the Ṛks it could not fill. Show the user one filled Ṛk before running it on the volume.
