# Brief: English of Sāyaṇa's Ṛgveda-bhāṣya-bhūmikā (his general introduction), from the Wikisource text

Read `docs/WSBOOK_BRIEF.md` and `tools/rv/BHASHYA_BRIEF.md` rules 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 and 8 first; they apply unchanged. What differs:

1. **What you translate.** Sāyaṇa's long general introduction to his Ṛgveda commentary: prose, mostly Mīmāṃsā-style argument (an objection, the answer, quotations of Jaimini's sūtras, of Śabara, of Brāhmaṇas and Smṛtis). The text is `sa.wikisource.org`, page `वेदभाष्यभूमिकासङ्ग्रहः`, the Ṛgveda part, in four parts (task files `M0_S001`–`M0_S004`, printed pages 89–137 of the compilation edition). Each "Ṛk" of the task is a printed page (`## Page 89`); the unit numbers are `SBh 0.S.PAGE.U`. A unit may begin or end in the middle of a sentence at a page boundary: render the unit as it stands, so that the English of two neighbouring units reads on in sequence.
2. **It is a scan transcription**, not a clean text like the sūkta pages. It has OCR slips (a word may read `हति` for `इति`, a letter may be wrong). Do not alter it. Translate the word that is plainly meant and, where you cannot be sure, put `**NOTE**{question in one sentence}` (no Devanagari inside the braces; cite supplied letters with `⟦ ⟧` and give the intended word in Roman letters). A spelling that is merely unusual is not a doubt (WSBOOK_BRIEF rule 1).
3. **Quotations.** A quoted sūtra, verse or Brāhmaṇa passage is cited with `⟦ ⟧` (a short one) and then translated; a long one is translated with its first words cited. References in the text (`(मी० २.१.३५)`, `(ऋ०. १०. ९०. ९)`) are copied with `⟦ ⟧` exactly as they stand.
4. **Verses of the Ṛgveda and other Veda texts quoted** are cited and translated as Sāyaṇa uses them: for his argument, not as a free rendering of the verse.
5. **The editors' footnotes** of the compilation are not Sāyaṇa and are not given to you; `[१]` marks in the text refer to them: leave the marks out.
6. **Do not open Vol 1** or any other volume, and do not look for another translation of this text: the English you write will afterwards be compared with the Kannada edition's version (Vol 1) by someone else, and the comparison is only worth anything if your English came from the Sanskrit alone.
7. Steps, check and report as in WSBOOK_BRIEF: `python3 tools/rv/wsbook.py task 0 S`, write `sayana_book/en/M0_S00S.md`, second reading of every unit against its Sanskrit, `python3 tools/rv/wsbook.py check 0 S` until VERIFIED. Do not commit or push. Report the check's last line, the NOTEs, and places of real doubt.
