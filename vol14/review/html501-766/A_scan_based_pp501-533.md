**प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha — Ṛk 6)**

(Kannada, translated:) यदि (*yadi*) — when; मातुः (*mātuḥ*) — of the earth, the maker of the world and of the form of the altar; स्वसा (*svasā*) — the ladles that are of sister-nature; घृतं (*ghṛtaṃ*) — the ājya (clarified butter); भरंति (*bharaṃti*) — filling; उप अस्थिते (*upa asthite*) — when Agni is placed near, (then); तासां (*tāsāṃ*) — of those ladles, Juhū and the rest; आगतौ (*āgatau*) — at the time of their coming; अध्वर्युः (*adhvaryuḥ*) — Agni who desires the sacrifice, or who is named Adhvaryu; युवः (*yuvaḥ*) — the barley grain (printed ಯುವ, "yuva"); वृष्टीव (*vṛṣṭīva*) — as it shows joy at the coming of rain; मोदते (*modate*) — rejoices.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha — Ṛk 6)**

(Kannada, translated:) When the ladles Juhū and the rest, being sisters to the earth in the form of the altar, filled with ājya, Agni comes near, then Agni, who desires the sacrifice, rejoices exceedingly, as the barley grain shows joy at the time of the coming of rain.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu — Ṛk 6)**

(Kannada, translated.)

In this Ṛk the meaning of the words मातुः स्वसा (*mātuḥ svasā*) is not stated clearly, but the bhāṣyakāra has fitted the meanings "altar" and "Juhū" to them according to the context and explained them so. For the word मातृ (*mātṛ*) various meanings are given according to context: mother, maker, altar, world, earth. Here he gives the meaning "sacrificial altar", and for the word स्वसा (*svasā*) he explains "the Juhū, which is like a sister to the altar, the wooden ladle with which ājya is offered". Although the sense fits well with this explanation, there remains a doubt as to whether these words have really been used in these meanings.

अध्वर्युः घृतं भरंती आगतौ युवः वृष्टीव मोदते (*adhvaryuḥ ghṛtaṃ bharaṃtī āgatau yuvaḥ vṛṣṭīva modate*) — The comparison stated in this illustration fits well. When the ṛtvij called Adhvaryu, having filled the ladles used for offering with ājya (ghee) for the oblation in the fire, comes near Agni, the joy that arises in Agni is described thus: as the plants of barley welcome with great joy the rain that they need, so Agni too is gladdened.

(Kannada, translated:) (अयमग्निः (*ayamagniḥ*) — this Agni.) स्वः (*svaḥ*) — himself; स्वाय (*svāya*) — for his own; धायसे (*dhāyase*) — for the rite, or for the holding (bearing) of the oblations; ऋत्विक् (*ṛtvik*) — as the ṛtvij of the form of the Hotṛ and the others described before; ऋत्विजं (*ṛtvijaṃ*) — the work to be accomplished by the Hotṛ and the others; कृणुतां (*kṛṇutāṃ*) — may he accomplish; वयं (*vayaṃ*) — we also; आत् (*āt*) — immediately after that; स्तोमं (*stomaṃ*) — the stotra (praise); यज्ञं च (*yajñaṃ ca*) — and the sacrifice based on it; अरं (*araṃ*) — fully; वनेम (*vanema*) — we worship (resort to); ररिम (*rarima*) — we also offer the oblation.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha — Ṛk 7)**

(Kannada, translated:) May this Agni, himself in the form of the ṛtvij, for his own rite, accomplish the work to be accomplished by the Hotṛ and the others. Immediately after that, we also fully resort to the stotra and to the sacrifice based on it, (accomplishing them), and we offer the oblation.

**English Translation** *(as printed, Ṛk 7)*

May he, the ministering priest, discharge the office of priest at his own ceremony, and may we worthily repeat (his) praise, and offer (him) sacrifice.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu — Ṛk 7)**

(Kannada, translated.)

**सः ऋत्विक् ऋत्विजं कृणुतां (*saḥ ṛtvik ṛtvijaṃ kṛṇutāṃ*)** — In this sacrifice, which Agni himself accomplishes, the wish is stated here that, as said before, he himself should carry out the duties of the many ṛtvijs who take part in the sacrifice — Hotṛ, Potṛ, Praśāstṛ and the rest — and bring the sacrifice to completion. The use of the words ऋत्विक् ऋत्विजं (*ṛtvik ṛtvijaṃ*) here indicates all the ṛtvijs, Hotṛ and the others. That is, Agni himself (in the form of each respective ṛtvij) does the works that all of them do. The intention is that Agni, who is of the form of the divine ṛtvij, does the works that the human ṛtvijs do.

**स्तोमं यज्ञं चादरं वनेमा ररिमा वयं (*stomaṃ yajñaṃ cādaraṃ vanemā rarimā vayaṃ*)** — If, as said above, Agni himself does the sacrificial works that all the ṛtvijs do, what is it that we have to do? The answer: we also, in the sacrifice, recite the stotras and the like and praise the gods, and offer the oblations and the like; thus the works done by us (the sacrificers and the others) are also described.

## Sūkta 6 (the sixth sūkta)

**॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (introduction to Sūkta 6)**

*Gist:* Sūkta 6 has eight Ṛks, ṛṣi Somāhuti Bhārgava, deity Agni, metre Gāyatrī; it is used at the Prātaranuvāka and Āśvina-śastra, and at the Upasad rite in the afternoon (as the Sāmidhenī verses).

> इमां म इत्यष्टर्चं षष्ठं सूक्तं सोमाहुत्यार्षमाग्नेयं गायत्रं । इमां मे गायत्रं हीत्यनुक्रांतं ॥ प्रातरनुवाकाश्विनशस्त्रयोर्गायत्रे छंदस्यस्य विनियोगः । सूत्रितं च । इमां मे अग्ने समिधमिमामिति त्रयाणामुत्तमामुद्धरेत् । आ. ४-१३ [?] । इति ॥ अपराह्णिक्यामुपसदीमां मे अग्ने समिधमिति तिस्र ऋचस्त्रिरभ्यस्ता नव सामिधेन्यः कृत्वा विनियोक्तव्याः । अथोपसदिति खंडे सूत्रितं । इमां मे अग्ने समिधमिमामिति तु सामिधेन्यः । आ. ४-८ । इति ॥

> *imāṃ ma ityaṣṭarcaṃ ṣaṣṭhaṃ sūktaṃ somāhutyārṣamāgneyaṃ gāyatraṃ | imāṃ me gāyatraṃ hītyanukrāṃtaṃ ‖ prātaranuvākāśvinaśastrayorgāyatre chaṃdasyasya viniyogaḥ | sūtritaṃ ca | imāṃ me agne samidhamimāmiti trayāṇāmuttamāmuddharet | ā. 4-13 [?] | iti ‖ aparāhṇikyāmupasadīmāṃ me agne samidhamiti tisra ṛcastrirabhyastā nava sāmidhenyaḥ kṛtvā viniyoktavyāḥ | athopasaditi khaṃḍe sūtritaṃ | imāṃ me agne samidhamimāmiti tu sāmidhenyaḥ | ā. 4-8 | iti ‖*

*(The two Āśvalāyana-sūtra references are given as printed: "Ā. 4-13" [?] (the second numeral doubtful, not zoomed) and "Ā. 4-8".)*

**Anuvāda (Kannada, translated):** This sūkta beginning इमां मे (*imāṃ me*) is the sixth sūkta in the first Anuvāka. It has eight Ṛks. The ṛṣi is the same Somāhuti Bhārgava who was the ṛṣi of the preceding sūkta; the deity is Agni; the metre Gāyatrī. In the Anukramaṇikā it is said: इमां मे गायत्रं हि (*imāṃ me gāyatraṃ hi*). The employment (viniyoga) of this sūkta, for the Gāyatrī-metre Ṛks at the time of the Prātaranuvāka and of reciting the Āśvina-śastra mantras, is explained by the sūtra of the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra: इमां मे समिधमिमामिति त्रयाणामुत्तमामुद्धरेत् (*imāṃ me samidhamimāmiti trayāṇāmuttamāmuddharet*) (Ā. 4-13 [?]). And at the Upasad rite performed at the afternoon time, the three Ṛks of this sūkta beginning इमां मे अग्ने समिधं (*imāṃ me agne samidhaṃ*), each recited three times, are to be used as nine Ṛks called Sāmidhenī; this is explained by the sūtra in the section of the Āśvalāyana Śrauta-sūtra called अथोपसत् (*athopasat*): इमां मे अग्ने समिधमिमामिति तु सामिधेन्यः (*imāṃ me agne samidhamimāmiti tu sāmidhenyaḥ*) (Ā. 4-8).

**उपसदं (उपसदं (*upasadaṃ*))** — उपसत् (*upasat*) is said to be an important part of the Jyotiṣṭoma sacrifice.

**समिधं (समिधं (*samidhaṃ*))** — इंध् दीप्तौ (*iṃdh dīptau*) (the root *indh*, to shine). **सूक्तेन—सुष्ठु उक्तेन (*sūktena—suṣṭhu uktena*)** — "Well-spoken", that is, a stotra or Ṛk that praises the qualities of the deities well. In the Ṛgveda a collection of many such Ṛks is called a *sūkta*. In sum, "sūkta" means a stotra.

**ऊर्जो नपात्—बलस्य नस्तः (*ūrjo napāt—balasya nastaḥ*)** *(as printed; the same reading as in the bhāṣya on p. 508)* — When fire is produced by churning in the two *araṇi* sticks, it arises only by churning with the use of much strength. Here room exists for both kinds of meaning.

**अया—अनया (*ayā—anayā*)** — For the convenience of the metre the letter न (*na*) of the word अनया (*anayā*) has been dropped, giving अया (*ayā*). Similarly the letter आ (*ā*) of the word आत्मना (*ātmanā*) has been dropped, and the word त्मना (*tmanā*) is used in many places. Such usages are numerous in the Ṛgveda.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu — Ṛk 7)**

(Kannada, translated.) **अंतः हि अग्ने ईयसे (*aṃtaḥ hi agne īyase*)** — "O Agni, you enter the hearts of people", that is, you know all the matters that are in people's hearts or minds: this is the sense intended. The same sense is indicated (by the following). The bhāṣyakāra has given as an example the Ṛk

> स होता सेदु दूत्यं चिकित्वाँ अंतरीयते ।

> *sa hotā sedu dūtyaṃ cikitvā~ aṃtarīyate |*

> अंतर्द्यावापृथिव्योर्मध्य ईयते गच्छति

> *aṃtardyāvāpṛthivyormadhya īyate gacchati*

that is, "he goes (moves about) in the middle of heaven and earth (the earth and the atmosphere)". Therefore the Ṛk given here as an example does not establish the meaning of the Ṛk in hand.

*(यष्टृणां (*yaṣṭṛṇāṃ*): the print is not clear to me [?]; not zoomed.)* Here Agni is described as knowing two kinds of births. The bhāṣyakāra explains this as knowing the birth, or order of origin, of the sacrificers who perform the sacrifice and of the gods who are worshipped in the sacrifice.

For in the world a messenger sent by the king, to learn the minds of the subjects, moves among them, learns their intentions and reports them to the king; in the same way Agni too enters people's minds and, as a well-wisher, makes their wishes known to the deities for whom the sacrifice is intended.

> *saḥ | vidvān | ā | ca | piprayaḥ | yakṣi | cikitvaḥ | ānuṣak |*
> *ā | ca | asmin | satsi | barhiṣi ‖ 8 ‖*

**॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Ṛk 8)**

*Gist:* Omniscient Agni, fill us on all sides with desired things; wise one, worship the gods in order and sit on this strewn grass.

> हे अग्ने स त्वं विद्वान् विशेषाभिज्ञः सन् आ च पिप्रयुः । सर्वतोऽस्मान्पूरय च काम्यैः । हे चिकित्वश्चेतनावन् आनुषक् अनुक्रमेण यक्षि । यज देवान् । आसत्सि । आसीद चास्मिन् स्तीर्णे बर्हिषि ॥

> *he agne sa tvaṃ vidvān viśeṣābhijñaḥ san ā ca piprayuḥ | sarvato 'smānpūraya ca kāmyaiḥ | he cikitvaścetanāvan ānuṣak anukrameṇa yakṣi | yaja devān | āsatsi | āsīda cāsmin stīrṇe barhiṣi ‖*

**प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha — Ṛk 8)**

(Kannada, translated:) सः (*saḥ*) — (O Agni) you; विद्वान् (*vidvān*) — being one who knows everything; आ च (*ā ca*) — in every way; पिप्रयुः (*piprayuḥ*) — fulfil our desires; चिकित्वः (*cikitvaḥ*) — you who are wise; आनुषक् (*ānuṣak*) — in order; यक्षि (*yakṣi*) — worship (the deities); अस्मिन् (*asmin*) — here, spread; बर्हिषि च (*barhiṣi ca*) — on the darbha seat too; आ सत्सि (*ā satsi*) — sit down.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha — Ṛk 8)**

(Kannada, translated:) O Agni, you who know everything, fulfil our desires in every way, and worship the deities in order. Sit also on the darbha seat spread here.

**English Translation** *(as printed, Ṛk 8)*

Agni, you are all-wise ; fulfil (our desires) : you, who are intelligent, worship ( the gods) in order ; and sit down on this sacred grass.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu — Ṛk 8)**

(Kannada, translated.) **आनुषक् (*ānuṣak*)** — the meaning is "in order". For this word Yāska gives *(as printed, the first words not zoomed [?])*:

> आनुषगिति नामानुपूर्वस्यानुषक्तं भवति । स्तृणंति बर्हिरानुषक् इत्यपि निगमो भवति

> *ānuṣagiti nāmānupūrvasyānuṣaktaṃ bhavati | stṛṇaṃti barhirānuṣak ityapi nigamo bhavati*

That is: आनुषक् (*ānuṣak*) is a synonym of अनुपूर्व (*anupūrva*) and अनुक्रम (*anukrama*) *(print: आनुक्रमु (*ānukramu*) [?])*, words that give the sense "one after another"; and he has given the example स्तृणंति बर्हिरानुषक् (*stṛṇaṃti barhirānuṣak*):

> आ घा ये अग्निमिंधते स्तृणंति बर्हिरानुषक् ।
> येषामिंद्रो युवा सखा ॥

> *ā ghā ye agnimiṃdhate stṛṇaṃti barhirānuṣak |*
> *yeṣāmiṃdro yuvā sakhā ‖*

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-45-1 [?], printed ल-ळ५-१; numerals not zoomed.)

In this example it is said that first they kindle the fire; after that they spread the darbha grass called बर्हिः (*barhiḥ*) as a seat. Here kindling the fire is one act; spreading the darbha is another act. After the first act, kindling the fire, the second act, spreading the darbha, is carried out. Thus, when one act is done in order after another, the word आनुषक् (*ānuṣak*) is used to indicate that order. In the Ṛk in hand also there are three acts of Agni: fulfilling our desires, worshipping the deities, and sitting on the darbha. The word आनुषक् (*ānuṣak*) is used here so that they may be done in due order.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu — Ṛk 1)**

(Kannada, translated.) **भारते (*bhārate*)** — The word भारत (*bhārata*) is read among the names of priests (ṛtvij) (Ni. 3-18 [?], as printed; the second numeral is doubtful, not zoomed). Bhārata means a priest. The word is formed from the root भृञ् भरणे (*bhṛñ bharaṇe*) and, according to the explanation of Skandasvāmin, the sense is:

> यज्ञद्वारेण नॄन् संभरतीति [?]

> *yajñadvāreṇa nṝn saṃbharatīti [?]*

— "by means of the sacrifice he upholds men (the sacrificers)", that is, by performing the sacrifice he thereby lifts up (saves) the people (the patrons). Therefore this priest is called Bhārata. Durgācārya, the commentator on the Nirukta, says:

> भ्रियंते दक्षिणाभिस्ते इति भारताः

> *bhriyaṃte dakṣiṇābhiste iti bhāratāḥ*

— "they are nourished (satisfied) by the dakṣiṇās and the like, so they are Bhāratas": having performed the sacrifice, they obtain satisfaction through sacrificial fees and so on, and so these priests are called Bhāratas. Thus the word भरत (*bharata*) has two kinds of etymological meaning. *(The first Skandasvāmin quotation: the print of नॄन् (*nṝn*) is not clear to me [?]; not zoomed.)*

In the same way, the prayer is made here that we may make our enemies yield without trouble.

**English Translation** *(as printed, Ṛk 5)*

Agni, descendant of Bharata, you are entirely ours, when sacrificed to with pregnant cows, with barren cows, or bulls.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu — Ṛk 5)**

(Kannada, translated.) **भारत (*bhārata*)** — The word भारत (*bhārata*) is read among the names of priests [?] (Ni. 3-19, not zoomed). Here, for the word भारत (*bhārata*), the bhāṣyakāra gives the explanation ऋत्विजां पुत्रस्थानीयः (*ṛtvijāṃ putrasthānīyaḥ*), "O Agni, who are like a son to the priests". At the beginning of a sacrifice the priests churn in the aranis and produce Agni; therefore it is fitting to say that Agni stands in the place of a son to the priests.

**वशाभिः उक्षभिः अष्टापदीभिः आहुतः (*vaśābhiḥ ukṣabhiḥ aṣṭāpadībhiḥ āhutaḥ*)** — the bhāṣyakāra explains:

> वशाभिर्वंध्याभिर्गोभिः उक्षभिः सेक्तृभिर्बलिवर्दैः अष्टापदीभिर्गर्भिणीभिश्चाहुतः ।

> *vaśābhirvaṃdhyābhirgobhiḥ ukṣabhiḥ sektṛbhirbalivardaiḥ aṣṭāpadībhirgarbhiṇībhiścāhutaḥ |*

(Kannada, translated, continued.) That is: "O Agni, you are propitiated with barren cows that we offer to you, with bulls capable of impregnating (cows), and with cows in calf." Sāyaṇa gives this explanation. Although there are these three kinds among cattle, the offering of cows into the fire as an oblation is nowhere seen. Alternatively, the words वशाभिः (*vaśābhiḥ*) and the like need not be given the meaning "cows"; other animals fit for sacrifice, goats and so on (अजादि (*ajādi*)), must be what is indicated here. The word अष्टापदीभिः (*aṣṭāpadībhiḥ*) means an animal together with eight feet: the four feet natural to an animal with young, and the four feet of the young in the womb, together make eight feet. Therefore the word अष्टापदीभिः (*aṣṭāpadībhiḥ*) is used to indicate a pregnant female animal.

Since ghee is offered into Agni in sacrifices and the like, that ghee itself is said to be Agni's food. Therefore Agni is addressed in many places by the word सर्पिरासुति (*sarpirāsuti*).

**॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Sūkta 8)**

*Gist:* Sūkta 8 has six Ṛks; ṛṣi Gṛtsamada, devatā Agni, metre Gāyatrī, the last Ṛk Anuṣṭubh; its use in the morning recitation and the Āśvina śastra was stated earlier.

**अनुवाद (Anuvāda)**

(Kannada, translated:) This sūkta beginning वाजयन्निव (*vājayanniva*) is the eighth sūkta in the first anuvāka. It has six Ṛks. The ṛṣi of this sūkta is Gṛtsamada, the seer of the maṇḍala; the devatā is Agni; the metre is Gāyatrī. Only the last Ṛk is in the Anuṣṭubh metre. In the Anukramaṇikā it is said: वाजयन्निवांत्यानुष्टुप् (*vājayannivāṃtyānuṣṭup*). It was already said earlier that, at the time of reciting the Prātaranuvāka and the Āśvina-śastra mantras, the Ṛks of this sūkta (except the last Ṛk) are applied (viniyoga) as Ṛks in the Gāyatrī metre.

**॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥ (Ṛk 1)**

*Gist:* O inner self, quickly praise the horses (or chariots) of Agni, who is of widespread fame and a bestower of fruit, as one desiring food praises his horses.

> हे अंतरात्मन् नु क्षिप्रमग्नेर्योगानश्वानुपस्तुहि । कीदृशस्याग्नेः । यशस्तमस्य प्रवृद्धयशसः ॥ मत्वर्थो लुप्यते ॥ मीळ्हुषः सेक्तुः फलस्य । ईदृशस्य योगान् स्तुहि । होतरिति वा संबोध्यं । स्तुतौ दृष्टांतः । वाजयन्निव । अन्नमिच्छन् पुरुष इव । स यथाश्वान् गमनाय स्तौति तद्वत् । यद्वोक्तलक्षणस्याग्नेर्गमनसाधनान्रथान् स्तुहि ॥

> *he aṃtarātman nu kṣipramagneryogānaśvānupastuhi | kīdṛśasyāgneḥ | yaśastamasya pravṛddhayaśasaḥ ‖ matvartho lupyate ‖ mīḷhuṣaḥ sektuḥ phalasya | īdṛśasya yogān stuhi | hotariti vā saṃbodhyaṃ | stutau dṛṣṭāṃtaḥ | vājayanniva | annamicchan puruṣa iva | sa yathāśvān gamanāya stauti tadvat | yadvoktalakṣaṇasyāgnergamanasādhanānrathān stuhi ‖*

**प्रतिपदार्थ (Pratipadārtha — Ṛk 1)**

(Kannada, translated:) यशस्तमस्य (यशस्तमस्य (*yaśastamasya*)) — of him who has glory spread very widely; मीळ्हुषः (मीळ्हुषः (*mīḷhuṣaḥ*)) — of him who is able to give fruits generously; अग्नेः (अग्नेः (*agneḥ*)) — of Agni; योगान् (योगान् (*yogān*)) — the horses; रथान् (रथान् (*rathān*)) — the chariots; वाजयन्निव (वाजयन्निव (*vājayanniva*)) — like one who desires food urging on the horses of his chariot; नु (नु (*nu*)) — quickly; उप स्तुहि (उप स्तुहि (*upa stuhi*)) — praise.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha — Ṛk 1)**

(Kannada, translated:) O inner self, as one who desires food urges on the horses of his chariot, so you quickly praise the horses and the chariots of Agni, who has glory spread very widely and is able to give fruits generously.

**English Translation** *(as printed, Ṛk 1)*

Praise quickly the yoked chariot of the glorious and munificent Agni, like one who is desirous of food.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu — Ṛk 1)**

(Kannada, translated.) In this Ṛk Sāyaṇa explains the meaning of the Ṛk by addressing the inner self (अंतरात्मन् (*aṃtarātman*)), saying that Agni's horses and chariots are to be praised. Here there is no need to address the inner self at all. In a sacrifice one may address any one of the ṛtvijas who take part, or the yajamāna.

**नु (नु (*nu*))** — नु इत्येषोऽनेककर्मा (*nu ityeṣo 'nekakarmā*). The Nirukta (Ni. 1-4) explains the word नु (*nu*) as having many meanings:

> नु इत्येषोऽनेककर्मा । इदं करिष्यतीति हेत्वपदेशे । कथं नु करिष्यतीत्यनुपृष्टे ।

> *nu ityeṣo 'nekakarmā | idaṃ kariṣyatīti hetvapadeśe | kathaṃ nu kariṣyatītyanupṛṣṭe |*

(Kannada, translated, continued.) The particle नु (*nu*) has many meanings. When one says इदं नु करिष्यति (*idaṃ nu kariṣyati*), the word नु (*nu*) has the sense of "because of this", i.e. it indicates a reason (hetvartha). When one says कथं नु करिष्यति (*kathaṃ nu kariṣyati*), नु (*nu*) is interrogative. Likewise अथाप्युपमार्थे भवति (*athāpyupamārthe bhavati*): the word नु (*nu*) is used also to indicate comparison. For example:

> अक्षो न चक्र्योः शूर बृहन्प्र ते मह्ना रिरिचे रोदस्योः ।
> वृक्षस्य नु ते पुरुहूत वया व्यूतयो रुरुहुरिंद्र पूर्वीः ॥

> *akṣo na cakryoḥ śūra bṛhanpra te mahnā ririce rodasyoḥ |*
> *vṛkṣasya nu te puruhūta vayā vyūtayo ruruhuriṃdra pūrvīḥ ‖*

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-24-3 [?], as read; the first numeral not certain)

(Kannada, translated, continued.) The author of the Nirukta quotes this Ṛk and gives वृक्षस्येव ते पुरुहूत शाखाः (*vṛkṣasyeva te puruhūta śākhāḥ*) ("like a tree, O much-invoked one, the branches" or "like the branches of a tree") as the explanation of the word नु (*nu*) in the sense of comparison. Likewise:

> नू मर्तो दयते सनिष्यन्यो विष्णव उरुगायाय दाशत् ।
> प्र यः सत्राचा मनसा यजात एतावंतं नर्यमाविवासात् ॥

> *nū marto dayate saniṣyanyo viṣṇava urugāyāya dāśat |*
> *pra yaḥ satrācā manasā yajāta etāvaṃtaṃ naryamāvivāsāt ‖*

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-100-1, as read)

(Kannada, translated, continued.) In Ṛks like this, नु (*nu*) has the meaning क्षिप्रम् (*kṣipram*) — "promptly, with alertness" (the Kannada gloss is जाग्रते (*jāgrate*) as printed ಜಾಗ್ರತೆಯಾಗಿ). And:

> नू इंद्र शूर स्तवमान ऊती ब्रह्मजूतस्तन्वा वावृधस्व ।
> उप नो वाजान्मिमीह्युप स्तीन्योयं [?] पाते स्वस्तिभिः सदा नः ॥

> *nū iṃdra śūra stavamāna ūtī brahmajūtastanvā vāvṛdhasva |*
> *upa no vājānmimīhyupa stīnyoyaṃ [?] pāte svastibhiḥ sadā naḥ ‖*

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-19-11, as read; the word स्तीन्योयं (*stīnyoyaṃ*) is as read from the strip, not zoomed)

(Kannada, translated, continued.) In Ṛks beginning like these, the word अद्य (*adya*) has the meaning "now, at once"; and as the author of the Nirukta says, नु इत्येषोऽनेककर्मा (*nu ityeṣo 'nekakarmā*) (Ni. 1-4), the word नु (*nu*) is used in still other meanings. In the present Ṛk the meaning is "quickly, speedily".

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu — Ṛk 4)**

(Kannada, translated.) **स्वः न भानुना (*svaḥ na bhānunā*)** — the meaning is "as the Āditya shines with his rays". The word स्वः (*svaḥ*) is read in the common names स्वः पृश्निः नाकः (*svaḥ pṛśniḥ nākaḥ*) and so on. The Nirukta says (Ni. 2-13 [?], not zoomed):

> साधारणान्युत्तराणि षट् दिवश्चादित्यस्य च ।

> *sādhāraṇānyuttarāṇi ṣaṭ divaścādityasya ca |*

(Kannada, translated, continued.) That is, the six words स्वः पृश्निः नाकः गौः विष्टपं नभः (*svaḥ pṛśniḥ nākaḥ gauḥ viṣṭapaṃ nabhaḥ*) are all common terms, both synonyms of the heaven-world and words for the Āditya, as the author of the Nirukta states. Then, explaining what the root of the word स्वः (*svaḥ*) is, in its sense of Āditya, and how the word is derived, he says:

> स्वरादित्यो भवति सु अरणः सु ईरणः स्वृतो रसान् स्वृतो भासं ज्योतिषां । स्वृतो भासेति वा ॥

> *svarādityo bhavati su araṇaḥ su īraṇaḥ svṛto rasān svṛto bhāsaṃ jyotiṣāṃ | svṛto bhāseti vā ‖*

(Ni. 2-14 [?], not zoomed)

(Kannada, translated, continued.) The Āditya has this name either because he is very far away (सु अरणः (*su araṇaḥ*), "going well"), or because he scatters and destroys the darkness completely, or because he enters the juices (rasa) and goes inside them, or because he spreads his own light in the sphere of radiance and becomes one with it, or because he becomes all-pervading in the form of light. And by the words एतेन द्यौर्व्याख्याता (*etena dyaurvyākhyātā*) ("by this the sky is explained") the Nirukta-author shows that for this very reason the heaven-world also has the name स्वः (*svaḥ*).

> स्वरादित्यो भवति स एनानि सारयति [?] ।

> *svarādityo bhavati sa enāni sārayati [?] |*

(Ni. 5-4 [?], not zoomed; the word एनानि (*enāni*) is as read from the strip, not zoomed)

(Kannada, translated, continued.) The Āditya also has the name स्वः (*svaḥ*) because he makes the days (ಅಹಸ್ಸ್ಗಳು) move along. Where the word is used in the Ṛgveda:

> प्रत्यङ् देवानां विशः प्रत्यङ्ङुदेषि मानुषान् । प्रत्यङ् विश्वं स्वर्दृशे ॥

> *pratyaṅ devānāṃ viśaḥ pratyaṅṅudeṣi mānuṣān | pratyaṅ viśvaṃ svardṛśe ‖*

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-50-5 [?], as read, not zoomed)

(Kannada, translated, continued.) In Ṛks like this the word स्वः (*svaḥ*) has the name of the heaven-world (svargaloka). And:

> त्मना वहंतो दुरो व्यृण्वन्नवंत विश्वे स्वर्दृशीके ।

> *tmanā vahaṃto duro vyṛṇvannavaṃta viśve svardṛśīke |*

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-69-10 [?], as read, not zoomed)

(Kannada, translated, continued.) In Ṛks like this, the word स्वः (*svaḥ*) means नभसि (*nabhasi*), "in the sky, in the atmosphere". And:

> दानो अग्ने बृहतो दाः सहस्रिणो दुरो न वाजं श्रुत्या अपा वृधि ।
> प्राची द्यावापृथिवी ब्रह्मणा कृधि स्वर्ण शुक्रमुषसो वि दिद्युतुः ॥

> *dāno agne bṛhato dāḥ sahasriṇo duro na vājaṃ śrutyā apā vṛdhi |*
> *prācī dyāvāpṛthivī brahmaṇā kṛdhi svarṇa śukramuṣaso vi didyutuḥ ‖*

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-2-2 [?], as read, not zoomed)

### Printed p. 533 (PDF 554)

*(Header: "Aṣṭaka 2, Adhyāya 5, Varga 29 ]", "Ṛgvedasaṃhitā", "533". The numeral file has no rows for this page. Header and reference numerals not zoomed.)*

(Kannada, translated, continued from the Special Topics of Ṛk 4.) In Ṛks like these the word स्वः (*svaḥ*) has been explained as "Āditya". In the same way, words that carry suffixes such as वत् (*vat*), e.g. स्वर्वत् (*svarvat*), are likewise to be explained according to the context. For example, (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-97-1 [?], numerals as read, not zoomed; the accent-like mark over स्वर्वा (*svarvā*) is as printed):

> या इंद्र भुज आभरः स्वर्वा असुरेभ्यः ।
> स्तोतारमिन्मघवन्नस्य वर्धय यो च त्वे वृक्तबर्हिषः ॥

> *yā iṃdra bhuja ābharaḥ svarvā asurebhyaḥ |*
> *stotāraminmaghavannasya vardhaya yo ca tve vṛktabarhiṣaḥ ‖*

(Kannada, translated, continued.) In this Ṛk — the commentator says "सुखवान्, स्वर्गवान् वा" ("possessed of happiness, or possessed of heaven"):

*Gist:* The word स्वः (*svaḥ*) is a synonym of "all"; everything that is born is of one's own self, hence "possessed of all".

> अथवा स्वशब्दः सर्वपर्यायः । सर्वं भूतजातं आत्मन एवोत्पन्नत्वात्स्वान् [?] ।

> *athavā svaśabdaḥ sarvaparyāyaḥ | sarvaṃ bhūtajātaṃ ātmana evotpannatvātsvān [?] |*

(Kannada, translated, continued.) Since the word स्वः (*svaḥ*) has the meanings happiness, heaven or "all", स्वर्वान् (*svarvān*) means "one who has happiness", "a dweller in heaven", or "one who has obtained everything". All these meanings fit when it is an epithet of Indra. And again (Ṛ. Saṃ. 8-40-11 [?], as read, not zoomed):

> तं शिशीता स्वध्वरं सत्यं सत्वानमृत्वियं ।
> उतो नु चिद्य ओहत आंडा शुष्णस्य भेदत्यज्मैः स्वर्वतीरपो नभंतामन्यके समे ॥

> *taṃ śiśītā svadhvaraṃ satyaṃ satvānamṛtviyaṃ |*
> *uto nu cidya ohata āṃḍā śuṣṇasya bhedatyajmaiḥ svarvatīrapo nabhaṃtāmanyake same ‖*

(Kannada, translated, continued.) In passages like these, where स्वर्वतीः अपः (*svarvatīḥ apaḥ*) is an epithet of water, दिव्यानि (*divyāni*) means "water produced in the heaven-world". This epithet also describes the qualities of Agni and the other deities. (Ṛ. Saṃ. 1-136-3 [?], as read, not zoomed):

> ज्योतिष्मतीमदितिं धारयत्क्षितिं स्वर्वतीमा सचेते दिवे दिवे जागृवांसा दिवेदिवे ॥

> *jyotiṣmatīmaditiṃ dhārayatkṣitiṃ svarvatīmā sacete dive dive jāgṛvāṃsā divedive ‖*

(Kannada, translated, continued.) Here स्वर्वती (*svarvatī*) is an epithet of Aditi. In this place it is explained as the commentator has explained it — "yajñaniṣpatti-dvārā स्वर्गवतीं (*svargavatīṃ*)" (through the accomplishment of sacrifice, "possessing heaven"). (Ṛ. Saṃ. 6-19-9 [?], as read, not zoomed; the words पश्चादोत्तरादधरादा [?] (*paścādottarādadharādā [?]*) and समोत्वर्वाजिंद्र [?] (*samotvarvājiṃdra [?]*) are as read from the strip, not zoomed):

> आ ते शुष्मो वृषभ एतु पश्चादोत्तरादधरादा पुरस्तात् ।
> आ विश्वतो अभि समोत्वर्वाजिंद्र द्युम्नं स्वर्वद्धेह्यस्मे ॥

> *ā te śuṣmo vṛṣabha etu paścādottarādadharādā purastāt |*
> *ā viśvato abhi samotvarvājiṃdra dyumnaṃ svarvaddhehyasme ‖*

(Kannada, translated, continued.) In Ṛks like these the meaning सुखयुक्तं धनं (*sukhayuktaṃ dhanaṃ*) ("wealth endowed with happiness") is also given. In the Ṛk in hand the meaning is "Āditya".

**भानुना (*bhānunā*)** — (Kannada, translated:) Since the word भानुः (*bhānuḥ*) is read in the twelve names of "day" (ahar-nāmāni) beginning with वस्तोः (*vastoḥ*), द्यौः (*dyauḥ*) and so on, it means the shining day. And भानुः भासते ह्येतत् ज्योतिषा (*bhānuḥ bhāsate hyetat jyotiṣā*) ("bhānu shines, for this shines by light") — since it shines by its own light, it is explained here as the rays of the sun.
