### Printed p. 2

*Running head: Sāyaṇa-bhāṣya-sahitā, Maṃ. 2, A. 3, Sū. 24.* (Pīṭhike, continued from the preceding page.)

Besides describing their great deeds with qualifying words, the deeds done by Bṛhaspati are described in the Ṛks addressed to Brahmaṇaspati, and the deeds of Brahmaṇaspati in the Ṛks addressed to Bṛhaspati, by mutual exchange. Therefore, although the names are different, the sage Gṛtsamada seems to have held the idea that these two deities are one and the same deity. As time went on this idea changed, and it seems to have given room to the idea that Bṛhaspati is one and Brahmaṇaspati another. Room for such changes is seen also in the case of other deities. For instance, in "mahān indraḥ" the word महान् (*mahān*) is an epithet of Indra, yet it is laid down that Indra is one deity and Mahān Indra another deity. In the same way this idea also fits the deities Agni, Agniḥ Śuciḥ, Agniḥ Pāvakaḥ, etc. This matter will be discussed in the last part of this Introduction.

The sage Yāska has said as follows about Brahmaṇaspati in his Nirukta:

> ब्रह्मणस्पतिः । ब्रह्मणस्पतिर्ब्रह्मणः पाता ना पालयिता ना ॥

> *brahmaṇaspatiḥ | brahmaṇaspatirbrahmaṇaḥ pātā nā pālayitā nā ‖*

(Reference as printed: ಸಿ. 10-14.)

Here the word ब्रह्म (*brahma*) (brahma) means food, wealth and hymn of praise (Ni. 3-9). Therefore ब्रह्मणः पाता (*brahmaṇaḥ pātā*) means "the protector of food", that is, the lord or master of food (nourishment). To show how Brahmaṇaspati is lord of food, Yāska gives as an example:

> अश्मास्यमवतं ब्रह्मणस्पतिर्मधुधारमभि यमोजसातृणत् ।
> तमेव विश्वे पपिरे स्वर्दृशो बहु साकं सिसिचुरुत्समुद्रिणं ॥

> *aśmāsyamavataṃ brahmaṇaspatirmadhudhāramabhi yamojasātṛṇat |*
> *tameva viśve papire svardṛśo bahu sākaṃ sisicurutsamudriṇaṃ ‖*

(Reference as printed: ಮಿ. ಸಂ. 2-24-4.)

giving this Ṛk as the example, and for it gives the explanation:

> अशनवंतमास्यंतनवंतमनातितं ब्रह्मणस्पतिर्मधुधारमभियमोजसा बले-
> नाभ्यतृणत्तमेव सर्वे पिबंति रश्मयः सूर्यदृशो बह्वेनं सह
> सिंचंत्युत्समुद्रिणमुदकवंतं ॥

> *aśanavaṃtamāsyaṃtanavaṃtamanātitaṃ brahmaṇaspatirmadhudhāramabhiyamojasā bale-*
> *nābhyatṛṇattameva sarve pibaṃti raśmayaḥ sūryadṛśo bahvenaṃ saha*
> *siṃcaṃtyutsamudriṇamudakavaṃtaṃ ‖*

(Reference as printed: ಸಿ. 10-13.)

The main purport is this: with the help of the sun's rays the clouds drink the water of the sea and cause that water to fall specially on the earth as rain; from the rain come the herbs and trees, and from the herbs and trees food and other nourishment is obtained; therefore Brahmaṇaspati is the lord of food. In the same way, when the word ब्रह्म (*brahma*) is taken to mean hymn of praise or wealth, one must say that Brahmaṇaspati is the lord of the mantras or of wealth.

*Running head: Ṛgveda-saṃhitā, A. 2, A. 7, Va. 1 (as printed), p. 3.*

The commentator (Sāyaṇa) has stated this very meaning when commenting on the Ṛk quoted above.

Mantras addressed to Brahmaṇaspati are numerous in the Ṛgveda. In those mantras it is said that Brahmaṇaspati gives wealth and food. Many people doubt whether this Brahmaṇaspati is a separate deity or a changed form of Agni. Since other deities such as Agni also give wealth and food, some argue that Brahmaṇaspati is a special form of Agni. Yāska is silent on this point, that is, he has said nothing clearly. Even in the Ṛks addressed to Brahmaṇaspati there is no authority whatever for saying that this deity is a changed form of Agni. Therefore it may be said freely that Brahmaṇaspati is a separate deity, like Indrāgnī.

In his Nirukta, explaining the derivation of the word Bṛhaspati, Yāska says:

> बृहस्पतिः बृहतः पाता वा पालयिता वा ।

> *bṛhaspatiḥ bṛhataḥ pātā vā pālayitā vā |*

(Reference as printed: ಸಿ. 10-11.)

On this the commentator Durgācārya says: "Why Bṛhaspati? 'Bṛhataḥ pātā or pālayitā': the protector, guardian of this great world or of water; पालयिता (*pālayitā*) also means protector only — the roots differ, the meaning is one." That is, he is the preserver of the great world or of water, the one who protects. He says that although for expressing the sense of the word पति (*pati*) (pati) there are words formed from different roots, पाता (*pātā*) and पालयिता (*pālayitā*), the meaning is only one.

In many places, explaining बृहतः पाता (*bṛhataḥ pātā*), Sāyaṇa gives for the word बृहतः (*bṛhataḥ*) the meaning बृहतः यज्ञाख्यकर्मणः (*bṛhataḥ yajñākhyakarmaṇaḥ*) or वृष्टाख्यकर्मणः (*vṛṣṭākhyakarmaṇaḥ*), that is, "of the rite in the form of sacrifice" or "of the rite in the form of granting rain". Since Bṛhaspati, as priest of the gods, performs the sacrificial rite, and since he brings about the rain that men need, both these epithets are natural. Yāska gives as an example the Ṛk describing Bṛhaspati's priesthood and his granting of rain:

> यद्देवापिः शंतनवे पुरोहितो होत्राय वृतः कृपयन्नदीधेत् ।
> देवश्रुतं वृष्टिवनिं रराणो बृहस्पतिर्वाचमस्मा अयच्छत् ॥

> *yaddevāpiḥ śaṃtanave purohito hotrāya vṛtaḥ kṛpayannadīdhet |*
> *devaśrutaṃ vṛṣṭivaniṃ rarāṇo bṛhaspatirvācamasmā ayacchat ‖*

(Reference as printed: ಮ. ಸಂ. 10-98-7.)

In the explanation of this Ṛk it is said: "Bṛhaspati was the Brahman; he gave speech to him", that is, Bṛhaspati became the priest called Brahman in the sacrifice performed by the king named Śantanu, taught him a mantra in the form of words, and when the sacrificer praised with that mantra, rain came. Besides this, in the Yajurveda and other texts:

> बृहस्पतिर्देवानां पुरोहित आसीच्छंडामर्कावसुराणां ।

> *bṛhaspatirdevānāṃ purohita āsīcchaṃḍāmarkāvasurāṇāṃ |*

(Reference as printed: ತೈ. ಸಂ. 6-4-10-1.)

It is said that Bṛhaspati was the priest of the gods, and that Śaṇḍa and Marka were priests of the asuras.

> ब्रह्म वै देवानां बृहस्पतिर्ब्रह्मणैवैनमभिचरति ।

> *brahma vai devānāṃ bṛhaspatirbrahmaṇaivainamabhicarati |*

(Reference as printed: ತೈ. ಸಂ. 2-2-9-1.)

> तस्मै विशः स्वयमेवा नमंते यस्मिन् ब्रह्मा राजनि पूर्व एति ।

> *tasmai viśaḥ svayamevā namaṃte yasmin brahmā rājani pūrva eti |*

(Reference as printed: ಋ. ಸಂ. 4-50-8.)

> अवस्यवे यो वरिवः कृणोति ब्रह्मणे राजा तमवंति देवाः ॥

> *avasyave yo varivaḥ kṛṇoti brahmaṇe rājā tamavaṃti devāḥ ‖*

(Reference as printed: ಋ ಸಂ. 4-50-9.)

> सोमं राजानमवसेग्निं गीर्भिर्हनामहे ।
> आदित्यान्विष्णुं सूर्यं ब्रह्माणं च बृहस्पतिं ॥

> *somaṃ rājānamavasegniṃ gīrbhirhanāmahe |*
> *ādityānviṣṇuṃ sūryaṃ brahmāṇaṃ ca bṛhaspatiṃ ‖*

(Reference as printed: ಋ. ಸಂ. 10-141-3.)

In these and similar Ṛks Bṛhaspati is described as an officiating priest (ṛtvij).

In the Ṛgveda Bṛhaspati has a prominent place. Besides being praised in eleven hymns, he is also praised in two hymns (Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-49; 7-97) together with Indra as a dual deity. The word Bṛhaspati occurs about 120 times in the Ṛgveda. In many places Bṛhaspati and Brahmaṇaspati are praised interchangeably in one and the same mantra. Even so, Bṛhaspati and Brahmaṇaspati are different deities.

Some Western scholars, and some among modern scholars too, have expressed the opinion that both are one deity, because in Ṛ. Saṃ. hymn 2-23 Bṛhaspati and Brahmaṇaspati are praised interchangeably. But the ancient Veda commentators such as Yāska, Śaunaka and Sāyaṇa have said clearly that Bṛhaspati is one and Brahmaṇaspati another. Of the 19 Ṛks of the above-mentioned hymn Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-23, only 6 Ṛks, namely 1, 5, 9, 11, 17, 19, praise Bṛhaspati; the remaining 13 Ṛks are addressed to Brahmaṇaspati. Yāska too has given different derivations for the word Bṛhaspati and the word Brahmaṇaspati. Therefore Bṛhaspati is one and Brahmaṇaspati another.

### Printed p. 5

*Running head: Ṛgveda-saṃhitā, A. 2, A. 7, Va. 1 (as printed), p. 5.*

Bṛhaspati has a distinction wholly different from the other deities. While the other deities are all special forms of nature or of a power of nature, Bṛhaspati seems to belong to the field of man's moral and religious life. The Ṛgveda has many examples that this deity, protector of praise, hymn or prayer, exerts his influence on the decision, resolve or power of the minds of the gods. In the 98th hymn of the 10th Maṇḍala of the Ṛgveda there is an account of a history concerning the royal sons of the Kuru line, Devāpi and Śantanu. The writers of the Nirukta describe thus how very important Bṛhaspati's part in it is:

> अत्रेतिहासमाचक्षते। देवापिश्चार्ष्ट्षषेणः शंतनुश्च कौरवा भ्रातरा बभूवतुः।
> स शंतनुः कनीयानभिषेचयांचक्रे । देवापिस्तपः प्रतिपेदे । ततः
> शंतनो राज्ये द्वादश वर्षाणि देवो न ववर्ष । तमूचुब्राह्मणा
> अधर्मस्त्वया चरितो ज्येष्ठं भ्रातरमंतरित्याभिषेचितं तस्मात्ते देवो न
> वर्षतीति । स शंतनुर्देवापिं शिशिक्ष राज्येन । तमुवाच देवापिः
> पुरोहितस्तेऽसानि याजयानि च त्वेति ॥

> *atretihāsamācakṣate| devāpiścārṣṭṣaṣeṇaḥ śaṃtanuśca kauravā bhrātarā babhūvatuḥ|*
> *sa śaṃtanuḥ kanīyānabhiṣecayāṃcakre | devāpistapaḥ pratipede | tataḥ*
> *śaṃtano rājye dvādaśa varṣāṇi devo na vavarṣa | tamūcubrāhmaṇā*
> *adharmastvayā carito jyeṣṭhaṃ bhrātaramaṃtarityābhiṣecitaṃ tasmātte devo na*
> *varṣatīti | sa śaṃtanurdevāpiṃ śiśikṣa rājyena | tamuvāca devāpiḥ*
> *purohitaste 'sāni yājayāni ca tveti ‖*

(Ni. 2-11)

Explanation: In former times, in the Kuru line, the king named Ṛṣṭiṣeṇa had two sons, Devāpi and Śantanu. Of them Devāpi was the elder and Śantanu the younger. After their father Ṛṣṭiṣeṇa died, his elder son Devāpi wished to become king. But his younger brother Śantanu gave him no opportunity, desired to be the ruler of the kingdom himself, drove out his elder brother Devāpi, and began to rule himself. The elder brother Devāpi, distressed, went to the forest and passed his time practising austerity. While matters stood so, no rain fell in his country for 12 years and a great famine came. Then King Śantanu, reflecting what should be done about it, asked the advice of the Brāhmaṇas. The Brāhmaṇas said to the king: according to the Dharmaśāstra the king's eldest son is the one entitled to the kingdom; therefore the rule should have been exercised by your elder brother Devāpi. But you gave him no opportunity, drove him out and seized the kingdom. Because you are thus practising adharma, the rain-god does not rain in your kingdom. King Śantanu repented of the wrong he had done, called his elder brother Devāpi, who was practising austerity in the forest, and handed the kingdom over to him. But Devāpi said that he did not want the kingdom, that he would practise austerity in the forest and take refuge in the path of renunciation, and that Śantanu should perform a sacrifice to remove the drought that had arisen in the kingdom, in which he (Devāpi) would take up the priesthood and help bring about rain. The king agreed, and Devāpi took the part of the ṛtvij called Hotṛ in his sacrifice.

Bṛhaspati became the ṛtvij called Brahmā. In that sacrifice, performed for the sake of causing rain, Devāpi the Hotṛ addressed Bṛhaspati, who was Brahmā: "O Bṛhaspati, you have great influence over the minds of the gods; therefore teach me the mantras that win them over." Being so asked, Bṛhaspati agreed, and taught the Hotṛ Devāpi mantras containing the prayers of the deities needed for causing rain. When he made offerings to the deities with those mantras, rain fell abundantly, it is said. Therefore this hymn is also named the "rain-desiring hymn" (vṛṣṭikāma-sūkta). This matter, with some variations, is also told in Purāṇic works such as the Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4-20-7, the Mahābhārata Ādiparva, and the Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9-22-14 to 17.

In the 108th hymn of the 10th Maṇḍala of the Ṛg-saṃhitā there is another legend. The Asuras called Paṇis had carried off the cows of the gods (the water-bearing clouds) and hidden them in an impregnable fortress. Indra sent Saramā, the divine bitch, to search for them. Saramā came to the Paṇis, engaged them in dispute, and asked them to give back the stolen cows. The Paṇis said: "What is the power of Indra? We are great heroes and our weapons are very sharp; so the gods cannot conquer us, nor can the gods learn the place where the cows are kept. Therefore, if Saramā leaves the side of the gods and joins our company, we will give her whatever wealth she wants." Though they urged her in many ways, Saramā did not agree; she returned from there and told the gods all this. Then Bṛhaspati by his own power found out the place where the cows were hidden. Afterwards the gods subdued them and brought the cows back. Such is the legend.

In many such acts of daring Bṛhaspati's part is very important. In particular, for helping men with the rain they need, Bṛhaspati's help is essential. It is this kind of prayer that is seen especially in the hymns addressed to Bṛhaspati.

Let us examine closely what Bṛhaspati's qualities and achievements are, as described in the Ṛgveda. Bṛhaspati was born of Heaven and Earth, and is nourished by their greatness (Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-93-8). But in one place (2-23-17) it is said that Tvaṣṭṛ produced him. He is named the father of the gods (2-26-3); and it is also said (10-72-8) that the gods were produced from him, as sparks of fire fly from the blow of a smith's hammer.

He is called Brahmā (the ṛtvij named Brahmā) (10-141-3). He has a connection with the ṛtvijs (7-10-4; 10-14-3). He is also named Aṅgirasa (4-40-1; 4-73-1; 10-47-6).

### Printed p. 7

*Running head: Aṣṭaka 2, Adhyāya 7, Varga 1 (as printed, the Aṣṭaka numeral being "೮"), Ṛgveda-saṃhitā, p. 7.*

He composes and utters words of praise; he is their lord and impeller (2-23-1, 2; 1-40-5; 10-98-7); by the mantra alone he accomplishes all his aims (2-24-3); he mounts the shining chariot called sacrifice and sets out to conquer the enemies of the gods and of the worshippers (2-23-3). He is the nourisher and protector of the faithful, their guide; he rescues them from calamities (2-23-4). He grants them wealth and the like (2-23-9). He is called the father of all the gods; he is endowed with all divine qualities (3-62-4; 4-50-6). He is of all forms; all things that everyone desires are in him (7-10-4, 7-97-4); he bears a golden spear (7-97-7); together with Indra he is also leader of the armies (10-103-8). He is the increaser of life (10-100-5); plants have sprung from him (10-97-15 and 19); his vehicle is said to be tawny horses (Ni. 2-28).

When Trita, thrown into a well by demons and calling the gods to his help, cried out in distress, Bṛhaspati heard it and released him from that perilous state (1-105-17); and together with Indra Bṛhaspati made the water that had been wrapped in darkness flow again (2-23-18 ; 1-56-5): so it is said.

Bṛhaspati by his own power broke open the stone door of the well full of honey. The gods drank that honey and poured down rain abundantly (2-24-3, 4). When Bṛhaspati split the fortresses of Vala with fire-spitting rays, the herds of cows became visible; he drove the cows out as one breaks an egg; seeing the honey (water) enclosed in rock, he split it with his lion-roar and brought it out (10-68-4 to 9). Bṛhaspati brought the cows out from the rocks; he seized the cows of Vala and made them his own (10-68-5).

Bṛhaspati is endowed with divinity (2-23-2); he is the own of all the gods (3-62-4; 4-50-6); and among the gods he has the most divinity; in the form of a god he gathers into himself all the gods and all things (2-24-11; 8-61-18). Being capable, he keeps the two ends of the earth far apart (4-50-1). The rising of the sun and moon one after the other is also his inimitable deed (10-68-10). In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā (of the Yajurveda) a connection of Bṛhaspati with certain stars is stated: Bṛhaspati is the deity of the Puṣya star (Tai. Saṃ. 4-4-10-1). In the Purāṇas and similar works Bṛhaspati is described as Vācaspati and as the presiding deity of the planet Bṛhaspati (Jupiter).

Some scholars have held that the deities Bṛhaspati and Brahmaṇaspati are transformed forms of Agni. A scholar named M. Langlois, in his work the translation of the Ṛgveda,

Part I, pp. 249, 254, 578, has supported with many examples that these two deities are transformed forms of Agni. A scholar named Wilson, too, in the introduction to the first part of his translation of the Ṛgveda, has expressed the same opinion; and the scholar Max Muller also (says):

Brihaspati and Brahmanaspati are both varieties of Agni, the priest and the purohit of the Gods and men, and as such he is invoked together with the Maruts &c.

That is: Bṛhaspati and Brahmaṇaspati are both transformed forms of Agni, standing in the position of ṛtvij and purohita to gods and men; from this point of view they are invoked together with the Marut deities in many places. We give one or two examples stating this identity of Agni with Bṛhaspati and Brahmaṇaspati.

**॥ संहितापाठः ॥**

> त्वम॑ग्न॒ इंद्रो॑ वृष॒भः स॒ताम॑सि॒ त्वं विष्णु॑रुरुगा॒यो न॑म॒स्यः॑ ।
> त्वं ब्र॒ह्मा र॑यि॒विद्ब्र॑ह्मणस्पते॒ त्वं वि॑धर्तः सचसे॒ पुरं॑ध्या ॥

> *tvamagna iṃdro vṛṣabhaḥ satāmasi tvaṃ viṣṇururugāyo namasyaḥ |*
> *tvaṃ brahmā rayividbrahmaṇaspate tvaṃ vidhartaḥ sacase puraṃdhyā ‖*

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-1-3, printed as "ಪು. ಸಂ.")

"O Agni, you are Indra, the best of the good; you are Viṣṇu, worshipful, of vast body; O Brahmaṇaspati, you are the wealthy ṛtvij called Brahmā": thus Agni is described as the form of various deities.

**॥ संहितापाठः ॥**

> तं शुभ्रमग्निमवसे हवामहे वैश्वानरं मातरिश्वानमुक्थ्यं ।
> बृहस्पतिं मनुषो देवतातये विप्रं श्रोतारमतिथिं रघुष्यदं ॥

> *taṃ śubhramagnimavase havāmahe vaiśvānaraṃ mātariśvānamukthyaṃ |*
> *bṛhaspatiṃ manuṣo devatātaye vipraṃ śrotāramatithiṃ raghuṣyadaṃ ‖*

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-26-2)

"We invite for our protection the bright Agni, friend of the human race; Mātariśvan, worthy to be spoken of in mantras; the wise one, who praises the gods, who is like a guest, who is swift: Bṛhaspati too we invite to come to the sacrificial rites connected with men," and so on.

Sāyaṇācārya, while commenting on Ṛk 5-43-12, says of the word Bṛhaspati:

**॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥**

> अथवा इयमाग्नेया। बृहतः परिवृढस्य कर्मणः स्वामाति बृहस्पतिरग्निरुच्यते।
> तथा नीलवर्णधूमपृष्ठत्वसदनसादनहिरण्यवर्णत्वादिलिंगैरप्यग्निरेव बृहस्पतिः ॥

> *athavā iyamāgneyā| bṛhataḥ parivṛḍhasya karmaṇaḥ svāmāti bṛhaspatiragnirucyate|*
> *tathā nīlavarṇadhūmapṛṣṭhatvasadanasādanahiraṇyavarṇatvādiliṃgairapyagnireva bṛhaspatiḥ ‖*

That is: "Or this mantra may be said to be addressed to Agni. Because he is lord of the great, vast sacrificial work in hand, Agni is named Bṛhaspati. Likewise, black smoke is his back; the sacrificial altar is his seat; and by marks such as golden colour Bṛhaspati should be called Agni himself."
