Besides describing their great deeds with qualifying words, the deeds done by Bṛhaspati are described in the Ṛks addressed to Brahmaṇaspati, and the deeds of Brahmaṇaspati in the Ṛks addressed to Bṛhaspati, by mutual exchange. Therefore, although the names are different, the sage Gṛtsamada seems to have held the idea that these two deities are one and the same deity. As time went on this idea changed, and it seems to have given room to the idea that Bṛhaspati is one and Brahmaṇaspati another. Room for such changes is seen also in the case of other deities. For instance, in "mahān indraḥ" the word महान् (*mahān*) is an epithet of Indra, yet it is laid down that Indra is one deity and Mahān Indra another deity. In the same way this idea also fits the deities Agni, Agniḥ Śuciḥ, Agniḥ Pāvakaḥ, etc. This matter will be discussed in the last part of this Introduction.

The sage Yāska has said as follows about Brahmaṇaspati in his Nirukta:

> ब्रह्मणस्पतिः । ब्रह्मणस्पतिर्ब्रह्मणः पाता ना पालयिता ना ॥

> *brahmaṇaspatiḥ | brahmaṇaspatirbrahmaṇaḥ pātā nā pālayitā nā ‖*

(Reference as printed: ಸಿ. 10-14.)

Here the word ब्रह्म (*brahma*) (brahma) means food, wealth and hymn of praise (Ni. 3-9). Therefore ब्रह्मणः पाता (*brahmaṇaḥ pātā*) means "the protector of food", that is, the lord or master of food (nourishment). To show how Brahmaṇaspati is lord of food, Yāska gives as an example:

> अश्मास्यमवतं ब्रह्मणस्पतिर्मधुधारमभि यमोजसातृणत् ।
> तमेव विश्वे पपिरे स्वर्दृशो बहु साकं सिसिचुरुत्समुद्रिणं ॥

> *aśmāsyamavataṃ brahmaṇaspatirmadhudhāramabhi yamojasātṛṇat |*
> *tameva viśve papire svardṛśo bahu sākaṃ sisicurutsamudriṇaṃ ‖*

(Reference as printed: ಮಿ. ಸಂ. 2-24-4.)

giving this Ṛk as the example, and for it gives the explanation:

> अशनवंतमास्यंतनवंतमनातितं ब्रह्मणस्पतिर्मधुधारमभियमोजसा बले-
> नाभ्यतृणत्तमेव सर्वे पिबंति रश्मयः सूर्यदृशो बह्वेनं सह
> सिंचंत्युत्समुद्रिणमुदकवंतं ॥

> *aśanavaṃtamāsyaṃtanavaṃtamanātitaṃ brahmaṇaspatirmadhudhāramabhiyamojasā bale-*
> *nābhyatṛṇattameva sarve pibaṃti raśmayaḥ sūryadṛśo bahvenaṃ saha*
> *siṃcaṃtyutsamudriṇamudakavaṃtaṃ ‖*

(Reference as printed: ಸಿ. 10-13.)

The main purport is this: with the help of the sun's rays the clouds drink the water of the sea and cause that water to fall specially on the earth as rain; from the rain come the herbs and trees, and from the herbs and trees food and other nourishment is obtained; therefore Brahmaṇaspati is the lord of food. In the same way, when the word ब्रह्म (*brahma*) is taken to mean hymn of praise or wealth, one must say that Brahmaṇaspati is the lord of the mantras or of wealth.

The commentator (Sāyaṇa) has stated this very meaning when commenting on the Ṛk quoted above.

Mantras addressed to Brahmaṇaspati are numerous in the Ṛgveda. In those mantras it is said that Brahmaṇaspati gives wealth and food. Many people doubt whether this Brahmaṇaspati is a separate deity or a changed form of Agni. Since other deities such as Agni also give wealth and food, some argue that Brahmaṇaspati is a special form of Agni. Yāska is silent on this point, that is, he has said nothing clearly. Even in the Ṛks addressed to Brahmaṇaspati there is no authority whatever for saying that this deity is a changed form of Agni. Therefore it may be said freely that Brahmaṇaspati is a separate deity, like Indrāgnī.

In his Nirukta, explaining the derivation of the word Bṛhaspati, Yāska says:

> बृहस्पतिः बृहतः पाता वा पालयिता वा ।

> *bṛhaspatiḥ bṛhataḥ pātā vā pālayitā vā |*

(Reference as printed: ಸಿ. 10-11.)

On this the commentator Durgācārya says: "Why Bṛhaspati? 'Bṛhataḥ pātā or pālayitā': the protector, guardian of this great world or of water; पालयिता (*pālayitā*) also means protector only — the roots differ, the meaning is one." That is, he is the preserver of the great world or of water, the one who protects. He says that although for expressing the sense of the word पति (*pati*) (pati) there are words formed from different roots, पाता (*pātā*) and पालयिता (*pālayitā*), the meaning is only one.

In many places, explaining बृहतः पाता (*bṛhataḥ pātā*), Sāyaṇa gives for the word बृहतः (*bṛhataḥ*) the meaning बृहतः यज्ञाख्यकर्मणः (*bṛhataḥ yajñākhyakarmaṇaḥ*) or वृष्टाख्यकर्मणः (*vṛṣṭākhyakarmaṇaḥ*), that is, "of the rite in the form of sacrifice" or "of the rite in the form of granting rain". Since Bṛhaspati, as priest of the gods, performs the sacrificial rite, and since he brings about the rain that men need, both these epithets are natural. Yāska gives as an example the Ṛk describing Bṛhaspati's priesthood and his granting of rain:

> यद्देवापिः शंतनवे पुरोहितो होत्राय वृतः कृपयन्नदीधेत् ।
> देवश्रुतं वृष्टिवनिं रराणो बृहस्पतिर्वाचमस्मा अयच्छत् ॥

> *yaddevāpiḥ śaṃtanave purohito hotrāya vṛtaḥ kṛpayannadīdhet |*
> *devaśrutaṃ vṛṣṭivaniṃ rarāṇo bṛhaspatirvācamasmā ayacchat ‖*

(Reference as printed: ಮ. ಸಂ. 10-98-7.)

It is said that Bṛhaspati was the priest of the gods, and that Śaṇḍa and Marka were priests of the asuras.

> आदित्यान्विष्णुं सूर्यं ब्रह्माणं च बृहस्पतिं ॥

> *somaṃ rājānamavasegniṃ gīrbhirhanāmahe |*
> *ādityānviṣṇuṃ sūryaṃ brahmāṇaṃ ca bṛhaspatiṃ ‖*

(Reference as printed: ಋ. ಸಂ. 10-141-3.)

In these and similar Ṛks Bṛhaspati is described as an officiating priest (ṛtvij).

In the Ṛgveda Bṛhaspati has a prominent place. Besides being praised in eleven hymns, he is also praised in two hymns (Ṛ. Saṃ. 4-49; 7-97) together with Indra as a dual deity. The word Bṛhaspati occurs about 120 times in the Ṛgveda. In many places Bṛhaspati and Brahmaṇaspati are praised interchangeably in one and the same mantra. Even so, Bṛhaspati and Brahmaṇaspati are different deities.

Some Western scholars, and some among modern scholars too, have expressed the opinion that both are one deity, because in Ṛ. Saṃ. hymn 2-23 Bṛhaspati and Brahmaṇaspati are praised interchangeably. But the ancient Veda commentators such as Yāska, Śaunaka and Sāyaṇa have said clearly that Bṛhaspati is one and Brahmaṇaspati another. Of the 19 Ṛks of the above-mentioned hymn Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-23, only 6 Ṛks, namely 1, 5, 9, 11, 17, 19, praise Bṛhaspati; the remaining 13 Ṛks are addressed to Brahmaṇaspati. Yāska too has given different derivations for the word Bṛhaspati and the word Brahmaṇaspati. Therefore Bṛhaspati is one and Brahmaṇaspati another.

Bṛhaspati has a distinction wholly different from the other deities. While the other deities are all special forms of nature or of a power of nature, Bṛhaspati seems to belong to the field of man's moral and religious life. The Ṛgveda has many examples that this deity, protector of praise, hymn or prayer, exerts his influence on the decision, resolve or power of the minds of the gods. In the 98th hymn of the 10th Maṇḍala of the Ṛgveda there is an account of a history concerning the royal sons of the Kuru line, Devāpi and Śantanu. The writers of the Nirukta describe thus how very important Bṛhaspati's part in it is:

> अत्रेतिहासमाचक्षते। देवापिश्चार्ष्ट्षषेणः शंतनुश्च कौरवा भ्रातरा बभूवतुः।
> स शंतनुः कनीयानभिषेचयांचक्रे । देवापिस्तपः प्रतिपेदे । ततः
> शंतनो राज्ये द्वादश वर्षाणि देवो न ववर्ष । तमूचुब्राह्मणा
> अधर्मस्त्वया चरितो ज्येष्ठं भ्रातरमंतरित्याभिषेचितं तस्मात्ते देवो न
> वर्षतीति । स शंतनुर्देवापिं शिशिक्ष राज्येन । तमुवाच देवापिः
> पुरोहितस्तेऽसानि याजयानि च त्वेति ॥

> *atretihāsamācakṣate| devāpiścārṣṭṣaṣeṇaḥ śaṃtanuśca kauravā bhrātarā babhūvatuḥ|*
> *sa śaṃtanuḥ kanīyānabhiṣecayāṃcakre | devāpistapaḥ pratipede | tataḥ*
> *śaṃtano rājye dvādaśa varṣāṇi devo na vavarṣa | tamūcubrāhmaṇā*
> *adharmastvayā carito jyeṣṭhaṃ bhrātaramaṃtarityābhiṣecitaṃ tasmātte devo na*
> *varṣatīti | sa śaṃtanurdevāpiṃ śiśikṣa rājyena | tamuvāca devāpiḥ*
> *purohitaste 'sāni yājayāni ca tveti ‖*

(Ni. 2-11)

Explanation: In former times, in the Kuru line, the king named Ṛṣṭiṣeṇa had two sons, Devāpi and Śantanu. Of them Devāpi was the elder and Śantanu the younger. After their father Ṛṣṭiṣeṇa died, his elder son Devāpi wished to become king. But his younger brother Śantanu gave him no opportunity, desired to be the ruler of the kingdom himself, drove out his elder brother Devāpi, and began to rule himself. The elder brother Devāpi, distressed, went to the forest and passed his time practising austerity. While matters stood so, no rain fell in his country for 12 years and a great famine came. Then King Śantanu, reflecting what should be done about it, asked the advice of the Brāhmaṇas. The Brāhmaṇas said to the king: according to the Dharmaśāstra the king's eldest son is the one entitled to the kingdom; therefore the rule should have been exercised by your elder brother Devāpi. But you gave him no opportunity, drove him out and seized the kingdom. Because you are thus practising adharma, the rain-god does not rain in your kingdom. King Śantanu repented of the wrong he had done, called his elder brother Devāpi, who was practising austerity in the forest, and handed the kingdom over to him. But Devāpi said that he did not want the kingdom, that he would practise austerity in the forest and take refuge in the path of renunciation, and that Śantanu should perform a sacrifice to remove the drought that had arisen in the kingdom, in which he (Devāpi) would take up the priesthood and help bring about rain. The king agreed, and Devāpi took the part of the ṛtvij called Hotṛ in his sacrifice.

Bṛhaspati became the ṛtvij called Brahmā. In that sacrifice, performed for the sake of causing rain, Devāpi the Hotṛ addressed Bṛhaspati, who was Brahmā: "O Bṛhaspati, you have great influence over the minds of the gods; therefore teach me the mantras that win them over." Being so asked, Bṛhaspati agreed, and taught the Hotṛ Devāpi mantras containing the prayers of the deities needed for causing rain. When he made offerings to the deities with those mantras, rain fell abundantly, it is said. Therefore this hymn is also named the "rain-desiring hymn" (vṛṣṭikāma-sūkta). This matter, with some variations, is also told in Purāṇic works such as the Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4-20-7, the Mahābhārata Ādiparva, and the Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9-22-14 to 17.

In the 108th hymn of the 10th Maṇḍala of the Ṛg-saṃhitā there is another legend. The Asuras called Paṇis had carried off the cows of the gods (the water-bearing clouds) and hidden them in an impregnable fortress. Indra sent Saramā, the divine bitch, to search for them. Saramā came to the Paṇis, engaged them in dispute, and asked them to give back the stolen cows. The Paṇis said: "What is the power of Indra? We are great heroes and our weapons are very sharp; so the gods cannot conquer us, nor can the gods learn the place where the cows are kept. Therefore, if Saramā leaves the side of the gods and joins our company, we will give her whatever wealth she wants." Though they urged her in many ways, Saramā did not agree; she returned from there and told the gods all this. Then Bṛhaspati by his own power found out the place where the cows were hidden. Afterwards the gods subdued them and brought the cows back. Such is the legend.

In many such acts of daring Bṛhaspati's part is very important. In particular, for helping men with the rain they need, Bṛhaspati's help is essential. It is this kind of prayer that is seen especially in the hymns addressed to Bṛhaspati.

Let us examine closely what Bṛhaspati's qualities and achievements are, as described in the Ṛgveda. Bṛhaspati was born of Heaven and Earth, and is nourished by their greatness (Ṛ. Saṃ. 7-93-8). But in one place (2-23-17) it is said that Tvaṣṭṛ produced him. He is named the father of the gods (2-26-3); and it is also said (10-72-8) that the gods were produced from him, as sparks of fire fly from the blow of a smith's hammer.

He is called Brahmā (the ṛtvij named Brahmā) (10-141-3). He has a connection with the ṛtvijs (7-10-4; 10-14-3). He is also named Aṅgirasa (4-40-1; 4-73-1; 10-47-6).

He composes and utters words of praise; he is their lord and impeller (2-23-1, 2; 1-40-5; 10-98-7); by the mantra alone he accomplishes all his aims (2-24-3); he mounts the shining chariot called sacrifice and sets out to conquer the enemies of the gods and of the worshippers (2-23-3). He is the nourisher and protector of the faithful, their guide; he rescues them from calamities (2-23-4). He grants them wealth and the like (2-23-9). He is called the father of all the gods; he is endowed with all divine qualities (3-62-4; 4-50-6). He is of all forms; all things that everyone desires are in him (7-10-4, 7-97-4); he bears a golden spear (7-97-7); together with Indra he is also leader of the armies (10-103-8). He is the increaser of life (10-100-5); plants have sprung from him (10-97-15 and 19); his vehicle is said to be tawny horses (Ni. 2-28).

When Trita, thrown into a well by demons and calling the gods to his help, cried out in distress, Bṛhaspati heard it and released him from that perilous state (1-105-17); and together with Indra Bṛhaspati made the water that had been wrapped in darkness flow again (2-23-18 ; 1-56-5): so it is said.

Bṛhaspati by his own power broke open the stone door of the well full of honey. The gods drank that honey and poured down rain abundantly (2-24-3, 4). When Bṛhaspati split the fortresses of Vala with fire-spitting rays, the herds of cows became visible; he drove the cows out as one breaks an egg; seeing the honey (water) enclosed in rock, he split it with his lion-roar and brought it out (10-68-4 to 9). Bṛhaspati brought the cows out from the rocks; he seized the cows of Vala and made them his own (10-68-5).

Bṛhaspati is endowed with divinity (2-23-2); he is the own of all the gods (3-62-4; 4-50-6); and among the gods he has the most divinity; in the form of a god he gathers into himself all the gods and all things (2-24-11; 8-61-18). Being capable, he keeps the two ends of the earth far apart (4-50-1). The rising of the sun and moon one after the other is also his inimitable deed (10-68-10). In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā (of the Yajurveda) a connection of Bṛhaspati with certain stars is stated: Bṛhaspati is the deity of the Puṣya star (Tai. Saṃ. 4-4-10-1). In the Purāṇas and similar works Bṛhaspati is described as Vācaspati and as the presiding deity of the planet Bṛhaspati (Jupiter).

Part I, pp. 249, 254, 578, has supported with many examples that these two deities are transformed forms of Agni. A scholar named Wilson, too, in the introduction to the first part of his translation of the Ṛgveda, has expressed the same opinion; and the scholar Max Muller also (says):

That is: Bṛhaspati and Brahmaṇaspati are both transformed forms of Agni, standing in the position of ṛtvij and purohita to gods and men; from this point of view they are invoked together with the Marut deities in many places. We give one or two examples stating this identity of Agni with Bṛhaspati and Brahmaṇaspati.

**॥ संहितापाठः ॥**

> त्वम॑ग्न॒ इंद्रो॑ वृष॒भः स॒ताम॑सि॒ त्वं विष्णु॑रुरुगा॒यो न॑म॒स्यः॑ ।
> त्वं ब्र॒ह्मा र॑यि॒विद्ब्र॑ह्मणस्पते॒ त्वं वि॑धर्तः सचसे॒ पुरं॑ध्या ॥

> *tvamagna iṃdro vṛṣabhaḥ satāmasi tvaṃ viṣṇururugāyo namasyaḥ |*
> *tvaṃ brahmā rayividbrahmaṇaspate tvaṃ vidhartaḥ sacase puraṃdhyā ‖*

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-1-3, printed as "ಪು. ಸಂ.")

"O Agni, you are Indra, the best of the good; you are Viṣṇu, worshipful, of vast body; O Brahmaṇaspati, you are the wealthy ṛtvij called Brahmā": thus Agni is described as the form of various deities.

**॥ संहितापाठः ॥**

> तं शुभ्रमग्निमवसे हवामहे वैश्वानरं मातरिश्वानमुक्थ्यं ।
> बृहस्पतिं मनुषो देवतातये विप्रं श्रोतारमतिथिं रघुष्यदं ॥

> *taṃ śubhramagnimavase havāmahe vaiśvānaraṃ mātariśvānamukthyaṃ |*
> *bṛhaspatiṃ manuṣo devatātaye vipraṃ śrotāramatithiṃ raghuṣyadaṃ ‖*

(Ṛ. Saṃ. 3-26-2)

"We invite for our protection the bright Agni, friend of the human race; Mātariśvan, worthy to be spoken of in mantras; the wise one, who praises the gods, who is like a guest, who is swift: Bṛhaspati too we invite to come to the sacrificial rites connected with men," and so on.

Sāyaṇācārya, while commenting on Ṛk 5-43-12, says of the word Bṛhaspati:

**॥ सायणभाष्यम् ॥**

> अथवा इयमाग्नेया। बृहतः परिवृढस्य कर्मणः स्वामाति बृहस्पतिरग्निरुच्यते।
> तथा नीलवर्णधूमपृष्ठत्वसदनसादनहिरण्यवर्णत्वादिलिंगैरप्यग्निरेव बृहस्पतिः ॥

> *athavā iyamāgneyā| bṛhataḥ parivṛḍhasya karmaṇaḥ svāmāti bṛhaspatiragnirucyate|*
> *tathā nīlavarṇadhūmapṛṣṭhatvasadanasādanahiraṇyavarṇatvādiliṃgairapyagnireva bṛhaspatiḥ ‖*

That is: "Or this mantra may be said to be addressed to Agni. Because he is lord of the great, vast sacrificial work in hand, Agni is named Bṛhaspati. Likewise, black smoke is his back; the sacrificial altar is his seat; and by marks such as golden colour Bṛhaspati should be called Agni himself."

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

सः इमां (*saḥ imāṃ*)—Here the two words सः इमां (*saḥ imāṃ*) have joined, and instead of स इमां (*sa imāṃ*) the form is सेमां (*semāṃ*); that is, the visarga after the *sa* has been dropped. Likewise, in this same mantra the words सः उत (*saḥ uta*) have joined to become सोत (*sota*). Such examples are many in the Ṛgveda.

सेदग्ने अस्तु सुभगः सदानुर्यस्त्वा नित्येन हविषा य उक्षैः । (*sedagne astu subhagaḥ sadānuryastvā nityena haviṣā ya ukṣaiḥ |*) (Pu. Saṃ. 4-4-7; reference as supplied; no gloss is given in the source)

सेद्यभवो यमवध यूयमिंद्रश्च मर्त्यं । (*sedyabhavo yamavadha yūyamiṃdraśca martyaṃ |*) (Pu. Saṃ. 4-37-6; reference as supplied; no gloss is given in the source)

In these verses (printed "ಮಕ್ಕಳಲ್ಲಿ", "in these children", sic as supplied) the words सः इत् (*saḥ it*) have joined to become सेत् (*set*).

सैनानीकेन गुविदत्रो अस्मे यष्टा देवा आयुजिष्ठः स्वस्ति । (*sainānīkena guvidatro asme yaṣṭā devā āyujiṣṭhaḥ svasti |*) (Ma. Saṃ. 2-9-6; reference as supplied; no gloss is given in the source)

In this ṛk the words सः एना (*saḥ enā*) have joined to become सैना (*sainā*). Since such usages do not accord with grammar, it is customary to call them Vedic (*chāndasa*) usages.

प्रभृतिं (*prabhṛtiṃ*)—प्रकर्षण बृतां स्तुतिं (*prakarṣaṇa bṛtāṃ stutiṃ*): the praise that we bear (hold), that is, make, in a very excellent manner. The word is formed from the root भृञ् भरणे (*bhṛñ bharaṇe*) preceded by the prefix प्र (*pra*). In the Ṛgveda this root is commonly used in the sense of praising in a special manner. Therefore the commentator explains the word *prabhṛti* as praise made in a special manner.

ईशिषे (*īśiṣe*)—सर्वस्य जगत ईश्वरो भवसि (*sarvasya jagata īśvaro bhavasi*): "you are the lord of the whole world", is the commentator's explanation. That is, all the gods, human beings, animals and the plant kingdom are under his control; therefore it is natural to say that you are the master of all. This idea is stated in such ṛks as Ṛ. Saṃ. 2-24-11; 4-50-1; 10-68-10; 10-97-15

### Printed p. 13

*Running head: [8. 2. 8. 7. 1.] Ṛgveda-saṃhitā, p. 13 (as supplied).*

and so on. Since the word *bṛhaspati* means lord of the mantras, Bṛhaspati accomplishes all his works through mantras alone: splitting the clouds, sending rain, driving away darkness, destroying enemies, and so on, he does by mantras only. Therefore the Ṛgveda has the very sentence बृहस्पतिर्हंत्यमित्रमर्कॆः (*bṛhaspatirhaṃtyamitramarkèḥ*) (Bṛhaspati destroys the enemy by the power of mantra alone).

अया (*ayā*)—अनया (*anayā*). The *n* of the word अनया (*anayā*) has been dropped, giving आया (*āyā*) [sic as supplied]. This is a Vedic usage.

अया नवया महा गिरा विधेम (*ayā navayā mahā girā vidhema*)—अनया नूतनया महत्या वेदरूपया स्तुतिलक्षणया वाचा परिचरेम (*anayā nūtanayā mahatyā vedarūpayā stutilakṣaṇayā vācā paricarema*): "we serve with this new, great word in the form of the Veda, having the nature of praise". The sense is: O Bṛhaspati, the praise we are now making is newly composed by us, that is, not seen (revealed) by earlier ṛṣis; and it is such as extols your qualities, which are महत्तूरितवाद (*mahattūritavāda*) [?] (the supplied word is unclear). With such praise-sentences in the form of the Veda we praise you.

मीढ्वान् (*mīḍhvān*)—सेक्ता स्तुतेर्वषिता (*sektā stutervaṣitā*). Although the word *mīḍhvān* is used of Indra and other gods in the sense कामानां वर्षिता (*kāmānāṃ varṣitā*), "one who showers desired objects", here it is used as an epithet of the sacrificer who praises. "One who showers praise" means the sacrificer who praises Bṛhaspati in a special manner.

तव सखा (*tava sakhā*)—मित्रभूतः (*mitrabhūtaḥ*): one who does good like a friend. Bṛhaspati is one who does favour, like a friend, to the sacrificer who praises him. That doing good or favour is the chief quality of a friend is stated by a Vedic sentence itself: न स सखा यो न ददाति सख्ये (*na sa sakhā yo na dadāti sakhye*) (Ṛ. Saṃ. 10-117-4): the man who gives nothing to his friend, that is, who does not help in time of trouble, is no friend at all. Here, because Bṛhaspati gives desired objects to the sacrificer who praises him, he is praised with the word *sakhā* (friend):

यो रेवान् यो अमीवहा वसुवित्सुष्टिवर्धनः । (*yo revān yo amīvahā vasuvitsuṣṭivardhanaḥ |*)
स नः सिषक्तु यस्तुरः ॥ (*sa naḥ siṣaktu yasturaḥ ‖*)
(Ma. 1-18-2)

That is, Bṛhaspati is the giver of wealth, nourishment and riches to him who praises him, and the remover of disease and affliction. Such a Bṛhaspati comes quickly to give desired objects to the sacrificer who praises him.

Since those who come under punishment are wicked doers of evil deeds, the commentator has given the word *nantvāni* the meaning "demons, asuras".

The idea is that he angrily split the clouds, which had held the waters back in themselves without raining, and made them rain.

*manyunā*—For the word *manyu* Yāska gives the derivation मन्युर्मन्यतेर्दिप्तिकर्मणः क्रोधकर्मणो वधकर्मणो वा मन्यंत्यस्मादिषवः (*manyurmanyaterdiptikarmaṇaḥ krodhakarmaṇo vadhakarmaṇo vā manyaṃtyasmādiṣavaḥ*) (Ni. 10-29): that is, he says that the word *manyu*, formed from the root *manyati*, has the sense of illumining, of anger, or of slaying. He also says that *manyu* may be the name of a deity of that name, and in support of this cites the Ṛk—

⟦ತ್ವಯಾ ಮನ್ಯೋ ಸರಥಮಾರುಜಂತೋ ಹರ್ಷಮಾಣಾಸೋ ಧೃಷಿತಾ ಮರುತ್ವಃ ।

(Pu. Saṃ. 10-84-1)

The deity Manyu is praised in the 83rd and 84th sūktas of the 10th Maṇḍala.

*śambarāṇi*—The word *śambara* is read among the names of the cloud (Ni. 2-21). For the word *śambara*, formed from the root *śamu upaśame*, the commentary says: शमयति नाशयति आसुरानिति शंबो वज्रः । शंबोऽस्य प्रहर्तृत्वेनास्ति । रो मत्त्वर्थीयः । प्रहरति हि वज्रः इंद्रप्रेरितो मेघान् (*śamayati nāśayati āsurāniti śaṃbo vajraḥ | śaṃbo 'sya prahartṛtvenāsti | ro mattvarthīyaḥ | praharati hi vajraḥ iṃdraprerito meghān*) (*śamba* is the thunderbolt, because it pacifies, that is destroys, the asuras; it is his as striker; the suffix *ra* is in the sense of possession; for the thunderbolt, impelled by Indra, strikes the clouds). Because it destroys asuras and others, Indra's thunderbolt is called *śamba*; since that thunderbolt, impelled by Indra, splits the clouds for rain, the cloud is called *śambara*. Or: यद्वा संपूर्वात् वृणोतेः इत्यपि संबरः सन् वर्णव्यतयेन शंबरः । संवियते मेघेनाकाशं इति शंबरः (*yadvā saṃpūrvāt vṛṇoteḥ ityapi saṃbaraḥ san varṇavyatayena śaṃbaraḥ | saṃviyate meghenākāśaṃ iti śaṃbaraḥ*) (or, from *vṛṇoti* with the prefix *saṃ*, it is *sambara*, and by interchange of letter *śambara*; the sky is covered by the cloud, hence *śambara*). That is, the word *sambara* may be formed from the root *vṛṇoti*, "to cover", with the prefix *saṃ*; by a change of letter *sambaraḥ* becomes *śambaraḥ*; since the sky is covered by the cloud, the cloud is called *śambara*. Or *śambara* may also mean water; the word *śambara* is also read among the 101 [as supplied] names of water (Ni. 2-24). Since such water is in this cloud, the cloud is called *śambara*.

Or, if the word *devatama* is taken to mean "most excellent of the gods", the words देवानां देवतमाय (*devānāṃ devatamāya*) suggest the sense "one who is like the most excellent even among the gods", "god of gods"; just as one who is the foremost among kings is called "king of kings", so Brahmaṇaspati is described here by the words देवानां देवतमाय (*devānāṃ devatamāya*). अश्मास्यं (*aśmāsyaṃ*)—the text gives अश्ववद्दृढतरास्यं (*aśvavaddṛḍhatarāsyaṃ*) (as supplied; the bhāṣya has अश्नवद्दृढतरास्यं (*aśnavaddṛḍhatarāsyaṃ*)); Sāyaṇa's explanation is "very firm like a stone". The main idea is: this Brahmaṇaspati struck, with his power, the cloud that is full of water and hard as a stone, and made that cloud pour its sweet water downward on the earth. The rays of the sun drink up (suck up) that very water, that is, the water lying on the earth, and again pour it on the earth in the form of rain. When the sun's sharp rays fall upon the sea, rivers, lakes and other reservoirs on the earth, a part of the water in them is heated by the heat of the rays, spreads in the atmosphere as vapour and appears in the form of cloud. That this cloud again rains the water it holds upon the earth is known to all. Modern science also supports this. Therefore the origin of cloud and the causes of rain stated here accord with experience and with science.

By reciting meaningful mantras in sacrifices and the like, rain falls, as the fruit of it, throughout the year, in all the months, that is, during the greater part of the year. The main idea is that the water stored in the clouds is released by the recitation of meaningful mantras in sacrifices and the like, and falls on the earth in the form of rain. Here the word वयुन (*vayuna*) is read among the names of wisdom (Nir. 3-13), the word भुवन (*bhuvana*) among the names of water (Nir. 2-24); the word शरत् (*śarat*) is used in the sense of year (Nir. 4-26).

The doors of the water that is to rain in future (the clouds) are broken open in the respective months or years and made to pour rain; that is, this Brahmaṇaspati, by his power of mantra, makes the clouds pour rain at the proper time, and makes heaven and earth dependent on one another. In due season rain [the sentence continues on p. 25]

(continued from p. 24) ... falls, plants and the like grow well on the earth, and with their help the people are enabled to perform sacrifices and the like. The gods in the heaven-world accept the oblations and the like given by men, are satisfied, and bring about rain. This in turn favours the sacrifices and the like. Therefore the heaven-world's pouring of rain, and the earth's producing plants and the like and helping the sacrificial acts, is called the mutual dependence of heaven and earth. Since this work goes on without effort on the part of heaven and earth, the main idea is that the power of mantra, or the greatness, of Brahmaṇaspati is the cause of such works.

In this sentence the words अभिनक्षंतः ये (*abhinakṣaṃtaḥ ye*) mean only "those who go searching", but since in many contexts it is said that those who search for the cows in this way are the Aṅgirā sages, it must be inferred that here too the Aṅgirā sages are meant. Although the word पणीनां (*paṇīnāṃ*) is in the genitive case, the bhāṣyakāra explains that it must be taken in the sense of the instrumental case, that is, in the sense "by the Paṇis, the Asuras". The word निधिं (*nidhiṃ*) means the wealth in the form of a herd of cows. For, since it is said plainly पणीनां (पणिभिः) गुहा हितं (*paṇīnāṃ (paṇibhiḥ) guhā hitaṃ*) ("the treasure hidden by the Paṇis in the cave"), and since the Paṇis had hidden only cows, the word निधि (*nidhi*) here means wealth in the form of cows, that is, the cows themselves.

The sense is: the Aṅgirā sages knew by their discernment the cave in which the Paṇis had hidden the cows; even so, they had already looked at that cave and examined it beforehand and learned where and how it should be entered. Accordingly they came again to a place convenient for entering that cave first.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

ब्रह्मणस्पतिः ऋतज्येन क्षिप्रेण धन्वना यत्र वष्टि तत् प्र अश्नोति (*brahmaṇaspatiḥ ṛtajyena kṣipreṇa dhanvanā yatra vaṣṭi tat pra aśnoti*)—सत्यमेव ज्या यस्य तेन इषूणां क्षेपकेण धनुषा ब्रह्मणस्पतिर्ब्रह्मणो मंत्रस्य पालयिता देवः यस्मिन् विषये कामयते तत्पकर्षेण प्राप्नोति (*satyameva jyā yasya tena iṣūṇāṃ kṣepakeṇa dhanuṣā brahmaṇaspatirbrahmaṇo maṃtrasya pālayitā devaḥ yasmin viṣaye kāmayate tatpakarṣeṇa prāpnoti*). Brahmaṇaspati is the god who protects the mantras; that is, with mantras he can accomplish any undertaking. The bowstring (ज्या (*jyā*)) of his bow is of the nature of truth; that is, truth itself is the string (cord) of his bow. Fitting such a string called truth to his bow, with arrows shot in quick succession (that is, with mantras), whatever matter Brahmaṇaspati aims at he accomplishes without fail. He does not miss the mark he has set; that is, he does not leave unaccomplished an undertaking he has resolved to carry out. Here the arrows are mantras, or the oblation offered to the gods. As the English translator Wilson says, "The arrows are oblations and mantras": the arrows are the oblation offered by him and the mantras uttered.

There is nothing that cannot be accomplished by these. Therefore they carry the qualifying expression साद्वी8 (*sādvī8*) [?] (so in the supplied text; evidently साध्वीः (*sādhvīḥ*)), साधिकाः (*sādhikāḥ*), that is, "accomplishing the intended work".

नृचक्षसः कर्णयोनयः—नृन्‌पश्यतः, सर्वज्ञस्य बृहस्पतेः, श्रोत्रेंद्रियेण ग्राह्याः मंत्रभूता आकर्णकृष्टा वा बाणाः । ब्रह्मणस्पतिः मंत्रैरेव सर्वं साधयतीति भावः (*nṛcakṣasaḥ karṇayonayaḥ—nṛnpaśyataḥ, sarvajñasya bṛhaspateḥ, śrotreṃdriyeṇa grāhyāḥ maṃtrabhūtā ākarṇakṛṣṭā vā bāṇāḥ | brahmaṇaspatiḥ maṃtraireva sarvaṃ sādhayatīti bhāvaḥ*). Sāyaṇa understands नृचक्षसः (*nṛcakṣasaḥ*) as "seeing men", that is, "all-knowing", and कर्णयोनयः (*karṇayonayaḥ*) as "perceptible by the organ of hearing, of the nature of mantras, or arrows drawn back to the ear". Here he has applied नृचक्षसः (*nṛcakṣasaḥ*) to Brahmaṇaspati and stated his omniscience. But if we take the view of the English scholars cited above, the arrows are of two forms, oblation and mantra. Of these the oblations are नृचक्षसः (*nṛcakṣasaḥ*), visible to men, and the mantras are कर्णयोनयः (*karṇayonayaḥ*), to be heard by the ears of men [the Kannada reads किविगैकेळसतक्कवु (*kivigaikeḷasatakkavu*), apparently a misreading of "to be heard by the ear"]. Thus the mantras employed by Brahmaṇaspati and the oblations given are capable of doing the intended work. This Sāyaṇācārya has explained by the sentence ब्रह्मणस्पतिः मंत्रेणैव सर्वं साधयति (*brahmaṇaspatiḥ maṃtreṇaiva sarvaṃ sādhayati*) ("Brahmaṇaspati accomplishes everything by mantra alone"). The text ends with a numeral-like mark (as supplied: "-௨௰௪-", i.e. apparently the Sūkta number 24; not read further).

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

पुरोहितः ब्रह्मणस्पतिः संनयः (*purohitaḥ brahmaṇaspatiḥ saṃnayaḥ*)—"he brings together, by mantras, those who have been separated." Yāska explains the word *purohita* as "they place him in front" (Ni. 2-12). Sāyaṇa says "set in front by the gods": the gods keep Brahmaṇaspati before them. He describes this Brahmaṇaspati as one who, by the power of his mantras, brings together in one place those who are separated from one another. Further, विनयः (*vinayaḥ*) (the text has संगशानां (*saṃgaśānāṃ*) [?], evidently for the bhāṣya's संगतानां (*saṃgatānāṃ*)): "he too is the one who separates those who are joined" — he explains that, by reciting mantras, he can make those who are together, through affection or other causes, live apart.

सुष्टुतः युधि सः (*suṣṭutaḥ yudhi saḥ*) (the text has स्तोत्यणामनुग्रहाय प्रादुर्भवति (*stotyaṇāmanugrahāya prādurbhavati*)): being praised, he appears in person, to show his favour, when battle comes upon them.

For the word चाक्षृः (*cākṣṛḥ*) (so in the supplied text; the Saṃhitā and Pada have चाक्ष्मो (*cākṣmo*) / चाक्ष्मः (*cākṣmaḥ*)) the explanation is सर्वस्य द्रष्टा सर्वसहो ना (*sarvasya draṣṭā sarvasaho nā*): Brahmaṇaspati sees everything directly and endures all kinds of sorrows and calamities. Then वाजं मती धना भरते (*vājaṃ matī dhanā bharate*) is explained: for those who praise him he makes food and excellent wealth increase, bringing rain-waters, and so he nourishes them.

From this: since the sacrifice is accomplished by the foods and wealth given by Brahmaṇaspati, the burning sun is left merely enjoying the oblations himself (केवलं हवींषि भुंजान एव वर्तते (*kevalaṃ havīṃṣi bhuṃjāna eva vartate*)). That is, because Brahmaṇaspati carries out the sacrifices of the sacrificers with foods and wealth, the sun, who illumines and protects everything, merely receives his shares of the oblations and stays idle; this states the extent of Brahmaṇaspati's nourishing of those who depend on him.

उत (*uta*)—and; आशिष्ठाः (*āśiṣṭhāḥ*)—swift-going; वह्नयः (*vahnayaḥ*)—horses; अनु शृण्वंति (*anu śṛṇvaṃti*)—may they listen to our praises; सभेयः (*sabheyaḥ*)—excellent in the assembly; विप्रः (*vipraḥ*)—and wise, the Adhvaryu; मती (*matī*)—with a hymn worthy of thought; धना (*dhanā*)—the wealth in the form of oblations; भरते (*bharate*)—offers; वीळुद्देषाः (*vīḷuddeṣāḥ*)—Brahmaṇaspati, who hates the mighty demons; वशा (*vaśā*)—produced from the cow; ऋणं (*ṛṇaṃ*)—and offered by the sacrificers, the avadāna (offering); अनु आददिः (*anu ādadiḥ*)—may he accept (for the reason that); स ह ब्रह्मणस्पतिः (*sa ha brahmaṇaspatiḥ*)—that Brahmaṇaspati indeed; समिथे (*samithe*)—in this sacrifice; वाजी (*vājī*)—is possessed of food.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

May the swift horses of Brahmaṇaspati listen to our praises. The Adhvaryu, excellent in the assembly and wise, offers with a hymn worthy of thought the wealth in the form of oblations. May Brahmaṇaspati, who hates the mighty demons, accept the avadāna (the oblation), which is produced from the cow and offered by the sacrificers. For that Brahmaṇaspati is indeed possessed of food at this sacrifice.

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

आशिष्ठा वयः अनु शृण्वंति— (*āśiṣṭhā vayaḥ anu śṛṇvaṃti—*) शीघ्रगामिनः ब्रह्मणस्पतेरश्वाः अस्माभिः कृतं (*śīghragāminaḥ brahmaṇaspateraśvāḥ asmābhiḥ kṛtaṃ*) स्तोत्रं अनुक्रमेण शृण्वंति । (*stotraṃ anukrameṇa śṛṇvaṃti |*) यद्वा ब्रह्मणस्पतिना कृतमनुशासनं शृण्वंति । (*yadvā brahmaṇaspatinā kṛtamanuśāsanaṃ śṛṇvaṃti |*) The horses of Brahmaṇaspati, moving with speed, listen to the praises of Brahmaṇaspati recited by the sacrificers or by the priests; or they listen to the command of Brahmaṇaspati. Therefore, the prayer of the sacrificers is stated: may those horses quickly bring Brahmaṇaspati to our sacrifice.

विप्रः मती धना भरते—मेधावी अध्वर्युः होता वा मननीयेन स्तोत्रेण (*vipraḥ matī dhanā bharate—medhāvī adhvaryuḥ hotā vā mananīyena stotreṇa*) हनिर्लक्षणानि धनानि पोषयति । (*hanirlakṣaṇāni dhanāni poṣayati |*) The word विप्र (*vipra*) is stated by Yāska (Ni. 3-19) among the names of the wise. Sāyaṇa gives two kinds of sense: that the wise Adhvaryu, or the priest called Hotṛ, recites excellent praises and gathers this abundant wealth for the sacrifice of Brahmaṇaspati; or that by praiseworthy hymns he wins the favour of Brahmaṇaspati (by his grace) and increases the wealth so acquired.

वीळुद्वेषाः वशा ऋणमाददिः— (*vīḷudveṣāḥ vaśā ṛṇamādadiḥ—*) Here the word वीळु (*vīḷu*) is read by Yāska among the names of strength (Ni. 3-9). By it the meaning is given that Brahmaṇaspati दृढान् राक्षसार्दी द्वेष्टि (*dṛḍhān rākṣasārdī dveṣṭi*) (as supplied), that is, subdues the demons and the like, who have abundant power. Thus Brahmaṇaspati, possessed of such greatness, अस्माभिः यज्ज्वभिः प्रदेयं अवदानात्मकं (हविः) (*asmābhiḥ yajjvabhiḥ pradeyaṃ avadānātmakaṃ (haviḥ)*) अनुक्रमेण आदाता भवतु (*anukrameṇa ādātā bhavatu*): the oblations that the officiating priests who perform the homa offer for him in the sacrifices (the materials given as a debt) are prayed to be accepted, as if a debt due were accepted without fail.

Here it may be said that the oblation-portion offered for Brahmaṇaspati is of the nature of a debt, and, since it must necessarily be offered, it is like a debt. Having explained the meaning thus, Sāyaṇa quotes: अवदानस्य ऋणत्वं च—जायमानो वै ब्राह्मणस्त्रिभिर्यणवा (*avadānasya ṛṇatvaṃ ca—jāyamāno vai brāhmaṇastribhiryaṇavā*) (the passage continues on the next page; the window ends here).

जायते ब्रह्मचर्यण ऋषिभ्यो यज्ञेन देवेभ्यः प्रजया पितृभ्य एष वा अनृणो यः पुत्री यज्वा ब्रह्मचारिवासी (*jāyate brahmacaryaṇa ṛṣibhyo yajñena devebhyaḥ prajayā pitṛbhya eṣa vā anṛṇo yaḥ putrī yajvā brahmacārivāsī*) (Tai. mahi—great. karma—the work. kariṣyataḥ—of him who will accomplish. brahmaṇaspateḥ—of Brahmaṇaspati. manyuḥ—the mantra, an instrument of meditation (or his wrath). yathāvaśam—according to his wish. satyaḥ—true in effect (not in vain). anubhavat [?]—became. yaḥ—the Brahmaṇaspati who. gāḥ—the cows (carried off by the Paṇis). udājat—released and brought out from the cave. saḥ—that same Brahmaṇaspati. dive—to the gods of the world of the gods. vi abhajat ca—divided and distributed (then that herd of cows). mahīva rītiḥ—like the currents of rivers such as the Gaṅgā, splitting into branches and flowing apart. śavasā—by their own strength. pṛthak—(to the houses of the gods) separately. āsarat—went.

**भावार्थ (Bhāvārtha)**

The mantra, an instrument of meditation, of Brahmaṇaspati who accomplishes great works, became fruitful in truth according to his wish. The same Brahmaṇaspati who released from the cave the cows carried off by the Paṇis divided them among the gods in the world of the gods. Then that herd of cows, like the currents of rivers such as the Gaṅgā breaking into branches and flowing apart, went by their own strength separately to the houses of the gods.

**Printed English translation**

The purpose of Brahmaspathi, engaging in a great work, has been successful, according to his wish ; for he it was who recovered (the stolen) cattle for (the dwellers in) heaven ; and distributed them ; so that of their own power they took different directions, like (the branches of) a mighty river.

### Printed p. 45

**विशेषविषयगळु (Viśeṣaviṣayagaḷu)**

ब्रह्मणस्पतेः मन्युः सत्यः (*brahmaṇaspateḥ manyuḥ satyaḥ*): here the word मन्यु (*manyu*) is explained in two ways, as मननसाधनो मंत्रः क्रोधो वा (*mananasādhano maṃtraḥ krodho vā*), a mantra or (क्रोध (*krodha*)) anger; since Brahmaṇaspati is one who performs great works, the mantra of this god, or his anger as well, is true. And सः यथा कामयते तथा करोति (*saḥ yathā kāmayate tathā karoti*): this god acts as he wishes, that is, he performs his actions (duties) with complete independence; so it is explained.

यः गाः उदजत्, सः दिवे विभजच्च (*yaḥ gāḥ udajat, saḥ dive vibhajacca*): this Brahmaṇaspati, पणिभिरपहृताः गाः उदगमयत् (*paṇibhirapahṛtāḥ gāḥ udagamayat*), brought back the cows seized by the demons, bringing them out of the cave and protecting them; द्युलोकाय तत्रत्येभ्यो देवेभ्यः विभक्ताश्च अकरोत् (*dyulokāya tatratyebhyo devebhyaḥ vibhaktāśca akarot*): he divided them and gave them to the gods dwelling in heaven. This narrative has been told earlier.

महीव रीतिः शवसा पृथक् असरत् यथा गंगादीनां स्रोतः विभक्तं सत् निम्म देशाभिमुखं प्रसरति तथा विभिन्नः तत्तद्देवगृहं आत्मीयेन बलेन अगच्छत (*mahīva rītiḥ śavasā pṛthak asarat yathā gaṃgādīnāṃ srotaḥ vibhaktaṃ sat nimma deśābhimukhaṃ prasarati tathā vibhinnaḥ tattaddevagṛhaṃ ātmīyena balena agacchata*): as the current of the Gaṅgā flows towards low-lying regions and branches, so this herd of cows, in separate droves, reached the houses of the respective gods; thus the comparison with the Gaṅgā's current is stated.

The word शवः (*śavaḥ*) is read by Yāska among the names of strength (Ni. 3-9).
